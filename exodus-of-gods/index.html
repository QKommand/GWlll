Author's Preface

When disagreement became Divine war

The previous record ended with Abaddon refusing to accept the restriction of the Gift of Magic. His opposition did not remain a dispute within the pantheon. With the Margonites at his side, Abaddon turned against the other Gods and brought Divine conflict directly into the mortal world.

The consequences were greater than a change in religion or political power. Seas disappeared, landscapes were poisoned, boundaries between Tyria and the Mists were damaged, and an entire Human civilization was transformed by its allegiance to a fallen God.

When the war ended, the surviving Gods faced the evidence of what beings like themselves could do simply by fighting upon Tyria. Their answer was departure.

— Commander Q

I

Abaddon turns against the Five

Abaddon's opposition to the restriction of magic developed into open rebellion against the other Five Gods. He gathered his followers, including the increasingly transformed Margonites, and prepared for war against the pantheon.

The Margonites had once been a Human maritime civilization of the Crystal Sea. Their devotion to Abaddon now carried them into a very different future. Through his power, many were transformed into demonic beings capable of serving as soldiers and servants beyond the limits of ordinary Human life.

Abaddon's forces eventually assaulted the Gates of Heaven, bringing the rebellion into direct conflict with the combined power of the other Gods.

Research Note // The Margonites

Jadoth and the beginning of the Margonite transformation were introduced in the previous volume. By this stage of the record, the important change is political as much as physical: a Human civilization devoted to Abaddon had become the military body of his rebellion.

II

Divine war reaches the mortal world

The struggle between Abaddon and the Five was unlike the mortal wars that had followed the Gift of Magic. These were beings capable of exerting extraordinary magical power directly upon the world around them, and the landscape became part of the battlefield.

The conflict centered upon the region of the Crystal Sea and the lands that bordered it. What had been a great inland sea surrounded by inhabited and fertile territory was exposed to the destructive force of Divine warfare.

Abaddon was ultimately forced toward the location that would become known as the Mouth of Torment, where the final battle between the fallen God and the Five took place.

Commander Q // Observation

Mortal wars can redraw a border. Divine war redrew the map. That difference appears to have mattered greatly to the Gods who survived it.

III

The Five defeat Abaddon

Abaddon was defeated by the combined strength of the other Gods. His fall ended the immediate Divine war, but the victory did not restore the region that had become its battlefield.

The destruction altered the landscape on an enormous scale. The Crystal Sea was transformed into the Crystal Desert, while the lands farther south became the sulfurous region known as the Desolation.

At the site of Abaddon's defeat, the damage reached beyond geography. A tear formed between the physical world and the Mists, creating the Mouth of Torment and connecting the region to the Realm of Torment.

IV

Abaddon is imprisoned beyond Tyria

The Five did not destroy Abaddon. Instead, they imprisoned the fallen God and his surviving followers within the Realm of Torment, where Divine restraints were created to prevent him from returning to the mortal world.

Balthazar forged the chains used to bind Abaddon, while many of the Forgotten were given responsibility for guarding the prison and the passages associated with it.

The conflict also carried pieces of the mortal world into the Mists. The Temple of the Six Gods was pulled into the Realm of Torment as Abaddon and his followers were imprisoned.

Imprisonment ended Abaddon's freedom, but it did not end his ability to influence events. From within the Realm of Torment, he would spend more than a thousand years working through followers and servants in preparation for another attempt to escape.

V

The Five bury the memory of Abaddon

Imprisoning Abaddon addressed the physical danger he posed, but the Five also acted against his influence upon mortal civilization. They attempted to remove knowledge of the fallen God from the historical and religious record.

Abaddon's worship was suppressed, records associated with him were destroyed or sealed, and his great temple in Orr was sunk beneath the region now known as the Straits of Devastation.

The result was an unusual historical gap. Later generations could remember the actions of the Gods without necessarily knowing that Abaddon had once stood among them or understanding his individual role in events such as the Gift of Magic.

Commander Q // Observation

Destroying a record does not undo the event recorded within it. It only changes which generation has to rediscover the truth later.

VI

The Five leave the physical world

The war against Abaddon demonstrated the danger created when Gods intervened directly in Tyria. The Five had defeated the rebellion, but the scars across the Crystal Desert and the Desolation remained as evidence of the cost.

The surviving Gods chose to leave Tyria and entered the Mists, allowing Humanity and the other mortal peoples of the world to continue without the same degree of direct Divine intervention.

Their departure became known as the Exodus of the Gods.

The Exodus did not sever every connection between Tyria and the Divine. The Gods continued to govern their realms within the Mists, while blessings, avatars, temples, and other forms of Divine influence remained part of mortal life for centuries afterward.

Historical Distinction

The Exodus describes the Gods leaving Tyria itself. It should not be confused with any earlier reduction in their day-to-day involvement with mortal affairs. After 0 AE, the Five were no longer physically resident in the world.

VII

The Exodus becomes Year Zero

The departure of the Gods became one of the defining chronological boundaries in Tyrian history. The Mouvelian Calendar placed the Exodus at 0 AE, dividing history into the eras Before the Exodus and After the Exodus.

The change represented more than a calendar. Humanity had entered Tyria under the guidance of the Gods, developed kingdoms beneath their influence, received widespread magic from them, and survived a war among them. Now mortal civilizations would increasingly determine their own political futures.

The Gods left behind temples, religions, magical traditions, the divided Bloodstones, and unresolved conflicts that would continue long after their departure.

The age of direct Divine rule had ended. The age of mortal kingdoms was already beginning.

End of Volume V

The road continues

The Gods had left the physical world, but the nations they helped shape remained. Human kingdoms expanded, borders hardened, and Guilds accumulated influence that increasingly rivaled the authority of kings.
