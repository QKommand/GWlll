Historical Archive // Record 05

The Exodus of the Gods

1 BE – 0 AE

The mortal wars over magic had been contained.

The argument that caused them was not.

Abaddon believed the gods had taken back too much of the power he had given to Tyria. His refusal to accept their decision turned a dispute over magic into a war between gods.

Archive Context

The previous archive covers why magic was restricted and how the Bloodstone system worked. This record begins with the consequence: Abaddon's rejection of that decision and the divine war that permanently changed the geography and spiritual history of Tyria.

Record 01 // The Dispute

Abaddon refuses to accept the new order

Abaddon had supported giving greater magical power to mortals.

When the other gods chose to restrict that power, Abaddon opposed them.

The disagreement became irreconcilable.

The god of knowledge and water turned against the rest of the pantheon.

Record 02 // The Margonites

A mortal civilization follows its god into war

The Margonites had developed along the coasts surrounding the Crystal Sea and became a powerful maritime culture.

Their devotion to Abaddon drew them into his rebellion.

Abaddon began transforming his followers into ethereal demonic beings, beginning with figures such as Jadoth.

A human civilization was becoming an army for a fallen god.

Record 03 // Divine War

Abaddon turns on the pantheon

Abaddon declared war on the other gods and assaulted the Gates of Heaven.

He was powerful enough to fight more than one god, but could not overcome the combined strength of the others.

The resulting conflict was unlike the mortal wars that had preceded it.

Divine power began reshaping the world itself.

Record 04 // The Fall

The rebellion ends at the Mouth of Torment

The other gods defeated Abaddon.

His fall occurred near the place later known as the Mouth of Torment.

He and his followers were cast into the Realm of Torment.

Victory did not leave the world untouched.

Record 05 // The Crystal Sea

A sea becomes a desert

The force unleashed during Abaddon's defeat transformed the geography of the region.

The floor of the Crystal Sea rose.

Water vanished from vast stretches of the landscape, creating the Crystal Desert.

Lands farther south were twisted into the sulfurous Desolation.

A Permanent Scar

The Crystal Desert was not always a desert. Its landscape is a surviving physical record of a war between gods.

Record 06 // The Realm of Torment

The fallen god is sealed away

Abaddon was imprisoned within the Realm of Torment.

His transformed Margonite followers were imprisoned with him.

The barrier between Tyria and the fallen god's prison remained scarred by the violence of his defeat.

Abaddon was contained.

He was not powerless.

Record 07 // The Forgotten God

The victors try to erase his influence

After Abaddon's defeat, the remaining gods attempted to remove knowledge of him from the world.

Records were destroyed.

His Orrian temple was sunk, and other places associated with the fallen god were hidden or removed.

Over time, Abaddon became the forgotten member of the pantheon.

His followers did not forget him.

Record 08 // The Exodus

The gods withdraw from the mortal world

The war had shown the devastation that could occur when gods fought directly upon Tyria.

The remaining gods chose to leave the physical world and withdraw into the Mists.

This departure became known as the Exodus of the Gods.

The gods continued to influence their followers, but the age in which they openly walked among mortals had ended.

Record 09 // Year Zero

The Exodus becomes the center of recorded time

The event was significant enough that later Tyrian historians used it as the dividing point of the Mouvelian Calendar.

Years before the departure became BE — Before the Exodus.

Years after became AE — After the Exodus.

The world had entered a new age.

Record 10 // After the Gods

Mortals are left to shape the next age

The Six Gods had transformed the world, guided humanity, intervened in magical crises, and fought a war that altered the face of continents.

Now they were gone from Tyria.

Human kingdoms, guilds, dynasties, religions, and rivalries would increasingly determine the course of recorded history.

The next centuries belonged to mortals.
