THE EXODUS OF THE GODS

Volume V // The Divine and Human Age

The Exodus of the Gods

The departure from Arah, the end of direct Divine rule, and the world left behind.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

The Gods leave Tyria

The Gods had withdrawn from public life before, retreating into Arah while remaining physically present in Tyria.

The Exodus was different.

After Abaddon's rebellion and the destruction caused by the Divine war, the remaining Five chose to leave the physical world entirely.

In 0 AE, the age of Gods ruling from Arah came to an end.

Historical Caution // Motive

Later traditions connect the Exodus with the damage caused by direct Divine intervention. The withdrawal itself is established, but the private reasoning of each God is not fully preserved.

— Commander Q

I

The pantheon survives the Divine war

Abaddon's defeat left the Divine order changed.

He had been imprisoned within the Realm of Torment, his worship was suppressed, and the accepted pantheon continued as Five.

The physical world carried the scars of the conflict. The Crystal Sea had become the Crystal Desert, while the Desolation preserved another region devastated by Divine power.

The surviving Gods now faced the consequences of a war between members of their own pantheon.

II

The Five choose withdrawal

The remaining Gods decided they would no longer rule Tyria through direct physical presence.

They had guided kingdoms, regulated magic, answered mortal appeals, and intervened in the world for generations.

The war against Abaddon showed how destructive Divine intervention could become when the Gods themselves entered conflict.

Their answer was distance.

Commander Q // Observation

The war did not prove that Divine power was useless. It proved that even Divine power carried consequences.

III

The Exodus

In 0 AE, the Five departed physical Tyria and withdrew into the Mists.

Arah was left behind.

The Gods no longer lived within the city, received mortal rulers there, or governed the world from a physical Divine capital.

Their departure became important enough that Human chronology would later measure history around it: before and after the Exodus.

IV

The Gods remain beyond Tyria

The Exodus did not mean that the Gods ceased to exist.

They remained within the Mists and continued to possess Divine realms, servants, and connections to mortal religion.

Grenth remained tied to the Underworld, while other Divine powers continued to influence worship, visions, blessings, prophecy, and spiritual encounters.

Abaddon remained imprisoned separately within the Realm of Torment.

The Gods had become distant rather than absent.

V

Orr inherits Arah

The departure of the Gods did not empty Arah of meaning or power.

The city became the capital of Orr, whose people continued living among Divine temples, palaces, gardens, crypts, reliquaries, vaults, and ancient collections.

Arah had once housed the Gods themselves. After the Exodus, it became both a Human capital and a monument to the age that had ended.

Orrian religion preserved the memory of Divine presence and the hope that the Gods might one day return.

Commander Q // Observation

Orr did not inherit a forgotten ruin. It inherited the city the Gods had left behind.

VI

Mortal kingdoms govern themselves

Human civilization continued after the Exodus without a Divine court physically ruling from Arah.

Cantha, Elona, Orr, Ascalon, and other Human societies increasingly depended upon mortal rulers, armies, guilds, religious orders, and political institutions.

The Gods remained objects of worship, but the disputes of kingdoms were now more firmly in mortal hands.

The centuries that followed would be shaped by those kingdoms competing, expanding, allying, and eventually fighting wars of their own.

VII

The Gods leave their history behind

The Gods left physical Tyria, but the structures and consequences of the Divine Age remained.

The divided Bloodstones remained scattered across the world. The Crystal Desert and Desolation remained. Arah remained filled with Divine and ancient relics. Abaddon remained alive within his prison.

His public worship had been suppressed, but hidden monuments, records, and his reliquary preserved traces of the time when he had stood among the Six.

The Exodus ended direct Divine rule. It did not erase the world that rule had created.

Archive Note

What remains uncertain

The decision of the Five

The Gods withdrew collectively, but no complete record preserves the individual reasoning of Dwayna, Balthazar, Melandru, Lyssa, and Grenth.

The manner of departure

The Exodus placed the Gods beyond physical Tyria, but no detailed surviving account describes the exact process by which they left Arah.

What the Gods took with them

Extensive collections remained in Arah after the Exodus, but no complete inventory survives showing what was deliberately removed and what was left behind.

End of Volume V

The age of mortal kingdoms begins

The Gods had left physical Tyria, but Human civilizations remained.

Their kingdoms would now continue without a Divine court standing above them in Arah.
