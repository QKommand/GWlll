Historical Archive // Record 05

The Exodus of the Gods

0 AE

The gods had already withdrawn from everyday mortal life and secluded themselves within Arah.

Then the crisis over magic shattered even that distant balance.

Abaddon's rebellion scarred the world, transformed his followers, fractured the pantheon, and ended with the remaining gods leaving Tyria behind.

Archive Context

Before the events of 1 BE, the gods had already reduced their direct contact with humanity and withdrawn into Arah.

This earlier retreat was not the Exodus. The gods remained physically present within Tyria.

The later crisis over magic, Abaddon's rebellion, and the destruction caused by divine war led to a second and far greater withdrawal: the Exodus of the Gods in 0 AE.

Record 01 // Before the Rebellion

The gods are distant, but still present

By 1 BE, the gods no longer lived openly among humanity as they once had.

They had gradually withdrawn into Arah, the divine city built above the sacred Artesian Waters.

Statues, temples, priests, blessings, and religious traditions maintained the connection between mortals and the pantheon.

But the gods themselves had become increasingly secluded.

Earlier Retreat

This withdrawal into Arah occurred before Abaddon's rebellion.

The gods had not yet left Tyria. They remained within the physical world when the crisis over magic began.

Record 02 // The Crisis of Magic

The gods reverse Abaddon's gift

In 1 BE, Abaddon had dramatically expanded mortal access to magic.

The resulting wars became so destructive that King Doric traveled to Arah and begged the gods to intervene.

The gods agreed.

They gathered much of the world's accessible magic into the ancient Bloodstone and divided it into five major pieces.

Mortal access to magic was drastically reduced.

Record 03 // The Rebellion

Abaddon rejects the restriction of magic

Abaddon had overseen the distribution of widespread magical power to the mortal races.

When the other gods chose to restrict that power, he fiercely opposed them.

His protests failed.

The disagreement became open rebellion against the pantheon.

Record 04 // The Margonites

A human civilization becomes Abaddon's army

The Margonites did not begin as demonic beings.

They were originally human, and their maritime civilization had already existed for centuries.

By 175 BE, Margonite ships dominated much of the Unending Ocean.

Over time, they became increasingly devoted to Abaddon.

In 1 BE, the Margonite lord Jadoth called upon Abaddon while fleeing a pursuing Forgotten fleet.

Abaddon answered him, destroyed the pursuing ships, and granted Jadoth supernatural power.

Later accounts describe Jadoth as the first of the transformed Margonites.

Through Abaddon's influence, other Margonite followers were transformed into powerful supernatural beings.

These transformed Margonites became his servants and soldiers in the coming divine war.

Human Origins

Ancient references to Margonites before 1 BE describe the same civilization before its transformation.

The name Margonite therefore predates their demonic form.

Record 05 // Divine War

Abaddon wages war against the pantheon

Abaddon and his transformed Margonite forces rose against the other gods.

Their rebellion became a conflict between divine powers capable of reshaping the mortal world around them.

The war eventually reached the region remembered as the Gates of Heaven.

There, Abaddon faced the combined power of the other gods.

Record 06 // Abaddon's Defeat

The fallen god is defeated

Abaddon was defeated by the other gods.

But defeating a god did not end the catastrophe.

The force unleashed during his fall tore at the boundary between Tyria and the Mists.

The place where that rupture formed became known as the Mouth of Torment.

The Mouth of Torment

The Mouth of Torment became a great breach between the physical world and the Realm of Torment.

Record 07 // A World Scarred

The Crystal Sea becomes a desert

The divine conflict permanently transformed the surrounding region.

The Crystal Sea and its verdant coastline were devastated.

The former sea became the vast Crystal Desert.

Lands farther south were transformed into the sulfurous Desolation.

More than a thousand years later, the geography still carried the physical scars of the war among the gods.

Record 08 // Realm of Torment

Abaddon is imprisoned in the Mists

Abaddon was not destroyed.

The fallen god and his surviving followers were imprisoned within the Realm of Torment.

The fallen Temple of the Six Gods was pulled into the realm along with him.

Forgotten guardians would later watch over his prison.

From the Realm of Torment, Abaddon continued attempting to influence Tyria for more than a thousand years.

Record 09 // Divine Censorship

The Five try to erase Abaddon from history

Defeating and imprisoning Abaddon was not the end of the gods' response.

The remaining Five Gods attempted to erase knowledge of the fallen god from mortal civilization.

Records associated with him were destroyed or suppressed.

His worship was driven underground.

His temple in Orr disappeared beneath the land later known as the Straits of Devastation.

Record 10 // What Mortals Remembered

Later histories inherit an incomplete story

The effort to erase Abaddon left gaps in the historical record.

Later accounts often described the gift of magic as an act of "the gods" rather than preserving Abaddon's individual role.

His name survived largely through forbidden worship, hidden records, ancient ruins, and servants still working toward his return.

Record 11 // The Final Withdrawal

The gods prepare to leave Tyria

The gods had withdrawn from everyday mortal life long before the rebellion.

But they had still remained physically present within Arah.

Abaddon's rebellion demonstrated how catastrophic direct conflict between gods could become.

The pantheon now faced a larger question: whether gods could remain physically present in Tyria without placing the mortal world at risk.

Two Withdrawals

First, the gods withdrew from public mortal life into Arah.

Then, after Abaddon's rebellion, they withdrew from Tyria itself.

Record 12 // The Exodus

The Five leave the physical world

The remaining Five Gods departed Tyria and withdrew into the Mists.

This was fundamentally different from their earlier retreat into Arah.

They were no longer merely secluded from humanity.

They had left the physical world itself.

Their departure became known as the Exodus of the Gods.

Not Complete Silence

The Exodus did not mean all divine influence vanished.

Blessings, avatars, temples, divine realms, and occasional communication continued after the gods left Tyria.

Record 13 // Year Zero

History begins counting from the Exodus

The Exodus became one of the great dividing lines of Tyrian history.

The event established Year 0 of the Mouvelian calendar.

Years before it became BE — Before the Exodus.

Years following it became AE — After the Exodus.

The age when gods physically lived within Tyria had ended.

Mortal civilizations now inherited the world and everything the gods had left behind.
