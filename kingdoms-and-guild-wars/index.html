THE MORTAL AGE EXPANDS

Volume VI // The Age of Mortal Kingdoms

Kingdoms and Guild Wars

On the rise of Human kingdoms, the growing power of Guilds, and a thousand years of conflict, trade, prophecy, and catastrophe after the Exodus.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

Mortals inherit the consequences

The Exodus ended the age when the Gods physically inhabited Tyria, but it did not remove the world they had helped shape. Human kingdoms remained, magical traditions continued, and the Bloodstones were still hidden within the Ring of Fire.

Over the next thousand years, political power became increasingly mortal. Kingdoms expanded, international trade connected distant continents, and Guilds grew from organizations of common interest into institutions capable of rivaling rulers and influencing nations.

This period also produced disasters and discoveries whose consequences would extend into the modern age. The Bloodstones returned to the wider world, Glint recorded the Flameseeker Prophecies, Lord Odran forced open paths into the Mists, Shiro Tagachi transformed Cantha, and the Guild Wars repeatedly drew kingdoms into conflicts that began beyond the authority of kings.

— Commander Q

I

The Human kingdoms stand on their own

In 2 AE, Orr became an independent Human nation. Its close association with Arah and the magical traditions surrounding the former City of the Gods gave the kingdom a distinctive place among the Human powers of continental Tyria.

Alongside Ascalon and the territories that would become Kryta, Orr formed part of a changing political landscape in which Human nations increasingly governed themselves without the physical presence of the Gods.

The first century after the Exodus was remembered as a period of relative prosperity. The Bloodstones were still hidden, kingdoms continued to develop, and the conflicts that would later define the Guild Wars had not yet consumed the continent.

II

Abaddon's Mouth erupts

In approximately 100 AE, the volcano known as Abaddon's Mouth erupted. The five major Bloodstones that had been placed within the Ring of Fire were scattered across Tyria.

For the first time since the Gods had hidden them, the Stones were again present in regions accessible to mortal civilizations. Their magical power attracted the attention of organizations capable of pursuing that power beyond the borders of any single kingdom.

The first of the conflicts remembered as the Guild Wars began during this period as Guilds competed for control of the Bloodstones. What began as a struggle between powerful organizations eventually drew Ascalon and Kryta into the fighting.

Research Note // First Guild War

The exact beginning and end of the First Guild War are not securely dated. The surviving record places its beginning sometime around the period following the eruption of Abaddon's Mouth and directly connects the conflict to competition over the scattered Bloodstones.

III

Guilds become powers of their own

Guilds had existed as organizations for trade, professions, religion, magic, military service, and other shared interests. As their wealth and influence expanded, some began operating across political borders and accumulating power that individual rulers could not easily control.

Military Guilds became particularly influential. They could enforce laws, protect commercial interests, maintain armed forces, and support factions whose loyalties did not always align with the governments of the lands in which they operated.

The Guild Wars demonstrated what could happen when these organizations fought one another. Conflicts that began outside the structures of national government could eventually pull entire kingdoms into war.

Commander Q // Observation

A kingdom knows where its border ends. A Guild does not necessarily have that problem. Once organizations can move wealth, soldiers, and loyalty across borders, kings are no longer the only people capable of starting wars.

IV

The continents grow closer

While conflict reshaped continental Tyria, trade was connecting the wider Human world. In 221 AE, Cantha entered the Age of the Hedgehog and expanded formal trade with Tyria, Elona, and northern regions.

Goods were not the only things crossing the seas. Political ideas, religious traditions, travelers, and knowledge moved with the same networks. Human societies remained distinct, but their histories were becoming increasingly connected.

In 272 AE, Glint compiled the Flameseeker Prophecies, recording visions concerning events that would not reach their fulfillment for centuries.

At the time, the prophecy belonged to an uncertain future. By 1072 AE, its words would guide events surrounding the Mursaat, the Door of Komalie, and the rise of the Titans.

V

Kryta separates from Elona

In 300 AE, Kryta was established as a colony of Elona. Human settlement expanded throughout the region and increasingly brought settlers into conflict with the Centaurs who also inhabited the western lands.

By 358 AE, Kryta had gained its independence and emerged as a kingdom in its own right. It joined Ascalon and Orr as one of the major Human powers of continental Tyria.

This political independence also mattered to the Guild Wars. Ascalon and Kryta repeatedly became the kingdoms most directly drawn into struggles between Guilds, turning organizational rivalries into national wars.

VI

The Scarab Plague ends a Dynasty

In 452 AE, the Scarab Plague began spreading through Elona. The disease devastated the population of Istan and killed the ruling family of the Primeval Dynasty.

Fahranur and much of Istan were abandoned as survivors attempted to escape the plague. By 456 AE, the worst years had passed, but the political order that had governed Elona for centuries was gone.

Later rulers attempted to fill the vacuum, producing further wars and dynastic struggles. Over time, Istan, Kourna, and Vabbi emerged as the distinct provinces familiar to later generations.

VII

Elona faces an Undead conqueror

By 757 AE, the Undead lord Palawa Joko had established the Bone Palace within the Desolation. A century later, he turned his attention toward the living provinces of Elona.

In 860 AE, Joko invaded and conquered much of the northern region, earning the name Scourge of Vabbi. His advance was finally stopped in 862 AE by Turai Ossa at the Battle of Jahai.

Joko was defeated but not destroyed. Turai's agents, associated with the beginnings of what became the Order of Whispers, helped imprison the Lich within the region.

Turai's victory eventually led him toward a different ambition. In 868 AE, he led the Great Pilgrimage into the Crystal Desert in search of Ascension.

VIII

Lord Odran opens the Mists

In 851 AE, the Human arcanist Lord Odran achieved something once thought beyond mortal reach. Through powerful magic and the sacrifice of lives, he opened a portal into the Mists and eventually reached the Rift itself.

Odran traveled through the Hall of Heroes and established hidden portals linking the Rift to other worlds. For a time, these passages remained concealed by his enchantments.

The spirits of the Hall eventually killed Odran. With his death, the magic concealing his portals failed, leaving routes through the Mists available to others.

Commander Q // Observation

Odran did not discover the Mists. He discovered that a sufficiently determined mortal could force open a door into them. Those are very different accomplishments, and the second may be the more dangerous one.

IX

Shiro Tagachi transforms Cantha

In 872 AE, the Imperial bodyguard Shiro Tagachi assassinated Emperor Angsiyan during the Harvest Ceremony at the Harvest Temple.

Shiro was killed by Saint Viktor, Archemorus, and Vizu, but his death released a massive wave of magical energy known as the Jade Wind.

The catastrophe reshaped southern Cantha. The Jade Sea became solid jade, while the Echovald Forest was petrified. Entire cultures survived within landscapes that had been permanently transformed in a matter of moments.

Cantha entered the Age of the Dragon, carrying the consequences of Shiro's death into the centuries that followed.

X

The international Human world begins to pull apart

The trade networks that had connected Human societies for centuries became less stable during the tenth century AE. In 902 AE, Cantha ended long-standing trade agreements with the northern Human kingdoms by Imperial decree.

By 940 AE, Canthan diplomatic establishments in Ascalon and Elona had closed. Piracy and political changes further weakened the routes that had once connected distant Human powers.

The continents were not entirely isolated, but the era of expanding international contact had given way to a period in which regional concerns increasingly dominated political life.

XI

The Guild Wars return

The First Guild War did not settle the competition among the Guilds. At an uncertain later date, a Second Guild War again drew Ascalon and Kryta into conflict.

The incomplete dating of the first two wars makes their individual campaigns difficult to reconstruct, but their broader effect is clear. Guild organizations had accumulated enough influence that disputes between them repeatedly destabilized Human kingdoms.

The wars were therefore not simply national conflicts between Ascalon and Kryta. The kingdoms were participants in struggles whose origins lay in competing Guilds, access to magical power, territorial influence, and rival political interests.

Historical Caution

The surviving chronology does not provide secure beginning or ending dates for the First and Second Guild Wars. Their placement in this record therefore follows the known sequence without assigning dates the sources do not preserve.

XII

The bloodiest Guild War begins

In 1013 AE, the Third Guild War began. Ascalon and Kryta were once again drawn into widespread fighting, and the conflict eventually became more destructive than either of the Guild Wars that preceded it.

Orr initially remained outside the conflict. King Reza eventually sent Orrian forces into the war in an attempt to impose peace, but Orr's entry only increased the scale of the casualties.

The Third Guild War continued for decades. Human kingdoms remained focused upon one another while the balance of power beyond their borders was changing.

The Charr, driven northward centuries earlier during Human expansion into Ascalon, were preparing a campaign that would bring the Guild Wars to an abrupt end.

End of Volume VI

The road continues

By the beginning of the eleventh century AE, the Human kingdoms were still fighting a war that had already lasted generations. They did not know that the conflict was approaching its end for reasons none of them had chosen.
