THE MORTAL AGE EXPANDS

Volume VI // The Age of Mortal Kingdoms

Kingdoms and Guild Wars

On the kingdoms that inherited the post-Exodus world, the growing power of Guilds, and the wars, catastrophes, discoveries, and rivalries that shaped the centuries before the Searing.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

The world after the Gods

The Exodus ended direct Divine rule, but it did not empty the world of everything the Gods had touched. Arah remained in Orr. The Human kingdoms continued to grow. Magic remained part of mortal life, and the divided Bloodstones still carried the consequences of decisions made during the final years of the Divine Age.

What changed most was responsibility. Kings, councils, Guilds, priesthoods, armies, merchants, and scholars increasingly shaped the course of history without a pantheon physically ruling from Arah. Over the centuries that followed, Human civilizations became wealthier, more interconnected, and more politically complex, but they also became capable of sustaining conflicts on a scale that no single ruler could easily contain.

Guilds became part of that transformation. Some remained professional, religious, mercantile, or social organizations, while others accumulated wealth, territory, soldiers, and influence that crossed the boundaries of kingdoms. When those organizations turned against one another, their disputes could become wars large enough to pull nations into them.

Yet the Guild Wars were only one part of this age. Elona endured plague, dynastic collapse, and the rise of Palawa Joko. Cantha survived the Jade Wind. Lord Odran forced passages into the Mists. Glint recorded a prophecy whose meaning would remain uncertain for centuries. Trade drew distant Human civilizations together even as political change later began pulling some of them apart.

By 1013 AE, these histories converged upon the beginning of the Third Guild War. The kingdoms believed they were entering another Human conflict.

They were approaching the end of an age.

— Commander Q

I

The kingdoms inherit the post-Exodus world

In the first years after the Exodus, Human civilization did not have to rebuild from nothing. The Gods had left, but the kingdoms, cities, temples, institutions, and magical traditions that had developed during the Divine Age remained.

Orr stood apart because its capital, Arah, had once housed the Gods themselves. In 2 AE, Orr emerged as an independent Human nation and inherited not only the City of the Gods, but the religious and magical traditions that had grown around it.

Ascalon continued developing in the east, while the western Human territories that would eventually become Kryta followed their own political path. Beyond continental Tyria, Cantha and Elona remained powerful Human civilizations with separate rulers, calendars, traditions, and interests.

The Exodus had removed the Gods from physical rule. It had not created a single Human world.

What followed was an age of kingdoms.

II

Abaddon's Mouth erupts

For roughly a century after the Exodus, the largest pieces of the divided Bloodstone remained concealed within the volcano known as Abaddon's Mouth in the Ring of Fire.

Then the volcano erupted.

The five great Bloodstone pieces were scattered from the place where the Gods had attempted to keep them beyond easy mortal reach. The magic contained within them could influence whatever lay around them, and the Stones once again became objects that ambitious mortals could seek, study, and fight over.

Their return to the wider world helped ignite the first conflict remembered collectively as one of the Guild Wars.

Guilds competed for control of the Bloodstones and the magical power associated with them. As those struggles expanded, the fighting eventually drew Ascalon and Kryta into the conflict.

Research Note // First Guild War

The eruption of Abaddon's Mouth and competition over the scattered Bloodstones are securely associated with the First Guild War, but the surviving chronology does not preserve an exact beginning or ending year for the conflict.

Commander Q // Observation

The Gods scattered the Bloodstones to prevent magical power from being gathered too easily in one place. Once the Stones returned to the world, mortals began fighting over who would gather that power anyway.

III

Guilds become powers beyond borders

Guilds were not created by the wars that carried their name. They had long served as organizations built around professions, commerce, religion, magic, military service, scholarship, and other shared interests.

What changed was their scale.

Wealthy and militarized Guilds could operate across the territories of several rulers at once. They could move goods, information, influence, and armed forces through networks that did not necessarily end at a national border. Some became powerful enough that disputes between Guilds could destabilize the kingdoms in which they operated.

This made the Guild Wars different from ordinary wars between kings. Ascalon and Kryta could become participants in conflicts whose origins did not begin with either throne.

A rivalry between organizations could become a rivalry between cities, then territories, then armies, and eventually kingdoms.

Commander Q // Observation

Kings ruled territory. Guilds could rule loyalty. Once a Guild possessed enough wealth and soldiers, that distinction mattered.

IV

The continents grow closer

War did not prevent the Human world from becoming more connected. During the third century AE, maritime trade strengthened links between civilizations separated by enormous distances.

In 221 AE, Cantha entered the Age of the Hedgehog. Trade expanded between Cantha, the kingdoms of Tyria, Elona, and lands farther north. Merchants and sailors carried more than cargo between ports. Languages, fashions, religious practices, technologies, stories, and political ideas traveled with them.

The kingdoms remained distinct, but distance no longer meant isolation.

In 272 AE, another connection was made, though not through trade. Glint recorded the visions later known as the Flameseeker Prophecies.

The prophecy described events far beyond the lifetime of anyone then living. For centuries its meaning remained uncertain. Only much later would its words become tied to the Mursaat, the Door of Komalie, the Titans, and the events of 1072 AE.

Commander Q // Observation

A prophecy can exist for centuries before anyone knows which generation will be unfortunate enough to understand it.

V

Kryta becomes a kingdom

In 300 AE, Kryta was established as an Elonian colony on the western coast of continental Tyria.

Settlement expanded through lands already inhabited by other peoples, including Centaurs. The growth of the colony therefore became part of a longer contest over territory rather than the settlement of an empty frontier.

Over the following decades, the colony developed its own political identity. By 358 AE, Kryta had separated from Elona and become an independent kingdom.

The political landscape of continental Tyria now included three major Human powers: Ascalon, Kryta, and Orr.

Their proximity ensured that the conflicts of the Guilds would rarely remain separate from the interests of the kingdoms themselves.

VI

The Scarab Plague breaks the Primeval Dynasty

Elona's political order was transformed by a disaster that no invading army had caused.

In 452 AE, the Scarab Plague spread through Elona and devastated Istan. The disease killed enormous numbers of people and wiped out the ruling family of the Primeval Dynasty.

Fahranur, the First City, was abandoned along with other affected settlements as survivors fled the outbreak. By 456 AE, the worst years of the plague had passed, but the dynasty that had ruled for centuries was gone.

The disappearance of that authority opened a long period of political change. New rulers and competing dynasties attempted to control Elona, while regional differences became increasingly important.

Over time, Istan, Kourna, and Vabbi emerged as distinct centers of Elonian political life.

Commander Q // Observation

Kingdoms often write their histories around battles. Sometimes a dynasty ends because an enemy never needed to raise a sword.

VII

Palawa Joko rises in the Desolation

The lands scarred during the ancient war against Abaddon eventually became the stronghold of another threat.

By 757 AE, Palawa Joko had established the Bone Palace within the Desolation. The Undead lord remained there for generations before turning his attention toward the living kingdoms of Elona.

In 860 AE, Joko launched a major invasion and seized much of northern Elona. His conquest of Vabbi earned him the name Scourge of Vabbi.

His advance ended two years later when Turai Ossa defeated him at the Battle of Jahai in 862 AE.

Joko was defeated, but killing him proved another matter. He was instead imprisoned, leaving the threat contained rather than destroyed.

Turai's victory made him one of the great figures of Elonian history, but military triumph did not satisfy him. In 868 AE, he led the Great Pilgrimage into the Crystal Desert, seeking the Ascension once associated with the trials of the Forgotten.

Once again, the history of the mortal kingdoms crossed paths with something much older.

VIII

Lord Odran forces open the Mists

In 851 AE, the Human arcanist Lord Odran crossed a boundary that had once seemed to belong only to Gods, spirits, and the dead.

Through powerful magic and the sacrifice of lives, Odran learned to open portals into the Mists. His experiments eventually carried him through the Rift and into the Hall of Heroes.

From there, he established hidden portals connecting different worlds through the Mists. Odran concealed these passages behind enchantments, allowing him to move through a network that few mortals even knew existed.

His control did not last.

The spirits of the Hall eventually killed him. After Odran's death, the magic hiding his portals failed and the routes he had created became accessible to others.

The Mists had always surrounded Tyria. Odran's achievement was proving that mortal magic could forcibly open a road into them.

Commander Q // Observation

The Gods left Tyria through the Mists. Eight centuries later, a mortal decided that the same boundary looked more like a locked door than a warning.

IX

The Jade Wind transforms Cantha

In 872 AE, a single assassination transformed the geography of southern Cantha.

During the Harvest Ceremony at the Harvest Temple, Imperial bodyguard Shiro Tagachi betrayed and killed Emperor Angsiyan.

Shiro was confronted and slain by Saint Viktor, Archemorus, and Vizu, but his death released an extraordinary wave of magical energy.

The event became known as the Jade Wind.

Across the south, seawater hardened into the vast expanse later called the Jade Sea, while the Echovald Forest was petrified. Communities survived, but the landscapes that had supported them were permanently altered.

The Kurzicks and Luxons would spend centuries adapting to a world shaped by the aftermath of that single day.

Commander Q // Observation

The ancient Dragonrises transformed landscapes over ages. The Jade Wind is a reminder that enough magic concentrated in one mortal event could do something horrifyingly similar in moments.

X

Old connections begin to weaken

The maritime networks that had connected distant Human societies for centuries did not disappear all at once, but political decisions increasingly placed strain upon them.

In 902 AE, Cantha ended long-standing trade arrangements with the northern Human kingdoms by Imperial decree.

During the decades that followed, formal connections weakened further. By 940 AE, Canthan diplomatic establishments in Ascalon and Elona had closed, while piracy and regional instability made long-distance travel less dependable.

Cantha was not yet completely cut off from the outside world, but the direction of Imperial policy had changed. The age in which greater trade continually pulled the Human continents closer together had begun to reverse.

At the same time, the northern kingdoms were becoming increasingly absorbed in conflicts of their own.

XI

The Guild Wars return

The First Guild War did not settle the rivalries that had allowed Guilds to drag kingdoms into conflict. At some later point, fighting erupted again in what became known as the Second Guild War.

Once more, Ascalon and Kryta became deeply involved. The surviving chronology does not preserve enough detail to reconstruct the conflict year by year, and even its exact beginning and ending remain uncertain.

That uncertainty should not obscure what the first two Guild Wars demonstrate. Powerful Guilds had become a permanent feature of Tyrian politics, and their disputes could repeatedly become problems for entire nations.

The wars also changed the relationship between the kingdoms themselves. Repeated intervention in Guild conflicts gave Ascalon and Kryta reasons to see one another not simply as neighboring Human powers, but as enemies associated with rival factions and interests.

By the beginning of the eleventh century AE, another Guild War was approaching.

Historical Caution // First and Second Guild Wars

The surviving record establishes the order of the first two Guild Wars and their connection to Ascalon, Kryta, and powerful Guild organizations, but it does not provide secure dates for their complete duration. This archive therefore preserves the uncertainty rather than assigning unsupported years.

XII

The Third Guild War begins

In 1013 AE, conflict erupted again. The Third Guild War would become the longest and bloodiest of the struggles that carried the name.

Ascalon and Kryta were once more drawn deeply into the fighting. Guild rivalries, national interests, and decades of accumulated hostility became increasingly difficult to separate from one another.

Orr initially remained outside the war. Its position changed when King Reza attempted to intervene and restore peace between the other Human kingdoms. Orrian forces entered the conflict, but intervention did not bring the fighting to an end.

Instead, the Third Guild War continued for decades.

Human kingdoms committed soldiers, resources, and attention to fighting one another while another power watched from beyond their northern borders.

The Charr had not forgotten the lands lost during the expansion of Ascalon. Their own society had changed, and new forces were preparing them for war.

The Human kingdoms did not yet understand that the Third Guild War would not end because one Guild defeated another or one king finally imposed peace.

It would end because the Charr invaded.

Commander Q // Observation

For nearly a thousand years after the Exodus, mortals inherited the freedom to shape their own kingdoms. By 1013 AE, they had also inherited the ability to become so absorbed in fighting one another that they failed to see the next war approaching.

End of Volume VI

The wars of Guilds meet a different kind of war

The centuries after the Exodus had produced kingdoms, trade networks, Guilds, magical discoveries, dynasties, prophecies, and catastrophes. Human civilizations had survived plague, Undead conquest, political division, and landscapes transformed by magic.

Yet the Third Guild War left the northern kingdoms looking inward at the moment danger was gathering beyond them.

The Charr were preparing to cross into Ascalon.

Their invasion would shatter the balance between the Human kingdoms, end the Third Guild War, and begin the chain of events that led directly into the first Guild Wars campaign.
