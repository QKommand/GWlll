THE AGE OF KINGDOMS

Volume VI // The Age of Mortal Kingdoms

Kingdoms and Guild Wars

The rise of mortal kingdoms, the growing power of Guilds, and the centuries leading to the Third Guild War.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

The age belongs to mortal powers

After the Exodus, Human civilizations continued without a Divine court ruling from Arah.

Kings, dynasties, Guilds, merchants, armies, and religious institutions increasingly shaped the political world. Trade connected distant civilizations, while wars, plague, magical catastrophes, and old rivalries repeatedly changed them.

Among the most important new powers were the Guilds. Some accumulated enough wealth, influence, and military strength that their disputes could draw entire kingdoms into war.

— Commander Q

I

The Human kingdoms take shape

The early post-Exodus centuries strengthened the political identities of the Human world.

Orr inherited Arah and its Divine traditions. Ascalon remained a major eastern kingdom, while Cantha and Elona continued along their own Imperial and dynastic paths.

In 300 AE, Kryta was established as an Elonian colony. It became independent in 358 AE, creating another major Human power on continental Tyria.

Ascalon, Kryta, and Orr would become the principal northern kingdoms caught in the conflicts later known as the Guild Wars.

II

Guilds compete for power

The scattered Bloodstones eventually became objects of mortal ambition.

Powerful Guilds competed for access to the Stones and the magical power associated with them. Their struggles drew Human nations into larger conflicts remembered as the Guild Wars.

Guilds were not simply military companies. They could represent trade, professions, religion, scholarship, magic, and political interests. The strongest possessed resources and loyalties that crossed national borders.

This allowed a dispute between Guilds to become a war between kingdoms.

Historical Caution // Early Guild Wars

The First and Second Guild Wars are securely part of the historical sequence, but their complete dates and campaigns are not preserved well enough to construct a precise year-by-year chronology.

Commander Q // Observation

Kings ruled land. Guilds could carry money, influence, soldiers, and loyalty across the borders those kings controlled.

III

A more connected Human world

Maritime trade continued linking distant Human civilizations even while political rivalries grew.

Cantha traded with Tyria and Elona, carrying goods, knowledge, customs, and travelers between continents.

In 272 AE, Glint recorded the visions later known as the Flameseeker Prophecies.

Their meaning would remain uncertain for centuries, until events surrounding the Mursaat and the Door of Komalie brought the prophecy into the center of recorded history.

IV

Elona survives plague and conquest

In 452 AE, the Scarab Plague devastated Elona and destroyed the ruling family of the Primeval Dynasty.

Fahranur was abandoned, and the collapse of the dynasty began a long period of political change from which Istan, Kourna, and Vabbi emerged as distinct powers.

Centuries later, Palawa Joko invaded Elona from the Desolation and conquered much of the north.

In 862 AE, Turai Ossa defeated Joko at the Battle of Jahai. Joko was imprisoned rather than destroyed.

In 868 AE, Turai led the Great Pilgrimage into the Crystal Desert in search of Ascension.

V

Mortals push magic beyond old boundaries

The ninth century AE produced two events that revealed how much magical power mortals could command.

In 851 AE, Lord Odran used powerful magic and sacrifice to open passages into the Mists, eventually reaching the Hall of Heroes and creating hidden routes between worlds.

In 872 AE, Shiro Tagachi assassinated Emperor Angsiyan during Cantha's Harvest Ceremony. Shiro's death released the Jade Wind.

The Jade Sea hardened and the Echovald Forest was petrified, permanently transforming southern Cantha.

Commander Q // Observation

By this age, mortals were no longer merely inheriting ancient magic. They were producing events capable of changing worlds and landscapes themselves.

VI

Cantha's northern ties weaken

The trade networks linking Cantha to the northern Human kingdoms gradually weakened under Imperial policy.

In 902 AE, long-standing trade agreements with the Tyrian kingdoms were dissolved.

By 940 AE, Canthan embassies in Ascalon and Elona had formally closed, while growing Corsair activity made travel more dangerous.

Cantha was not yet completely isolated, but official contact with the wider Human world had sharply declined.

VII

The first wars fail to settle the rivalry

The First Guild War did not resolve the competition between powerful Guilds and the kingdoms drawn behind them.

A Second Guild War followed, again involving Ascalon and Kryta.

The exact chronology remains incomplete, but the pattern is clear. Guild rivalries had become a lasting part of northern Tyrian politics, repeatedly turning institutional disputes into national conflicts.

By the beginning of the eleventh century AE, the old hostility between Ascalon and Kryta had never truly disappeared.

VIII

The Third Guild War begins

In 1013 AE, war erupted again between Guilds aligned with the northern Human kingdoms.

Ascalon and Kryta became the first major kingdoms drawn into the conflict.

The Third Guild War would outlast the previous wars and become the bloodiest conflict to carry the name.

Orr would eventually be pulled into the fighting as well, but those later decades belong to the next record.

In 1013 AE, the important change was simpler: another Guild War had begun, and this one would still be raging when the Charr invaded more than half a century later.

Commander Q // Observation

The Third Guild War began as another Human conflict. It would end only when something larger forced the kingdoms to stop fighting one another.

End of Volume VI

The longest Guild War has begun

By 1013 AE, Human kingdoms had spent centuries building political, commercial, and magical power.

Now Ascalon and Kryta were at war again.
