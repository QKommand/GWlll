Volume II // The Ancient World

The Elder Races

On the Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun, and the different paths their civilizations followed after the Dragonrise.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Author's Preface

The survivors inherit a changed world

The previous record followed the Elder Races through the Dragonrise and the different choices they made to survive it. When the dragons returned to sleep, those choices did not disappear with the crisis that had produced them.

The Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun emerged into a world that had lost cities, knowledge, populations, and enormous quantities of accessible magic. Their alliance was gone, but each people still carried the consequences of what had happened during the Dragonrise.

Their histories now separated. Some rebuilt. Some withdrew. Some declined. Others entered wars that would leave only fragments of once powerful civilizations behind.

Research Note // The Elder Races

The term "Elder Races" is useful for describing the ancient peoples known to have survived the previous Dragonrise, but it should not be mistaken for a single culture. Their societies, magical traditions, beliefs, and political interests remained distinct.

— Commander Q

I

Five peoples, five paths

The end of the Dragonrise did not restore the world that had existed before it. The surviving Elder Races entered the next age carrying different strengths, losses, and ideas about what should happen to the magic that remained.

The Seers had preserved enormous reserves of magic. The Mursaat had survived by withdrawing from ordinary perception. The Forgotten maintained their connection to Glint, while the Dwarves and Jotun continued along paths shaped by their own traditions and losses.

Their shared war against the Elder Dragons was over. From this point forward, their histories are better understood separately.

Elder Race

Seers

Powerful magical practitioners whose surviving works include the Shadowstone, the Bloodstone, ancient artifacts, and rites of transformation.

Elder Race

Mursaat

Powerful spellcasters who survived the Dragonrise beyond ordinary perception and later fought a devastating war against the Seers.

Elder Race

Forgotten

Serpentine spellcasters whose history became closely connected to Glint, the Crystal Desert, and the preservation of ancient knowledge.

Elder Race

Dwarves

A long-lived civilization whose fortresses, kingdoms, craftsmanship, and magical protections endured through later ages.

Elder Race

Jotun

Descendants of a once sophisticated magical civilization whose surviving stelae preserve fragments of astronomy, history, and lost learning.

II

The Seers return magic to the world

Once the Elder Dragons had returned to dormancy, the Seers began releasing the magic they had preserved during the Dragonrise.

The world receiving that magic was no longer united against a common threat. Surviving Mursaat accounts describe scattered peoples competing violently over access to magical power as it returned.

The same act that had helped preserve Tyria through the Dragonrise therefore created a new problem. Magic could sustain civilization, but it could also become something civilizations fought to possess.

The Mursaat viewed the Seers' decisions with particular hostility. Their resentment over the failed assault against Zhaitan had survived the Dragonrise, and the return of magic did nothing to repair that division.

Commander Q // Observation

Preserving magic solved the problem of keeping it away from the dragons. It did not solve the question of what everyone would do with it once the dragons were gone.

III

The Seers

The Seers were far more than the creators of the Bloodstone. Surviving ruins and records from Castora reveal a complex civilization with temples, fortified settlements, magical craftsmen, formal training, and rites capable of permanently transforming those who underwent them.

Seer artisans worked with advanced forms of magic that included teleportation, illusion, soul-bound security, magical concealment, chronomancy, and the creation of enchanted instruments. Their surviving artifacts show a culture that approached magic as both a discipline and a craft.

The dagger known as Porsul Ret could allow an attuned wielder to teleport to the place where the weapon struck. Accounts preserved by Isgarren also describe a Seer assassin who created illusory copies of the dagger and moved between them during combat.

The Soul Key could seal containers using fragments of its owner's soul, while the Ombraband produced light capable of revealing magically hidden or shrouded people and objects. The enchantment upon the Ombraband remained functional long after the civilization that created it had vanished.

Research Note // Castora

Modern investigation of Castora has found unusually concentrated magic across the island. The Seer quarry is specifically described as containing pure, untouched ley magic, adding another piece to the surviving picture of Seer magical practice.

IV

The Divitium Nava

Among the most revealing discoveries on Castora is the Divitium Nava, a Seer rite that could elevate those who successfully completed it into a changed state of existence.

The journal of a Seer named Lasia describes the rite as an honor that could bring lasting prestige to a family. Her elder sister, Meliore, had already undergone the transformation and later worked as a skilled magical artisan.

Success was not guaranteed. Lasia followed the required procedure but was rejected by the process. Her body weakened afterward, her ability to move deteriorated, and healers believed the damage would eventually overtake her.

Her account also reveals something of everyday Seer culture. Apprentices trained under accomplished craftspeople, specialists worked with chronomantically infused materials, and advancement depended upon exceptional magical control.

A damaged Seer inscription found elsewhere on Castora declares that the Seers considered themselves masters and protectors of Tyria who had no need of gods or spirits to guide them. The same inscription claims that they could rise toward godhood through their own rites and strength.

Research Note // Seer Belief

The inscription preserves how at least some Seers understood themselves. It should be read as evidence of Seer ideology rather than proof that their claims about godhood were objectively true.

V

The Seer–Mursaat War

The hostility left by the Dragonrise eventually became open war between the Seers and Mursaat.

Castoran records preserve part of the conflict from inside Seer society. Lasia wrote that what had once been described as small skirmishes had grown into a larger war. Her brother Mazeo left Castora as the conflict intensified, while her sister Meliore received orders to transfer to the bastions of Stellis Quor to assist preparations for the siege of Apavim.

The war was severe enough that Seer outposts on Castora were scheduled for abandonment. Another surviving record, written on the final night before a Seer community left the island, describes temples being abandoned and guardians being permanently bound to protect Castora's secrets.

Those leaving did not expect to return.

Their fear was justified. The Mursaat possessed Spectral Agony, a magical attack that proved devastating against their enemies. The Seers were eventually reduced to only a small surviving population.

Commander Q // Observation

The Castoran journals change the scale of this war. It was not only a clash between ancient magical powers. There were families waiting for each other, craftsmen reassigned to fortresses, settlements preparing to close, and people who understood that leaving home might mean abandoning it forever.

VI

The abandonment of Castora

Castora was not simply forgotten. At least some of its Seer settlements were deliberately evacuated as the conflict with the Mursaat worsened.

A surviving account from the Cloister of Stars records the final night before departure. The writer had already accepted that the people leaving Castora were unlikely ever to return and believed they were marching toward the extinction of their own civilization.

Sidony ordered magical beasts to remain behind as guardians. Their bindings were designed not to weaken with time, allowing them to continue protecting Castora's secrets even if the Seers themselves never returned.

This deliberate sealing helps explain why so much Seer material survived on the island. Temples, magical devices, artifacts, ley-rich sites, and bound guardians remained after their creators were gone.

Castora became less a dead ruin than a sealed archive whose keepers never came home.

VII

The Mursaat

The Mursaat survived both the Dragonrise and the war that followed, although they too emerged diminished. Their ability to exist beyond ordinary perception remained one of the defining features of their magic.

Their own historical writings preserve a civilization deeply conscious of what it believed the other Elder Races had done during the assault against Zhaitan. The Seers in particular became lasting enemies.

The Mursaat combined powerful spellcraft with magical constructs, including the Jade creations used in their ancient wars. Spectral Agony gave them another weapon that proved extraordinarily difficult for their enemies to resist.

Much later, the ruins of Janthir would preserve further evidence of their presence, while Mursaat knowledge, weapons, and political ambitions would again become important to the wider history of Tyria.

Research Note // Mursaat Sources

Mursaat historical tablets are invaluable because they preserve details unavailable elsewhere, but they were written by participants with strong opinions about the other Elder Races. Their factual claims and their judgments of motive should not be treated as the same thing.

VIII

The Forgotten

The Forgotten survived the Dragonrise with a history already tied closely to Glint. Their magic had freed her from Kralkatorrik's control, and that relationship continued long after the dragons returned to sleep.

Later accounts describe the Forgotten as wardens and guides who shepherded the development of other peoples. They also became closely associated with the Crystal Desert, where their ruins, rituals, and guardians would endure for ages.

Their relationship with Glint became one of their most lasting legacies. Guardians remained with her for centuries, and knowledge connected to the Forgotten continued to shape events surrounding the Crystal Desert long after the civilization itself had largely disappeared from view.

Later histories also connect the Forgotten with the Human Gods and wars fought in their service. That part of their story belongs to the next age, when the Gods became an increasingly important force in Tyria.

IX

The Dwarves

The Dwarves followed one of the longest continuous histories among the Elder Races. They survived the Dragonrise and continued building, fighting, and preserving their traditions through later ages.

Their strongholds and settlements spread through the Shiverpeak Mountains, and later Dwarven history would include kingdoms, civil wars, powerful forges, and elaborate magical defenses.

Some of those defenses survived their creators. Sealed Dwarven ruins could remain inaccessible long after the people who placed the enchantments were gone, showing that Dwarven magical engineering was built with remarkable endurance.

Unlike the Seers, whose surviving history increasingly became a story of disappearance, the Dwarves remained visible participants in Tyrian affairs for thousands of years.

Their final transformation belongs to a much later age.

X

The Jotun

The Jotun survived, but the civilization preserved in their oldest records was gradually lost.

Ancient Jotun possessed extensive knowledge of magic, astronomy, and the movement of the heavens. Their surviving stelae preserve fragments of that learning, although later generations retained only portions of what their ancestors had known.

The loss of magical power was not absolute. Long after the creation of the Bloodstone changed the magical world, exceptional Jotun bloodlines could still produce individuals of considerable power.

One such figure was Nuada, the mother of Dagda and one of the few known female chieftains in Jotun history. Her magical strength frightened the elders of the greater tribe enough that they killed Nuada and most of her family.

Dagda was spared because of her youth and because the elders wished to preserve the magical potential of the bloodline under their control.

Nuada's history shows that the decline of Jotun magic was not a clean disappearance. Powerful remnants survived within particular families even as the wider civilization lost much of the knowledge that had once defined it.

XI

What the Elder Races left behind

By the later ancient era, the five peoples once joined against the Elder Dragons had reached very different places in their histories.

The Seers had been devastated by war, leaving behind sealed settlements and magical works whose purpose was increasingly forgotten. The Mursaat endured in secrecy. The Forgotten remained bound to Glint and the Crystal Desert. The Dwarves continued building kingdoms in the mountains, while the Jotun survived amid the remnants of their earlier civilization.

Their influence remained embedded throughout Tyria. Ancient artifacts continued functioning. Magical seals remained closed. Ruins preserved records. Bloodlines carried abilities their origins could no longer fully explain.

Even the conflicts of this age endured. The Bloodstone, Mursaat concealment, Seer artifacts, Forgotten rituals, Dwarven magic, and Jotun knowledge would all surface again in later history.

Commander Q // Observation

An ancient civilization does not have to remain alive to remain influential. Sometimes its longest history begins after it is gone, when everyone else starts discovering what it left behind.

XII

A new power enters the record

The history of the Elder Races did not end when another great power entered Tyria. Their civilizations continued along their separate paths while beings later worshiped as the Human Gods became increasingly active in the world.

Surviving traditions place the Gods first in Orr, where the Artesian Waters and the future city of Arah became closely connected to their presence.

Their arrival marked the beginning of another major transition in Tyrian history. Old peoples remained, ancient rivalries continued, and the artifacts of the previous ages were still scattered across the world, but the balance of power was beginning to change.

The next record follows that transition through the surviving traditions of Orr, the spread of Humanity, and the rise of civilizations that would define much of the written history that followed.

End of Volume II

The road continues

The Elder Races survived the Dragonrise, but survival carried them toward very different futures. Some endured openly, others vanished from ordinary sight, and some left behind little more than ruins, artifacts, and histories waiting to be recovered.

Their story does not end here. The Bloodstone, Glint, the Mursaat, Forgotten knowledge, Dwarven strongholds, and the remnants of Seer civilization will all return when later events bring them back into the historical record.
