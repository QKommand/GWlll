THE SURVIVORS

Volume II // The Ancient World

The Elder Races

On the Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun, and the different histories that followed the previous Dragonrise.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

The histories separate

The previous record ended with magic returning to Tyria after the Elder Dragons fell back into slumber. The alliance that had formed against them did not survive the crisis in the same way its members did.

The Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun carried different losses into the age that followed. Old disagreements remained unresolved, populations had been diminished, and knowledge that had once supported entire civilizations now survived unevenly.

From this point forward, there is no single history of the Elder Races. Their stories divide into wars, migrations, kingdoms, ruined settlements, surviving bloodlines, magical traditions, and artifacts that continued functioning long after the people who made them were gone.

Research Note // Elder Races

"Elder Races" refers here to the five peoples associated with the ancient alliance against the Elder Dragons. It should not be read as a complete list of every ancient people or intelligent species living in Tyria during this period.

— Commander Q

I

A world with magic again

When the Elder Dragons returned to slumber, the Seers began releasing the magic they had preserved during the Dragonrise.

The power returned to a world whose old alliances had already fractured. Mursaat records describe peoples fighting over access to magic as it became available once more.

The return of magic was therefore both a recovery and a source of new conflict. What had been hidden to protect the world from the Elder Dragons could now be used again by the peoples who survived them.

The old alliance no longer determined how that power would be shared.

Commander Q // Observation

The Seers had solved one problem: how to keep magic away from the dragons. The world that followed still had to answer a harder question — who should control it once the dragons were gone?

II

The Seers

The surviving works of the Seers reveal a civilization whose relationship with magic extended far beyond the creation of the Bloodstone.

Their artifacts show expertise in teleportation, illusion, concealment, soul-bound magic, magical craftsmanship, and other forms of power that remain difficult to reproduce even in later ages.

The dagger known as Porsul Ret could allow an attuned wielder to return across great distances to a place marked by the weapon.

The Ombraband produced light capable of exposing people and objects hidden by magical concealment, while another Seer focus known as the Soul Key could be used to create magically sealed containers bound to fragments of their owner's soul.

These objects were not isolated curiosities. Together with the Bloodstone, Shadowstone, surviving temples, magical fountains, astrolabes, and other ruins, they reveal a society that had developed several distinct disciplines of magical practice.

III

Castora and the Seer world

The island of Castora preserves some of the richest surviving evidence of Seer civilization. Its ruins include temples, outposts, magical workshops, ancient artifacts, and areas saturated with concentrated magic.

The island itself remains unusually rich in magical energy. At a Seer quarry, the local ley magic has been described as particularly pure and untouched, while other sites preserve fountains, enchanted devices, and magical materials left behind by the civilization that once occupied them.

Castora also provides something artifacts alone cannot: fragments of ordinary Seer life.

Surviving journals refer to families, apprenticeships, craftsmen, outposts, military assignments, and the difficult expectations placed upon those attempting advanced magical work.

Together these records make Castora more than an archaeological site. It preserves a partial view of a living Seer society before war forced its settlements to close.

Research Note // Castora

Castora's concentrated magic, Seer artifacts, and surviving ruins establish a major Seer presence on the island. They do not by themselves prove that the Seers created Castora's ley network or every magical feature found there.

IV

The Divitium Nava

One of the most revealing Seer records concerns a rite known as the Divitium Nava.

A Seer named Lasia recorded her preparation for the process alongside her brother Mazeo. Their elder sister, Meliore, had undergone the rite years earlier and was remembered as one of those who had ascended before them.

The process demanded extraordinary control. Lasia underwent it but failed. Her body weakened, movement became increasingly difficult, and healers believed that the damage would eventually overtake her.

Her journal does not provide enough information to define every change brought about by a successful Divitium Nava. What it does establish is that the Seers possessed formal transformative rites whose outcome could permanently alter the individual who underwent them.

A separate damaged inscription from Castora preserves an equally revealing statement of Seer belief. Its writer calls the Seers the stewards and masters of Tyria and declares that they required no gods or spirits to guide them, claiming instead that they could rise toward godhood through their own rites and strength.

Research Note // Seer Belief

The inscription records how at least some Seers understood their own civilization. Its claims about mastery and godhood belong to Seer ideology and should not be treated as objective descriptions of what the rites actually achieved.

V

The Seer–Mursaat War

The hostility created during the Dragonrise eventually developed into open war between the Seers and the Mursaat.

Castoran records preserve the conflict from inside Seer society. Lasia wrote that fighting once described as small skirmishes had grown into something much larger. Her brother Mazeo left Castora, while Meliore received orders connected to the bastions of Stellis Quor and preparations for the siege of Apavim.

The war placed enough pressure on Castora that Seer outposts were scheduled to be abandoned in the years ahead.

The Mursaat possessed a devastating magical attack known as Spectral Agony. The Seers eventually developed methods of resisting it, but the war had already taken an enormous toll.

By the end of their conflict, the Seers had been reduced to a small surviving population, while the Mursaat had also suffered heavy losses.

Commander Q // Observation

Ancient wars often survive as a list of battles and ruined cities. The Castoran journals preserve something rarer: people realizing that their homes may not survive the war even if they do.

VI

The abandonment of Castora

Castora was not simply forgotten. Surviving records show that at least some Seer communities deliberately prepared their settlements for abandonment.

A record from the Cloister of Stars describes the final night before departure. The writer believed the people leaving the island were marching toward the destruction of their civilization.

Sidony ordered magical beasts to remain behind to protect the island's secrets. Their bindings were deliberately made to endure rather than weaken with time.

The Seers therefore left more than empty buildings. They left active guardians, sealed places, enchanted artifacts, and magical systems intended to continue functioning after their departure.

Those defenses survived.

Historical Caution // Extinction

The Castoran writer expected the Seers to face extinction, but that expectation should not be read as proof that every Seer died. Later history confirms that at least a small number survived.

VII

The Mursaat endure

The Mursaat survived both the Dragonrise and the war that followed it, although their civilization was also greatly diminished.

Their ability to exist beyond ordinary perception remained central to their survival. Mursaat magic also included powerful Jade constructs, Spectral Agony, and other forms of spellcraft that made them dangerous even in greatly reduced numbers.

Their surviving historical writings reveal a people who continued to define themselves partly through the memory of the Elder Alliance and the failures they believed had occurred during the assault on Zhaitan.

At some point after their ancient wars, Mursaat activity also reached places such as Janthir and the Fire Islands, where later history would again bring them into contact with the wider world.

Those later events belong to later volumes. For now, the important point is that the Mursaat did not vanish with the end of their war against the Seers.

They endured in secrecy.

VIII

The Forgotten

The Forgotten followed a history increasingly shaped by their relationship with Glint.

Their magic had freed her from Kralkatorrik's control during the Dragonrise, and Glint in turn protected the Forgotten while the Elder Dragons remained active.

Later accounts describe the Forgotten as wardens who guided the development of other peoples. They became closely associated with the lands that would later be known as the Crystal Desert, where their ruins, rituals, guardians, and magical works remained for ages.

Their connection with Glint endured for centuries. Forgotten guardians remained with her, while their knowledge would later become tied to the trials and rituals surrounding Ascension.

Still later, the Forgotten would serve the Human Gods and fight wars in their name. That belongs to the next stage of their history rather than the ancient Dragonrise itself.

Research Note // Forgotten Chronology

Older traditions place the Forgotten entering the world of Humans at dates that do not align neatly with evidence of their much older involvement in the previous Dragonrise. These records should be preserved as different traditions rather than forced into one exact chronology.

IX

The Dwarves

The Dwarves remained one of the most enduring peoples associated with the Elder Alliance.

They continued building settlements and strongholds throughout the Shiverpeak Mountains, eventually developing kingdoms and political traditions that remained active well into recorded history.

Dwarven construction was supported by magical defenses capable of lasting far beyond the people who created them. Sealed ruins and protected chambers could remain inaccessible long after their original occupants were gone.

The wider Depths of Tyria preserve additional examples of long-lasting magical security. Enchanted weapons, magical guardians, traps, gateways, and other defenses remained active around important chambers and underground complexes.

Not all of those systems can be securely assigned to the Dwarves. The Depths contain structures from different periods and cultures, and some of their builders remain unknown.

What the surviving evidence does show is that the underground world had been occupied, defended, and reshaped repeatedly over a very long period.

Commander Q // Observation

The Depths are easy to mistake for one civilization's underground world. They are closer to layers of history built on top of one another: old roads, fortresses, gateways, tombs, mines, ruins, and defenses whose creators did not all belong to the same age.

X

The Jotun

The Jotun survived, but much of the civilization remembered by their oldest records did not.

Their ancestors possessed sophisticated knowledge of magic, astronomy, history, and the movements of the heavens. Surviving stelae preserve fragments of this learning, but later generations inherited only parts of what their civilization had once known.

Magical ability did not disappear completely. Important bloodlines could still produce individuals of considerable power long after the Jotun had lost much of their earlier magical tradition.

One example was Nuada, the mother of Dagda and one of the few known female chieftains in Jotun history. Nuada possessed enough magical power that tribal elders feared what her family represented.

Nuada and most of her family were killed. Dagda was spared so that the magical potential carried through the family line would not disappear completely.

Her history demonstrates that Jotun decline was not the same thing as the complete disappearance of Jotun magic. Fragments of the old civilization survived in knowledge, bloodlines, and monuments even when the society capable of fully understanding them was gone.

XI

Beyond the Elder Races

The five peoples of the Elder Alliance were never the only ancient inhabitants of Tyria.

Other intelligent peoples possessed histories of their own, some reaching far beyond the surviving written record. They should not be folded into the Elder Races simply because they are ancient.

Among the clearest examples are the Djinn, ancient elemental beings whose history is deeply connected to Elona.

Djinn existed in the region long before the rise of the later kingdoms that would dominate its written history. They were powerful magical beings with their own identities, relationships, territories, and traditions.

Their presence is an important reminder that the Elder Alliance represents one part of the ancient world, not the whole of it.

Other peoples likewise possessed histories that did not pass through the alliance against the Elder Dragons. Their stories become more visible as the historical record moves into particular regions.

Research Note // Djinn and Seers

Djinn and Seers share some visual similarities, but no surviving evidence currently establishes that the two peoples are related. The resemblance should remain an observation rather than a historical conclusion.

XII

What the ancient world left behind

By the later ancient period, the peoples once joined against the Elder Dragons had reached very different places in their histories.

The Seers had been devastated by war, leaving abandoned settlements, guarded ruins, artifacts, and magical knowledge scattered behind them.

The Mursaat endured in secrecy.

The Forgotten remained tied to Glint and the Crystal Desert. The Dwarves continued building civilizations through the mountains, while the Jotun preserved only fragments of the knowledge their ancestors had once possessed.

Beyond them, ancient peoples such as the Djinn remained part of regions whose histories would soon become more clearly documented.

The physical world preserved all of them unevenly. Ruins remained sealed. Guardians continued their watch. Enchanted weapons still defended important places. Magical artifacts continued functioning, and knowledge crossed from one civilization into another.

The ancient world had not disappeared.

It had become the foundation beneath everything that followed.

Commander Q // Observation

Ancient civilizations rarely vanish cleanly in Tyria. Their people may disappear, but their doors stay locked, their guardians keep moving, their weapons remain enchanted, and sooner or later someone finds what they left behind.

Archive Note

Where the ancient record remains incomplete

The surviving histories of the Elder Races are not evenly preserved. Some peoples left written accounts, while others are known largely through ruins, inscriptions, artifacts, later traditions, and the testimony of their enemies.

The Seers

Castora has greatly expanded the surviving record of Seer civilization, but large portions of their history remain unknown. Their exact origins, the full nature of their rites, and the fate of many settlements are still unresolved.

The Seer–Mursaat War

Surviving evidence establishes a major conflict and the devastation it caused, but the complete sequence of campaigns, settlements, and battles has not been recovered.

The Depths of Tyria

The underground world contains ruins, enchanted guardians, weapons, gateways, fortresses, and other structures belonging to different cultures and ages. Similarity of function does not prove common origin.

Ancient peoples beyond the Alliance

The five Elder Races provide one framework for the ancient world, but they do not define its full population. Djinn and other peoples possessed their own histories outside the Elder Alliance.

These gaps should remain visible. The purpose of the archive is not to make the ancient world appear more complete than the surviving evidence allows.

End of Volume II

A new power enters the record

The Dragonrise had ended thousands of years earlier, but its survivors and their works continued shaping Tyria.

The Seers had left behind the Bloodstone and a scattered magical legacy. The Mursaat remained hidden. The Forgotten guarded knowledge connected to Glint. The Dwarves continued building, the Jotun carried fragments of a lost intellectual tradition, and ancient peoples such as the Djinn remained rooted in their own regions.

Into this world came beings from beyond the Mists whose presence would begin another major transformation.
