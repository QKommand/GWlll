The Elder Races

Author's Preface

The histories separate

The previous Dragonrise had briefly united the Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun against a common threat. Once the Elder Dragons returned to slumber, that alliance did not survive.

Magic gradually returned to Tyria, while the power sealed within the Seers' Bloodstone remained preserved. The civilizations that survived now followed separate paths shaped by old grievances, war, migration, and decline.

Research Note // Elder Races

"Elder Races" refers here to the five peoples of the ancient alliance. They were not the only intelligent peoples living in Tyria during this period.

— Commander Q

I

The Seers

The Seers emerged from the Dragonrise with a powerful magical tradition and the original Bloodstone still preserving the magic hidden within it.

Their surviving works reveal expertise far beyond that single artifact. Seer magic included teleportation, concealment, magical detection, soul-bound devices, enchanted weapons, and complex transformative rites.

Artifacts such as Porsul Ret, the Ombraband, and the Soul Key preserve fragments of those disciplines, while ruins across Castora provide a broader view of the civilization that created them.

II

Castora preserves the Seer world

Castora contains some of the clearest surviving evidence of Seer civilization.

Temples, outposts, workshops, enchanted devices, and magically saturated sites survive across the island. Journals also preserve glimpses of families, apprenticeships, craftsmen, soldiers, and settlements.

These records show the Seers as more than mysterious artificers. They lived within organized communities whose people trained, worked, formed families, and served in wars that increasingly threatened their homes.

Research Note // Castora

Castora establishes a major Seer presence and preserves extraordinary magical sites, but not every magical feature on the island can be securely attributed to Seer construction.

III

The Divitium Nava

Seer records from Castora describe a transformative rite known as the Divitium Nava.

Lasia prepared for the rite alongside her brother Mazeo. Their elder sister, Meliore, had successfully undergone it years earlier.

Lasia failed. Her body weakened afterward, and healers believed the damage would eventually kill her.

The surviving record does not fully explain what a successful transformation accomplished, but it shows that the Seers pursued forms of personal advancement capable of permanently altering the individual.

A damaged Castoran inscription also preserves the belief of at least some Seers that their people could rise through their own knowledge and rites without depending upon gods or spirits.

Historical Caution // Seer Belief

Claims of mastery and godhood belong to Seer belief. They should not be treated as proof that the Divitium Nava literally made its practitioners Gods.

IV

The Seer–Mursaat War

The hostility left by the previous Dragonrise eventually became open war between the Seers and Mursaat.

Castoran journals describe skirmishes growing into a larger conflict. Seer forces were drawn toward places such as Stellis Quor and Apavim, while settlements on Castora prepared for evacuation.

The Mursaat wielded Spectral Agony, a devastating magical attack. The Seers eventually developed defenses against it, but the conflict had already reduced both civilizations.

Commander Q // Observation

The Castoran journals preserve the war at a human scale: not only armies and fortresses, but people watching the conflict move steadily closer to home.

V

The Seers leave Castora

Surviving records show that Seer communities prepared Castora for deliberate abandonment as the war intensified.

A record from the Cloister of Stars describes the final night before departure. Sidony ordered magical beasts to remain behind and protect the island's secrets.

The bindings placed upon those guardians were designed to endure. Castora was therefore left with active defenses, sealed places, and magical works that continued functioning long after its communities were gone.

Historical Caution // Seer Survival

Castoran records feared the destruction of Seer civilization, but later history confirms that at least a small number of Seers survived.

VI

The Mursaat endure in secrecy

The Mursaat survived the Dragonrise and their later war with the Seers, though their population had also been greatly reduced.

Their ability to exist beyond ordinary perception remained central to their survival, alongside powerful magical practices such as Spectral Agony and the use of Jade constructs.

Later Mursaat activity reached places including Janthir and the Fire Islands. Those events belong to later periods, but they confirm that the Mursaat did not disappear with the ancient war.

VII

The Forgotten remain with Glint

The Forgotten followed a history increasingly shaped by their relationship with Glint.

They had freed her from Kralkatorrik's control during the Dragonrise, and she later protected them while the Elder Dragons remained active.

The Forgotten became closely associated with the lands later known as the Crystal Desert, leaving behind guardians, rituals, ruins, and magical traditions connected to Glint and eventually to the trials of Ascension.

Their later service to the Human Gods belongs to the next age of their history.

VIII

The Dwarves build through the Shiverpeaks

The Dwarves became one of the longest-lasting peoples of the former Elder Alliance.

They established settlements and strongholds throughout the Shiverpeak Mountains, developing kingdoms and political traditions that remained active well into recorded history.

Their underground world also preserves long-lasting defenses, sealed chambers, guardians, mines, roads, and fortifications.

Not every ancient structure in the Depths of Tyria belongs to the Dwarves. The region contains layers of construction from different peoples and periods.

IX

The Jotun lose much of their old knowledge

The Jotun survived, but later generations inherited only fragments of the civilization their ancestors had once possessed.

Older Jotun had developed sophisticated knowledge of magic, astronomy, history, and the movements of the heavens. Surviving stelae preserve pieces of that learning.

Magical ability did not vanish completely. Powerful bloodlines still appeared long afterward, including the family of Nuada and her daughter Dagda.

Their history shows that cultural decline did not mean the complete disappearance of Jotun magical potential.

X

The alliance was only part of the ancient world

The five Elder Races were never the only ancient inhabitants of Tyria.

Other peoples possessed histories that developed outside the alliance against the Elder Dragons. Among the clearest examples are the Djinn, ancient elemental beings deeply connected to Elona.

Their presence is a reminder that the Elder Alliance provides one path through ancient history, not a complete list of every civilization that existed during the age.

Research Note // Djinn and Seers

Djinn and Seers possess some visual similarities, but no surviving evidence establishes a historical relationship between the two peoples.

XI

The ancient world becomes inheritance

By the later ancient period, the former allies had reached very different places in their histories.

The Seers had been devastated by war. The Mursaat endured in secrecy. The Forgotten remained tied to Glint. The Dwarves continued building through the mountains, while the Jotun preserved fragments of a much older intellectual tradition.

Their ruins, artifacts, magical defenses, guardians, and surviving knowledge remained embedded in Tyria. Later civilizations would repeatedly discover and reuse what these peoples had left behind.

Commander Q // Observation

In Tyria, a civilization can disappear without its work disappearing with it. Ancient history survives in sealed doors, active guardians, enchanted objects, and knowledge waiting for someone else to find it.

Archive Note

What remains incomplete

Seer history

Castora preserves much more of Seer civilization than was previously known, but their origins, many settlements, and the full nature of their rites remain uncertain.

The Seer–Mursaat War

The conflict and its devastation are established, but its complete sequence of campaigns and battles has not been recovered.

The Depths of Tyria

Underground ruins and magical defenses belong to different cultures and periods. Similar construction or purpose is not enough to identify a builder.

End of Volume II

Another people enters the record

The ancient civilizations remained scattered across Tyria in ruins, surviving populations, and magical works.

Into this older world came beings from beyond the Mists, followed by the people who would build the next great civilizations of the historical record.
