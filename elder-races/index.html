Author's Preface

Survival did not create unity

The previous record ended with the Elder Dragons gradually returning to slumber. That tells us how the Dragonrise ended, but it does not tell us what happened to the peoples who survived it.

Five civilizations appear repeatedly in the surviving accounts of that age: the Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun. Modern scholars often group them together as the Elder Races, but that name should not give the impression that they shared one culture, government, or destiny.

They cooperated because the Elder Dragons threatened all of them. Once that cooperation failed, each people chose a different way forward, and those choices are the subject of this record.

— Commander Q

I

An Alliance against the Elder Dragons

During the previous Dragonrise, the Seer leader Sidony rallied the Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun into an Alliance against the Elder Dragons. Their cooperation brought together several of the most advanced civilizations known from the ancient world.

The Alliance allowed its members to exchange knowledge and prepare a coordinated resistance, but the surviving record also suggests that distrust existed among them. Their common enemy was powerful enough to bring these peoples together, but it was not enough to erase the differences between them.

II

The Alliance breaks at Zhaitan

One of the greatest tests of the Alliance came when the Forgotten and Mursaat prepared an assault against the Elder Dragon Zhaitan. Before the attack began, Sidony withdrew the Seers from the operation, while the Dwarves and Jotun also failed to join the battle.

The Forgotten and Mursaat continued without them and suffered devastating losses. For the Mursaat, the absence of their allies was not simply a failed military decision. They remembered it as a betrayal.

That interpretation would become one of the defining divisions of the ancient world. The Alliance had been created to preserve its members against the Elder Dragons, but the failed assault left those same members increasingly suspicious of one another.

Commander Q // Observation

Alliances formed during a catastrophe are often strongest while everyone agrees on the enemy. The real test comes when survival demands sacrifice and each side begins asking who is carrying the burden. The Elder Alliance failed that test.

III

Two answers to the same threat

After direct resistance proved unable to stop the Elder Dragons, the Seers and Mursaat chose very different methods of survival.

The Seers turned their attention toward denying the Dragons access to magic. Their work produced the Shadowstone and eventually the Bloodstone, whose origin was covered in the previous volume. The important point here is what that solution demanded from the other Elder Races: their magic would have to be placed beyond the reach of the Dragons as well.

The Mursaat refused. Rather than surrender their magic to the Bloodstone, they used their Shadowcraft to shift themselves into a shadowed existence out of phase with ordinary Tyria. In doing so, they escaped the Dragonrise while keeping their magical power.

The same crisis had therefore produced two opposing philosophies. The Seers attempted to preserve the world by removing magic from the Dragons' reach, while the Mursaat preserved themselves by withdrawing from the world altogether.

IV

The Forgotten free Glint

The Forgotten pursued a different path. In approximately 1680 BE, they performed a ritual upon Glint, a Champion and Scion of the Elder Dragon Kralkatorrik, freeing her will from her master's control.

The ritual cost Glint much of her former memory, but it allowed her to choose her own allegiance. She became an ally of the surviving Elder Races and helped conceal them from the Elder Dragons as the Dragonrise approached its end.

The relationship between Glint and the Forgotten continued after the Dragons returned to slumber. In later ages, the Forgotten would become closely associated with the Crystal Desert and with the trials that Humans would know as Ascension.

Research Note // Forgotten Chronology

Human traditions place the arrival of the Forgotten from the Mists at approximately 1769 BE. That account exists alongside much older evidence connecting the Forgotten to the Elder Alliance during the previous Dragonrise. I therefore treat the 1769 BE tradition as a Human historical account rather than a secure date for the beginning of the Forgotten presence in Tyrian history.

V

The old betrayal becomes a new war

The Mursaat eventually returned after the Elder Dragons had fallen back into slumber, but their resentment toward the Seers had survived with them. The resulting Seer–Mursaat War continued for centuries and became one of the longest conflicts inherited from the broken Elder Alliance.

The Mursaat possessed a devastating magical technique known as Spectral Agony. The Seers eventually developed a defense against it, but their answer came too late to change the course of the war. The Seers were driven to near extinction, while the Mursaat also suffered severe losses.

After the conflict ended, the Mursaat established Bava Nisos in Janthir upon the battleground where their war with the Seers had reached its conclusion. Long after both civilizations faded from ordinary knowledge, their ruins and weapons preserved evidence of a feud that had begun during the age of the Elder Dragons.

VI

Two civilizations continue on different paths

The Dwarves and Jotun did not share the violent post-Dragonrise history of the Seers and Mursaat, but neither civilization emerged from the ancient age unchanged.

The Dwarves rebuilt and remained a recognizable part of Tyrian civilization. Their settlements spread through the Shiverpeak Mountains, and later generations established kingdoms such as Deldrimor. Dwarven tradition also preserved memories of ancient threats long after much of the wider world had forgotten them.

The Jotun followed a much different course. Their ancestors had possessed sophisticated knowledge of magic, astronomy, and the movements of the heavens, but much of that learning disappeared as their civilization declined. Their surviving stelae became fragments of a far greater intellectual tradition.

The contrast is important. Both peoples survived the Dragonrise, yet survival alone did not determine what their civilizations would become afterward.

VII

The ancient world leaves its inheritance

By the end of this period, the Elder Alliance existed only as history. The civilizations that had formed it were now divided by war, isolation, decline, and different ideas about how their knowledge should be preserved.

Their influence did not disappear with them. The Bloodstone remained. Glint remained free. The Forgotten carried their knowledge forward, the Dwarves rebuilt, and the ruins of the Seers, Mursaat, and Jotun preserved evidence that later civilizations would repeatedly rediscover.

Many of the mysteries encountered in later Tyrian history therefore begin here, but their later consequences belong in the periods where they actually unfold. There will be time enough to return to the Bloodstones, the Mursaat, Ascension, and Glint when those stories reach the foreground again.

Commander Q // Observation

Ancient history has a habit of looking finished only because most of its witnesses are gone. In Tyria, ruins have an inconvenient tendency to remind us that old decisions are rarely as dead as we think they are.

VIII

History begins to change hands

While the Elder Races followed the paths left to them after the Dragonrise, another power had already entered the history of Tyria. The Six Human Gods came to the world from the Mists and first set foot at the Artesian Waters in Orr during this ancient period.

Their arrival did not immediately make Humanity the center of Tyrian history. That transition came later. By 786 BE, the Six Gods appeared in Cantha and brought Humans with them, beginning the first securely dated Human settlement in the chronology that follows.

The next volume therefore leaves the Elder Races behind for a time and follows the Gods, Humanity, and the beginning of an age whose records become increasingly familiar to modern historians.
