Historical Archive // Record 02

The Elder Races

Ancient Tyria – 1769 BE

The previous Dragonrise did not leave an empty world behind.

Several ancient civilizations survived, each carrying a different piece of Tyria's oldest knowledge into the ages that followed.

Archive Context

This record focuses on the elder races themselves rather than retelling the Dragonrise that nearly destroyed them. Their histories matter because their discoveries, rivalries, rituals, and ruins continue influencing Tyria thousands of years later.

Record 01 // Those Who Remained

Five peoples carried the ancient world forward

The surviving records identify five especially ancient civilizations: dwarves, Forgotten, jotun, mursaat, and Seers.

They were not one culture and did not share one destiny.

Some remained visible in the world. Some declined. Some hid. Some nearly vanished entirely.

Record 02 // The Seers

Masters of ancient magic

The Seers were among the most magically advanced peoples of ancient Tyria.

Their greatest surviving legacy was the Bloodstone, originally created as part of their attempt to preserve magic during the ancient Dragonrise.

Their later war with the mursaat reduced the Seers to near extinction.

By the Guild Wars era, only remnants of their civilization remained.

Lasting Legacy

The Seers disappeared as a civilization, but one of their creations became central to human magical history thousands of years later.

Record 03 // The Mursaat

The Unseen Ones

The mursaat were powerful spellcasters capable of shifting themselves between Tyria and the Mists.

Their withdrawal from the ancient struggle allowed them to survive while other elder civilizations suffered enormous losses.

Their conflict with the Seers continued after the Dragonrise, and their use of Spectral Agony helped bring the Seers close to extinction.

Centuries later, humans in Kryta would encounter the mursaat and worship them as the Unseen Ones.

Record 04 // The Jotun

A civilization remembers what it lost

The jotun were once far more sophisticated than the scattered societies encountered in later ages.

Ancient stelae preserve fragments of their history and describe an older age in which giant peoples held greater influence over Tyria.

Over time, the jotun lost much of their former knowledge, organization, and magical power.

Their surviving monuments became some of the few records remaining from the ancient world.

Record 05 // The Dwarves

One of Tyria's longest-lived civilizations

The dwarves survived into recorded human history while preserving traditions reaching back into the ancient world.

Their civilization eventually centered around the kingdom of Deldrimor in the Shiverpeak Mountains.

Dwarven legends remembered ancient forces such as the Great Destroyer, long before most peoples understood its connection to Primordus.

Of the elder races, the dwarves remained one of the most visible links between ancient and modern Tyria.

Record 06 // The Forgotten

Serpents, caretakers, and ritualists

The Forgotten were an ancient serpent race with deep knowledge of magic and the Elder Dragons.

Human traditions later claimed that the Six Gods brought them into Tyria around 1769 BE.

Older evidence, however, places the Forgotten within the struggles against the Elder Dragons long before humanity's recorded arrival.

Their later history would become closely tied to Glint, the Crystal Desert, and the trials of Ascension.

Historical Uncertainty

The Forgotten are a good example of how Tyrian history can contain competing traditions. Human accounts describe one origin, while older events suggest their presence stretches much farther back.

Record 07 // Glint's Liberation

The Forgotten change a dragon champion

Around 1680 BE, the Forgotten performed a ritual upon Glint.

Glint had served Kralkatorrik as one of his champions.

The ritual freed her from the Elder Dragon's control, allowing her to develop an independent will.

This act became one of the Forgotten's most important contributions to later Tyrian history.

Record 08 // A Changing World

The elder age gives way to another

By the time humanity began entering the historical record, the elder races no longer dominated Tyria.

The Seers were nearly gone. The mursaat had withdrawn. The jotun had declined. Dwarven civilization remained strong but regional. The Forgotten increasingly acted as guardians rather than rulers.

The world was ready for new civilizations to occupy the spaces left behind.
