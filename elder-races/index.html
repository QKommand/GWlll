The Elder Races

Ancient Tyria

When the Elder Dragons returned to sleep, Tyria was not empty. Five ancient peoples remained, carrying memories of a world most later civilizations would never know.

Some preserved knowledge. Some lost it. Some disappeared into the Mists. And some would survive long enough to shape history thousands of years later.

Archive Context

The exact chronology of the elder races is incomplete. Much of what survives comes from ancient stelae, magical ruins, later discoveries, and records preserved by civilizations that were already in decline.

Record 01 // Those Who Remained

Five elder peoples survive

The peoples most often remembered as Tyria's elder races were the dwarves, Forgotten, jotun, mursaat, and Seers.

They were among the most magically advanced civilizations known to have survived the previous Dragonrise.

Yet survival did not leave them untouched.

Their alliances had fractured, populations had fallen, and entire bodies of knowledge had already begun to disappear.

Record 02 // The Seers

The architects of the Bloodstone

The Seers were among the most powerful magical peoples of ancient Tyria.

During the Dragonrise, they developed the Shadowstone and later the Bloodstone as a way to conceal magic from the Elder Dragons.

Their conflict with the mursaat would eventually devastate their civilization.

Lasting Legacy

The Seers' Bloodstone survived into the human age and became central to the later history of magic.

Record 03 // The Mursaat

Those who stepped outside the world

The mursaat refused the plan to surrender their magic to the Bloodstone.

Instead, they withdrew from the elder alliance and phased themselves into the Mists.

This allowed them to avoid much of the destruction that consumed the other civilizations.

Their feud with the Seers continued for centuries and pushed the Seers close to extinction.

Record 04 // The Jotun

Giants who remembered the stars

The jotun were once far more advanced than the brutal tribes later encountered in the Shiverpeaks.

Their ancient stelae preserved knowledge of the sky, celestial movements, and the earlier Dragonrise.

Over time, however, jotun civilization declined.

Much of their old knowledge was forgotten, leaving later generations surrounded by the remains of an age they could no longer fully understand.

Record 05 // The Dwarves

A people beneath the mountains

The dwarves endured through the ancient Dragonrise and established civilizations within and around the Shiverpeak Mountains.

Their oldest surviving architecture dates back thousands of years.

In later ages, dwarven history would become dominated by the kingdom of Deldrimor, the Stone Summit rebellion, and legends of the Great Dwarf and Great Destroyer.

Future Connection

The dwarves would ultimately become one of the first peoples to directly confront Primordus and his Destroyers during the next Dragonrise.

Record 06 // The Forgotten

Guardians and caretakers

The Forgotten were an ancient serpentine people whose history reaches back to the previous Dragonrise.

Later human traditions claimed that the Forgotten arrived in Tyria from the Mists in 1769 BE.

Those traditions sit beside older evidence showing that the Forgotten had already existed during the Elder Dragon conflicts.

In later ages they became remembered as caretakers, teachers, and guardians of powerful magical knowledge.

Record 07 // The Forgotten and Glint

Breaking a dragon's control

In approximately 1680 BE, the Forgotten performed a ritual upon Glint, the scion and champion of Kralkatorrik.

The ritual freed Glint from the Elder Dragon's control.

She lost much of her previous memory, but gained the ability to choose her own path.

Glint later helped conceal surviving peoples until the Elder Dragons finally returned to slumber.

Record 08 // The Age of Giants

A remembered age after the dragons

Jotun traditions describe an Age of Giants after the Elder Dragons entered hibernation.

According to these stories, giant peoples including the jotun and norn once ruled over and guided parts of Tyria.

How literally these traditions should be understood remains uncertain.

What is clear is that the centuries following the Dragonrise gave surviving peoples room to rebuild.

Record 09 // A Changing World

Younger races move into the open

With the Elder Dragons asleep, the balance of power began changing.

Races such as the charr, kodan, and tengu became increasingly prominent.

The charr united beneath the Khan-Ur and expanded their territory across the north.

Older civilizations remained, but they no longer stood alone at the center of Tyrian history.

Record 10 // Before Humanity

An ancient world awaits newcomers

By the time humanity entered the historical record, Tyria had already endured civilizations rising and falling, wars between ancient magical peoples, and at least one devastating Dragonrise.

The ruins, grudges, artifacts, and surviving races of this older world would become part of humanity's inheritance, whether humans understood them or not.

Soon, another force would arrive from the Mists.

The Six Gods.
