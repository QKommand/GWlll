THE SEERS AND THE ELDER DRAGONS

Peoples Archive // Elder Race

The Seers

On an ancient people who fought the Elder Dragons, learned to contain immense magical power, forged defenses against threats from beyond Tyria, and left works that survived long after their civilization faded.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

More than the creators of the Bloodstone

For much of modern history, the Seers have been known through fragments. Their surviving appearances were rare, their civilization seemed to have vanished, and much of what remained of their history was tied to the Bloodstone and their ancient war with the Mursaat.

The surviving record now gives us a broader view. The Seers fought the Elder Dragons, studied ways to store immense amounts of magic, developed powerful artifacts, built constructs and magical infrastructure, and understood threats that could reach Tyria through the Mists. Their civilization included artificers, rituals, fortified sites, and methods of working directly with ley energy.

The Bloodstone remains one of their greatest known achievements, but it should no longer be allowed to stand in for everything the Seers were.

What survives instead points toward a civilization that spent much of its history trying to understand how Tyria could be protected from powers capable of reaching it from both within and beyond the world.

— Commander Q

I

An ancient civilization faces the Dragons

The Seers were already an advanced magical people when the Elder Dragons rose during the previous Dragonrise. They lived alongside other ancient civilizations including the Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun.

The Seers did not simply hide from the Dragonrise. They took part in the struggle against the Elder Dragons and joined the other Elder Races in an alliance intended to resist them. The Seer leader Sidony played an important role in that coalition.

Direct resistance proved costly. The Elder Dragons were not merely large enemies that could be defeated through ordinary warfare. They consumed the magic of Tyria itself, making every prolonged conflict part of a much larger problem.

The Seers eventually began looking for another answer. Rather than relying only upon destroying the Dragons, they turned toward controlling the magic upon which those Dragons fed.

II

The assault on Zhaitan divides the Alliance

One of the greatest divisions among the Elder Races came when the Forgotten and Mursaat prepared an assault against the Elder Dragon Zhaitan.

Before the attack began, Sidony withdrew the Seers from the operation. The Dwarves and Jotun also failed to join the battle, leaving the Forgotten and Mursaat to continue without them.

Their losses were devastating. The Mursaat remembered what happened as a betrayal, and the anger created during the failed campaign survived long after the Elder Dragons returned to slumber.

The Seers, meanwhile, had shifted their attention toward another plan. Their efforts became focused upon the development of the Shadowstone and eventually the Bloodstone.

Commander Q // Observation

Sidony's withdrawal is one of those moments where the same event can look entirely different depending upon who survived it. The Mursaat remembered allies abandoning them before a battle. The Seers appear to have decided that another battle would not solve the larger problem.

III

The Seers learn to contain power

The Seers created artifacts known as Shadowstones during the previous Dragonrise. These objects acted as conduits for magical energy, increasing the magical abilities of the Seers who worked with them while also containing excess power.

Shadowstone was therefore more than a weapon. It was part of a larger method for gathering, holding, and directing magical energy. That knowledge became important as the Seers searched for a way to remove large quantities of uncorrupted magic from the reach of the Elder Dragons.

The surviving Seer Isgarren still recognizes Shadowstone as ancient magic belonging to his people. His ability to interact with dormant Shadowstone power also shows that the connection between the material and the Seers was deeper than simple ownership of an artifact.

The work eventually inspired something much larger.

Research Note // Shadowstone and Bloodstone

Isgarren directly identifies Shadowstone as ancient Seer magic used to store immense power and confirms that one technology inspired the other. The Bloodstone should therefore be understood as part of a longer Seer history of magical containment, rather than an isolated invention.

IV

The Bloodstone preserves Tyria's magic

Seer research eventually produced the original Bloodstone, an artifact capable of storing an enormous amount of magical energy. The Seers used it as part of their answer to the Elder Dragons.

Much of Tyria's remaining uncorrupted magic could be gathered into the Bloodstone and removed from the environment where the Dragons might otherwise consume it. The plan depended upon the surviving ancient races surrendering access to part of their own magical power for the sake of preserving it.

The Mursaat refused. Rather than place their magic within the Seers' system, they relied upon Shadowcraft to withdraw from ordinary Tyria and survive the Dragonrise elsewhere.

The Bloodstone remained after the Elder Dragons returned to slumber. Thousands of years later, the Human Gods came into possession of the artifact and changed its purpose, eventually dividing it into the five Bloodstones known to later Human history.

Commander Q // Observation

The first Bloodstone was not created to decide who deserved magic. It was created because something was eating the world's magic. Later civilizations found new uses for the same solution.

V

The artificers of Eternity's Garden

Surviving Seer sites show that artifacts such as the Bloodstone were produced by a civilization with a developed tradition of magical craftsmanship. Eternity's Garden preserves one of the clearest examples of that work.

The forge there was used by Seer artificers capable of weaving pure ley magic into powerful objects. Their work included artifacts such as Ancora and Legavo, while the same tradition is connected to relics that became important far beyond Seer history.

Among those relics were the Scepter of Orr and the Staff of the Mists. Both were created as part of efforts to protect Tyria against extraplanar threats.

This greatly widens what we know about Seer priorities. Their civilization was not focused only upon the Elder Dragons. They also understood that danger could enter Tyria through the Mists and developed magical works intended to defend the world against those threats.

VI

The Seers prepare for threats beyond Tyria

The creation of the Scepter of Orr and Staff of the Mists shows that the Seers understood the danger of hostile powers entering Tyria from beyond the world. Their defensive thinking therefore extended beyond the Dragon Cycle.

The Elder Dragons were creatures tied to Tyria and its magic. Extraplanar threats presented a different problem, arriving through the Mists rather than rising from within the world itself. The Seers developed artifacts intended to help protect Tyria against that kind of intrusion.

Those defenses did not stop every later arrival. The Six Gods eventually crossed from the Mists and entered Tyria. After their arrival they were drawn toward the powerful magical waters beneath the land that became Arah.

The surviving record does not tell us whether the Seers encountered the Gods at this time, whether the ancient defenses were still functioning, or whether those defenses were ever intended to prevent beings like the Six from entering.

It establishes only that the Seers had recognized extraplanar intrusion as a danger before the Gods became part of Human history.

Open Historical Question

The arrival of the Gods and their movement toward the magical waters of Orr should not be treated as evidence that the Seers controlled Arah or the Artesian Waters. What existed around those waters before the Gods established themselves there remains an important unanswered question.

VII

Castora preserves the world they built

The ruins of Castora preserve some of the clearest surviving evidence of Seer civilization. Here, their history can be studied through more than isolated artifacts. Structures, constructs, workshops, magical defenses, ritual sites, and ley-powered systems remain together within the same landscape.

At the Lost Basilica, ancient Seer ruins stand near the Artesian Ley Spring. Across the complex lies the Shattered Capstone, a heavily damaged structure protected by Seer constructs where exposed ley energy can still be observed.

Other sites preserve similar relationships between construction and natural magic. Artesian water and ley energy are present around the Temple of the Blessed Mantle, while ley energy also rises through the Spire of Exaltation.

These locations show that Seer construction often existed beside or directly interacted with powerful magical phenomena. The exact purpose of individual structures, including the Shattered Capstone, remains uncertain.

Commander Q // Observation

Castora is valuable because it gives us something ruins elsewhere rarely provide: context. A strange doorway by itself tells us very little. A doorway beside Seer constructs, Seer machinery, a Seer forge, and a functioning magical system tells us much more. It gives us something against which other ancient sites can finally be compared.

VIII

Divitium Nava

Seer knowledge was preserved through more than machinery and artifacts. Their civilization also practiced rites capable of granting or awakening extraordinary magical understanding.

One such rite was Divitium Nava, performed at the Spire of Exaltation and associated with the artifact Ancora. Surviving knowledge of the rite shows that its completion could grant an individual enormous magical power and understanding.

The ritual demonstrates that Seer magical practice was not divided neatly between technology and spellcraft. Artifacts, ley energy, constructed sites, inherited knowledge, and ritual could all form parts of the same magical system.

How common such rites were within Seer civilization and who was permitted to undergo them remain unknown.

IX

The old alliance ends in war

The Elder Dragons eventually returned to slumber, but the division created during the previous Dragonrise did not disappear with them. The Mursaat eventually returned, carrying their anger toward the Seers with them.

The resulting Seer–Mursaat War continued for centuries. The Mursaat wielded the devastating magical technique known as Spectral Agony, while the Seers eventually developed a defense against it.

That answer came too late to save their civilization. The Seers were driven close to extinction, while the Mursaat also suffered severe losses during the long conflict.

The Mursaat later established Bava Nisos in Janthir upon ground connected to the final stages of the war. The ruins and weapons left behind preserved evidence of a conflict whose origins reached all the way back to the failed alliance against the Elder Dragons.

X

A civilization becomes a memory

By the time Human kingdoms dominated much of the written historical record, the Seers had almost disappeared from ordinary knowledge. Their cities no longer stood as living centers of civilization, and most people encountered their history only through ruins or artifacts whose creators had been forgotten.

Their works continued to shape events despite that disappearance. The Bloodstone passed into the hands of the Human Gods and became central to the later history of mortal magic. The Scepter of Orr survived into the age of Human kingdoms and became part of conflicts involving the dead, the Mists, and the fate of Orr.

Seer structures and magical systems also continued to function long after the people who built them were gone. Constructs remained active, ancient seals survived, and artifacts retained power across periods of history that destroyed entire civilizations.

The Seers had faded from the world, but they had not completely disappeared.

XI

Isgarren carries Seer knowledge forward

Isgarren survived into the modern age, providing a rare living connection to the civilization described throughout this record. Unlike scholars attempting to reconstruct Seer history from ruins, he possesses direct knowledge of his people's magic and their struggle during the previous Dragonrise.

His understanding of Shadowstone remains especially important. Isgarren recognizes the material as ancient Seer magic used to contain immense power, and his heritage allows him to interact with dormant Seer systems that others cannot easily manipulate.

Through the Wizard's Court and later the Astral Ward, knowledge connected to ancient Seer magic continued to influence the protection of Tyria. The Ward has constructed vaults intended to contain dangerous magical objects, including Bloodstone-related artifacts.

This does not mean every ancient vault, door, or Bloodstone structure should be attributed to the Seers or Astral Ward. It does show that methods of dealing with dangerous magical power survived through Isgarren into a much later age.

Research Note // Surviving Knowledge

Isgarren's survival makes Seer history unusual. Archaeologists are not working only with dead ruins. At least part of the civilization's knowledge continued through someone who remembered the age in which those systems were created.

XII

The civilization behind the artifacts

The Seers are easy to reduce to a list of powerful objects. The Shadowstone, Bloodstone, Scepter of Orr, Staff of the Mists, Ancora, and other surviving works are often easier to find than the people who made them.

Taken together, however, the evidence gives us a much larger civilization. The Seers fought the Elder Dragons, studied the storage and movement of magical power, developed defenses against extraplanar threats, built ley-powered infrastructure, maintained artificers and constructs, and practiced rites capable of passing extraordinary magical knowledge from one individual to another.

Their history also carries the cost of failure. They could preserve magic from the Elder Dragons, but they could not preserve their alliance with the Mursaat. They could create objects that survived thousands of years, but they could not prevent their own people from being driven toward extinction.

That contrast may be the clearest surviving lesson of their history. The Seers became extraordinarily good at preserving power.

Preserving a civilization proved harder.

Commander Q // Observation

I began studying the Seers because of the Bloodstone. The longer I followed the record, the less the Bloodstone looked like the whole story. Castora preserves workshops, defenses, rituals, constructs, and ley systems behind the famous artifacts. The greater discovery may not be that the Seers created extraordinary things. It may be that they understood magic as an entire system in ways later civilizations are still rediscovering.

Unresolved Questions

What the record still cannot answer

Much more of Seer history is visible today than was known to earlier generations, but several of the largest questions remain open.

The original size of Seer civilization is uncertain, as is the location of many of their population centers. Castora preserves major ruins, but it is not yet clear how that landscape related to the wider Seer world.

The exact purpose of several surviving structures also remains unknown. The Shattered Capstone still releases ley energy, but the cause of its destruction and its original role within the Lost Basilica complex have not been established.

Similar architectural forms can also be observed at sites elsewhere in Tyria, including circular arches associated with later magical civilizations. Similarity alone does not establish a common builder, and those comparisons should remain observations until stronger evidence appears.

The arrival of the Six Gods raises another unanswered question. The Seers had already created artifacts intended to help protect Tyria from extraplanar threats, yet the Gods successfully entered the world and were drawn toward the powerful magical waters beneath Orr.

What stood around those waters before the Gods arrived, what the Gods knew of the Seers, and whether any surviving Seer systems were still active there remain outside the confirmed historical record.

For now, they belong here as questions rather than answers.

— Commander Q
