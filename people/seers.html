THE SEERS AND THE ELDER DRAGONS

Peoples Archive // Elder Race

The Seers

On an ancient people who survived the Dragonrise, preserved magic from the Elder Dragons, fought a centuries-long war with the Mursaat, and left magical works whose influence still reaches the modern age.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

A civilization hidden behind its artifacts

For much of modern history, the Seers were easier to recognize through the things they left behind than through any complete history of the people themselves. The Bloodstone survived. The Scepter of Orr survived. A handful of Seers survived. Their civilization did not.

That imbalance shaped how later generations remembered them. The Seers became the people who created the Bloodstone, opposed the Mursaat, and occasionally appeared when ancient magic returned to trouble the present.

New discoveries have made that picture considerably larger. Castora preserves Seer workshops, ritual sites, defenses, writings, constructs, tombs, political memories, and artifacts in the landscape where they were actually used. Janthir has likewise expanded the surviving record of their war with the Mursaat.

What emerges is not simply a civilization capable of making powerful objects. The Seers were a people who built institutions around magic, argued over how that power should be used, attempted to preserve Tyria through one Dragonrise, and then nearly destroyed themselves in a conflict that survived long after the Dragons returned to sleep.

— Commander Q

I

The Seers enter the ancient alliance

During the previous Dragonrise, the Seers were one of the five peoples remembered as the Elder Races, alongside the Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun.

They participated in an alliance intended to resist the Elder Dragons, but almost everything known about the coalition comes through later and sometimes partisan accounts. Mursaat records in particular remember the alliance through the betrayal they believed followed.

The Seer leader Sidony, remembered as the doyen of her people, initially supported war against the Dragons. That position changed before a major assault against Zhaitan. Sidony withdrew Seer participation and redirected her people toward another solution: the work that produced the Shadowstone and Bloodstone.

That decision became one of the defining moments of later Seer history.

II

Sidony withdraws from the assault

The Mursaat and Forgotten prepared armies for a direct assault upon Zhaitan. The plan relied upon ancient forces that included Mursaat jade constructs and the enchanted armies of the Forgotten.

Before the attack, Sidony withdrew the Seers. The Dwarves and Jotun also did not take part, leaving the Mursaat and Forgotten to continue largely on their own.

The assault failed with enormous losses. Later Mursaat accounts described the withdrawal of the other Elder Races as abandonment and betrayal, a memory that remained important when the surviving peoples emerged after the Dragonrise.

The Seer record points toward another interpretation. Their attention had shifted away from defeating a Dragon directly and toward preventing the Dragons from consuming the world's remaining uncorrupted magic.

Commander Q // Observation

We know that Sidony changed course. We do not know every reason that convinced her. The Mursaat saw an ally abandoning a battle. The Seers were already pursuing a different answer to the same catastrophe. Both can be true without pretending we know the conversation that occurred between them.

III

Shadowstone stores immense power

The Shadowstone belongs to the ancient magical tradition of the Seers. Surviving examples and Isgarren's own knowledge identify it as exceptionally powerful Seer magic capable of storing enormous amounts of energy.

Shadowstone remained useful long after the age in which it was created. Ancient Seer vaults could still be sealed through the material, while dormant fragments retained enough power to respond to Seer magic in the modern era.

Its importance lies in what it reveals about Seer magical practice. Long before the Human Gods divided the Bloodstone, the Seers were already developing ways to remove magical energy from ordinary circulation, contain it, and preserve it in stable forms.

The Bloodstone arose from that broader history of magical containment.

Research Note // Shadowstone

Shadowstone and Bloodstone are related parts of Seer magical history, but they should not be treated as interchangeable materials. The surviving record identifies Shadowstone as ancient Seer magic capable of storing immense power and connects its development to the work that eventually produced the Bloodstone.

IV

The Seers hide magic from the Dragons

The greatest surviving result of this work was the original Bloodstone. The Seers used it during the previous Dragonrise to preserve magic that would otherwise have been available to the Elder Dragons.

This purpose is important because it predates every later Human use of the artifact. The original Bloodstone was not created to divide mortal magic into schools, regulate Human spellcasters, or determine who should possess magical power.

It was created in response to the Dragon Cycle.

As the Elder Dragons consumed magic from the world, the Seers placed surviving uncorrupted magic within the stone so that it could endure until the Dragons returned to slumber.

The Mursaat chose another method of survival. Rather than place their magic within the Seer solution, they withdrew from ordinary Tyria, slipping into another state or layer of existence where the Dragons could not consume their power.

Once the Dragonrise ended, the Seers released the magic they had preserved back into the world. The Bloodstone itself endured and eventually passed into the history of the Human Gods, who later altered and divided it for a very different purpose.

Commander Q // Observation

The Bloodstone has carried several meanings because several civilizations reused it. For the Seers it was first a shelter for magic. Everything the Gods later did with it belongs to another chapter.

V

A civilization of artificers

Castora makes it possible to see the Bloodstone within a much wider Seer tradition of magical craftsmanship. The island preserves workshops, vaults, enchanted weapons, ritual objects, and structures designed to interact directly with powerful magic.

Among the most important surviving works are Ancora, Legavo, and Mulmund.

Legavo is the ancient name of the artifact later known as the Scepter of Orr. Mulmund became known in later records as the Staff of the Mists. Both were created by the Seers as part of efforts to protect Tyria from dangers capable of reaching the world through the Mists.

Ancora belonged to another branch of Seer practice. The staff was used during Divitium Nava, a dangerous rite through which Seers attempted extraordinary magical transformation.

These artifacts show that Seer artifice did not revolve around a single problem. Their craftsmen created works concerned with storage, defense, ritual transformation, and the movement or control of magical power.

VI

The Seers prepared for more than Dragons

Legavo and Mulmund widen the known purpose of Seer magical defenses. These artifacts were created not to fight Elder Dragons, but to help protect Tyria against threats capable of entering from outside the world.

That distinction matters. The Elder Dragons belonged to Tyria's own magical system. Extraplanar enemies presented a different danger, one involving passages through the Mists and beings whose origins lay beyond Tyria itself.

The Seers therefore understood at least some of the dangers associated with the wider cosmology long before most later Tyrian civilizations possessed reliable knowledge of those realms.

What the surviving evidence does not tell us is how broad these defenses were. The existence of two powerful artifacts does not establish a planetary barrier, nor does it prove that every later arrival from the Mists was something the Seers expected to prevent.

Historical Caution // The Gods

The Six Gods later entered Tyria from the Mists, but their arrival should not automatically be treated as a failure of Seer defenses. The surviving sources do not establish that Legavo, Mulmund, or any other known Seer system was intended to block beings such as the Gods from entering the world.

VII

Castora preserves Seer civilization in place

Castora has become the most important surviving landscape for the study of Seer civilization because it preserves more than isolated relics.

Across the island stand basilicas, ritual grounds, vaults, monuments, defensive works, magical constructs, workshops, enchanted weapons, and remains of systems that continued functioning long after the civilization that maintained them disappeared.

Several sites are closely associated with strong natural magic. Seer workings can be found around concentrated ley energy, while surviving records describe the use of pure ley magic in their artificing processes.

That relationship does not prove that the Seers created Castora's ley network or every magical phenomenon on the island. What it does establish is that they knew how to locate powerful magical sources and incorporate them into structures, rituals, and crafted objects.

The island also preserves something less spectacular but historically more valuable: fragments of ordinary Seer society. Personal writings, tombs, disputes, political judgments, failed rituals, and abandoned settlements reveal people living inside the magical civilization that their artifacts once represented alone.

Commander Q // Observation

An artifact tells us that somebody knew how to make it. Castora tells us something about the society in which that person lived. That is the difference between discovering Seer objects and beginning to recover Seer history.

VIII

Divitium Nava and the pursuit of transformation

Among the most remarkable practices recovered from Castora is Divitium Nava, the Rite of Divinity.

The rite was performed with Ancora and at a Seer site associated with concentrated magical power. Successful candidates could emerge with extraordinary magical ability and greatly extended life.

The process was dangerous. Castoran records preserve individuals who prepared for the rite and failed, sometimes catastrophically. The existence of tombs associated with failed candidates makes clear that Divitium Nava was not a predictable mechanism that simply elevated anyone who attempted it.

Isgarren is one known Seer who successfully underwent the ancient rite. His later method of elevating members of the Wizard's Court represents a different process and should not be treated as identical to Divitium Nava.

The rite also reveals something important about Seer attitudes toward magic. At least some among them did not see magical mastery as a fixed condition of birth. They developed formal practices through which an individual might attempt to become something more.

Research Note // Ascension

Several unrelated traditions in Tyrian history use the language of ascension or divinity. Divitium Nava belongs specifically to Seer magical practice and should not be assumed to function like the Forgotten Ascension trials of the Crystal Desert or other forms of magical transformation.

IX

The ancient alliance becomes a centuries-long war

When the Elder Dragons returned to slumber, the anger created during the failed alliance remained. The surviving Mursaat eventually returned to Tyria, and hostility between their people and the Seers developed into open war.

The conflict lasted for centuries.

During that war the Mursaat developed and used Spectral Agony, a magical attack that caused devastating suffering and death among its victims. The weapon became one of the defining tools of Mursaat warfare.

Sidony herself was eventually killed during the conflict. Later records connect her death directly to Spectral Agony, making the war deeply personal as well as political for the surviving Seers.

Seer knowledge eventually produced methods of surviving the attack. In the modern era, Isgarren can shield others from Spectral Agony while remaining nearby, showing that some effective defense against the Mursaat technique survived through him.

That knowledge did not save Seer civilization. The war drove their people toward extinction while also inflicting enormous losses upon the Mursaat.

X

Castora is abandoned

The decline of the Seers can be seen most clearly in the final records left on Castora. The island was not simply abandoned in a single, well-documented moment. Surviving texts preserve the fear and uncertainty of people preparing to leave while the wider conflict with the Mursaat worsened.

One surviving record from the final night describes protections placed upon Castora and the expectation that its secrets would remain guarded after the Seers departed.

Those protections endured.

Magical bindings remained upon the island's guardians, ancient defenses continued functioning, and places associated with Seer ritual and artifice survived largely beyond the reach of the outside world.

The surviving writer expected the Seers might be destroyed, but expectation is not proof that every Seer died. Later history confirms that at least isolated individuals survived.

Historical Caution // Extinction

The fall of Seer civilization and the survival of individual Seers are different questions. Castoran records show a people who feared extinction and were abandoning important sites. Later appearances prove that the species was not completely erased.

XI

A Seer returns during the Flameseeker Prophecies

By the era of the original Guild Wars, the Seers had become almost unknown to Humanity. One surviving member appeared during the events surrounding the Flameseeker Prophecies.

The Seer recognized the Mursaat as an ancient enemy and possessed knowledge essential to fighting them. Through an infusion process, the Seer altered armor so that its wearer could better survive Spectral Agony.

To the Human heroes of 1072 AE, this appeared as an unusual intervention in a contemporary war. Seen through the larger historical record, it was the continuation of a conflict that had begun thousands of years earlier.

The Mursaat encountered by Humanity and the Seer who opposed them were remnants of the same ancient struggle.

XII

Isgarren survives into the modern world

The discovery that Isgarren is a Seer changed the position of the entire civilization within the modern historical record.

Isgarren is not merely a scholar who recovered Seer knowledge. He remembers the civilization itself. He knew Sidony, participated in the history surrounding the ancient alliance, survived the war with the Mursaat, and retained direct knowledge of magical practices that later civilizations knew only through artifacts.

His later life eventually became bound to the Wizard's Court and the hidden defense of Tyria against threats ordinary nations rarely knew existed. Through those institutions, knowledge carried by one surviving Seer continued to influence events many ages after Castora had been abandoned.

Castora also revealed that his relationship with the Seers had ended badly. Isgarren had been judged by his own people and banished, with Sidony casting the deciding judgment. A magical curse connected to that punishment survived into the modern era.

His survival therefore should not be mistaken for a seamless continuation of Seer civilization. Isgarren carried some of its knowledge forward while also spending ages separated from the society that produced it.

Commander Q // Observation

Isgarren gives us something historians almost never get from a civilization this old: a witness. Unfortunately, witnesses have opinions, grudges, omissions, and histories of their own. That makes him more useful than a ruin and considerably more complicated.

XIII

The modern expedition reopens Seer history

The modern expedition to Castora brought surviving Seer history out of isolated ruins and into an active conflict.

Director Vloxx sought Ancora because he intended to perform Divitium Nava. The pursuit forced Isgarren and his allies into sites tied directly to his own past and revealed records that greatly expanded modern knowledge of Seer society.

Vloxx eventually completed the ancient rite in 1339 AE, proving that a Seer magical process created ages earlier could still transform a modern practitioner with sufficient knowledge and access to its required tools.

The same events also brought an end to part of Castora's ancient magical inheritance. The All Seer was defeated, and the curse imposed upon Isgarren by Sidony was eventually broken. The fog surrounding Castora cleared as the old punishment ended.

Seer history had ceased to be purely archaeological.

Their old artifacts could still be used. Their old rites could still be completed. Their old judgments could still affect the living.

XIV

The civilization behind the famous relics

The Seers are still easiest to identify through the extraordinary works that survived them. The Bloodstone, Shadowstone, Legavo, Mulmund, and Ancora each entered histories much longer than the civilization that created them.

But those objects now sit within a fuller context. The Seers had leaders and councils. They had artificers, ritual specialists, sacred places, fortified sites, magical constructs, political disputes, and methods of transformation that could fail as easily as succeed.

They participated in an alliance against the Elder Dragons, then abandoned one strategy in favor of preserving the world's magic. They survived the Dragonrise only to spend centuries fighting former allies. Their works endured even while their population collapsed.

That makes their history more complicated than the simple image of an ancient race that knew more about magic than everyone who followed.

Their knowledge was extraordinary.

Their decisions were still decisions made by people.

Some preserved the world. Others helped destroy relationships their civilization could not survive losing.

Commander Q // Observation

The Bloodstone brought me to the Seers because it looked like their great surviving achievement. Castora changed the question. The more of their world we recover, the more the Bloodstone looks like one product of a much larger civilization rather than the definition of it.

Unresolved Questions

What the record still cannot answer

The Seers are considerably less mysterious than they once were, but much of their civilization remains outside the surviving record.

Castora preserves major Seer sites, yet the full extent of their civilization is unknown. It is not clear how many other population centers existed, how widely their people were distributed across Tyria, or how Castora compared with their other lands.

Sidony's decision before the assault on Zhaitan also remains only partly understood. We know she withdrew Seer support and redirected her people toward the Shadowstone and Bloodstone. The surviving evidence does not preserve every motive behind that choice.

The origin and chronology of individual Seer artifacts are likewise incomplete. We now know the identities of Legavo and Mulmund and the use of Ancora in Divitium Nava, but the exact dates at which many of these works were created remain uncertain.

The relationship between Seer civilization and the world's older magical geography also requires caution. Castora contains concentrated ley magic and sites built to use it, but that does not establish that the Seers created those ley systems.

Similar caution applies elsewhere. Later civilizations used ancient artifacts and sometimes built over magical sites that long predated them. Resemblance, proximity, and later ownership should not be mistaken for proof of Seer construction.

Finally, the ultimate fate of the Seers as a people remains open. Their civilization collapsed, most of their known population died, and isolated survivors persisted into later ages. Whether Isgarren represents one of the last surviving Seers or merely one of the last currently known remains unanswered.

— Commander Q
