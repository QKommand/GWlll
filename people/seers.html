Peoples Archive // Elder Race

The Seers

On an ancient people whose magic survived the Dragonrise, the Mursaat war, and the collapse of their civilization.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

The people behind the Bloodstone

For most of modern history, the Seers were known through what survived them. The Bloodstone endured. Other artifacts remained. A few members of their people appeared in later ages. Their civilization did not.

Castora has changed that record.

The island preserves Seer ritual sites, workshops, defenses, writings, tombs, constructs, political memories, and magical systems where they were once used. Those discoveries place famous artifacts inside the society that created them.

The Seers were not simply ancient magicians who knew how to preserve magic. They had leaders, institutions, disagreements, sacred places, dangerous rites, and a long conflict with the Mursaat that their civilization did not survive.

— Commander Q

I

The Seers choose another way to survive

During the previous Dragonrise, the Seers joined the Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun in an alliance against the Elder Dragons.

The alliance did not remain united.

Before a major assault upon Zhaitan, the Seer leader Sidony withdrew her people. The Dwarves and Jotun also did not participate, leaving the Mursaat and Forgotten to continue the attack with greatly reduced support.

The assault failed with heavy losses.

Later Mursaat records remembered the withdrawal as betrayal. The Seers had instead turned toward a different answer to the Dragonrise: preserving magic before the Elder Dragons could consume it.

Commander Q // Observation

The Mursaat saw allies leaving a battle. The Seers were already changing the problem they intended to solve.

II

The Seers preserve magic

Seer magical practice already included ways to contain enormous amounts of power.

Shadowstone belongs to that tradition. Surviving examples and later Seer knowledge identify it as ancient magic capable of storing great quantities of energy. Fragments remained useful long after the age in which they were created.

The greatest surviving result of Seer work during the Dragonrise was the original Bloodstone.

Its first purpose was straightforward: preserve magic beyond the reach of the Elder Dragons.

This predates every later Human use of the artifact. The Seers did not create the Bloodstone to divide magic into schools or regulate Human spellcasters.

Those functions came much later, after the Human Gods acquired the stone and altered it for another age.

Research Note // Shadowstone

Shadowstone and Bloodstone belong to the same broad history of Seer magical containment, but they should not be treated as interchangeable materials or as different names for the same artifact.

Commander Q // Observation

The Bloodstone accumulated meanings as later civilizations reused it. For the Seers, its first importance was survival.

III

Castora reveals a civilization of artificers

Castora places the Bloodstone inside a much broader Seer tradition.

The island preserves workshops, ritual grounds, basilicas, vaults, monuments, enchanted defenses, constructs, weapons, and magical systems that remained active long after their makers disappeared.

Among the surviving artifacts are Ancora, Legavo, and Mulmund.

Legavo is the ancient name of the artifact later known as the Scepter of Orr. Mulmund became known as the Staff of the Mists. Their histories connect Seer artifice to threats capable of reaching Tyria through the Mists.

Ancora belonged to another part of Seer magical life. It was used in a dangerous rite of transformation known as Divitium Nava.

Castora also preserves the Seneschals, altered creatures bound through Seer magic to defend important parts of the island.

Together these works show a civilization concerned with magical storage, defense, ritual, transformation, and control.

Historical Caution // Castora

Seer structures and magic are widespread across Castora, but that does not establish Seer authorship for every magical feature of the island or its underlying ley network.

IV

Divitium Nava

One of the most unusual practices recovered from Castora is Divitium Nava, the Rite of Divinity.

The rite used Ancora and a Castoran source of concentrated magical power to transform a chosen candidate.

Those who survived could emerge with extraordinary magical ability and greatly extended life.

Survival was not guaranteed.

Tombs and surviving records preserve Seers who died attempting the rite, while other evidence shows that preparation could be lengthy and dangerous.

Isgarren is one known Seer who successfully underwent Divitium Nava.

His later method of transforming members of the Wizard's Court drew upon related knowledge but should not be treated as identical to the older Seer rite.

Research Note // Ascension

Tyrian history contains several traditions described as ascension or divinity. Divitium Nava belongs specifically to Seer practice and should remain distinct from the Forgotten trials in the Crystal Desert and other magical transformations.

V

Former allies become enemies

The Elder Dragons eventually returned to sleep. The anger left by the failed alliance did not.

When the Mursaat returned to ordinary Tyria, hostility between their people and the Seers became open war.

The conflict lasted for centuries.

The Mursaat used Spectral Agony, a magical attack capable of inflicting terrible suffering and death upon its victims.

Sidony was eventually killed during the war, with later accounts connecting her death to Spectral Agony.

Seer knowledge produced methods of resisting the attack. Thousands of years later, surviving Seers still possessed ways to protect others from it.

That knowledge did not save their civilization.

The war inflicted devastating losses and pushed the Seers toward collapse.

Commander Q // Observation

The Seers survived the Dragonrise. Their civilization was broken by what came after it.

VI

Castora is abandoned

Castora preserves part of the final decline of Seer civilization.

Surviving writings describe people preparing to leave while the war with the Mursaat continued.

Protections were placed upon important sites and secrets before their departure.

Those protections endured.

Guardians remained bound. Magical defenses continued functioning. Ritual sites and artifacts survived without the civilization that had maintained them.

The surviving writers feared that their people might be destroyed.

Later history shows that Seer civilization did collapse, but not every Seer died with it.

Historical Caution // Extinction

The end of Seer civilization and the extinction of the Seers are different claims. Later appearances establish that isolated members survived long after their ancient society disappeared.

VII

An ancient war resurfaces in 1072 AE

By the era of the original Guild Wars, the Seers had become almost unknown to Humanity.

One surviving Seer appeared during the events of the Flameseeker Prophecies.

The Seer recognized the Mursaat as an ancient enemy and possessed knowledge essential to surviving their magic.

Through an infusion process, armor could be altered to provide protection against Spectral Agony.

To the Humans involved, this was part of a contemporary struggle against the White Mantle and its hidden masters.

The Seer and Mursaat were continuing a war that had begun thousands of years earlier.

VIII

Isgarren carries Seer history into the present

The discovery that Isgarren is a Seer changed the modern record of his people.

He did not merely recover their knowledge. He remembers the civilization itself.

Isgarren knew Sidony, lived within ancient Seer society, survived the conflict with the Mursaat, and retained magical knowledge later peoples encountered only through ruins and artifacts.

His relationship with his own people had also ended badly.

Castoran records revealed that Isgarren was judged by a Seer council. Sidony cast the deciding judgment that resulted in his banishment, and a magical curse tied that punishment to Castora itself.

The modern expedition forced Isgarren back into that history.

In 1339 AE, the ancient curse was finally broken. The old bindings associated with Castora's defenses were altered, and the fog that had helped isolate the island cleared.

Commander Q // Observation

Isgarren gives us something ruins cannot: memory. He also gives us everything difficult about memory. He was part of this history, not an observer standing outside it.

IX

Seer magic becomes current history again

The modern expedition to Castora proved that Seer history remains capable of changing the present.

Director Vloxx came to the island seeking Ancora so that he could perform Divitium Nava.

In 1339 AE, he succeeded.

An ancient Seer rite created ages before modern Tyria still possessed enough power to transform a living practitioner.

The same expedition uncovered Seer laws, memorials, political divisions, ritual traditions, magical craftsmanship, and failures that had previously been almost entirely absent from the historical record.

The Bloodstone remains one of their greatest known achievements.

It is no longer enough to define them.

The Seers were a civilization that survived an Elder Dragon cycle, preserved magic against extinction, created works that endured for thousands of years, and then lost itself in a war with former allies.

Their population collapsed.

Their magic did not.

Open Record

The full extent of Seer civilization remains unknown. Castora preserves major sites, but other settlements may have existed elsewhere. The number of surviving Seers is likewise unresolved. Isgarren is a known survivor, not proof that he is the last.

Commander Q // Observation

The Bloodstone first led me to the Seers because it looked like their defining achievement. Castora makes it look different. It was one remarkable thing made by a civilization capable of many remarkable things.

End of Record

Return to the archive

Seer civilization disappeared, but its magic survived in artifacts, ruins, rituals, defenses, and the few members of the people who endured beyond its fall.

Castora has made their history clearer.

It has also given us more questions to answer.
