THE HUMAN MARGONITES

Peoples Archive // Human Civilization

The Margonites

On the seafaring Human civilization of the Crystal Sea, its devotion to Abaddon, and the transformation that carried part of its people into the Realm of Torment.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Overview

Before the demons, there was a Human civilization

The Margonites were an ancient Human people associated with the Crystal Sea, its islands, and the surrounding coastlines. By 175 BE, surviving chronology records Margonite settlements along the coasts north and west of Elona and describes their vessels as ruling the Unending Ocean.

They were therefore a maritime civilization before they were the demonic servants remembered from the Nightfall crisis. Their history included ships, settlements, political rulers, religious devotion, conflict with the Forgotten, and expeditions undertaken in search of Ascension.

The Margonites became closely associated with Abaddon, then the God of Water and Knowledge. Their loyalty to him survived his conflict with the other Gods and ultimately drew many of them into his rebellion. Abaddon transformed loyal Human Margonites into demonic beings who did not die of old age, and those followers were eventually imprisoned with him in the Realm of Torment.

The transformation dominates the surviving record because those Margonites endured long after the Human civilization around the Crystal Sea had collapsed. It should not be mistaken for the beginning of the Margonite people.

Historical Record // I

A seafaring civilization

The earliest securely dated Margonite record belongs to 175 BE, when their settlements appear along the coastlines north and west of Elona and their ships are described as ruling the Unending Ocean.

Their world centered upon the Crystal Sea, a large body of water occupying the region that would later become the Crystal Desert. Islands and coastal settlements placed the Margonites within a landscape naturally connected by ships rather than roads.

Their surviving history therefore points to a people accustomed to movement across water. They reached distant shores, maintained coastal settlements, and possessed enough naval strength for later chronology to remember Margonite vessels as a dominant presence on the ocean.

The surviving record does not identify a capital or preserve a complete map of Margonite territory. It is safer to describe them as a powerful maritime civilization centered upon the Crystal Sea than to reconstruct borders that the sources no longer provide.

Commander Q // Observation

The transformed Margonites survived longer than the world that produced them, which is probably why they dominate the record. The harder task is remembering that the civilization came first.

Historical Record // II

Abaddon becomes central to their history

The Margonites became devoted followers of Abaddon, who before his fall was counted among the Six as the God of Knowledge and Water.

The surviving sources describe their loyalty far more clearly than they describe how it began. We do not know when Abaddon first became especially important to Margonite worship, whether every Margonite community followed him with equal devotion, or how his cult interacted with worship of the other Gods during the earlier centuries of their civilization.

By the final period before his fall, however, followers identified with Abaddon strongly enough that conflict between the Gods was reflected in Margonite action. Their later rebellion should therefore be understood as the culmination of an existing religious relationship, not the moment that relationship began.

Historical Record // III

King Khimaar leads his people toward Ascension

One of the few named rulers preserved from the Human period is King Khimaar, identified as a king of the then-Human Margonites.

Khimaar and many of his people entered lands associated with the trials of Ascension. They came into conflict with the Forgotten, and Khimaar died along with many of the Margonites who followed him.

His spirit endured in the Crystal Desert, where later travelers encountered him during their own path toward Ascension. Even in death, Khimaar remembered the fate of his people and regarded the Forgotten as enemies.

His surviving title establishes a Margonite monarchy at least during part of their history. It does not tell us whether one royal line ruled the entire civilization, whether different regions possessed their own rulers, or how Margonite government changed over time.

Chronology Note // King Khimaar

Khimaar belongs to the Human period of Margonite history, but no secure date for his reign or expedition is preserved. His position should therefore remain undated rather than being forced into the better-known events of 1 BE.

Historical Record // IV

The Temple of the Six is desecrated

Before the Exodus, Margonites associated with Abaddon desecrated the Temple of the Six Gods, defacing the representations of the other Gods. The act became one of the surviving signs that loyalty to Abaddon had moved into open hostility toward the rest of the pantheon.

The Forgotten responded against the Margonites, adding another episode to a hostility that already existed between the two peoples. By this point, Margonite religious loyalty and older conflict with the Forgotten had become part of the same growing crisis.

The temple itself survived in an unexpected form. When Abaddon was defeated and cast into the Realm of Torment, he pulled the Temple of the Six into his prison with him. Its desecrated remains could still be encountered there during the events of Nightfall.

Historical Record // V

Jadoth and the first Margonite problem

In 1 BE, the Scriptures of Abaddon record Jadoth fleeing across the water while pursued by Forgotten warships. He called upon Abaddon for help, and the God answered by destroying the pursuing fleet and granting power to his follower.

The scripture calls Jadoth the first of the Margonites.

That creates a genuine historical problem.

Margonite settlements and maritime power are recorded long before 1 BE, and King Khimaar belongs to a period when the Margonites were still Human. Jadoth therefore cannot be the founder of the Human civilization in the ordinary chronological sense.

One possible reading is that the scripture remembers Jadoth as the first of the transformed Margonites, or as the first in some religious sense important to Abaddon's followers. The source itself does not resolve the contradiction.

Historical Accounts // "First of the Margonites"

The conflict between the 1 BE scripture and the older chronology should be preserved rather than silently harmonized. Jadoth may have been the first transformed Margonite, but that remains an interpretation, not a statement directly made by the surviving text.

Historical Record // VI

Human followers become demonic Margonites

During the conflict surrounding Abaddon's fall, loyal Human followers were transformed into the demonic beings later known throughout the Realm of Torment as Margonites.

Their new bodies were powerful, magical, and no longer subject to death from old age. The transformation allowed Margonites to endure across spans of time impossible for ordinary Humans.

That endurance did not make them mindless extensions of Abaddon. Margonites retained individual thought and the ability to disagree with their patron. By the era of Nightfall, figures such as The Lost and The Apostate had rejected the path expected of them.

The transformation also came with a more fundamental loss. The surviving account of The Lost describes a people robbed of their future, and later records identify their long life and demonic power as coming at the cost of sterility. They could endure as individuals without continuing through ordinary generations.

Commander Q // Observation

The Margonites gained longevity at the same moment their civilization lost one of the simplest ways a civilization survives: children. Their transformation preserved lives while narrowing the future of the people themselves.

Historical Record // VII

The Crystal Sea is destroyed

Abaddon's conflict with the other Gods ended in open war. His transformed Margonite followers fought beside him against the opposing pantheon and the forces allied with them.

The fighting devastated the Margonite homeland. The Crystal Sea was destroyed and became the Crystal Desert, while the lands along Elona's northern reaches became the sulfurous Desolation.

This was the collapse of the Human Margonite world. Settlements that had depended upon islands, coasts, and open water belonged to a geography that no longer existed.

The transformation of the Margonites and the transformation of their homeland therefore occurred within the same final crisis. One allowed followers of Abaddon to endure; the other destroyed the civilization from which they had come.

Historical Record // VIII

Abaddon and the Margonites are imprisoned

Abaddon was defeated at the Mouth of Torment by the other Gods. Because his power could not simply be destroyed, he was imprisoned within the Realm of Torment.

His transformed Margonite followers were cast out with him. The Forgotten participated in the war and later served among the wardens responsible for maintaining Abaddon's imprisonment.

The Realm of Torment became the center of Margonite existence after the destruction of the Crystal Sea. Margonite cities, temples, military positions, and other strongholds existed within the realm, including places later known as Ar'Challah and Torc'qua.

This later society should not be treated as a simple continuation of the maritime Human civilization. Its people had been physically transformed, displaced into another realm, cut off from ordinary generations, and organized around a defeated God whose prison became their new world.

Historical Record // IX

The Margonites emerge during Nightfall

More than a thousand years after Abaddon's imprisonment, Margonite forces remained active within the Realm of Torment.

In 1075 AE, Abaddon's attempt to break free brought those forces back into open conflict with the mortal world. Margonites fought throughout the Realm of Torment and supported the campaign intended to bring about Nightfall.

Their continued presence demonstrates the consequence of their altered lifespan. The Margonite society encountered during Nightfall had endured without the normal succession of Human generations.

Yet that society was no longer completely united. The Lost chose to abandon the future offered by his people, while The Apostate rejected Abaddon's claim to eternal Divine authority after concluding that the God himself had inherited power from an earlier deity.

Their dissent matters because it separates Margonite identity from obedience to Abaddon. Transformation had shaped their people, but it had not erased individual judgment.

Historical Record // X

Abaddon's death ends the cause that defined them

The Nightfall crisis ended with the death of Abaddon in 1075 AE.

His released Divine power was taken up by Kormir, who ascended into the pantheon as his successor. Abaddon's remaining Margonite forces suffered heavily during the fighting, and later histories describe the culling of his surviving followers.

The surviving evidence does not require us to claim that every Margonite everywhere was destroyed at that moment. What can be said safely is that the political and religious order centered upon Abaddon was broken.

By then, the civilization that had once sailed the Crystal Sea had already been gone for more than a thousand years. Its sea had become a desert, its transformed descendants could no longer renew themselves through children, and the God around whom their later society had formed was dead.

The Margonites had survived the destruction of their homeland.

Whether their people could survive the destruction of their purpose is another question entirely.

Culture and Society

What survives of the Human Margonites

Ordinary Margonite life is poorly preserved because most surviving sources concern the events surrounding Abaddon, Ascension, the Forgotten, the destruction of the Crystal Sea, or the transformed Margonites of the Realm of Torment.

Their maritime character is the clearest feature of the older civilization. Margonite settlements stood along coasts north and west of Elona, while their ships became powerful enough for surviving chronology to remember them as rulers of the Unending Ocean by 175 BE.

The Margonites also possessed political rulers. King Khimaar establishes the use of kingship during at least one period of their history, although the surviving sources do not tell us whether his authority extended over every Margonite settlement or how succession worked.

Religion became another major surviving feature. Abaddon was central enough to their later identity that his conflict with the pantheon drew followers into desecration, war, transformation, and eventual exile. What is largely missing are the details historians would normally want most: household life, family structure, law, trade practices, clothing, ordinary architecture, regional differences, and the beliefs of Margonites who may not have supported Abaddon.

That imbalance in the evidence matters. The surviving Margonite record tells us much more about how their civilization ended than how most Margonites actually lived within it.

Historical Accounts

One name, two historical conditions

The word Margonite creates confusion because it describes both the original Human people and the transformed demonic followers encountered after Abaddon's rebellion.

The Human civilization unquestionably predates Jadoth. Margonite settlements and maritime power are recorded by 175 BE, while King Khimaar is explicitly identified as belonging to the period when the Margonites were Human.

Yet the 1 BE Scriptures of Abaddon call Jadoth the first of the Margonites.

The sources do not provide an explicit reconciliation. The most economical explanation is that Jadoth was considered first within the transformed or religious Margonite order associated with Abaddon's intervention, rather than the first Human Margonite.

That interpretation fits the chronology, but the archive should preserve the disagreement rather than present the solution as established fact.

Unresolved Questions

What the record still cannot answer

Where was the political center of Human Margonite rule?

Margonite settlements and maritime power are well attested, but the surviving sources do not securely identify a capital for their Human civilization.

What territory did their rulers actually govern?

The record places Margonites around the Crystal Sea, along coastlines north and west of Elona, and across major ocean routes, but it does not preserve a reliable political border.

When did King Khimaar live?

Khimaar belongs to the period when the Margonites were still Human, but no secure date for his reign or expedition toward Ascension survives.

What did Jadoth's title actually mean?

Scripture calls Jadoth the first Margonite in 1 BE even though the people existed long before him. Whether the title refers to transformation, religious status, or some other distinction remains unresolved.

Did every Margonite community support Abaddon?

The surviving histories overwhelmingly preserve the followers who remained loyal enough to fight for him or accept transformation. They tell us far less about Margonites who may have rejected his rebellion.

Did any untransformed Human Margonites survive 0 AE?

The collapse of the civilization and destruction of the Crystal Sea are well established, but the record does not provide a complete accounting of every Margonite population during the catastrophe.

Commander Q's Notes

A civilization hidden behind its survivors

The Margonites present an unusual problem for a historian. The people who survived longest are also the people least representative of the civilization as it originally existed.

The figures encountered during Nightfall had already been transformed, displaced from Tyria, and living within the Realm of Torment for more than a thousand years. Their bodies, society, and relationship with Abaddon belonged to the aftermath of the catastrophe.

The older fragments point toward something broader. Human Margonites sailed the Unending Ocean, settled coastlines, obeyed kings, sought Ascension, fought the Forgotten, and developed a relationship with Abaddon while he was still counted among the Gods.

That civilization was destroyed before later history learned to fear the demonic Margonites.

Their transformed bodies preserved individuals across centuries, but the inability to produce another generation meant that survival itself had become a dead end. Their homeland disappeared at the same time, when the Crystal Sea became the Crystal Desert.

This leaves the Margonites remembered through the very events that erased most evidence of who they had been.

That is why their Human history matters.

Calling them demons is accurate for the beings who served Abaddon in the Realm of Torment. It is not a complete description of the people whose ships once crossed the Crystal Sea.

Their ending survived more clearly than their life.

The archive should not confuse the two.

— Commander Q
