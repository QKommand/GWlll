THE HUMAN MARGONITES

Peoples Archive // Human Civilization

The Margonites

On the Human rulers of the Crystal Sea, their devotion to Abaddon, and the transformation that carried their civilization into the Realm of Torment.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Overview

Before the demons, there was a Human nation

The Margonites were an ancient Human people whose civilization grew around the Crystal Sea and the surrounding coastlines. They were sailors, settlers, worshippers, soldiers, and rulers of a maritime region that no longer exists in its original form.

Their history became closely tied to Abaddon, the God of Water and Knowledge who later became known as the fallen God of Secrets. The Margonites were among his most devoted followers, and their loyalty eventually drew them into his conflict with the other Gods.

After Abaddon's rebellion began, many Margonites were transformed from Humans into powerful demonic beings. Those transformed followers survived for more than a thousand years within the Realm of Torment, where their descendants in name, if not in generation, remained loyal to Abaddon until the events of the Nightfall crisis.

The transformation is the part of their history most often remembered. It should not be mistaken for the beginning of their people.

Historical Record // I

A civilization of the Crystal Sea

The earliest Margonites were Human. Their civilization developed around the Crystal Sea, where islands, coastal settlements, and sea routes allowed them to build a culture shaped by travel and maritime power.

By the Orrian Golden Age, the Margonites were already an established political force. They controlled the Crystal Sea and many miles of the surrounding coastline, placing them among the important Human powers of the ancient world alongside Cantha, Elona, Orr, and the developing settlements of Ascalon.

Older records describe Margonite ships traveling the waters of the Crystal Sea and the wider Unending Ocean. Their position gave them access to routes connecting Elona with lands and peoples beyond its immediate borders.

Their exact capital has not been identified in the surviving record. What remains clear is that their civilization was larger than a single settlement or religious community. They ruled a maritime territory that later disappeared with the destruction of the Crystal Sea.

Commander Q // Observation

Later histories remember the Margonites standing beside Abaddon in the Realm of Torment. That makes it easy to forget what they lost. Before they became an army of a fallen God, they were a Human people with ships, rulers, trade routes, and a sea they could call home.

Historical Record // II

The people of Abaddon

Margonite religion centered strongly upon Abaddon. During this period he was worshipped as the God of Water and Knowledge, domains that would have been familiar to a people whose power depended heavily upon the sea.

The surviving record does not explain exactly when Abaddon became the central Divine figure of Margonite worship, nor whether every Margonite followed him with equal devotion. By the final years before his rebellion, however, the connection between the people and their patron God had become one of the defining features of Margonite identity.

This devotion later shaped how the Margonites responded when Abaddon came into conflict with the rest of the pantheon. Their loyalty was not created by his fall. It had developed while he still stood among the Six.

Historical Record // III

King Khimaar seeks Ascension

The Margonites were ruled, at least during part of their history, by kings. One of the few whose name survives is King Khimaar.

Khimaar led Margonites into the lands associated with the Forgotten in an attempt to achieve Ascension. The journey brought his followers into direct conflict with the ancient serpentine people who guarded the trials and sacred lands connected to that path.

Many Margonites died in the fighting. The surviving account does not provide a complete date for Khimaar's expedition, but the event confirms that the Margonites possessed organized political leadership and that their history with the Forgotten began before Abaddon's final rebellion.

Their interest in Ascension also shows that Margonite religion was not limited to ordinary temple worship. Their rulers were willing to lead expeditions into dangerous sacred lands in search of greater spiritual or magical understanding.

Historical Record // IV

The Temple of the Six

A great temple dedicated to the Six Gods once stood on the shores of the Crystal Sea. By the time relations between Abaddon and the other Gods began to collapse, the temple had become a place where Margonite loyalty to their patron was expressed openly.

The Margonites defaced the images of the other Gods while leaving their devotion to Abaddon clear. The act was more than damage to a religious site. It showed that the division within the pantheon was now being reflected among their Human followers.

The Forgotten responded with force and nearly destroyed the Margonites involved in the conflict. The hostility between the two peoples, already seen during King Khimaar's expedition, had now become part of a wider religious and political struggle.

Research Note // The Temple

The Temple of the Six survived the destruction of the Crystal Sea in an unusual way. It was later drawn into the Realm of Torment, where its ruins were still encountered more than a thousand years later during the Nightfall crisis.

Historical Record // V

Jadoth calls upon Abaddon

One of the most important surviving Margonite accounts concerns Jadoth, a devoted follower of Abaddon who was pursued across the Crystal Sea by a Forgotten armada.

Facing destruction, Jadoth called upon Abaddon for aid. The God answered. The pursuing fleet was destroyed, and Jadoth received power from his patron.

Later scripture remembers Jadoth as the first of the Margonites. Taken literally, that statement conflicts with the much older Human Margonite civilization now known to have existed before him.

The most likely reading is that the scripture is referring to Jadoth as the first of the transformed Margonites rather than the first Human member of the Margonite people. The surviving record does not state this directly, so the distinction should remain marked as an interpretation rather than a settled fact.

Historical Accounts // Jadoth

Ancient religious texts and later historical evidence use the word Margonite in different ways. The Human nation clearly existed before Jadoth, while the transformed beings later serving Abaddon were also called Margonites. This may explain why Jadoth could be remembered as the first without being the founder of the Human civilization.

Historical Record // VI

The Margonites are transformed

As Abaddon's conflict with the other Gods deepened, his Human followers began to change. Abaddon transformed loyal Margonites into powerful demonic beings capable of surviving conditions that ordinary Humans could not.

Their new forms were long-lived and strongly magical. Individuals who underwent the transformation could survive for centuries, allowing some of Abaddon's followers from the ancient rebellion to remain alive into the era of Nightfall.

The transformation did not remove their minds or their ability to make choices. Margonites remained capable of loyalty, doubt, disagreement, and betrayal. Later figures such as The Lost and the Apostate show that transformed Margonites could question Abaddon and abandon his cause.

Their long lives came with another cost. The transformed Margonites no longer produced children, leaving them without the normal cycle of generations through which a people renews itself.

Commander Q // Observation

Immortality sounds different when a people can no longer have children. The Margonites gained bodies that could survive centuries, but they lost the generations that might have changed their culture. They endured, but endurance and a future are not always the same thing.

Historical Record // VII

The Margonites follow Abaddon into war

The dispute between Abaddon and the other Gods eventually became open rebellion. The transformed Margonites formed a major part of his forces and fought beside their patron against the rest of the pantheon.

The war devastated the lands surrounding the Crystal Sea. Divine power tore through the region as Abaddon and his enemies fought, destroying the maritime world the Margonites had once ruled.

The Crystal Sea was ruined and became the Crystal Desert, while lands farther south were transformed into the sulfurous region known as the Desolation. What had once been islands, coastlines, ports, and open water became a scarred landscape.

The Margonites therefore lost more than a war. Their original homeland disappeared with it.

Historical Record // VIII

Exile into the Realm of Torment

Abaddon was eventually defeated by the other Gods. He was stripped of much of his power and imprisoned within the Realm of Torment.

His Margonite followers were drawn into the same fate. Many remained with him in the Realm of Torment, where they continued to serve as soldiers, priests, guards, and agents of the fallen God.

The Forgotten became part of the forces responsible for maintaining Abaddon's prison, placing the ancient enemies of the Margonites on opposite sides of the barrier that held him.

The remaining Gods later withdrew from direct life in Tyria during the Exodus. The Margonites, however, remained active within the Mists, waiting beside the God they had chosen to follow.

Historical Record // IX

The Margonites return during Nightfall

More than a thousand years after Abaddon's defeat, the Margonites remained active within the Realm of Torment. Their master's efforts to escape his prison brought them back into direct conflict with the peoples of Tyria during the events of Nightfall.

Margonite forces fought throughout the Realm of Torment and supported Abaddon's attempt to break the barriers between his prison and the mortal world. Some among them still served with the same devotion their people had shown before the Exodus.

Others had begun to question what their civilization had become. The Lost described the Margonites as a people without children or meaningful renewal, while the Apostate rejected Abaddon's claim to unquestioned Divine authority.

These dissenters are important because they show that Margonite identity survived separately from simple obedience. Even after transformation and more than a thousand years within Torment, individual Margonites remained capable of choosing whether Abaddon still deserved their loyalty.

Historical Record // X

The end of Abaddon's rule

Abaddon's attempt to escape the Realm of Torment ended in his death in 1075 AE. His power was taken up by Kormir, who replaced him among the Six Gods.

With Abaddon gone, the cause that had shaped Margonite history for more than a thousand years was broken. Many Margonites were destroyed during the fighting, while the fate of every surviving member of the people is not completely known.

By this point, little remained of their original Human civilization beyond ruins, names, religious records, and the transformed beings who still remembered their patron.

The sea they once ruled had become a desert. Their temples had been destroyed or drawn into Torment. Their kings were ancient memory, and their people could no longer produce another generation.

What survived was history.

Culture and Society

What is known of Margonite life

Much of ordinary Margonite life disappeared with the destruction of the Crystal Sea. Most surviving records focus upon Abaddon, war, Ascension, or the transformed Margonites encountered during the Nightfall crisis.

Their maritime character is well established. The Margonites controlled the Crystal Sea and surrounding coastlines, traveled by ship, and maintained a presence across islands and coastal settlements. This would have placed navigation, sea travel, ports, and control of shipping routes at the center of their civilization.

Their political history also included kings, with King Khimaar providing the clearest surviving example. Whether the Margonites were ruled by one continuous monarchy, several regional kings, or changing forms of government is not yet known.

Worship of Abaddon became one of the strongest surviving markers of Margonite identity. The destruction of so much of their homeland, however, leaves us with very little knowledge of their daily customs, architecture, family life, laws, or traditions outside religion and war.

Historical Accounts

Human Margonites and transformed Margonites

Older records sometimes make Margonite history difficult to follow because the same name is used for two forms of the same people.

The original Margonites were Humans who lived around and ruled the Crystal Sea. Later, Abaddon transformed many of his followers into demonic beings who continued to call themselves Margonites.

This distinction is especially important when reading the story of Jadoth. Religious scripture calls him the first of the Margonites even though Margonite kings and Human civilization clearly existed before his transformation.

The surviving evidence does not fully explain the wording. The simplest interpretation is that Jadoth was being remembered as the first transformed Margonite, but that remains an interpretation rather than a direct statement preserved in the record.

Unresolved Questions

What the record still cannot answer

Where was the Margonite capital?

The Margonites ruled the Crystal Sea and large areas of surrounding coastline, but no surviving source has securely identified their main political center.

How large was their territory?

Their control of the Crystal Sea and surrounding coasts suggests a large maritime realm, but the borders of that territory remain unknown.

When did King Khimaar live?

Khimaar belongs to the Human period of Margonite history, but his exact place within the chronology has not been securely dated.

What does "first of the Margonites" mean?

Jadoth is given this title in religious tradition, even though the Margonite nation predates him. The record does not directly explain the difference.

Where did the Temple of the Six originally stand?

The temple stood on the shore of the Crystal Sea before being drawn into the Realm of Torment, but its exact location on a modern map remains uncertain.

Did all Human Margonites follow Abaddon?

The surviving histories focus heavily upon his loyal followers. They do not tell us whether every community within the Margonite realm supported the rebellion or accepted transformation.

Commander Q's Notes

A people remembered by their ending

The Margonites are difficult to study because history preserved the part of their story that destroyed almost everything that came before it.

We know Abaddon's soldiers. We know the Realm of Torment. We know the transformed bodies, the temples, the war, and the destruction of the Crystal Sea. Those events left scars large enough to survive.

Ordinary Margonite life did not leave the same kind of mark.

Yet the pieces that remain tell us that this was once a very different people. They had kings. They sailed the Crystal Sea. They held coastlines and islands. Their rulers sought Ascension, their ships crossed great waters, and their relationship with Abaddon began before he became the enemy of the other Gods.

That matters because the transformed Margonites were not a new race appearing from nowhere. They were the surviving shape of an older Human civilization.

Their transformation preserved individuals while ending generations. Their rebellion preserved their loyalty while destroying their homeland. By the time Abaddon finally died, the sea, cities, and Human Margonites were already gone.

If we remember them only as demons, then Abaddon's rebellion takes something from them a second time.

The first loss was their civilization.

The second would be forgetting that they ever had one.

— Commander Q
