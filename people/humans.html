Overview

A people from beyond Tyria

Humans are not native to Tyria. They were brought from another world through the Mists by the Six Gods. The exact cause of that migration remains uncertain, but newer records describe it as a flight from their world of origin.

From that beginning, Humanity spread across Tyria and developed into distinct civilizations. Cantha became the oldest enduring Human center, Elona grew into a major southern power, Ascalon emerged as a young and militarized kingdom, the Margonites ruled the Crystal Sea, and Orr became a center of magic, faith, and ambition beneath the Gods themselves.

Major Early Powers

Cantha, Elona, Ascalon, Margonites, Orr

Historical Record // I

Humanity crosses the Mists

Humanity's oldest shared story begins with migration. Their people crossed from another world into Tyria under the guidance of the Six Gods.

The name and fate of that earlier world remain unknown. What survives is the fact that Humanity arrived as a displaced people and began building new lives in a world already filled with ancient peoples, spirits, and civilizations.

Historical Record // II

Settlement becomes civilization

The oldest firmly dated Human settlement in the surviving record is Cantha in 786 BE.

Over the centuries that followed, Human populations spread and developed along different paths. Cantha grew through dynasties and tradition. Elona became prosperous and culturally influential. Ascalon became a young frontier kingdom defined by borders and military pride.

The Margonites developed into a powerful maritime civilization around the Crystal Sea, while Orr grew beneath the presence of Arah and the Gods.

Historical Record // III

Orr becomes the great Human frontier

By the era later revealed through Guild Wars 3, Humanity had already lived in Tyria for more than seven hundred years, and Orr stood at the center of a new period of Human expansion.

Orr was rich in magic and home to the Vael, magical spirits tied to the region's extraordinary energy. Humans reacted to them in different ways: as sacred beings, natural forces, mysteries to study, or sources of power.

That difference shaped Orrian society. The Mavenwrights saw magical resources as tools for innovation and progress, while the Order of the Vael protected Vael spirits and the lands they inhabited.

Orr therefore became an early example of a tension that would follow Humanity for centuries: the desire to build and expand against the responsibility to live within a world that already had its own life and order.

Historical Record // IV

Divine rule gives way to mortal history

Human civilization changed sharply when conflict over magic led to Abaddon's rebellion and the Exodus of the Gods.

The Crystal Sea was destroyed, the Margonite world collapsed, and the Gods withdrew from direct life in Tyria. Humanity was left to govern its kingdoms, preserve its faith, and face its conflicts without Divine rule standing visibly above them.

Historical Record // V

Human kingdoms rise, divide, and go to war

In the centuries after the Exodus, Human kingdoms became increasingly independent and politically distinct.

Guilds grew into major political forces. Rivalries between kingdoms hardened. The Guild Wars consumed generations, while separate crises reshaped Cantha, Elona, Kryta, Ascalon, and Orr in different ways.

By the end of the original Guild Wars era, Humanity remained widespread and powerful, but its old unity under the Gods had long since disappeared.

Historical Record // VI

Human dominance begins to recede

The centuries before Guild Wars 2 transformed the Human world again.

Orr was lost. Ascalon fell. Elona came under the rule of Palawa Joko. Cantha withdrew from much of the world. Kryta endured, but Humanity entered the modern era with fewer secure centers of power than it had once known.

At the same time, Tyria's other peoples rose into greater political importance. Humanity remained one of the world's major civilizations, but no longer stood at its center.

Historical Record // VII

Humanity survives by changing

In the modern era, Human history is no longer the story of one expanding people. It is the story of several old civilizations learning how to exist within a wider Tyrian world.

Kryta remains a major Human kingdom. Elona is free of Joko. Cantha has reopened to the wider world. Human soldiers, scholars, rulers, traders, and adventurers now work beside other peoples in alliances that would have been difficult to imagine during the age of the old kingdoms.

The Gods remain central to Human culture and memory, but Humanity's future is increasingly shaped by mortal choices rather than Divine intervention.

Commander Q's Notes

A history of beginning again

Humanity's history is easier to understand when viewed as a series of new beginnings.

They began again after crossing the Mists. They began again when settlements became kingdoms, when the Gods departed, when wars destroyed old powers, and when the Elder Dragon era forced the surviving nations to look beyond themselves.

That persistence may be Humanity's defining trait. Their history is full of ambition and conflict, but it is also full of rebuilding.

The Humans of modern Tyria are not the same people who first followed the Six into this world. They are the descendants of everyone who chose to stay, adapt, and make a future here.

— Commander Q
