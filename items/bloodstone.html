Author's Preface

An artifact with more than one history

The Bloodstone is difficult to study because nearly every age that encountered it added another purpose to an artifact that was already ancient. Human tradition once remembered the Gods as its creators. Later discoveries pushed the beginning of the story thousands of years farther into the past.

The original Bloodstone belonged to the Seers. During the previous Dragonrise, they used it to preserve magic from the Elder Dragons, whose feeding was draining magical power from the world. The stone survived that age and eventually passed into the possession of the Six Gods.

The Gods did not leave it unchanged. When magical conflict spread among mortal civilizations, the ancient stone became part of their answer. It was divided into five major pieces, sealed with the blood of King Doric, and placed within Abaddon's Mouth. A later eruption scattered those pieces across Tyria.

From there, the Bloodstone entered a very different history. Guilds fought over magical power. The Mursaat and White Mantle used one stone within their sacrificial system. Gadd experimented upon another beneath the mountains. Centuries later, a Bloodstone in Maguuma absorbed enormous quantities of released Dragon magic before exploding.

The artifact remained the same material, but the meaning attached to it changed with every civilization that found it.

Historical Record // I

The Bloodstone begins with the Dragon Cycle

During the previous Dragonrise, the Elder Dragons consumed the magic of Tyria as they fed. Cities fell and ancient peoples were forced to find different ways of surviving a world that was steadily losing the magical power on which many of their civilizations depended.

The Seers responded by attempting to preserve part of that magic beyond the Dragons' reach. Their solution was the original Bloodstone, a magical repository capable of holding an extraordinary amount of power.

Seer work with other magical materials and artifacts, including Shadowstone, shows that magical containment was already part of their larger tradition. The surviving record connects that earlier work with the development of the Bloodstone, although not every step between the two technologies has been preserved.

The Bloodstone therefore did not begin as a weapon or prize. It began as an attempt to keep magic from being consumed.

Commander Q // Observation

That first purpose matters. Later generations fought over the Bloodstones because of what they could gain from them. The Seers created the original because they were trying to keep something from being lost.

Historical Record // II

The stone survives the Dragonrise

The Seers placed preserved magic within the Bloodstone while the Elder Dragons continued feeding upon the world. Other ancient peoples chose different methods of survival.

The Mursaat, for example, relied upon their ability to withdraw from ordinary Tyria rather than place their fate entirely within the Seers' solution. Their surviving accounts preserve considerable hostility toward the Seers and other former allies, especially after the failed ancient campaign against Zhaitan.

Eventually the Elder Dragons returned to slumber. The civilizations that emerged afterward inherited a world depleted by the Dragonrise, while the magic held inside the Bloodstone remained preserved.

At some later point, that preserved magic returned to the wider world.

Historical Caution

Surviving accounts establish the Seers' use of the Bloodstone to preserve magic from the Elder Dragons. They do not preserve a complete technical account of how the magic was gathered, how much was stored, or every step by which it was later released.

Historical Record // III

The Gods inherit something older than Arah

Thousands of years later, the Six Gods came to Tyria and established themselves in the region that became Orr. Their city of Arah accumulated powerful objects and relics from several older civilizations.

The Bloodstone was among the ancient treasures that eventually came into Divine possession.

Exactly how the Gods acquired it is not preserved. Neither is there a complete record of what they knew about the Seers who had created it. What can be said with confidence is that the Bloodstone already possessed a history stretching back to the previous Dragonrise before it entered the better-preserved Human record.

That gap later created an understandable error. Human histories remembered what the Gods did to the Bloodstone and eventually credited them with creating the Bloodstones themselves.

The distinction became visible only after knowledge of the Seers and the ancient Dragon Cycle returned.

Historical Record // IV

Mortal access to magic becomes a crisis

In 1 BE, mortal access to magic expanded dramatically. The change is remembered most strongly through Abaddon, whose support for greater magical power among mortals placed him at odds with the other Gods.

Magic itself was far older than this event. The Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, Jotun, and other peoples had practiced forms of magic long before the Human kingdoms rose. What changed was the scale at which magical power became available within the civilizations of that age.

Existing rivalries became more destructive as greater magic entered them. Wars spread until King Doric traveled to Arah and asked the Gods to intervene.

Their response returned the ancient Bloodstone to the center of history.

Historical Record // V

One ancient stone becomes five

The Gods divided the original Bloodstone into five major pieces. Four became associated with the recognized schools of Aggression, Destruction, Preservation, and Denial.

The fifth became the Keystone, the piece capable of bringing the divided magical system together.

King Doric's blood was used to seal the stones, tying his line to their protection. The five Bloodstones were then placed within Abaddon's Mouth in the Ring of Fire.

This is the moment preserved most clearly in older Human tradition. From the perspective of those records, the Bloodstones appeared here as a Divine solution to mortal magical warfare.

From the longer historical record, however, the event was a repurposing. The Gods took a Seer artifact created to preserve magic during a Dragonrise and turned it into part of a system intended to regulate how magic could be used.

Historical Accounts

Older Human sources describe the Gods as creating the Bloodstones because those histories begin with the Divine division of the artifact. Later evidence places the creation of the original Bloodstone with the Seers during the previous Dragonrise.

Historical Record // VI

Abaddon's Mouth returns the stones to the world

The Bloodstones did not remain together beneath Abaddon's Mouth. A later eruption scattered the great fragments away from the volcano and returned them to the wider world.

From that point forward, their histories separated. Bloodstones would be encountered in the Ring of Fire, Maguuma, beneath the mountains, and eventually within the historical record surrounding Janthir.

Their dispersal also had political consequences. Powerful Guilds sought access to Bloodstones and other magical resources, and competition over that power helped feed the conflicts remembered as the Guild Wars.

An artifact originally created to remove magic from danger had become something mortal powers actively pursued.

Historical Record // VII

The Mursaat turn Bloodstone toward sacrifice

During the events of 1072 AE, the Maguuma Bloodstone became part of the machinery by which the Mursaat attempted to prevent the Flameseeker Prophecies from reaching fulfillment.

Through the White Mantle, Humans identified as the Chosen were brought into a religious system that presented their selection as an honor. In reality, they were being prepared for sacrifice.

Their deaths were connected to Mursaat methods for capturing souls and empowering the defenses surrounding the Door of Komalie. The Bloodstone formed part of that system, but the surviving evidence does not show that Bloodstone simply consumes souls by its own nature.

That distinction is important. The Mursaat were not merely standing beside a magical rock and allowing it to act. They had constructed rituals, devices, and a political system around the power available to them.

Commander Q // Observation

By the Guild Wars era, the Bloodstone had become almost the opposite of what it had been during the Dragonrise. The Seers used it to preserve something from destruction. The Mursaat built a system around it that required people to die.

Historical Record // VIII

Gadd finds a Bloodstone beneath the mountains

In 1078 AE, the Asuran researcher Gadd led an expedition into the underground region known as the Bloodstone Caves. Deep within the complex lay another of the scattered Bloodstones.

Gadd did not approach the stone as a religious relic. He approached it as a source of magical energy that could be studied, measured, and exploited.

Reaching it required passing through a subterranean complex containing constructed entrances, inscriptions, magical defenses, ruins, and other features that cannot be explained simply by the natural formation of the caves.

The Bloodstone rested deeper within the site, where Gadd attempted to extract and work with its magical essence.

The surrounding area was also affected by an Aura of the Bloodstone. Death within the affected region could transfer restorative energy to nearby creatures, showing that the stone altered magical conditions around itself even without being physically handled.

Historical Caution // Bloodstone Caves

The constructed features of Bloodstone Caves are real, but their builders have not been securely identified. The record does not establish that the Seers built the complex, nor does it establish that the complex was created specifically for the Bloodstone.

Commander Q // Observation

This is the part of the Bloodstone record I would most like to see answered. A scattered Bloodstone ended up beneath the mountains. That much is straightforward. The sealed doors, inscriptions, defenses, and ruins surrounding the route to it are not.

Historical Record // IX

Dragon magic overwhelms an ancient reservoir

The modern Dragonrise returned the Bloodstones to the problem that had created them in the first place: enormous concentrations of magic moving through Tyria.

When Zhaitan and later Mordremoth died, the magic they had consumed did not disappear with them. It returned to the world's magical system and moved through the Ley Lines.

The Maguuma Bloodstone absorbed a considerable amount of that released power. At the same time, the White Mantle had returned to the region and was conducting experiments involving Bloodstone magic.

In 1329 AE, the Bloodstone exploded. The blast shattered the stone and transformed the surrounding landscape into Bloodstone Fen.

The region became saturated with unstable magical energy. Creatures exposed to Bloodstone magic changed physically and behaviorally, while spirits connected to earlier sacrifices remained visible around the shattered site.

A large portion of the released energy was later taken by Balthazar, carrying the Bloodstone's accumulated power into yet another conflict.

Historical Record // X

The five stones followed different roads

The Gods created five major Bloodstones from the original Seer artifact, but the complete later history of all five has never been reconstructed with certainty. Several major locations can nevertheless be identified.

Known Site

Ring of Fire

A Bloodstone remained within the Ring of Fire region and was encountered during the events of the Flameseeker Prophecies. Its later condition has not been fully established.

Known Site

Bloodstone Fen

The Maguuma Bloodstone became central to Mursaat and White Mantle activity before exploding in 1329 AE and transforming the surrounding region.

Known Site

Bloodstone Caves

A Bloodstone was found deep beneath the mountains during Gadd's expedition in 1078 AE. The present condition of the deeper site remains uncertain.

Known Association

Janthir

Bloodstone history also reaches Janthir, where Mursaat activity, Bloodstone material, and the Keystone became part of the surviving record.

Historical Study // I

What Bloodstone is known to do

The clearest property of Bloodstone is its capacity to hold enormous quantities of magical energy. That was the purpose for which the original artifact was created, and the later stones retained their character as major magical reservoirs.

Bloodstone can also alter the magical environment around it. The aura within Bloodstone Caves changed what happened when creatures died nearby, while the Maguuma Bloodstone saturated an entire region with unstable energy after its destruction.

Its power can be extracted and used separately from the main stone. Gadd experimented with Bloodstone essence, while later organizations developed their own methods of working with fragments and concentrated Bloodstone energy.

Heavy exposure can alter living creatures. Those affected by concentrated Bloodstone magic have shown physical mutation, aggression, and an intense hunger for additional magical energy.

Other effects depend upon the systems built around Bloodstone. The Mursaat use of sacrifice and captured souls is the clearest example. It should not automatically be treated as a natural function of Bloodstone itself.

Historical Study // II

Why the Gods were once credited with its creation

Human history preserved the Bloodstone from the moment it became part of Divine intervention in mortal magic. From that perspective, the Gods divided the stone, established its schools, sealed it with Doric's blood, and hid the resulting Bloodstones.

Knowledge of the Seers and the previous Dragonrise was far less complete. The older origin of the artifact disappeared from the histories most Humans inherited.

Later discoveries restored that missing beginning. The Seers created the original Bloodstone. The Gods inherited it, altered it, divided it, and gave the artifact a second history.

Both traditions therefore describe real parts of the record. They begin at different points.

Commander Q // Observation

This is one of my favorite kinds of historical correction because it does not require throwing the older account away. The Gods did make the Bloodstones known to Human history. They simply did so from a Bloodstone that was already ancient.

Unresolved Questions

What the Bloodstone record still cannot answer

Who built Bloodstone Caves?

The route toward the Bloodstone passes through constructed doors, ruins, magical defenses, and inscriptions. No surviving evidence has securely identified the people responsible for the complex.

Did the complex predate the Bloodstone?

The Bloodstone reached the underground region after the stones were scattered, but the surviving record does not establish whether the surrounding structures were already present or were built later.

Who created the inscriptions?

Magical inscriptions appear within Bloodstone Caves and around other Bloodstone sites, but resemblance alone is not enough to assign them securely to the Seers, Gods, Mursaat, or another civilization.

Which surviving stone represented each school?

Four of the five Bloodstones were associated with Aggression, Destruction, Preservation, and Denial. The surviving stones have not all been matched to those schools with certainty.

Where did the Gods first recover the original stone?

The Bloodstone eventually entered Divine possession and became part of the treasures associated with Arah, but the surviving record does not explain where the Gods found it or how it was brought there.

What survives within Bloodstone Caves today?

The ancient entrance remains identifiable in the modern landscape, but the condition of the deeper complex and the Bloodstone once studied there has not been confirmed.

Commander Q's Notes

Every age found a different Bloodstone

The Bloodstone is one artifact, but it has never had only one history.

To the Seers, it was an answer to the Elder Dragons. To the Gods, it became a way of imposing structure upon mortal magic. To the Guilds, Bloodstones represented power worth fighting over. The Mursaat incorporated one into a system of sacrifice. Gadd saw another as a research problem. The White Mantle returned to the Maguuma stone centuries later because there was still power left to exploit.

None of those uses erases the others. They accumulated. That is what makes the Bloodstone valuable as a historical object rather than simply a magical one.

It also makes the unanswered parts more interesting. Bloodstone Caves still bother me precisely because we know enough to see that something is missing. A stone scattered from the Ring of Fire ended up beneath the mountains, yet the route toward it passes through an ancient constructed complex.

Centuries later, the entrance can still be placed in the modern landscape near Bramble Pass. That gives the old expedition a geographical anchor, but geography cannot tell us who built what lies beyond it.

The temptation is to choose a familiar ancient civilization and fill in the blank. The Seers are an obvious candidate because they created the Bloodstone. The Mursaat are another because of their later use of Bloodstones. Dwarven and other underground ruins only make the comparison messier.

None of that is evidence.

Until something identifies the builders, the complex should remain exactly what it is: a surviving piece of architecture surrounding one of Tyria's oldest magical artifacts, with its history still unfinished.

— Commander Q
