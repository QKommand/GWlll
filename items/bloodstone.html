THE SEERS CREATE THE BLOODSTONE

Items & Artifacts // Ancient Magical Record

Bloodstone

An ancient store of magic created by the Seers, later divided by the Gods and scattered across Tyria.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Overview

An artifact older than Human history

The Bloodstone is one of the oldest known magical artifacts in Tyria. It was created by the Seers during the previous rise of the Elder Dragons, when those Dragons were consuming the world's magic. The Seers gathered uncorrupted magic and placed it within the Bloodstone so that the Dragons could not reach it.

Thousands of years later, the Six Gods came into possession of the artifact. Following the spread of magic among the mortal races and the conflicts that followed, the Gods divided the original Bloodstone into five pieces. Four were tied to the schools of Aggression, Destruction, Preservation, and Denial, while the fifth became the Keystone.

The divided stones were sealed with the blood of King Doric and placed within Abaddon's Mouth. A later eruption scattered them across Tyria. From that point forward, the Bloodstones became part of the histories of guilds, the Mursaat, the White Mantle, the Asura, the Elder Dragons, and Balthazar.

Historical Record // I

The Seers and the first Bloodstone

During the previous Dragonrise, the Elder Dragons fed on the magic of Tyria. The Seers were among the ancient peoples attempting to survive that age, alongside races such as the Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun.

The Seers began searching for a way to keep uncorrupted magic away from the Dragons. Their earlier work produced the Shadowstone, an artifact that could increase the magical strength of a Seer. Their research eventually led to the creation of the much greater Bloodstone.

The Seers gathered a large amount of Tyria's remaining uncorrupted magic and stored it within the stone. The Mursaat refused to surrender their own magic to this plan and instead used their Shadowcraft to remove themselves from ordinary Tyria until the Dragonrise had passed.

The Elder Dragons eventually returned to sleep, and the Bloodstone survived the age that had led to its creation.

Commander Q // Observation

The first purpose of the Bloodstone was simple. The Dragons were consuming magic, and the Seers needed somewhere safe to put it. Nearly every later use of the artifact came from people finding a new purpose for something that had originally been built as a place of protection.

Historical Record // II

The Gods inherit an older artifact

Thousands of years later, the Six Gods came to Tyria and eventually established Arah in Orr. They gathered many powerful magical artifacts there, including the Bloodstone.

The surviving record does not explain exactly where the Gods found the Bloodstone or how much they understood about the Seers who created it. What is clear is that the artifact was already ancient when it entered their possession.

This is important because older Human accounts later described the Gods as the creators of the Bloodstones. That version of history remembered what the Gods did with the artifact, but not the much older history that came before them.

Historical Record // III

The Gift of Magic

In 1 BE, Abaddon helped give the mortal races of Tyria much greater access to magic. Magic itself was not new, since the Elder Races had used it long before Humanity entered the historical record, but its wider distribution changed how mortal civilizations used it.

Conflict grew as magical power became part of existing rivalries. The fighting became dangerous enough that King Doric traveled to Arah and asked the Gods to intervene.

The Gods agreed that unrestricted access to magic could not continue. Their answer was to change the Bloodstone itself.

Historical Record // IV

The Bloodstones' Unmaking

The Gods divided the original Bloodstone into five major pieces. Four were tied to the recognized schools of magic: Aggression, Destruction, Preservation, and Denial.

The fifth piece became the Keystone. Unlike the four stones connected to the schools of magic, the Keystone could bring the divided system together again.

The Bloodstones were sealed with the blood of King Doric, and his descendants were given responsibility for protecting them. The five stones were then placed within Abaddon's Mouth, the great volcano in the Ring of Fire Islands.

Historical Accounts

Early Human histories said that the Gods created the Bloodstones. Later discoveries showed that the Seers created the original artifact during the previous Dragonrise. The Gods were responsible for dividing that older Bloodstone into the five stones remembered by Human history.

Historical Record // V

The scattering of the Bloodstones

The Bloodstones did not remain together in Abaddon's Mouth. The volcano later erupted and scattered the fragments across Tyria.

One Bloodstone remained within the Ring of Fire region. Another reached the Maguuma Jungle. A third crashed through the Shiverpeak Mountains and came to rest deep underground. Other fragments eventually became connected to the lands of Janthir.

Once separated, the stones became independent sources of magical power. They were no longer hidden together under the protection established by the Gods, and their presence began to shape later conflicts.

Historical Record // VI

The Bloodstones and the Guild Wars

Human guilds began seeking control of the scattered Bloodstones and other sources of magical power. Competition between those guilds helped fuel the wars that eventually drew Ascalon, Kryta, and Orr into a larger conflict.

By this period, the original purpose of the Bloodstone had largely disappeared from Human knowledge. What the Seers had created to keep magic away from the Elder Dragons had become something people sought for the power it could provide.

Historical Record // VII

The Maguuma Bloodstone

By 1072 AE, the Mursaat and their White Mantle servants had learned to use Bloodstone as part of a system involving magical sacrifice. The Eye of Janthir identified magically gifted Humans known as the Chosen, who were then taken by the White Mantle.

The Chosen were told that their selection was an honor, but they were instead being prepared for sacrifice. Their souls could be captured within Mursaat soul batteries, which were used to help maintain the seals surrounding the Door of Komalie.

This use of the Bloodstone showed that the artifact could be part of magic involving death and souls, though the surviving evidence does not show that the Bloodstone automatically consumed souls on its own. The Mursaat combined it with their own ritual methods and magical technology.

Historical Record // VIII

The Bloodstone Caves

In 1078 AE, the Asuran researcher Gadd searched for another Bloodstone beneath the Shiverpeak Mountains. This fragment had crashed through the mountains after the eruption of Abaddon's Mouth and eventually came to rest within the underground region known as the Bloodstone Caves.

The caves contained more than natural tunnels. Gadd's expedition encountered ruins, magical defenses, inscriptions, enchanted weapons, and a sealed entrance. The party had to recover magical inscriptions before it could proceed deeper into the complex.

The Bloodstone itself rested on the deepest known level. Gadd studied the stone and attempted to extract its magical essence, making the expedition one of the clearest examples of Bloodstone being treated as something that could be examined and used through research.

The entire area was also affected by an Aura of the Bloodstone. Deaths within the affected region could heal nearby creatures, showing that a Bloodstone could change the magical conditions around it even when no one was directly using the stone.

Commander Q // Observation

The Bloodstone itself is only part of the mystery. The caves contain doors, inscriptions, wards, ruins, and other signs that someone deliberately built or changed the site. We know the Bloodstone crashed into these mountains. We do not know whether the complex was already there or whether someone built it around the stone afterward.

Historical Record // IX

The modern Dragonrise

The return of the Elder Dragons again changed the flow of magic across Tyria. As Dragons died, the magic they had consumed returned to the world and moved through Tyria's larger magical network.

After Mordremoth died in 1328 AE, the Maguuma Bloodstone absorbed a large amount of released magic. At the same time, the White Mantle had been carrying out experiments around the artifact.

In 1329 AE, the Bloodstone became unstable and exploded. The blast transformed the surrounding region into what is now known as Bloodstone Fen.

Creatures exposed to large amounts of Bloodstone magic became physically changed, aggressive, and increasingly hungry for more magic. Spirits connected to the old sacrifices also appeared around the shattered site.

Much of the magical power released by the explosion was then absorbed by Balthazar, who used that power during his later campaign against the Elder Dragons.

Known Locations

Where the Bloodstones have been found

The five Bloodstones were scattered across different parts of Tyria, but the full path of every fragment has not been reconstructed. Several major sites are known with confidence.

Known Site

Ring of Fire

A Bloodstone remained within the Ring of Fire Islands and was encountered during the events of the Flameseeker Prophecies. Its modern condition is uncertain.

Known Site

Bloodstone Fen

The Maguuma Bloodstone was used by the Mursaat and White Mantle before exploding in 1329 AE. Its destruction reshaped the surrounding region.

Known Site

Bloodstone Caves

The Shiverpeak Bloodstone rests somewhere beyond the sealed caves near modern Bramble Pass. Its present condition has not been confirmed.

Known Site

Janthir

Bloodstone material and major Bloodstone sites are known from Janthir. The Mursaat also carried the Keystone to Bava Nisos.

Properties

What Bloodstone can do

The main known property of Bloodstone is its ability to store enormous amounts of magic. The original artifact preserved uncorrupted magic through the previous Dragonrise, and the divided stones continued to act as major magical reservoirs.

Bloodstone can also affect the environment around it. The Shiverpeak Bloodstone altered the effect of deaths inside its caves, while the shattered Maguuma Bloodstone flooded the surrounding area with unstable magic.

Bloodstone energy can be removed from the stone and used separately. Gadd attempted to extract Bloodstone essence, and later groups developed their own methods of using Bloodstone fragments and concentrated Bloodstone magic.

Heavy exposure can also affect living creatures. Bloodstone-crazed creatures have shown physical changes, violent behavior, and a strong need to consume additional magic.

The Mursaat also used Bloodstones as part of rituals involving captured souls. It remains uncertain which of these later uses were understood by the Seers when they created the original artifact.

Historical Accounts

Why older records name the Gods

For much of recorded Human history, the Gods were believed to have created the Bloodstones. This account came from a time when knowledge of the Seers and the previous Dragonrise was much more limited.

Later discoveries established that the original Bloodstone was created by the Seers thousands of years earlier. The Gods later found the artifact, used it in their dealings with magic, and divided it into the five stones known to later civilizations.

The older record was therefore not describing the beginning of the artifact. It was describing the point when the Bloodstone entered Human history.

Unresolved Questions

What the record still cannot answer

Who built Bloodstone Caves?

The caves contain doors, ruins, inscriptions, wards, and other constructed features, but no surviving record clearly identifies their builders.

Did the caves exist before the Bloodstone arrived?

The Bloodstone is said to have crashed through the mountains. It is not known whether it entered an older underground complex or whether the site was built around the stone later.

Who created the inscriptions?

Similar markings appear around Bloodstones and within Bloodstone Caves. Their origin has not been securely tied to the Seers, Gods, Mursaat, or another people.

Which stone belonged to each school of magic?

Four Bloodstones were tied to Aggression, Destruction, Preservation, and Denial, but the known physical stones have not been matched to those schools with certainty.

What remains inside Bloodstone Caves?

The ancient entrance still exists near Bramble Pass, but the deeper complex and the current state of the Shiverpeak Bloodstone remain unknown.

Commander Q's Notes

The history people kept adding to

The Bloodstone has passed through so many hands that almost every age understood it differently. The guilds saw a source of power, the Mursaat found a tool they could use in their rituals, the White Mantle built belief around it, and Gadd approached it as something that could be studied.

Even the oldest Human histories began too late. They remembered the Gods dividing the Bloodstone and assumed that was where the story started. Only later did we learn that the artifact had already survived an entire Dragonrise before Humanity knew it existed.

The part of the record I find most difficult to leave alone is Bloodstone Caves. We know that a fragment crashed through the mountains and ended up deep below the surface, but there is an ancient sealed door leading toward it. Beyond that door are ruins, inscriptions, wards, and defenses.

The same entrance can still be found near Bramble Pass centuries later. Orr sank and later rose again, the coastline changed, and modern maps no longer resemble the old ones perfectly, but the entrance remained in place.

That gives us a useful historical anchor. What it does not give us is an answer. Someone built that complex, but the record does not yet tell us whether they built it because the Bloodstone arrived or whether the stone crashed into a place that was already there.

Until stronger evidence appears, that question belongs in the archive exactly as it is: unanswered.

— Commander Q
