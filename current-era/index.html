The Current Era

On Castora, Ancora, Divitium Nava, and the rediscovery of a Seer history that remained unfinished long after their civilization fell.

Author's Preface

The present has reached the ancient world

The previous record ended with Janthir reopening histories that had seemed safely contained in the distant past. Mursaat ruins became active sites of investigation, Titans returned to Tyria, and modern forces followed their trail into the same Realm of Torment that had shaped events centuries earlier.

Castora carries that movement much farther.

The Seers are no longer relevant only because one of their people appeared during the Flameseeker Prophecies or because their civilization created the first Bloodstone. Castora preserves places where they lived, experimented, worshiped, argued, performed rituals, bound guardians, and attempted to transform themselves through magic.

It also changes the position of Isgarren. The ancient wizard who spent centuries hidden above Tyria can now be placed within a Seer society that possessed its own leaders, laws, sacred places, and disagreements long before he became the figure known to the modern Wizard's Court.

At the center of the current conflict stands Ancora, a Seer staff connected to the rite known as Divitium Nava. Director Vloxx came to Castora seeking that power, but the expedition has uncovered far more than a single artifact.

For much of this archive, the ancient world had to be reconstructed from fragments.

On Castora, some of those fragments have started answering one another.

— Commander Q

I

The Inquest sail for Castora

In 1338 AE, a group of Inquest under the command of Director Vloxx sailed toward Castora.

The expedition did not begin as an archaeological mission. Vloxx was searching for something specific: the ancient Seer artifact called Ancora.

The Inquest gained an early lead. By the time the Commander and allies assembled their own voyage across the Unending Ocean, Vloxx's forces were already ahead of them.

Reaching Castora proved difficult in its own right. The island had long been surrounded by dangerous seas and an unusual fog that complicated travel and helped keep the region isolated from the wider world.

The expedition nevertheless reached its shores.

What waited there was not an empty island containing one useful relic.

Castora held the remains of an entire Seer world.

Commander Q // Observation

Vloxx went looking for a staff. Historians should probably thank him for accidentally leading the rest of Tyria into an archaeological crisis.

II

Castora was never simply lost

Castora had appeared in scattered records long before the modern expedition reached it.

Ancient Margonite sailing accounts are among the earliest surviving references to the island, while later rumors, shipwrecks, and incomplete records kept its name alive without providing the wider world a reliable understanding of what existed there.

The expedition discovered that Castora had become a refuge and important magical center for the Seers.

They built basilicas, temples, statues, ritual grounds, vaults, and other structures across the island. Castora also served as a place where Seer magic could be developed and tested.

Concentrated magic remained part of the island long after the Seer civilization that used it had declined. Ancient mechanisms still functioned. Guardians still defended important places. Magical effects remained embedded in the landscape.

Castora had not preserved an untouched civilization.

It had preserved enough of one to change the surviving record.

Historical Caution // Castora

Seer structures and magical systems are widespread across Castora, but their presence should not be expanded into claims the surviving evidence does not establish. A Seer artifact does not prove that every magical feature of the island was created by Seers, and the survival of their works does not mean their civilization remained intact.

III

The Seers left more than ruins

The Seer remains on Castora reveal a civilization deeply invested in magical control, ritual, protection, and transformation.

Among the most striking survivals are the Seneschals, magically altered Castoran creatures bound by Seer magic to protect the island and its secrets.

Their continued existence demonstrates the durability of Seer enchantment. Whatever happened to the people who created those bindings, the magic did not simply fade when its makers disappeared.

Other sites preserve traps, constructs, enchanted defenses, ritual spaces, and artifacts whose functions point toward a culture in which powerful magic was incorporated directly into important places.

The island also preserves Seer writing and memorials. Tombs record individuals lost during dangerous magical rites, while sacred sites reveal that the pursuit of magical transformation carried both prestige and risk.

These discoveries make the Seers easier to understand as a people rather than merely as the civilization responsible for the Bloodstone.

They had internal disagreements, leaders, ritual specialists, sacred spaces, failures, ambitions, and ideas about what their own people could become.

IV

Isgarren was part of the world Castora remembers

Castora forced Isgarren to confront a history he had spent ages separated from.

The ancient wizard is himself a Seer.

His relationship with his people was not peaceful. During the old Seer civilization, Isgarren came into conflict with others of his kind and was brought before a council to determine his fate.

The decision divided the council.

Sidony cast the deciding judgment that resulted in Isgarren's banishment from the Seer ritual center on Castora.

The punishment went beyond exile.

A powerful curse was placed upon him and tied to Castora itself. Ancient defenses would reject him, Ancora would harm him if he attempted to wield it, and returning to the island exposed him once again to a sentence imposed in an age almost entirely forgotten by modern Tyria.

Isgarren's long absence from Castora therefore had a reason preserved within Seer history.

He had not simply left.

He had been cast out.

Commander Q // Observation

Isgarren spent centuries appearing to modern Tyria as an ancient mystery with very few equals. Castora does something useful to that image. Here he becomes one Seer among other Seers, subject to their laws, their judgments, and the consequences of his own history.

V

Ancora was built for transformation

The artifact sought by Vloxx was eventually identified more clearly.

Ancora is an ancient Seer staff tied to the ritual known as Divitium Nava, the Rite of Divinity.

The rite was performed at a specific well of power on Castora. Ancora and the magical qualities of that place were used to attune chosen candidates more deeply to the flow of magic through Tyria.

Successful candidates could emerge transformed into beings of extraordinary magical power and longevity.

Success was not guaranteed.

Seer tombs preserve the memory of individuals who died attempting the rite. Other surviving records show that preparation could consume years or centuries, and that failure could destroy the candidate rather than elevate them.

Isgarren himself had undergone this older form of ascension.

The practice he later used to elevate members of the Wizard's Court was not identical to Divitium Nava. It was a later and more controlled form of transformation derived from knowledge whose roots reached back to his own people.

Research Note // Divitium Nava

"Ascension" has been used for several different magical transformations throughout Tyrian history. The Seer Rite of Divinity should not automatically be treated as identical to the Ascension trials once administered by the Forgotten in the Crystal Desert or to the later process used by Isgarren when creating Wizards.

VI

Vloxx wanted more than another artifact

Director Vloxx's interest in Ancora was never simply the acquisition of a powerful relic for the Inquest.

He intended to perform Divitium Nava upon himself.

Vloxx argued that Tyria had become dangerously dependent upon powerful individuals and institutions deciding what knowledge or strength others were allowed to possess. In his view, Isgarren and the Tyrian Alliance represented part of the problem rather than its solution.

He claimed that the power he sought could help prepare Tyria for dangers still ahead and that extraordinary ability should not remain concentrated in the hands of a few ancient guardians.

His methods made those claims difficult to accept without suspicion.

The Inquest stripped Castora for knowledge, disturbed ancient sites, experimented with Seer magic, and treated dangerous discoveries as tools to be mastered before their consequences were understood.

Yet Vloxx increasingly separated his own purpose from the organization he commanded.

The question surrounding him became more complicated than whether he wanted power.

The question became what he believed that power would allow him to change.

Historical Caution // Vloxx

Vloxx has stated ambitions concerning Tyria's future and the distribution of power, but his complete plan has not yet entered the historical record. His stated motives should therefore be preserved as his claims rather than treated as proof of what he ultimately intends.

VII

Vloxx reaches the Rite of Divinity

In 1339 AE, the pursuit moved deeper into Castora toward the ritual grounds required for Divitium Nava.

Vloxx possessed Ancora.

The Commander and allies attempted to reach him before he could complete the rite, but the ancient Seer process could not be stopped in time.

Vloxx completed Divitium Nava.

The transformation granted him extraordinary magical power and a form of ascended existence rooted directly in the ancient Seer tradition.

His achievement immediately changed the balance of the conflict.

Vloxx no longer required the Inquest in the same way he had before arriving on Castora. When Kuda asserted control over the organization, Vloxx relinquished it.

Kuda became the new Director of the Inquest.

Vloxx walked away with the power he had come to Castora seeking.

Commander Q // Observation

Ancient magical knowledge has now done what ruins always threaten to do when someone understands them: it has stopped being archaeological evidence and become present-day power.

VIII

Castora releases an ancient punishment

Vloxx's ascension did not resolve Castora's oldest surviving conflict.

Sidony's curse still bound Isgarren to a punishment imposed by his own people ages before the modern expedition reached the island.

Understanding the curse required returning once more to Seer language, ritual, and surviving records rather than simply overpowering the magic that sustained it.

The ancient All Seer construct remained connected to the enchantment over Castora. After it was defeated, the old curse could finally be confronted directly.

The surviving wording was interpreted, revised, and used in a ritual that broke the punishment laid upon Isgarren.

The effects extended beyond him.

The fog surrounding Castora cleared, while ancient bindings associated with the island's guardians were also brought to an end.

A judgment imposed within Seer society had survived far longer than the society that created it.

In 1339 AE, that judgment finally ended.

IX

Castora changes what the archive knows about the Seers

The importance of Castora now extends well beyond Ancora.

Earlier histories could describe the Seers through a small number of extraordinary acts. They preserved magic during an ancient Dragonrise. They created the first Bloodstone. They fought a devastating war against the Mursaat. A handful of survivors appeared in later ages.

Castora gives that history texture.

The Seers possessed ritual specialists, councils, sacred sites, magical craftsmen, long-lived guardians, internal laws, ideological disagreements, and formal methods for attempting extraordinary transformation.

Their history also contained failure.

Candidates died attempting Divitium Nava. Political disputes ended in exile. Magical protections became curses. Guardians created to preserve secrets remained bound long after those secrets had lost their original keepers.

None of this diminishes what the Seers accomplished.

It makes them more understandable as a civilization.

They were not simply the wise ancient people who knew how to save magic.

They were people who had to decide what should be done with that knowledge once they possessed it.

Commander Q // Observation

The Seers become more interesting the less we treat them as a footnote attached to the Bloodstone. Castora gives us something rarer than another powerful artifact: evidence of how they actually lived with their power.

X

The record remains open

The archive presently reaches 1339 AE.

Castora has moved from rumor into explored geography. The Seer civilization that once occupied its sacred sites is better understood than it was only a short time ago.

Ancora has been recovered and used. Divitium Nava has been performed in the modern age. Director Vloxx has ascended and separated himself from leadership of the Inquest, while Kuda now commands the organization.

Isgarren's exile has been placed within the history of his own people, and the curse imposed upon him by Sidony has finally been broken.

The Castoran expedition has therefore accomplished something the people who first sailed toward the island could not have fully anticipated.

It has turned surviving Seer history into current Tyrian history.

Several questions remain unresolved, particularly Vloxx's ultimate purpose and what he intends to do with the power he acquired through the Rite of Divinity.

Those questions belong to events still unfolding.

Current Archive Position

1339 AE // Record Ongoing

The record currently reaches the events following Vloxx's completion of Divitium Nava and the breaking of Sidony's curse on Castora. This volume remains open and should continue forward as later events become part of the historical record.

Volume XI // Current Record

History is still being written

The Road Through Time began with fragmentary evidence from an ancient Dragonrise and followed civilizations through the Elder Races, the arrival of the Gods, Human kingdoms, the Guild Wars, the modern Dragon Cycle, and the world that followed it.

Castora has now brought the record back into direct contact with one of the peoples near its beginning.

The Seers once appeared in this archive through ruins, a Bloodstone, isolated survivors, and the memories of their enemies. Their own surviving places now tell a larger story.

That story is not finished.

Neither is this volume.
