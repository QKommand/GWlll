Author's Preface

The road bends toward the ancient world

The end of the Elder Dragon Cycle did not leave Tyria without a past. If anything, removing the Dragons from the center of every crisis made older histories easier to see. Janthir returned the Mursaat, the Seers, and the Titans to modern attention, while the disappearance of the Scepter of Orr into the Mists left another ancient question unresolved.

The trail now reaches Castora. What began as an expedition led by the Inquest has exposed ruins, magical practices, and knowledge tied to one of the oldest civilizations in the historical record.

This era is less about returning to old problems and more about understanding what survived them. The ancient world did not vanish cleanly. Its ruins, artifacts, and magical systems remain woven into the present.

— Commander Q

I

Director Vloxx sails for Castora

In 1338 AE, an Inquest expedition under Director Vloxx traveled to Castora, a distant island that had remained largely outside the affairs of modern Tyria.

The expedition was searching for Ancora. Whatever Vloxx expected to find, the journey placed the Inquest within a region containing evidence of a history far older than the organization itself.

Castora had existed on the edge of Tyrian knowledge for generations, but exploration now moved it from distant geography into the active historical record.

Commander Q // Observation

There is a recurring mistake in Tyrian history: assuming that because a place has been absent from our maps, it has also been absent from history. Castora appears determined to correct that assumption.

II

A distant island reveals an older past

Castora's importance does not come simply from its isolation. The island contains ruins and surviving traces connected to civilizations whose histories reach back thousands of years.

The conflicts of Janthir had already forced modern Tyria to reconsider what remained of the Mursaat and Seers. Castora carried that question farther.

The island represents another surviving piece of the world that existed before Humanity became one of Tyria's dominant civilizations.

III

The Seers return to the center of history

The Seers once stood among the Elder Races that survived the ancient Dragonrise. Their mastery of magic produced the original Bloodstone and helped preserve uncorrupted magic from the Elder Dragons.

Their later war with the Mursaat nearly destroyed their civilization. By the original Guild Wars era, only isolated survivors were known.

Discoveries on Castora therefore carry unusual weight. Seer ruins and surviving knowledge suggest that their historical legacy extended beyond the fragments previously known to modern scholars.

Research Note // The Seer Record

Evidence of Seer construction or magical knowledge should not automatically be treated as evidence that an unknown population of living Seers survived there. Ruins, artifacts, preserved systems, and living members of a civilization are separate claims.

IV

Ancora draws the past into the present

The search for Ancora placed ancient magical knowledge at the center of the Castoran expedition. Its significance extends beyond the ambitions of the Inquest or the immediate struggle surrounding the island.

Seer magic belongs to a tradition that predates the arrival of Humanity, the Human Gods, the Exodus, and the Guild Wars. Its reappearance connects the current era directly to the earliest volumes of this archive.

The Bloodstones already demonstrated how an artifact created in the ancient world could continue shaping later civilizations for thousands of years. Castora raises a similar question about knowledge itself.

Commander Q // Observation

An artifact can survive because someone buried it. Knowledge is harder. If a magical practice survives thousands of years, then somewhere along the road someone understood enough to preserve it.

V

Ancient practice becomes a modern concern

Among the Seer traditions brought back into modern attention is the Rite of Divinity. Its appearance reinforces a pattern that has become increasingly clear since the events of Janthir.

Ancient magical systems did not necessarily disappear when the civilizations that created them declined. Some survived through structures, artifacts, rituals, or records that later peoples could discover and use.

Castora therefore marks more than the discovery of old ruins. It represents the return of ancient magical practice to a world whose institutions were built thousands of years after that practice began.

VI

The archive reaches 1339 AE

By 1339 AE, the oldest layers of Tyrian history have again become part of the affairs of the modern world.

The Elder Dragon Cycle has ended, while Aurene remains responsible for regulating the magical balance left behind. The Tyrian Alliance represents a growing effort toward cooperation among the world's nations, and contact with the Mists has moved far beyond what earlier generations considered possible.

At the same time, Janthir and Castora have brought attention back to the Seers, the Mursaat, ancient magical systems, and conflicts that began before Humanity entered the historical record.

The present now depends upon understanding a past that Tyria once believed had disappeared.
