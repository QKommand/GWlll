Volume VII // The Guild Wars Era

The Guild Wars Era

On the collapse of old kingdoms, the fulfillment of ancient prophecies, the death of a fallen God, and the first signs that the Elder Dragon Cycle was returning.

Author's Preface

An old war gives way to a different world

The previous record ended with the Third Guild War consuming the Human kingdoms of continental Tyria. For more than half a century, Ascalon and Kryta had remained locked in conflict while Orr attempted, unsuccessfully, to bring the fighting to an end.

Then the Charr invaded.

Within only a few years, Ascalon was devastated by the Searing, Orr disappeared beneath the sea, Kryta passed under White Mantle rule, and the Flameseeker Prophecies began moving toward their fulfillment. Events that had developed separately over centuries suddenly collided.

The crises did not remain in Tyria. Shiro Tagachi returned to Cantha. Abaddon's followers attempted to bring Nightfall to Elona. A mortal woman ascended into the Human pantheon, while disturbances beneath the surface drove the Asura from their homes and brought the Dwarves into their final war.

These are the events remembered through the original Guild Wars campaigns and the years immediately after them. Together they form something larger than a record of individual heroes and battles. They mark the point where the history of kingdoms begins to reconnect with the much older history of Tyria itself.

By the end of this era, the most important discovery was not that another kingdom had fallen or another enemy had been defeated.

It was that an Elder Dragon was beginning to stir.

— Commander Q

I

The final decades of the Third Guild War

The Third Guild War continued for decades after its beginning in 1013 AE. Ascalon and Kryta remained the principal Human powers locked in the conflict, while Orr eventually committed forces in an attempt to impose peace.

Orrian intervention failed. The war continued, draining soldiers and attention from kingdoms whose rivalries had become more important to them than dangers beyond their borders.

Kryta was already experiencing another change. During the later years of the war, Saul D'Alessio encountered the ancient Mursaat. Believing the beings who had saved him to be Divine, Saul proclaimed them the Unseen Ones and founded the religious order that became the White Mantle.

The Mursaat were not Gods. They were survivors of a civilization whose history reached back to the previous Dragonrise and the ancient war against the Seers.

Their return to Human history therefore brought one of the oldest conflicts in this archive into the politics of a kingdom that knew almost nothing about it.

Research Note // Saul D'Alessio

Records place the founding of the White Mantle during the closing years of the Third Guild War, but the exact sequence surrounding Saul's exile, his first encounter with the Mursaat, and later events connected with Janthir is not preserved with complete certainty.

II

The Charr invade Ascalon

In 1070 AE, the Charr launched their great invasion south into Human territory.

Their forces brought with them enormous magical Cauldrons provided through the Flame Legion's new religion. At the Great Northern Wall, the Charr performed the Ritual of the Searing.

Fire fell across Ascalon.

The Searing destroyed farmland, settlements, forests, and much of the kingdom's ability to sustain itself. The Great Northern Wall was breached soon afterward, allowing the Charr armies to push deeper into Ascalonian territory.

The invasion accomplished what decades of negotiation had failed to achieve. The Third Guild War ended as the Human kingdoms were forced to turn away from one another and defend themselves against the Charr.

Kryta faced invasion as well. Saul D'Alessio and the White Mantle fought the Charr with the aid of their Mursaat patrons, helping preserve the kingdom while King Jadon disappeared from public rule.

The order that helped save Kryta increasingly became the order that governed it.

Commander Q // Observation

The Third Guild War did not end because Ascalon, Kryta, and Orr found peace. It ended because another war made their old one impossible to continue.

III

The Cataclysm destroys Orr

In 1071 AE, the Charr invasion reached Orr.

Much of the Orrian military had only recently returned from the Guild War, and the Charr advanced toward the heart of a kingdom whose capital still contained the remains of the ancient City of the Gods.

Vizier Khilbron, advisor to King Reza, turned to forbidden magic while searching for a means to stop the invasion.

The result was the Cataclysm of Orr.

The spell destroyed the invading Charr but also devastated Orr itself. Much of the peninsula sank beneath the sea, its cities were shattered, and most of its population died.

Arah, the temples of the Gods, ancient vaults, magical collections, and the infrastructure of the kingdom disappeared beneath the waters with it.

Khilbron survived the destruction in changed form. He became an Undead Lich, and the consequences of the spell he cast would follow him into the events of the next year.

Historical Caution // Khilbron

Orr's destruction and Khilbron's use of the forbidden magic are established. Surviving accounts complicate the question of how much he understood beforehand about the spell, its true source, and the destruction it would cause.

IV

The Flameseeker Prophecies

Two years after the Searing, the surviving kingdom of Ascalon remained trapped in a war it could no longer realistically win.

Prince Rurik chose another path. Rejecting King Adelbern's determination to remain in Ascalon, Rurik led refugees west across the Shiverpeak Mountains toward Kryta.

He died during the crossing, but many of the refugees reached their destination.

Kryta presented a different danger. The White Mantle now governed much of the kingdom under the influence of the Mursaat. Humans identified by the Eye of Janthir as Chosen were taken to the Maguuma Bloodstone and sacrificed.

Their souls were used to power the magical system that helped keep the Door of Komalie sealed. The Mursaat knew that opening the Door would release the Titans and bring about the destruction foretold for them in Glint's prophecy.

The Shining Blade exposed the truth behind the Mantle's sacrifices. The conflict eventually led through the Crystal Desert, Ascension, and an encounter with Glint herself.

The prophecy recorded in 272 AE was reaching its end.

At the Door of Komalie, the Undead Lich manipulated events toward opening the portal. The Titans emerged, while the Mursaat guarding the Door were devastated.

Khilbron was defeated, and the Titans that escaped into Tyria were later hunted down.

The Flameseeker Prophecies had been fulfilled.

Commander Q // Observation

The Mursaat had survived an Elder Dragon cycle, disappeared from the world, and endured their war with the Seers. In the end, the threat they feared most came from a prophecy they had spent generations trying to prevent.

V

Shiro returns to Cantha

During the same year, Cantha faced a crisis rooted in its own past.

Shiro Tagachi, whose death two centuries earlier had unleashed the Jade Wind, returned as a spirit and began manipulating events throughout the Empire.

A supernatural sickness known as the Affliction spread across Cantha, transforming its victims into violent creatures. The outbreak reached from Shing Jea into Kaineng City and through the Jade Sea and Echovald Forest.

Shiro also corrupted the dragon Kuunavang. She was later freed from his influence and became an ally in the struggle against him.

Kurzick and Luxon forces, normally bitter rivals, were drawn into the same conflict as the Imperial defenders and foreign allies arriving from Tyria.

Shiro was defeated again before he could complete his return to mortal life.

His defeat ended the immediate crisis, but it did not remove the Afflicted from Cantha. The Empire would spend years dealing with what he had left behind.

VI

The aftermath of the Affliction

Shiro was gone, but the Afflicted remained scattered throughout Cantha.

In 1073 AE, Reiko Murakami, then associated with the Ministry of Flame, began rallying Canthans around the goal of cleansing the Empire of the Afflicted.

Her influence continued to grow. In 1077 AE, the Ministry of Purity was formally established with Reiko at its head.

Its message was simple and initially difficult to oppose: protect Cantha, eliminate the Afflicted, and restore order after years of fear.

The power created by that campaign would eventually extend well beyond the creatures Shiro had left behind.

VII

Abaddon's return

In 1075 AE, the consequences of a war fought more than a thousand years earlier returned to the mortal world.

Warmarshal Varesh Ossa and other followers of Abaddon worked to bring about Nightfall, weakening the boundaries between Tyria and the Realm of Torment.

Demonic forces entered Elona as portions of the physical world began overlapping with Abaddon's realm. The conflict spread from Istan into Kourna, Vabbi, the Desolation, and eventually beyond Tyria itself.

The struggle also led to the release of Palawa Joko, who had remained imprisoned since his defeat by Turai Ossa centuries earlier.

Joko helped provide passage through the Desolation. He was useful.

He was also free again.

Within the Realm of Torment, Abaddon was finally confronted and defeated.

His death created another problem. The magical power held by a God could not simply be allowed to escape uncontrolled.

Kormir, former Spearmarshal of the Sunspears, absorbed that power and ascended as the Goddess of Truth.

More than a thousand years after Abaddon had been removed from the pantheon, a mortal took the place his fall had left empty.

Commander Q // Observation

The Gods imprisoned Abaddon rather than simply destroying him. Nightfall finally revealed part of the reason: killing a God does not destroy the power that made them one.

VIII

The ground begins to move

Other conflicts were changing the societies that would inherit the next age.

In 1076 AE, Pyre Fierceshot and his Warband openly challenged the Flame Legion's Shaman caste. The Titans worshiped as Charr Gods had been exposed as creatures rather than true Divinities, weakening the religious order that had helped unite the Charr invasion.

Two years later, earthquakes began opening passages across Tyria.

Beneath the surface, the Destroyers were spreading through the Depths. Their advance devastated underground settlements and forced many Asura toward the surface.

The crisis exposed a subterranean world much older and more complicated than most surface peoples understood. Natural caverns, Dwarven settlements, Asuran gateway networks, abandoned ruins, magical guardians, enchanted weapons, and structures of uncertain origin existed throughout the Depths.

Humans, Asura, Norn, Dwarves, and Charr rebels were drawn into the same expanding conflict.

IX

The Great Destroyer

The Destroyer invasion brought the Dwarves to the fulfillment of one of their oldest religious traditions.

To fight the creatures spreading through the Depths, King Jalis Ironhammer invoked the Rite of the Great Dwarf.

The Dwarves underwent the Transformation, becoming beings of living stone bound to their struggle against the Destroyers.

Not every Dwarf accepted the transformation in the same way, and later survivors would demonstrate that the Rite did not erase every older political or ideological division among their people.

The immediate war culminated in the defeat of the Great Destroyer.

That victory prevented the Destroyer armies from overrunning the surface.

It also revealed what they served.

Deep beneath Tyria rested Primordus, an Elder Dragon whose approaching awakening had begun stirring its minions into action.

Historical Correction // Primordus

Primordus did not fully awaken in 1078 AE. The defeat of the Great Destroyer delayed the Elder Dragon's awakening for roughly two generations. Primordus would finally awaken in 1120 AE.

Commander Q // Observation

The Great Destroyer looked like the enemy. It was actually the warning.

X

Kryta breaks the White Mantle

The conflict that began when the truth behind the White Mantle was exposed did not end with the Flameseeker Prophecies.

The civil war that had divided Kryta since 1072 AE reached its decisive stage in 1079 AE.

Princess Salma, supported by the Shining Blade and allied forces, challenged the White Mantle government and the remaining Mursaat who continued supporting it.

The struggle culminated in the Battle for Lion's Arch.

Salma's forces prevailed. The White Mantle lost open control of Kryta, while the surviving Mursaat were reduced even further after the destruction they had already suffered during the Flameseeker Prophecies.

Salma became Queen of Kryta and founded a new royal line whose descendants would continue shaping the kingdom into the following centuries.

The same period also brought Gwen and Keiran Thackeray closer together, connecting the Ebon Vanguard with a family whose name would remain important long after the original Guild Wars era ended.

XI

Winds of Change

In Cantha, the Ministry of Purity used the continuing Afflicted crisis to expand its authority.

What had begun as a campaign against Shiro's remaining creatures gradually broadened. The Ministry increasingly treated political rivals, outsiders, and groups it considered dangerous to Canthan society as threats to the Empire's purity.

Opposition formed against Reiko Murakami and the increasingly authoritarian movement she controlled.

Reiko was eventually defeated. Ashu, the young survivor who had become an important symbol of the Ministry, succeeded her as its leader.

The immediate conflict ended, but Cantha did not return to the society that existed before the Affliction.

The Ministry survived, Imperial nationalism continued to grow, and the political direction of the Empire was turning increasingly inward.

XII

The end of the Guild Wars Era

In 1080 AE, King Adelbern recalled the Ebon Vanguard from the north.

The Vanguard moved south and fortified the settlement of Ebonhawke, creating a major Human stronghold near the Blazeridge Mountains.

The decision would become increasingly important as Charr control expanded through the former kingdom of Ascalon and Human territory continued shrinking.

By then, most of the crises remembered through the original Guild Wars campaigns had reached their immediate conclusions.

The Flameseeker Prophecies had been fulfilled. Shiro Tagachi had been defeated. Abaddon was dead. Kormir had ascended. Kryta had overthrown the White Mantle. The Ministry of Purity had survived its own internal struggle. The Great Destroyer had fallen.

Yet the most important consequence of those years had only begun to unfold.

The Destroyers were not an isolated subterranean race. They were servants of an Elder Dragon.

For the first time in thousands of years, the Dragon Cycle was no longer merely ancient history.

Primordus had been delayed.

It had not been stopped.

Commander Q // Observation

The histories of the Guild Wars seemed enormous to the people who lived through them: kingdoms fell, prophecies were fulfilled, a fallen God died, and a mortal became Divine. Beneath all of it, something older had begun waking up.

End of Volume VII

The Dragon Cycle returns

The Guild Wars Era began with Human kingdoms fighting one another and ended with those same civilizations confronting dangers whose histories reached far beyond any kingdom.

The Mursaat returned from the ancient world and were nearly destroyed. The Dwarves transformed themselves to continue a war beneath Tyria. The Asura were driven from parts of the Depths, while the Norn and Charr became increasingly tied to events beyond their traditional territories.

Most importantly, the Great Destroyer's defeat had revealed that Primordus was beginning to stir.

The victory of 1078 AE bought Tyria time.

It did not end the cycle.
