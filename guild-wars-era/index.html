Author's Preface

The world changes very quickly

The previous volume ended in 1013 AE with the beginning of the Third Guild War. For decades, the Human kingdoms of continental Tyria remained consumed by a conflict whose origins reached back through generations of Guild rivalry.

Then the scale of history changed. The Charr invaded Ascalon, Orr disappeared beneath the sea, an ancient prophecy reached fulfillment, Shiro Tagachi returned to Cantha, and Abaddon's thousand-year struggle against his imprisonment finally came to an end.

By 1078 AE, even those events began to look smaller. Creatures were emerging from beneath the earth, ancient civilizations were being driven from their homes, and the Dwarves transformed themselves to fight an enemy connected to something much older than the kingdoms above them.

This is the period remembered through the original Guild Wars campaigns. More importantly for this archive, it is the bridge between the age of Human kingdoms and the return of the Elder Dragons.

— Commander Q

I

The old war continues

The Third Guild War continued for more than half a century after its beginning in 1013 AE. Ascalon and Kryta remained bitter rivals, while Orr eventually entered the conflict in a failed attempt to bring the fighting to an end.

Elsewhere, events were already creating the political order that would shape the coming crisis. In 1067 AE, the exiled Saul D'Alessio encountered the Mursaat and came to worship them as the Unseen Ones.

The faith Saul founded became the White Mantle. His followers later traveled to Janthir, deepening their connection to the Mursaat and the mysterious Eye of Janthir.

Human kingdoms remained focused upon one another, unaware that another army was preparing beyond their northern frontier.

II

The Charr end the Guild War

In 1070 AE, the Charr launched a massive invasion southward. At the Great Northern Wall, their forces performed the Ritual of the Searing, using magical Cauldrons to call fire down across Ascalon.

The devastation transformed the kingdom. Settlements burned, farmland became wasteland, and the Great Northern Wall that had defended Human Ascalon for generations was breached.

The invasion also accomplished something decades of diplomacy had failed to do. The Third Guild War ended as Ascalon, Kryta, and Orr were forced to defend themselves against the Charr rather than continue fighting one another.

Kryta survived through the intervention of the White Mantle and their Mursaat allies. With King Jadon gone, the order that had saved Kryta increasingly became the power governing it.

Commander Q // Observation

The Third Guild War did not end because anyone finally won. It ended because the kingdoms suddenly had a larger problem.

III

Orr disappears beneath the sea

In 1071 AE, the Charr invaded Orr while much of the kingdom's military strength was still away from home. Their advance threatened the heart of the most magically sophisticated Human kingdom on the Tyrian continent.

Vizier Khilbron turned to forbidden magic in an attempt to stop the invasion. The spell unleashed the Cataclysm of Orr, destroying the kingdom and sinking most of the land beneath the sea.

Nearly the entire population died. Khilbron survived, but not as the man he had been. He became the Undead Lich, carrying Orr's tragedy into the events that followed.

The same year also brought renewed contact between distant Human societies. The Second Treaty of Lion's Arch formalized resurgent trade between Cantha and the devastated Tyrian kingdoms.

Historical Caution

Accounts of Khilbron's motives are not entirely consistent. The destruction of Orr is certain. Whether he fully understood what his spell would do is much less secure.

IV

Glint's prophecy reaches fulfillment

By 1072 AE, Ascalon's survivors were fighting simply to endure. Prince Rurik led refugees west across the Shiverpeaks toward Kryta after disagreeing with King Adelbern over the future of the kingdom. Rurik died during the crossing, but the refugees continued.

Kryta offered safety only on the surface. The White Mantle had been sacrificing Humans identified as Chosen, using their souls to power devices that protected the Mursaat from the prophecy that foretold their destruction.

The resistance of the Shining Blade exposed the truth behind the Mantle's rule. The road eventually led through the Crystal Desert, to Glint, and finally to the Door of Komalie.

The Flameseeker Prophecies, recorded eight centuries earlier, were fulfilled. The Mursaat were devastated and the Door opened, releasing the Titans into Tyria.

The Undead Lich was defeated, and the Titan threat was eventually contained. One prophecy had ended, but Tyria had little time to recover.

V

Shiro Tagachi returns

In Cantha, another piece of ancient history returned to the present. Shiro Tagachi, whose death had unleashed the Jade Wind two centuries earlier, returned and spread a supernatural plague known as the Affliction.

Victims were transformed into violent creatures as the disease spread from Shing Jea through Kaineng City and into the Echovald Forest and Jade Sea.

Shiro also corrupted the dragon Kuunavang. She was eventually freed from his influence and joined the forces opposing him.

Shiro was defeated again in 1072 AE, ending his immediate threat. The Afflicted, however, remained throughout Cantha and created a political problem that survived the man who had caused it.

VI

Cantha turns recovery into politics

The Afflicted remained a threat after Shiro's defeat. In 1073 AE, Reiko Murakami began organizing Canthans around the goal of removing them from the Empire.

By 1077 AE, her movement had become the Ministry of Purity. Its campaign promised security, order, and the final elimination of the Afflicted.

The Ministry's rise mattered because the struggle against the Afflicted was no longer only a response to Shiro. It was becoming a source of political power in its own right.

VII

Abaddon's rebellion reaches its final chapter

In 1075 AE, Varesh Ossa attempted to bring about Nightfall, weakening the barriers between Tyria and the Realm of Torment so that Abaddon could escape his thousand-year imprisonment.

Demons entered Elona and portions of the landscape began changing under Abaddon's influence. During the struggle, the long-imprisoned Palawa Joko was also freed, creating consequences that would extend far beyond the immediate crisis.

Abaddon was eventually confronted within the Realm of Torment and killed. His Divine power could not simply vanish, however, without catastrophic consequences.

Kormir absorbed the released power and ascended into the pantheon as the Goddess of Truth, inheriting much of the Divine authority once held by Abaddon.

Commander Q // Observation

The Gods had imprisoned Abaddon because destroying him was not a simple solution. More than a thousand years later, mortals discovered why. A God's power does not politely disappear when its owner dies.

VIII

Old orders begin to break

In 1076 AE, Pyre Fierceshot led his Warband against the Flame Legion's Shaman caste. The Titans once presented to the Charr as Gods had already been exposed as false, and opposition to the religious order built around them was becoming organized.

Two years later, earthquakes began spreading across Tyria. Underground passages opened, and creatures known as Destroyers attacked settlements beneath the surface.

The crisis forced the Asura from parts of their subterranean civilization and brought Humans into much greater contact with both the Asura and the Norn. Charr rebels, Human forces, Asura, Norn, and Dwarves became part of the wider resistance against the Destroyers.

IX

The Dwarves make their final choice

The Destroyer invasion brought the ancient Dwarves to a turning point. Through the Rite of the Great Dwarf, the Dwarven people underwent the Transformation, becoming beings of living stone.

Their purpose was to fight the Destroyers in a war that would continue beneath Tyria long after most surface civilizations had lost sight of it.

The Great Destroyer was eventually defeated in 1078 AE. Its destruction halted the immediate invasion, but investigation of the threat revealed something far more important.

The Destroyers were servants of Primordus.

Historical Correction

Primordus did not fully awaken in 1078 AE. The Elder Dragon was beginning to stir, and the Great Destroyer acted as its Champion. The Great Destroyer's defeat delayed the greater awakening, but it did not prevent it.

X

Kryta ends White Mantle rule

In 1079 AE, the long conflict over Kryta's government reached its conclusion. Forces loyal to Salma and the Shining Blade confronted the White Mantle and their remaining Mursaat allies.

Salma emerged victorious and claimed the Krytan throne, ending the White Mantle's open control of the kingdom. The Mursaat, already devastated during the Flameseeker Prophecies, were reduced even further.

Events surrounding Gwen, Keiran Thackeray, and the Ebon Vanguard also unfolded during this period, connecting the recovering Human kingdoms to the next generation of Tyrian history.

XI

Cantha changes after the Afflicted

In Cantha, the Ministry of Purity entered the final stage of its campaign against the Afflicted. The movement had grown considerably since Reiko Murakami first began organizing it.

The campaign succeeded in reducing the Afflicted threat, but the Ministry's expanding definition of "purity" increasingly placed political opponents and other groups within its sights.

Reiko was eventually defeated, while Ashu assumed leadership of the Ministry. The events remembered as Winds of Change therefore ended one crisis while leaving Cantha with a powerful institution that would continue shaping its future.

XII

The world reaches another threshold

In 1080 AE, the Ebon Vanguard withdrew from the north and fortified Ebonhawke. The fortress became one of the last great centers of Human resistance in the lands once belonging to Ascalon.

By this point, the campaigns that defined the original Guild Wars era were largely over. The Flameseeker Prophecies had been fulfilled, Shiro was gone, Abaddon was dead, Kryta had reclaimed its monarchy, and the Destroyers had been forced back.

None of those victories changed what had been discovered beneath Tyria.

Primordus was stirring.

The same Dragon Cycle that had shaped the earliest volumes of this archive was beginning again.

Commander Q // Observation

This is where the road begins to turn back toward its beginning. For thousands of years, civilizations built their histories over the memory of sleeping Dragons. By 1080 AE, the warning had arrived that those histories were about to meet the Dragons again.

End of Volume VII

The road continues

The heroes of the Guild Wars era had faced fallen Gods, Undead armies, ancient prophecies, Titans, the Afflicted, and the Destroyers. The greater threat was still beneath their feet.
