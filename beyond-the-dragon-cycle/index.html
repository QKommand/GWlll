Author's Preface

After the Dragons

The death of Soo-Won ended the Elder Dragon Cycle. Aurene remained to maintain the world's magical balance, but the Dragon wars no longer stood at the center of every crisis.

Other histories quickly took their place.

The Wizard's Tower concealed an organization that had defended Tyria for centuries. The Mists contained civilizations with conflicts of their own. Mursaat, Seer, Jotun, Titan, and Orrian histories returned to the present through survivors, ruins, artifacts, and places that had remained beyond ordinary knowledge.

The end of the Dragon Cycle did not close the ancient record.

It opened parts of it that had been hidden.

— Commander Q

I

Aurene withdraws from ordinary affairs

In 1336 AE, Aurene stepped away from ordinary Tyrian affairs to concentrate upon maintaining the world's magical balance.

She had become the sole surviving Elder Dragon and carried a burden unlike that held by the Dragons who came before her.

Aurene could filter different forms of magic without continuing the old cycle of consumption and slumber, but maintaining that balance required her attention.

Tyria could no longer expect an Elder Dragon ally to enter every political or military crisis.

The post-Dragon world would have to govern itself.

II

The Wizard's Tower reveals its purpose

Soon after Aurene's withdrawal, events surrounding the Wizard's Tower exposed a history that had remained largely hidden from the nations below it.

Behind the Tower stood Isgarren, the Wizard's Court, and their stronghold at Amnytas.

They watched threats extending beyond ordinary Tyrian borders and into the Mists.

Serving beside them was the Astral Ward.

The Ward had been established centuries earlier and had spent generations fighting dangers almost entirely absent from public Tyrian history.

The Commander was not witnessing the beginning of a new organization.

The Commander was discovering an old one.

III

The Kryptis war crosses the Mists

The Wizard's hidden war became public when Kryptis forces began breaching Tyria.

Their attacks reached the Wizard's Tower, Amnytas, and other places defended by the Astral Ward.

The Kryptis were not Dragon minions or mindless demons. They belonged to a society centered upon the realm of Nayos.

Their invasion was tied to Eparch, the Midnight King, whose rule was opposed by Peitha and other Kryptis.

The Commander eventually crossed into Nayos and joined Peitha's rebellion.

Tyrians and Kryptis fought together against Eparch.

Eparch was defeated, and Peitha emerged as ruler of Nayos.

The war ended through a change of government rather than the destruction of the people who had first appeared as Tyria's enemy.

Commander Q // Observation

Nayos looked simple from the other side of a portal: demons were invading Tyria. Crossing that portal made the history much less convenient.

IV

Ancient peoples return to the record

The Wizard's Court also preserved living connections to peoples usually encountered only through ancient history.

Mabon was a Mursaat who had spent centuries serving with the Court.

His life stood apart from the Mursaat history most Tyrians knew through the White Mantle, the Chosen, and the Flameseeker Prophecies.

Mabon died during the Kryptis crisis after resisting possession.

His existence remains important because it separates the history of a people from the choices of every individual belonging to it.

Dagda provided another connection to the ancient world as a Jotun member of the Wizard's Court.

Investigations also returned attention to the Seers. At the Bloodstone Caves, the spirit of Sidony offered another surviving link to the civilization that had once preserved magic from the Elder Dragons.

Historical Caution // Survival

Individual survivors, spirits, and artifacts do not establish that ancient civilizations continued unchanged into the modern age. They show that their histories survived in more places than the public record once recognized.

V

The Tyrian Alliance forms

The Elder Dragon wars had repeatedly forced nations and peoples to cooperate against threats no border could contain.

The Pact had been created primarily as a military answer to the Dragons.

The world after them needed something broader.

In 1337 AE, representatives from major powers gathered in Lion's Arch for the first meeting of the Tyrian Alliance.

The Alliance created a forum for shared political problems before those problems became another global war.

Cooperation that had once been temporary was beginning to become part of Tyria's political structure.

Its first major test would lead toward Janthir.

VI

Janthir reveals the Mursaat world

An expedition involving the Commander, Tyrian Alliance, and Astral Ward traveled toward the Isles of Janthir.

Janthir was already familiar through names associated with the White Mantle and the Eye of Janthir, but the islands themselves remained poorly understood.

The expedition encountered the Lowland Kodan, whose communities had histories independent of the Mursaat ruins surrounding them.

Those ruins changed the Mursaat record.

During the Guild Wars era, the Mursaat appeared mainly as hidden patrons of the White Mantle. Ancient tablets later connected them to the previous Dragonrise and their war with the Seers.

Janthir preserved evidence of the Mursaat as a civilization on its own ground.

Places such as Bava Nisos belonged to a history far older than Saul D'Alessio or the White Mantle.

Mabon had shown that Mursaat lives existed outside the familiar Krytan story.

Janthir revealed more of the world those lives came from.

VII

The Titans return

The Janthir expedition soon encountered another enemy belonging to an older history: the Titans.

Titans had emerged through the Door of Komalie during the Flameseeker Prophecies and had also been connected to the beings presented to the Charr as Gods during the years surrounding the Searing.

More than two centuries later, powerful Titans again appeared in Janthir.

Their presence was connected to Bava Nisos and passages through the Mists toward the Realm of Torment.

The crisis brought another relic of the original Guild Wars era back into use.

Livia proposed the Scepter of Orr as a weapon against the Titans.

An artifact tied to Orr, Khilbron, Undead magic, and the Flameseeker Prophecies had returned to active history.

Historical Caution // Titans

The Foundry of Failed Creations is a major source of Titans, but surviving testimony should not be forced into the claim that every Titan in history originated from one location.

VIII

The war returns to the Foundry

Investigation of the Titan threat eventually led through the Mists to the Foundry of Failed Creations.

The Foundry stood within the Domain of Anguish in the Realm of Torment.

It had already been encountered during the original Guild Wars, when heroes entered the facility and fought forces associated with the creation of Titans.

In 1338 AE, modern forces returned.

The campaign ended the immediate Titan threat to Janthir and carried the Tyrian Alliance into a place tied directly to Abaddon and one of the oldest major crises of the previous age.

The road from Janthir had crossed Mursaat ruins, the Scepter of Orr, Titans, and the Realm of Torment.

None of those histories had actually ended where the old records stopped following them.

IX

The road turns toward Castora

By the end of the Titan crisis, the modern world had reopened several histories once thought largely closed.

Mursaat civilization had returned through Janthir. Seer history had resurfaced through surviving artifacts, spirits, and the investigations surrounding the Wizard's Court.

Then another ancient name entered the record again.

Castora.

The island had already survived in fragments of Margonite navigation and scattered references tied to ancient Seer history.

In the modern age, interest in Castora returned through the Inquest and the search for the Seer artifact known as Ancora.

The oldest volumes of this archive had followed the Seers through the previous Dragonrise, the Bloodstone, and their war with the Mursaat.

The next record follows that history onto ground where the Seers once lived.

Commander Q // Observation

The road through time has finally done something strange. Instead of taking us farther into the past, it has brought the past forward to meet us.

End of Volume X

The archive reaches the present

The Elder Dragon Cycle was over. Nayos had changed rulers. The Tyrian Alliance had begun turning cooperation between nations into a lasting institution.

Janthir had returned Mursaat ruins, Titans, the Scepter of Orr, and the Realm of Torment to active history.

The next path leads toward the Seers.
