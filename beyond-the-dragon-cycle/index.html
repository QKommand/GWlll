THE WORLD AFTER THE DRAGONS

Volume X // Beyond the Dragon Cycle

Beyond the Dragon Cycle

On the hidden civilizations, secret wars, and ancient histories that emerged once the Elder Dragons no longer stood at the center of Tyrian affairs.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

Removing one great mystery revealed several others

For much of recorded history, the Elder Dragons formed the largest pattern behind Tyrian catastrophe. Their awakenings moved populations, destroyed kingdoms, redirected magic, and eventually forced civilizations that had once regarded one another as enemies to build common institutions.

The death of Soo-Won and the rise of Aurene ended that ancient cycle, but history did not become simpler. Instead, removing the Dragons from the center of the story exposed parts of the world that had remained concealed behind them.

The Wizard's Tower was not merely an unexplained object floating above Kryta. The Mists contained civilizations with politics of their own. The Mursaat had not vanished from history as completely as once believed, and threats remembered from the oldest Guild Wars records could still return to the present.

There is a useful lesson in that. History rarely ends when one great problem is solved. More often, removing the largest obstacle simply allows us to see what was standing behind it.

— Commander Q

I

Aurene leaves mortal history to mortals

In 1336 AE, Aurene withdrew from ordinary Tyrian affairs and settled into her role as the principal conduit for the world's magical balance.

Her withdrawal did not mean she had abandoned Tyria. Maintaining the flow of magic had become a responsibility large enough to require her attention beyond the normal political and military struggles of the world.

The change was nevertheless significant. For years, Aurene had fought beside the Commander against Gods, Elder Dragons, and other threats. Now the nations and organizations of Tyria would increasingly have to meet new dangers without an Elder Dragon fighting directly at their side.

The Age of Aurene therefore began with an unusual kind of Divine distance. Tyria still possessed a powerful protector, but one whose greatest service required her to remain apart from ordinary affairs.

II

A hidden history reveals itself

Not long afterward, events surrounding the Wizard's Tower drew the Commander into an organization whose history had remained largely outside the knowledge of ordinary Tyrian nations.

The Wizard's Court operated from Amnytas under the leadership of the ancient wizard Isgarren. Serving alongside the Court was the Astral Ward, a force dedicated to defending the Wizards and confronting threats that originated beyond ordinary Tyria.

The Ward itself was not new. It had been formally established in 604 AE, when Taryn became its first Warden. For more than seven centuries, its members had fought dangers most people living below them never knew existed.

The discovery changed the scale of known Tyrian history. While kingdoms fought Guild Wars, while Dragons awakened, and while the Pact marched into Orr, another war had existed almost entirely outside the public record.

Archive Connection

The Astral Ward was established in 604 AE, long before the events of the original Guild Wars. Its discovery in 1336 AE therefore represents the revelation of an old institution, not the founding of a new one.

III

The Mists produce another kind of enemy

The secrecy surrounding the Wizard's Court could not survive the crisis that followed. Creatures known as the Kryptis began breaching Tyria from the Mists, attacking both individuals and the hidden strongholds of the Wizards.

The Kryptis were not mindless demons. They belonged to a society originating in the realm of Nayos, and their attacks formed part of a much older conflict between their ruler, Eparch, and Isgarren's Court.

Their offensive compromised Amnytas and the Wizard's Tower, forcing the Court and Astral Ward into a defensive war that could no longer remain hidden.

The threat marked an important change from the conflicts of the Dragon Cycle. Tyria was not confronting a creature tied to its own magical ecosystem. It was dealing with an organized civilization from another realm.

IV

A Mursaat dies defending Tyria

Among the senior Wizards was Mabon, whose presence complicated a history Tyria had long considered settled. Mabon was a Mursaat.

To most modern Tyrians, the Mursaat belonged to the story of the White Mantle, the Flameseeker Prophecies, and the ancient crimes committed in the name of the Unseen Ones. Yet Mabon had spent centuries within the Wizard's Court rather than among the Mursaat powers encountered during the Guild Wars era.

During the Kryptis invasion, Mabon was possessed and ultimately died while resisting the corruption.

His death weakened the Wizard's Court at a critical moment, but historically it accomplished something else as well. It returned the Mursaat to the living present rather than leaving them safely contained in ancient history.

Commander Q // Observation

It is easy to turn a civilization into a single sentence after enough centuries have passed. "The Mursaat betrayed the Elder Races." "The Mursaat ruled through the White Mantle." Mabon is a useful warning against that habit. A people can have a history without every individual being reducible to it.

V

An invasion becomes a civil war

The Kryptis initially appeared to Tyria as a single invading force, but their society was divided beneath the authority of Eparch, the ruler known as the Midnight King.

Peitha, a member of the Kryptis royal line, opposed Eparch and began gathering support for rebellion. Her goals created an unusual alliance between Kryptis rebels and the very Tyrian forces their people had been invading.

The war therefore changed direction. Rather than simply seal Tyria against another realm, the Commander and the Wizard's Court chose to support an internal struggle over who would rule Nayos.

By the later stages of the campaign, the conflict had crossed fully through the Veil and into Inner Nayos.

Tyrian forces were no longer merely defending their own world from the Mists. They had entered the politics of another one.

VI

Nayos changes rulers

In 1337 AE, Peitha's rebellion broke through the defenses surrounding Eparch's rule. The campaign culminated in the defeat of the Midnight King.

Eparch fell, and Peitha assumed the throne as the new Midnight King of Nayos.

The outcome distinguished the Kryptis war from many of the Commander's earlier victories. Nayos was not destroyed. The Kryptis were not eliminated.

Instead, the immediate threat to Tyria ended through political revolution and a change of leadership within the invading civilization itself.

That distinction would matter in an age increasingly defined by alliances rather than extermination.

VII

Cooperation becomes an institution

The Pact had been created in response to a specific military problem: defeating Zhaitan. The world that emerged after the Dragon Cycle required something broader.

In 1337 AE, representatives of major Tyrian powers gathered to form the Tyrian Alliance.

The idea reflected more than another temporary coalition. Human, Charr, Norn, Asura, Sylvari, Canthans, and other powers now inhabited a political world in which threats routinely crossed borders that no individual nation could control.

The long-term solution could no longer be assembling an emergency alliance whenever another Dragon or God appeared.

Cooperation itself was beginning to become part of Tyrian government.

VIII

The road returns to Janthir

The Alliance's first years quickly drew attention toward the Isles of Janthir, a region whose name had appeared repeatedly throughout much older Tyrian history.

Centuries earlier, Saul D'Alessio and the White Mantle had traveled to Janthir during their service to the Mursaat. The Eye of Janthir became one of the most recognizable symbols of that faith.

The islands also contained ruins tied to the older Mursaat civilization, including evidence connected to conflicts whose origins stretched back far beyond Humanity's arrival in Tyria.

An expedition led the Commander, representatives of the Alliance, and members of the Astral Ward toward the region.

There they encountered the Lowland Kodan, whose settlements and traditions represented another culture largely unknown to the wider political powers of Central Tyria.

IX

An enemy from the Guild Wars era returns

The Janthir expedition soon encountered creatures whose history was already familiar to the oldest surviving military records: Titans.

During the Flameseeker Prophecies in 1072 AE, Titans had emerged through the Door of Komalie after the Mursaat were weakened. Their presence had also been tied to the beings worshiped by parts of ancient Charr society as Gods.

More than two and a half centuries later, Titans had resurfaced in Janthir.

Their return made the historical pattern difficult to ignore. The modern age was increasingly intersecting with unfinished business from the original Guild Wars era.

Commander Q // Observation

We have reached the strange part of keeping a long archive. Eventually something you filed under "ancient enemy" walks back into the room.

X

Janthir connects the present to the Elder Races

Investigation of Janthir gradually exposed material connected not only to the White Mantle, but to the much older history of the Mursaat themselves.

Their conflict with the Seers had begun thousands of years before the Guild Wars and reached back toward the ancient Dragonrise described near the beginning of this archive.

What once appeared to be a mostly linear story now formed a circle. The Mursaat encountered by Humanity during the Flameseeker Prophecies were descendants of a civilization already ancient before Humanity arrived in Tyria.

Mabon had demonstrated that pieces of that civilization survived in unexpected places. Janthir now offered physical evidence of the world from which he and the other Mursaat had come.

The Seers, likewise, were no longer relevant merely because they created the first Bloodstone. Their own ancient conflict was becoming important to the present again.

XI

Livia brings an ancient weapon back into the war

The renewed Titan threat eventually drew another artifact from the Guild Wars era back into active history: the Scepter of Orr.

The Scepter had already passed through the hands of figures connected to Orr, the Undead Lich, the Flameseeker Prophecies, and other conflicts stretching back centuries.

By 1338 AE it was in the possession of Livia, whose own life connected the modern Krytan state to events surrounding the original Guild Wars and the White Mantle.

The artifact's ability to influence powerful beings made it valuable against the resurfaced Titans.

The war was now gathering objects, enemies, and survivors from several different periods of history into the same conflict.

XII

The Titan trail leads into the Realm of Torment

The effort to end the Titan threat eventually carried the Alliance beyond Janthir and into the Realm of Torment.

There stood the Foundry of Failed Creations, the ancient location associated with the creation and command of Titans during the conflicts surrounding Abaddon.

The location connected the present conflict directly to both the Guild Wars era and the much older history of the fallen God.

In 1338 AE, Alliance forces entered the Foundry and brought the renewed Titan crisis to its conclusion.

The immediate threat was defeated and the path linking the Foundry to Tyria was closed.

XIII

One ancient thread disappears into the Mists

The Titan crisis ended, but the Scepter of Orr did not simply return to a museum or vault.

The ancient artifact was lost into the Mists, leaving its ultimate fate unresolved.

That disappearance is an appropriate ending for this period. The age after the Dragons had begun by revealing a hidden world and ended by opening still more questions about artifacts and civilizations whose histories were far from complete.

Janthir had drawn the archive backward toward the Mursaat, the Seers, the Titans, the Realm of Torment, and the original Guild Wars.

The next road would go farther still.

Commander Q // Observation

I once thought the purpose of this archive was to move forward through time. Janthir makes a different argument. Sometimes moving forward means finally learning enough to understand what happened at the beginning.

End of Volume X

The road continues

By 1338 AE, the Titan threat had been contained, the Tyrian Alliance had survived one of its first major crises, and the history of Janthir had reopened questions surrounding some of Tyria's oldest peoples.

Then attention turned toward an island long treated more like legend than geography.

Volume XI // Current Era

In 1338 AE, Director Vloxx and the Inquest sent an expedition toward Castora in search of the ancient Seer artifact known as Ancora.

The journey opens the events of Visions of Eternity and carries the Road Through Time directly into the present age.
