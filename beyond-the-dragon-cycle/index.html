THE WORLD AFTER THE DRAGONS

Volume X // Beyond the Dragon Cycle

Beyond the Dragon Cycle

On the hidden histories revealed after the Elder Dragons, the war in Nayos, the founding of the Tyrian Alliance, and the return of ancient conflicts once thought buried.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

The world did not become smaller

The death of Soo-Won ended one of the oldest patterns in Tyrian history. Aurene inherited the burden of filtering the world's magic, and the cycle of Elder Dragons awakening, consuming magic, devastating civilizations, and returning to sleep finally came to an end.

It would have been reasonable to expect the world to become simpler after that.

It did not.

The disappearance of the Elder Dragons from the center of history revealed how much had existed beyond their shadow. The Wizard's Tower concealed an organization that had defended Tyria for centuries. The Mists held civilizations with wars and rulers of their own. Ancient peoples whose stories appeared finished returned through surviving individuals, artifacts, ruins, and records that had remained outside ordinary history.

This period also changed the way the present relates to the past. Mabon revealed that a Mursaat could stand apart from the history most Tyrians associated with his people. Investigations surrounding the Wizard's Court returned attention to the Seers and other ancient cultures. Janthir exposed the physical remains of the Mursaat world, while the Titans drew modern forces back toward conflicts first encountered more than two centuries earlier.

The Dragon Cycle had ended.

The deeper history of Tyria was only becoming more visible.

— Commander Q

I

Aurene steps away from ordinary affairs

In 1336 AE, Aurene withdrew from ordinary Tyrian affairs to concentrate upon her role in maintaining the world's magical balance.

Her withdrawal did not mean that she had abandoned Tyria. The death of the other Elder Dragons had left Aurene carrying a responsibility unlike anything previously held by a single surviving Dragon. Magic still had to move through the world without collapsing into the instability exposed during the final crisis in Cantha.

The difference was political as much as magical. Aurene had fought beside the Commander through several of the greatest conflicts of the previous years. Tyria had grown accustomed to possessing an Elder Dragon who could answer, intervene, and enter battle alongside mortal allies.

That arrangement could not become the foundation of the new age.

Mortal civilizations would once again have to govern, negotiate, and defend themselves without expecting a greater power to solve every crisis on their behalf.

Commander Q // Observation

There is an echo here of much older history. The Gods once placed distance between themselves and mortal affairs. Aurene's reasons were different, but the result was familiar: the world remained protected by a greater power that could no longer serve as its everyday government.

II

The Wizard's Tower was never merely a tower

Soon after Aurene's withdrawal, events surrounding the mysterious Wizard's Tower drew the Commander into a history that had remained largely invisible to the nations below it.

For generations, the floating structure had been a familiar mystery above the waters near Garenhoff. Its purpose, inhabitants, and movements were the subject of rumor rather than public knowledge.

Behind that mystery stood Isgarren and the Wizard's Court.

Their stronghold at Amnytas and the larger network surrounding the Tower existed beyond the political order familiar to most Tyrians. The Wizards concerned themselves with threats extending through the Mists and with dangers capable of reaching Tyria from beyond the boundaries normally defended by kingdoms, Orders, or the Pact.

Serving alongside the Court was the Astral Ward.

The organization was ancient by modern standards. It had been established in 604 AE, with Taryn serving as its first Warden. For more than seven centuries, its members had fought battles almost entirely absent from public Tyrian history.

Archive Connection // The Astral Ward

The discovery of the Astral Ward in 1336 AE should not be confused with its creation. By the time the Commander encountered it, the organization already possessed centuries of its own history.

III

A war crosses the Veil

The hidden world surrounding the Wizard's Court became impossible to conceal when Kryptis forces began breaching Tyria from the Mists.

The Kryptis were initially encountered as demonic attackers capable of possession and incursion through weaknesses in the barriers between realms. Their assault reached the Wizard's Tower, Amnytas, and other locations where the Astral Ward attempted to contain them.

They were not, however, a mindless horde.

The Kryptis belonged to a society centered upon the realm of Nayos. Their invasion was tied to the rule of Eparch, the Midnight King, and to an older conflict between Eparch and Isgarren.

The discovery changed the nature of the war.

Tyria was not confronting another species of corrupted minion created by an Elder Dragon. It was confronting the forces of another civilization with its own ruler, nobility, factions, ambitions, and internal struggles.

IV

A living Mursaat changes an old history

Among the Wizards defending Tyria was Mabon.

Mabon was a Mursaat.

By the time most Tyrians encountered his people in recorded history, the Mursaat were associated with the White Mantle, the sacrifice of the Chosen, the Flameseeker Prophecies, and their ancient conflict with the Seers.

Mabon did not fit comfortably into that inheritance. He had spent centuries among the Wizard's Court, defending a world that members of his own people had once manipulated from behind the White Mantle.

During the Kryptis invasion, Mabon became the target of possession. He resisted, but the corruption eventually overcame him.

Mabon died in 1336 AE.

His death was a severe loss for the Wizard's Court, but his existence was historically just as important. The Mursaat were no longer represented only by the actions of Lazarus, the White Mantle, or the surviving accounts of their ancient civilization.

One of them had spent centuries choosing another path.

Commander Q // Observation

Civilizations are easier to summarize than people. A history can record what the Mursaat did without pretending every Mursaat made the same choices. Mabon is the clearest surviving reason not to confuse those two things.

V

The Wizard's Court reopens older histories

Mabon was not the only sign that the hidden institutions surrounding Isgarren had crossed paths with peoples whose histories reached far beyond the modern age.

Dagda, a Jotun Wizard, provided another living connection to a people whose earlier civilization had been transformed by the ancient Dragon Cycle and the loss of much of its magical power.

Investigations connected to the Court also returned attention to the Seers. Artifacts associated with them led researchers back toward old sites and surviving traces of a civilization that had once preserved magic from the Elder Dragons.

At the Bloodstone Caves, the spirit of the Seer Sidony provided another surviving connection to that ancient world.

These discoveries did not mean the Elder Races had suddenly returned as unified civilizations. The Seers remained scattered and poorly understood. Jotun civilization had long since changed from its ancient form. The Mursaat encountered in Human history had been devastated.

What changed was access to their history.

The modern world was beginning to encounter evidence that had remained hidden, isolated, or inaccessible while the Elder Dragon crisis dominated attention.

Historical Caution // Survival

The appearance of individual survivors, spirits, or artifacts does not establish the continued existence of the ancient civilizations in their former shape. It does establish that their histories survived in more places than the modern record once recognized.

VI

The invasion becomes a revolution

The Kryptis invasion had presented Nayos as a hostile realm united under Eparch.

It was not.

Peitha, a noble of House Nephus and member of the Kryptis royal line, opposed Eparch's rule. Other Kryptis also suffered beneath the Midnight King's authority and could distinguish between loyalty to their people and loyalty to their ruler.

Peitha's rebellion created an alliance that would have seemed unlikely at the beginning of the invasion: Tyrian defenders fighting beside Kryptis against other Kryptis.

The Commander and allies crossed the Veil into Nayos, becoming participants in a war whose outcome would determine not whether the Kryptis continued to exist, but who would govern them.

The response to the invasion had become intervention in the politics of another realm.

VII

Eparch's rule ends

The campaign in Nayos eventually reached Eparch himself.

Peitha's rebellion, supported by the Commander, Wizard's Court, Astral Ward, and allied Kryptis, broke the Midnight King's hold over the realm.

Eparch was defeated.

The result was not the destruction of Nayos and not the extermination of the Kryptis.

Their political order changed.

Peitha emerged as the ruler of Nayos, while the relationship between her people and Tyria entered a new and uncertain stage.

The resolution differed sharply from many of the wars that defined the previous age. The threat ended not because an entire people was destroyed, but because divisions within that society made another government possible.

Commander Q // Observation

The Elder Dragon wars taught Tyria to cooperate against enemies too powerful to face alone. Nayos demanded another lesson: sometimes the people on the other side of a war are also trying to survive their ruler.

VIII

Temporary cooperation becomes political structure

The alliances created during the Elder Dragon wars had repeatedly proven that threats crossing borders required responses larger than any individual nation.

The Pact had represented one answer, but it was primarily a military organization built to confront the Elder Dragons.

The post-Dragon world required something broader.

In 1337 AE, leaders and representatives from major powers gathered in Lion's Arch for the inaugural meeting of the Tyrian Alliance.

The Alliance created a forum in which nations and peoples could address shared problems before those problems became another world-ending emergency.

Its creation marked a significant change in political history. The cooperation that had once been improvised whenever Dragons, Gods, or other threats appeared was beginning to become an institution in its own right.

It would not take long for that institution to be tested.

IX

The road returns to Janthir

The Alliance's first major crisis drew attention toward the Isles of Janthir.

The name was already part of Tyrian history. Saul D'Alessio and later members of the White Mantle had been associated with Janthir through their relationship with the Mursaat, while the Eye of Janthir had become one of the most recognizable instruments of their religion.

Yet the islands themselves remained poorly understood by the wider modern world.

An expedition involving the Commander, Tyrian Alliance, and Astral Ward entered the region and encountered the Lowland Kodan, whose communities had developed beyond the political networks familiar to Central Tyria.

Janthir was not empty wilderness surrounding abandoned Mursaat ruins. It was a living region with peoples and histories of its own.

Beneath and beyond that living landscape, however, were the remains of another civilization.

X

The Mursaat return as an archaeological civilization

Janthir changed the historical position of the Mursaat once again.

During the Guild Wars era, they had appeared primarily as the hidden power behind the White Mantle. Ancient tablets and later discoveries had pushed their history backward toward the previous Dragonrise and their war against the Seers.

Janthir added something different.

The region preserved ruins and evidence belonging to the Mursaat as a civilization rather than merely as secret patrons of a Human religious order.

Places such as Bava Nisos connected the modern expedition to Mursaat history on its own ground. White Mantle activity in the region belonged to a later layer laid over something far older.

The distinction matters.

The Mursaat did not begin with Saul D'Alessio. Their dealings with Humanity represented only the late surviving chapter of a history stretching thousands of years farther back.

Mabon had shown that individual Mursaat histories survived outside the White Mantle.

Janthir now revealed more of the world those Mursaat had inherited.

Commander Q // Observation

There is a difference between finding another story about the Mursaat and finally walking through places where the Mursaat had a story of their own.

XI

The Titans return to Tyria

The Janthir expedition soon encountered another enemy whose name belonged to an earlier age: the Titans.

Titans had played a central role in the fulfillment of the Flameseeker Prophecies. They emerged through the Door of Komalie after the Mursaat defenses collapsed and spread across Tyria before being hunted down.

Their history reached farther still. Titans had also been tied to the forces presented to the Charr as Gods during the years surrounding the Searing.

More than two and a half centuries after those events, new Titans appeared in Janthir.

Among them were powerful progenitor Titans whose presence was connected to Bava Nisos and a passage through the Mists leading toward the place from which many Titans had historically originated.

What had first appeared to be a local crisis among Mursaat ruins was becoming another conflict with roots in the Realm of Torment.

Historical Caution // Titan Origins

The Foundry of Failed Creations is a major historical source of Titans, where tormented souls can be forged together into new beings. Surviving ancient testimony, however, suggests Titans as a broader category may not be limited entirely to creations originating from that one facility.

XII

The Scepter of Orr returns to active history

The renewed Titan threat required another connection to the original Guild Wars era.

At a later meeting of the Tyrian Alliance, Livia proposed using the Scepter of Orr against the Titans.

The artifact had already accumulated a remarkable history.

It had been connected to Orr, Vizier Khilbron, the Flameseeker Prophecies, the control of Undead beings, and conflicts spanning generations. Livia herself had survived from the original Guild Wars era into the modern age, carrying knowledge that most historians could access only through written records.

By 1338 AE, the Scepter was no longer merely an artifact from an older campaign.

It had become a weapon in a present war.

Commander Q // Observation

By this point Janthir had gathered an improbable collection of histories into the same conflict: Mursaat ruins, White Mantle records, Titans, Livia, the Scepter of Orr, and a passage toward the Realm of Torment. None of them were actually unrelated. We simply encountered their histories centuries apart.

XIII

The war reaches the Foundry

Investigation of the Titans eventually traced the threat through the Mists to the Foundry of Failed Creations.

The Foundry stood within the Domain of Anguish in the Realm of Torment.

It was not a new place.

During the era surrounding Abaddon, the Foundry had already been associated with the creation of Titans from tormented souls. Heroes had entered it during the original Guild Wars and fought the forces operating there.

More than two centuries later, the same location had again become relevant to the security of Tyria.

Alliance forces entered the Foundry in 1338 AE, confronting the source sustaining the renewed Titan crisis.

The campaign succeeded.

The Titans threatening Janthir were defeated and the immediate danger to Tyria was brought to an end.

The victory also completed an extraordinary historical loop. A war that began among ruins associated with the Mursaat had carried modern Tyrian forces through the same realm that once held Abaddon and produced Titans encountered during the Flameseeker Prophecies.

XIV

The past is no longer safely behind Tyria

The Titan crisis ended, but the investigations that surrounded it changed the historical landscape.

The Scepter of Orr again passed beyond ordinary reach into the Mists, leaving another ancient artifact with an unresolved future.

More importantly, Janthir had reopened histories that the modern world had once treated as finished.

The Mursaat were no longer merely the vanished Unseen Ones of Krytan history.

The Seers were no longer important only because an unknown member of their people created the original Bloodstone.

The Titans were no longer enemies contained within the Flameseeker Prophecies.

The Realm of Torment was no longer only the place where Abaddon's story ended.

The modern world had begun walking through the remains of conflicts whose origins stretched back toward the oldest volumes of this archive.

That is perhaps the defining feature of the age beyond the Dragon Cycle.

History stopped behaving like a road where one period simply replaced the one before it.

The oldest surviving paths began meeting the newest ones.

Commander Q // Observation

I once thought the difficulty of this archive would be reaching far enough into the past to understand the present. Janthir suggests the opposite problem: we may need to keep watching the present until the past finally explains itself.

End of Volume X

The road reaches the present

By 1338 AE, the Elder Dragon Cycle had ended, the Kryptis war had changed the political order of Nayos, and the Tyrian Alliance had begun transforming cooperation between nations into something more permanent.

Janthir had also carried the historical record backward. Mursaat civilization, the Seers, Titans, the Scepter of Orr, and the Realm of Torment were no longer subjects belonging only to old books.

Then another ancient name returned to the record.

Castora.

Volume XI // Current Era

In 1338 AE, Director Vloxx and the Inquest sent an expedition toward Castora in pursuit of the ancient Seer artifact known as Ancora.

The journey carries the archive into Visions of Eternity, the rediscovery of Castora, surviving Seer history, and the events unfolding in the present age.
