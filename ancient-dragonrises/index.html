Historical Archive // Record 01

Ancient Dragonrises

c. 20,000 BE – Ancient Past

Long before Kryta had kings, before the first Guild War, and before humanity built its great kingdoms, Tyria already knew catastrophe.

Beneath the world slept beings whose awakening could change continents.

When the Elder Dragons rose, civilizations did not fight for territory. They fought to remain part of history.

Archive Scope

This entry follows one of the earliest known Dragonrises in Tyrian history: the awakening of the Elder Dragons, the devastation they caused, the disappearance of the Giganticus Lupicus, the elder races' failed attempts to resist them, the Seers' creation of the first Bloodstone, and the eventual return of the dragons to slumber.

Dates from this period are approximate. Much of what survives comes from ruins, ancient testimony, magical records, and the memories of civilizations that endured the Dragonrise.

Record 01 // Before Recorded History

Tyria before the familiar world

The oldest reaches of Tyrian history are not a clean written record.

They survive as ruins, magical artifacts, ancient writings, racial memory, and fragments preserved by civilizations that endured long enough to remember.

By roughly 20,000 BE, the Elder Dragons had already begun one of the earliest known Dragonrises.

Enormous creatures known as the Giganticus Lupicus also inhabited this ancient world.

Their fossilized remains would survive for thousands of years, leaving behind evidence of a world far older than the civilizations familiar to modern Tyrians.

Record 02 // Dragonrise

The Elder Dragons awaken

The Elder Dragons emerged as immense concentrations of magical power.

Their rising was not simply the appearance of powerful creatures.

They consumed enormous amounts of magical energy, corrupted living creatures, created armies of minions, and transformed the regions surrounding them.

Entire civilizations were forced to adapt to a world being steadily stripped of both life and magic.

The Dragon Cycle

The ancient Dragonrise was part of a repeating pattern. Elder Dragons awakened, consumed magic, devastated the world around them, and eventually returned to slumber when available magic became scarce.

Record 03 // Resistance

The elder races try to fight back

Several powerful civilizations lived through this Dragonrise, including the Seers, mursaat, Forgotten, dwarves, and jotun.

They did not immediately surrender the world to the dragons.

Ancient alliances formed, and some of these peoples attempted to confront the Elder Dragons directly.

The Forgotten and mursaat, for example, fought against Zhaitan.

Those efforts ultimately failed.

The dragons could not be defeated by ordinary warfare.

Record 04 // c. 10,000 BE

The Giganticus Lupicus disappear

The ancient Giganticus Lupicus survived deep into this forgotten age.

By approximately 10,000 BE, the last known members had disappeared from Tyria.

Their extinction occurred during the broader age shaped by repeated Elder Dragon activity, although the surviving record does not preserve a complete account of exactly how they vanished.

Thousands of years later, their enormous remains could still be found across Tyria.

Historical Caution

The disappearance of the Giganticus Lupicus should not be treated as a single event occurring at the beginning of the Dragonrise. Their final disappearance is placed much later, around 10,000 BE.

Record 05 // The Problem of Magic

How do you hide food from a dragon?

The Elder Dragons fed upon magic.

That created a brutal problem for the civilizations trying to survive them.

Magic was one of their greatest tools, but every reserve left exposed was another source of power the dragons could consume.

Direct resistance had failed.

Survival now required finding a way to remove the world's remaining uncorrupted magic from the dragons' reach.

Record 06 // The Bloodstone

The Seers seal magic away

The Seers created one of the most important artifacts in Tyrian history: the original Bloodstone.

Using the power of the Shadowstone, they gathered the remaining uncorrupted magic and sealed it within a single great repository.

This denied the Elder Dragons access to a vast source of magical energy.

The Bloodstone was not originally the collection of separate stones known to later humanity.

It began as one great magical artifact.

Why This Matters

The Bloodstone begins here. Thousands of years later, the Six Gods would use it again, divide it, and turn this ancient survival mechanism into one of the most important sources of magical conflict in human history.

Record 07 // The Long Silence

The Elder Dragons return to slumber

With much of the world's remaining uncorrupted magic sealed beyond their reach, the Elder Dragons gradually weakened.

Eventually, they returned to sleep.

Tyria survived.

But survival did not mean the world returned to what it had been before.

Species had vanished. Civilizations had been broken. Magic had been hidden away. And the dragons remained beneath the world.

The catastrophe ended, but its machinery was still there.

Record 08 // What Remained

The survivors inherit a changed world

The Dragonrise passed into ancient memory, but not every civilization disappeared with it.

Some survived by hiding. Others sacrificed their magic. Some escaped into places the dragons could not easily reach.

Their choices would echo for thousands of years.

The Bloodstones, Glint, the Mists, Ascension, and conflicts still unfolding in the modern age all carry pieces of this ancient world forward.

To understand those connections, the road must now leave the dragons and follow the people who survived them.
