Author's Preface

Where Tyrian history begins

Every historian eventually reaches a point where the road becomes difficult to follow. Written accounts disappear, dates become estimates, and the surviving evidence is reduced to ruins, fossils, magical artifacts, and the memories of peoples old enough to remember a world that no longer exists.

That is where this history begins. It does not begin with humanity, the kingdoms of Ascalon and Kryta, or even the arrival of the Six Gods. It begins much earlier, with Tyria enduring one of the forces that shaped nearly every age that came after: the awakening of the Elder Dragons.

Note on Dating

The earliest recorded Dragonrise is placed at approximately 20,000 BE. A later and better documented Dragonrise appears around 10,000 BE. Because the evidence from these periods is incomplete, events from the ancient past should not be treated as one continuous awakening.

— Commander Q

I

The earliest Dragonrise

Around 20,000 BE, the surviving historical record places one of the earliest known awakenings of the Elder Dragons. Very little is known about the civilizations that witnessed it, which makes this period one of the most difficult parts of Tyrian history to reconstruct.

What can be established is that the Elder Dragons were already part of Tyria's history long before the nations familiar to modern scholars existed. Their awakenings were tied to enormous concentrations of magic, and their presence could alter both the land and the creatures living upon it.

Eventually, as the available magic of the world diminished, the dragons returned to slumber. The cycle did not end there, however, and later civilizations would be forced to confront the same threat again.

II

The Dragon Cycle

The Elder Dragons did not remain active indefinitely. Their awakenings followed a larger cycle connected to the amount of magic available in the world.

When magic was abundant, the dragons could awaken, consume it, and spread their corruption through both living creatures and the regions surrounding them. Their minions extended that influence even farther, turning an individual dragon into the center of a much larger ecological and magical disaster.

As accessible magic became scarce, the Elder Dragons gradually returned to slumber. Centuries or even millennia could pass before another awakening, giving new civilizations enough time to rise in a world that might no longer remember what had come before.

Commander Q // Observation

Most histories are organized around kingdoms, dynasties, and wars. The Elder Dragons require a larger scale. Civilizations rose and fell within the boundaries of a cycle that was older than any nation that tried to understand it.

III

The previous Dragonrise

By approximately 10,000 BE, another Dragonrise appears in the surviving record. Although the chronology remains incomplete, this period is better understood than the far older awakening that preceded it.

Enormous creatures known as the Giganticus Lupicus still inhabited the ancient world, but by around this period the last known members had disappeared. Their remains endured long after the creatures themselves, leaving behind physical evidence of a world far older than the civilizations known to modern Tyrians.

Other peoples survived the same age. Among them were the Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, dwarves, and Jotun. Their attempts to survive the Dragonrise would shape much of the history that followed.

IV

Resistance among the elder races

The civilizations of this age did not simply wait for the dragons to destroy them. Several attempted to organize resistance, preserve what they could, and find ways to survive a threat that ordinary warfare could not overcome.

The surviving record describes efforts at cooperation among several elder races, although those alliances were neither permanent nor free from conflict. The Forgotten and Mursaat, for example, took part in efforts against Zhaitan, but those efforts ultimately failed to produce a military victory.

The deeper problem was the nature of the dragons themselves. They were feeding on the same force that powered the civilizations attempting to stop them: magic.

V

The first Bloodstone

When direct resistance failed, the Seers pursued a different solution. Rather than defeat the Elder Dragons through force, they sought a way to deny them access to the uncorrupted magic that remained in the world.

Their experiments included the Shadowstone, an artifact connected to the Seers' attempts to preserve magic beyond the reach of the dragons. Those efforts eventually led to the creation of the original Bloodstone, one of the most consequential magical artifacts in Tyrian history.

The Seers gathered much of the world's remaining uncorrupted magic and sealed it within the great repository. By removing that magic from the open world, they denied the Elder Dragons access to a significant source of power.

At this point in history, the Bloodstone was not the collection of separate stones known to later civilizations. It existed as a single great artifact, created for survival rather than conquest.

Research Note // Bloodstone

Surviving accounts connect the Shadowstone to the Seers' work on the Bloodstone, while other records describe additional resources involved in its creation. The exact process is not fully documented.

Commander Q // Observation

The later history of the Bloodstone makes it easy to think of it as a source of conflict, but its origin was very different. It was first created as a survival measure by a civilization trying to keep magic away from the Elder Dragons.

VI

The dragons return to sleep

The sealing of so much uncorrupted magic helped deny the Elder Dragons the energy they sought, but it was only one part of the struggle to survive the Dragonrise. Different civilizations relied on different strategies, including concealment, retreat, sacrifice, and the preservation of knowledge.

As accessible magic became increasingly scarce, the Elder Dragons gradually returned to slumber. Tyria survived another cycle, but the world that emerged from it was different from the one that had entered it.

Entire species had disappeared, ancient civilizations had been weakened or divided, and much of the world's magic had been placed beyond ordinary reach. The dragons were sleeping again, but the choices made during their awakening would continue to shape the ages that followed.

VII

The world left behind

When the Dragonrise ended, the civilizations that remained inherited both a damaged world and the consequences of the choices they had made to survive it.

The Seers still had enemies. The Nursaat carried their own memories of betrayal. The Forgotten preserved knowledge that would become important thousands of years later, while the Dwarves and jJotun followed very different paths through the ages that came after.

The Bloodstone also remained, still holding the magic that had once been hidden from the Elder Dragons. Its purpose would eventually change, and future civilizations would use it in ways its creators could never have intended.

The next part of the record follows the peoples who survived this ancient age and examines what became of them after the dragons returned to sleep.

End of Volume I
