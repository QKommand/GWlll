EARLIEST RECORDED DRAGONRISE

Volume I // The Ancient World

Ancient Dragonrises

On the Elder Dragons, the cycles of magic that shaped ancient Tyria, and the peoples who survived the previous Dragonrise.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

Where the road becomes uncertain

There is a point in every history where written records give way to fragments. Dates become approximations, civilizations are known through ruins rather than chronicles, and events must be reconstructed from the accounts of peoples who survived long enough to remember them.

That is where this record begins.

Ancient Tyria was repeatedly transformed by the awakening of the Elder Dragons. These creatures consumed vast quantities of magic, corrupted living beings and entire landscapes, and forced the peoples of their age to choose between resistance, retreat, concealment, and preservation.

The oldest Dragonrises remain poorly documented. The previous Dragonrise, however, left enough evidence for us to begin following the choices made by the civilizations that endured it.

Note on Dating

The oldest chronological references place an ancient Dragonrise around 20,000 BE. The previous Dragonrise is generally placed more than ten thousand years before the modern age. These periods are separated by enormous spans of time and should not be treated as one continuous awakening.

— Commander Q

I

The earliest Dragonrise

Around 20,000 BE, the surviving chronology places one of the earliest known awakenings of the Elder Dragons. Little remains from the peoples who witnessed it, and the individual events of that age are almost entirely lost.

What survives more clearly is the pattern itself. The Elder Dragons awakened during periods when magic had accumulated across Tyria, consumed that power, and extended their influence through corrupted creatures, champions, and transformed environments.

Eventually the dragons returned to dormancy. Whatever survived the awakening was left to recover while magic gradually accumulated once again.

The cycle would repeat.

Research Note // Ancient Evidence

The earliest Dragonrises are known more securely as part of a repeating pattern than as detailed historical narratives. Their existence is clearer than the exact order of events within them.

II

The Dragon Cycle

The Elder Dragons did not remain awake forever. Their activity followed a larger relationship with the amount of magic available in the world.

When magic accumulated, the dragons could awaken and begin feeding upon it. Their champions and minions extended their influence far beyond the dragons themselves, allowing corruption to spread through regions, creatures, and entire ecosystems.

Different dragons expressed this power in different ways. Some relied upon corrupted armies. Others used specialized champions or magical structures that gathered, carried, amplified, or redirected their influence.

As accessible magic diminished, the Elder Dragons eventually returned to dormancy. During the long ages that followed, magic gathered again and civilizations rebuilt without knowing exactly when the next awakening would begin.

Commander Q // Observation

The Dragon Cycle is difficult to fit inside the ordinary scale of history. Kingdoms can rise, disappear, and become archaeological mysteries during the sleep of a single Elder Dragon.

III

The world before the previous Dragonrise

By the time the Elder Dragons awakened again, Tyria was home to several powerful civilizations and creatures whose surviving remains reveal a complex ancient world.

Among the largest creatures known from this age were the Giganticus Lupicus. Their enormous skeletons remain scattered across Tyria and Elona, although the cause and exact timing of their disappearance remain uncertain.

The Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun also belonged to this period. Each possessed its own traditions, settlements, magical practices, and political interests.

They were not one civilization, nor did they approach magic in the same way. The Dragonrise would force them into cooperation, but it would never erase the differences between them.

IV

An alliance against the dragons

Surviving Mursaat tablets describe an alliance formed among the Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun during the previous Dragonrise.

The alliance brought together peoples with very different ideas about how the crisis should be faced. According to the Mursaat account, the Jotun concealed themselves, the Dwarves withdrew into fortified strongholds, and the Seers turned their efforts toward preserving magic for the world that might remain after the dragons slept.

The Mursaat and Forgotten instead attempted direct resistance.

The account is important, but so is its author. The Mursaat remembered these choices through the anger of a people who believed their allies had abandoned them. Their descriptions preserve both historical information and the grievances that followed.

Research Note // Mursaat Testimony

The Mursaat tablets are among the most detailed surviving accounts of the previous Dragonrise. Their descriptions of events are valuable, while their accusations of betrayal reflect the Mursaat interpretation of those events.

V

The assault on Zhaitan

The Mursaat and Forgotten eventually launched a direct assault against the Elder Dragon Zhaitan.

Mursaat records describe armies that included Jade Constructs and Enchanted Armors. Despite the magical strength assembled against Zhaitan, the attack failed.

Many Mursaat and Forgotten were lost.

The Mursaat never forgot that the Seers, Dwarves, and Jotun had not joined the battle. Their resentment over the failed assault would survive long after the Elder Dragons returned to sleep.

The Forgotten took another path after the disaster, eventually withdrawing toward the lands now associated with the Crystal Desert.

Commander Q // Observation

The alliance did not collapse because its members failed to recognize the danger. It collapsed because they disagreed about what survival required.

VI

Glint and the Forgotten

The Forgotten also achieved something direct warfare had failed to accomplish: they broke an Elder Dragon's control over one of its champions.

During the previous Dragonrise, the Forgotten performed a ritual upon Glint, then a champion of Kralkatorrik, and freed her from the Elder Dragon's corruption and control.

Glint became independent of her former master and later helped shelter the Forgotten from the Elder Dragons until the cycle ended.

Their relationship endured long after the Dragonrise. The Forgotten remained closely connected to Glint, preserving knowledge and traditions that would continue influencing the Crystal Desert for ages.

The event also provides one of the clearest surviving examples from the ancient world that Elder Dragon corruption was not always irreversible.

VII

The Seers preserve magic

The Seers chose a strategy built around preservation rather than direct victory.

A surviving history of the Bloodstones states that during the previous Elder Dragon awakening, the Seers placed magic within a great stone so that the dragons could not consume it.

The plan worked.

The original Bloodstone was therefore not created as a weapon, a source of sacrifice, or a means of regulating mortal spellcraft. Those purposes belonged to much later periods.

Its first known purpose was to keep magic out of the Elder Dragons' reach and preserve that power for the world that would remain after they returned to sleep.

The Seers also possessed other ancient magical works. Among them was the Shadowstone, which predates the Bloodstone and demonstrates that the Seers were already developing sophisticated methods of manipulating magical power.

Research Note // Bloodstone

The surviving evidence establishes that the Shadowstone predates the Bloodstone, but it does not securely document every step between the two artifacts. Their exact technological relationship should therefore remain open.

Commander Q // Observation

Later history remembers the Bloodstone through war, sacrifice, and catastrophe. Its beginning was much simpler: hide the magic, survive the dragons, and leave something for the world that came afterward.

VIII

The Mursaat withdraw

After the failed assault against Zhaitan, the Mursaat abandoned direct resistance and turned instead toward preserving themselves.

Their tablets state that they "slid sideways into another layer of awareness", placing themselves beyond ordinary perception so that the Elder Dragons could not consume their magic.

Their survival therefore depended neither upon defeating the dragons nor surrendering their power. They removed themselves from the world as most creatures experienced it and waited.

This ability to exist outside ordinary perception remained one of the defining traits of Mursaat magic throughout their later history.

IX

A world without its former magic

As the Dragonrise continued, enormous quantities of Tyria's accessible magic disappeared.

The Mursaat tablets describe cities falling into ruin and civilization entering a diminished age as the dragons consumed what remained.

Different peoples endured that collapse in different ways. The Seers had preserved magic. The Mursaat had concealed themselves. The Forgotten remained under Glint's protection. The Dwarves relied upon fortified refuges, while the Jotun survived through methods that are less clearly preserved.

Much of what existed before the Dragonrise did not survive it intact.

Settlements disappeared. Knowledge was lost. Populations were reduced. Even civilizations that endured would emerge changed.

X

The dragons return to sleep

Eventually the Elder Dragons' great consumption of magic could no longer continue.

As accessible magic became scarce, the dragons returned to dormancy.

Tyria had survived another cycle, but not by defeating the Elder Dragons in one decisive war. Survival came through a collection of imperfect solutions: resistance, concealment, retreat, magical preservation, fortified refuges, and the protection of knowledge.

The dragons were sleeping again.

The peoples that remained now had to determine what to do with the world they had inherited.

XI

Magic returns to the world

Once the Elder Dragons were dormant, the Seers began releasing the magic they had preserved.

That magic returned to a world that no longer shared the unity forced upon it by the Dragonrise.

Mursaat accounts describe competing peoples fighting over access to the returning power. The old alliance had disappeared, while resentment between its former members remained.

The Forgotten continued their relationship with Glint. The Mursaat emerged carrying memories of betrayal. The Seers remained custodians of knowledge and magic, while the Dwarves and Jotun followed paths of their own.

The end of the Dragonrise therefore did not restore the ancient world.

It began another one.

Archive Note

What remains uncertain

The previous Dragonrise is one of the earliest periods for which recognizable historical narratives survive, but those narratives remain incomplete.

The Giganticus Lupicus

Their remains establish that they inhabited the ancient world, but no surviving account securely explains why they disappeared.

The Mursaat account

Much of the alliance's history survives through Mursaat tablets. Their account preserves important events, but their descriptions of the Seers, Jotun, and Dwarves also reflect longstanding Mursaat grievances.

The Forgotten chronology

The Forgotten are firmly connected to the previous Dragonrise, yet later traditions place other events in their history at dates that do not always align cleanly with the older record.

Ancient magical works

Ruins throughout Tyria preserve long-lasting magical defenses, guardians, enchanted weapons, seals, and other systems whose creators are not always known. Their survival demonstrates how much ancient magical infrastructure can outlast the societies that built it, but individual sites should not be assigned to a particular civilization without evidence.

These uncertainties are part of the record rather than problems to be hidden. Ancient history survives through fragments, and some questions remain open because the people who could have answered them are gone.

End of Volume I

The survivors inherit Tyria

The Elder Dragons returned to sleep, but the choices made during the Dragonrise survived them.

The Seers had preserved magic. The Mursaat had withdrawn beyond ordinary perception. The Forgotten had freed Glint. The Dwarves had endured in their strongholds, while the Jotun carried forward what remained of a once sophisticated magical culture.

Their alliance was over.

What followed was not another shared history, but five increasingly different ones.
