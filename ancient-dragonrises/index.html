Volume I // The Ancient World

Ancient Dragonrises

On the Elder Dragons, the ancient world, and the civilizations that survived long enough to shape the ages that followed.

Author's Preface

Where Tyrian history begins

Every historian eventually reaches a point where the road becomes difficult to follow. Written accounts disappear, dates become estimates, and what remains must be reconstructed from ruins, fossils, magical artifacts, surviving testimony, and records written thousands of years after the events they describe.

That is where this history begins. It does not begin with humanity, the kingdoms of Ascalon and Kryta, or even the arrival of the Human Gods. Tyria was already ancient by then. Civilizations had risen, fought, built, and disappeared long before humanity entered the surviving record.

Among the greatest forces shaping that forgotten world were the Elder Dragons. Their awakenings consumed enormous amounts of magic and devastated civilizations across Tyria. Those who survived the last ancient Dragonrise did so through very different choices, and many of those decisions would continue shaping the world thousands of years later.

Note on Dating

The oldest surviving chronological references place an ancient Dragonrise around 20,000 BE, while the better documented previous Dragonrise occurred roughly 10,000 years before the modern era. The surviving evidence does not support treating every ancient reference to the Elder Dragons as part of one continuous awakening.

— Commander Q

I

The earliest Dragonrise

Around 20,000 BE, the surviving historical record places one of the earliest known awakenings of the Elder Dragons. Little is known about the peoples who witnessed this age, and even less survives concerning the individual events of the Dragonrise itself.

What can be established is that the Elder Dragons were already part of Tyria's history long before the nations familiar to modern scholars existed. Their awakenings were connected to the accumulation and consumption of magic, and their influence could transform both living creatures and the lands around them.

Eventually the dragons returned to slumber. Whatever civilizations survived were left to rebuild in a world whose earliest history is now largely lost.

Research Note // Ancient Evidence

The earliest portions of Tyrian history are reconstructed from later accounts, archaeological remains, ancient magical records, and the testimony of long-lived peoples. The evidence establishes the antiquity of the Dragon Cycle more clearly than it establishes the exact sequence of events within each awakening.

II

The Dragon Cycle

The Elder Dragons did not remain active indefinitely. Their awakenings formed part of a larger cycle tied to the amount of magic available in Tyria.

When magic accumulated in sufficient quantities, the dragons awakened and began consuming it. Their influence extended far beyond their own bodies through champions, corrupted creatures, and other specialized minions. Entire regions could be transformed as a dragon's corruption spread through the surrounding environment.

As the world's accessible magic diminished, the dragons eventually returned to dormancy. Thousands of years could pass before another awakening, giving civilizations time to rise in a world that might no longer remember the previous one.

The cycle was therefore larger than any single kingdom or people. Nations could rise and disappear entirely between one awakening and the next.

Commander Q // Observation

Most histories are measured through kings, dynasties, and wars. The Elder Dragons require another scale entirely. Civilizations existed inside a cycle older than any nation that eventually tried to understand it.

III

The world before the previous Dragonrise

By the time of the previous Dragonrise, Tyria was already home to powerful civilizations and ancient peoples whose histories began long before humanity.

Among the creatures inhabiting this world were the enormous Giganticus Lupicus, whose skeletal remains can still be found across Tyria and Elona. Their disappearance belongs to this remote period, although the surviving evidence does not clearly establish what caused their extinction.

Other peoples endured. The Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun all existed before humanity became part of Tyria's recorded history. These civilizations possessed magic, settlements, artifacts, and traditions of their own.

Magic itself therefore predates the arrival of the Human Gods and their later involvement with mortal civilization.

When the Elder Dragons awakened again, these peoples faced the same problem every later civilization would eventually confront: how to survive enemies that fed upon the very magic powering the world around them.

IV

The elder races unite

Surviving Mursaat records describe an alliance formed among several of the ancient races during the previous Dragonrise.

The alliance included the Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun, but the races did not remain united in how they believed the dragons should be confronted.

The Mursaat account states that the Mursaat and Forgotten favored direct resistance, while the Seers focused on preserving magic for the world that might exist after the dragons returned to sleep. The same records accuse the Dwarves and Jotun of withdrawing rather than joining the coming assault.

These tablets preserve the Mursaat perspective, including their resentment toward the other elder races. Their accusation of betrayal should therefore be read as part of that surviving account rather than as an impartial judgment of the alliance.

What is clear is that cooperation failed. The elder races had identified a common enemy, but they could not agree on how Tyria should survive it.

V

The assault on Zhaitan

The Mursaat and Forgotten eventually attempted direct war against Zhaitan.

According to surviving Mursaat tablets, the two peoples assembled armies that included Jade Constructs and Enchanted Armors and launched an assault against the Elder Dragon.

The attack failed. Large numbers of Mursaat and Forgotten were lost, and the surviving Mursaat blamed the other elder races for refusing to join them.

The failed campaign became one of the foundations of their later hostility toward the Seers, Dwarves, and Jotun. The Forgotten followed a different path afterward, while the Mursaat increasingly turned toward preserving themselves.

The dragons had proven that even the magical armies of the ancient world could not easily destroy them.

VI

Glint and the Forgotten

Direct war was not the only attempt to resist the Elder Dragons. During the same ancient cycle, the Forgotten developed magic capable of breaking Glint free from Kralkatorrik's corruption and control.

Glint had served as Kralkatorrik's champion, but the Forgotten ritual restored her independent will. In gratitude, she later sheltered the Forgotten while the Elder Dragons remained active.

This stands as one of the clearest surviving examples from the ancient world that an Elder Dragon's corruption could be broken.

The Forgotten would remain connected to Glint long after the dragons returned to sleep, and that relationship would eventually become central to the history of the Crystal Desert.

VII

The Seers preserve magic

The Seers pursued another solution. Rather than attempt to defeat every Elder Dragon through force, they sought to preserve magic itself beyond the dragons' reach.

Their research included the Shadowstone, an artifact created before the Bloodstone and connected to the Seers' developing methods of containing and manipulating magical power.

Those efforts eventually led to the creation of the original Bloodstone.

The Bloodstone was not originally several separate stones, nor was it created by the Human Gods. It began as a single Seer artifact made during an age when the Elder Dragons were consuming the magic of the world.

The Seers gathered and stored enormous amounts of uncorrupted magic within it, keeping that power beyond the dragons' reach until the cycle ended.

It was one of the most consequential acts of magical preservation in Tyrian history.

Research Note // Shadowstone and Bloodstone

The Shadowstone predates the Bloodstone and represents an earlier stage of Seer magical research. Surviving records establish that sequence more clearly than they establish every material, ritual, or process used in the Bloodstone's creation. Later civilizations would radically change its purpose, but its first known role was preservation.

Commander Q // Observation

The Bloodstone is remembered most often for the wars, sacrifices, and magical disasters connected to it in later ages. Its beginning was almost the opposite. It was created because the Seers were trying to leave something behind for a world they were not certain would survive.

VIII

The Mursaat disappear from the world

After the failure against Zhaitan, the Mursaat adopted a different strategy for surviving the remaining Dragonrise.

Their own records state that they "slid sideways into another layer of awareness", removing themselves from ordinary perception so that the Elder Dragons could not consume their magic.

The Mursaat therefore survived not by defeating the dragons, but by placing themselves outside the world as most creatures perceived it.

This ability would remain central to their identity thousands of years later, when humans encountered them as the beings called the Unseen Ones.

Much later, magical artifacts such as the Orrian Veil of Ilya would demonstrate that this state of existence could be perceived or exposed under the right conditions.

For now, however, the Mursaat withdrew and waited.

IX

The world without magic

The Elder Dragons continued consuming Tyria's accessible magic.

Mursaat records describe prosperous cities falling into ruin while civilizations weakened or disappeared. As magic became scarce, society across much of the world entered a diminished age.

The exact experience of every surviving people is not known. The Dwarves relied heavily upon their strongholds. The Jotun survived but would never fully recover the civilization or magical knowledge of their distant ancestors. The Seers had hidden enormous reserves of magic. The Mursaat had removed themselves from ordinary reality.

Each civilization endured the same catastrophe differently.

When the dragons finally returned to sleep, the world they left behind was no longer the world that had faced them.

X

The dragons return to sleep

Eventually, the available magic of Tyria became too scarce to sustain the Elder Dragons' continued activity.

One by one, they returned to dormancy.

Tyria survived another Dragonrise, but survival had carried a cost. Ancient populations had been devastated, cities had disappeared, alliances had collapsed, and much of the world's remaining magical power had been deliberately hidden away.

The Elder Dragons were sleeping again.

Their absence did not restore what had been lost. The surviving civilizations now had to decide what came next.

XI

Magic returns to Tyria

With the dragons dormant, the Seers began releasing the magic they had preserved.

The world receiving that magic was fragmented and very different from the one that had existed before the Dragonrise.

Mursaat accounts describe societies divided into competing peoples and tribes, with conflict erupting over control of the returning magical power.

The surviving elder races also carried old disagreements into the new age. The Mursaat had not forgotten what they considered the abandonment of their assault against Zhaitan. The Seers had followed a strategy the Mursaat despised. The Forgotten increasingly pursued their relationship with Glint, while the Dwarves and Jotun followed their own paths.

The common enemy was sleeping.

The alliance created to survive it was finished.

XII

What survived the ancient world

The previous Dragonrise left behind more than ruins. Many of the choices made during this age survived the civilizations that first made them.

The Bloodstone remained as a vast reservoir of Seer-preserved magic, while the Shadowstone survived as evidence of the magical research that preceded it.

The Mursaat retained their ability to exist beyond ordinary perception and continued using powerful constructs and magical weapons.

The Forgotten preserved knowledge capable of breaking Elder Dragon corruption and maintained their relationship with Glint.

Dwarven fortifications and magical safeguards survived long beyond the people who first constructed them, while the Jotun endured as descendants of a civilization whose ancient magical achievements greatly exceeded those known to later generations.

These remnants became part of the foundation upon which later peoples would build their own histories.

Humanity had not yet entered this story. The gods worshiped by later human civilizations had not yet become part of Tyria's recorded affairs.

The world was already old.

Archive Note

Where the record remains uncertain

Ancient history rarely leaves one clean account. Some events are supported by physical remains and surviving records, while others reach us through the histories of peoples who had their own loyalties, enemies, and interpretations of what happened.

Giganticus Lupicus

Their existence in the ancient world is established through widespread remains, but the surviving evidence does not clearly explain the circumstances of their disappearance.

The Mursaat account

Much of what is known about the failed alliance against the Elder Dragons survives through Mursaat tablets. Their descriptions of the Seers, Dwarves, and Jotun preserve Mursaat grievances and should not automatically be treated as a neutral account.

The Forgotten chronology

Ancient Mursaat records place the Forgotten beside them during the previous Dragonrise and describe their later movement toward the Crystal Desert. Other surviving chronologies place the departure of the beings called the Serpents from human lands much later. The surviving records do not align cleanly.

Creation traditions

Accounts concerning the creation of Tyria, the earliest origins of the Elder Dragons, or events beyond the oldest surviving historical evidence should be treated as ancient traditions unless stronger evidence independently confirms them.

The absence of certainty is not a reason to discard these accounts. It is simply part of studying a period whose witnesses lived thousands of years before modern history began.

End of Volume I

The world after the dragons

The Dragonrise ended, but its consequences did not. The Seers had preserved the world's magic. The Mursaat had survived by abandoning ordinary reality. The Forgotten had freed Glint. The Dwarves had endured in their strongholds, and the Jotun survived the collapse of a civilization whose former achievements would increasingly become legend.

With the Elder Dragons sleeping, these peoples entered a new age. Their old alliance was gone, their disagreements remained, and control of magic would soon create new conflicts among the survivors.
