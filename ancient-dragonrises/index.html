Volume I // The Ancient World

Ancient Dragonrises

On the Elder Dragons, the ancient cycles of magic, and the civilizations that endured their awakening.

Compiled and Annotated By

Author's Preface

Before kingdoms kept records

The earliest history of Tyria survives in fragments: ruins, magical artifacts, scattered accounts, and the memories of civilizations old enough to have witnessed events beyond ordinary historical reach.

Among those events were the Dragonrises, recurring awakenings of the Elder Dragons during periods when magic had accumulated across the world.

The oldest known awakening is only faintly recorded. The previous Dragonrise is different. Enough evidence survived to reveal the peoples who faced it and the choices they made.

Note on Dating

An ancient Dragonrise is placed around 20,000 BE. The better-documented previous Dragonrise occurred thousands of years later. They were separate awakenings and should not be treated as one event.

— Commander Q

I

The earliest known Dragonrise

Around 20,000 BE, surviving chronology places one of the earliest known awakenings of the Elder Dragons.

Little is known about the peoples who experienced it. What survives is the larger pattern: the dragons awakened when magic was abundant, consumed enormous quantities of that power, spread their influence through champions and corrupted creatures, and later returned to dormancy.

During the ages that followed, magic accumulated again.

II

The civilizations of the ancient world

By the time the Elder Dragons awakened again, Tyria supported several powerful civilizations.

The Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun possessed their own settlements, magical traditions, and political interests.

The enormous Giganticus Lupicus also belonged to the ancient world. Their skeletons survive across Tyria and Elona, although the cause and timing of their disappearance remain uncertain.

The coming Dragonrise forced civilizations that were otherwise independent of one another into a temporary alliance.

III

An alliance against the Elder Dragons

Mursaat tablets describe an alliance between the Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, Dwarves, and Jotun during the previous Dragonrise.

Their responses to the crisis quickly diverged. The Jotun concealed themselves, the Dwarves withdrew into fortified strongholds, and the Seers focused on preserving magic.

The Mursaat and Forgotten chose direct resistance.

The surviving account is valuable, but it was written from the Mursaat perspective. Their description of the alliance carries the resentment of a people who later believed their allies had abandoned them.

Research Note // Mursaat Testimony

Mursaat tablets preserve some of the clearest surviving testimony from the previous Dragonrise, but their accusations of betrayal reflect Mursaat interpretation as well as historical memory.

IV

The assault on Zhaitan

The Mursaat and Forgotten eventually attempted a direct assault against Zhaitan.

Their forces included magical constructs such as Jade Constructs and Enchanted Armors, but the attack failed and both peoples suffered heavy losses.

The defeat ended the alliance as a meaningful united front. The Mursaat carried their anger toward the Seers, Dwarves, and Jotun long after the battle.

Commander Q // Observation

Their alliance did not fail because its members misunderstood the danger. They disagreed over what survival required.

V

The Forgotten free Glint

The Forgotten achieved something direct warfare had not: they freed an Elder Dragon champion from its master's control.

They performed a ritual upon Glint, then a champion of Kralkatorrik, breaking the corruption that bound her to the Elder Dragon.

Glint later sheltered the Forgotten during the Dragonrise, and their relationship continued long afterward in the lands that would become the Crystal Desert.

Her liberation remains one of the clearest ancient examples that Elder Dragon corruption could be undone.

VI

The Seers preserve magic

The Seers pursued survival through preservation rather than direct victory.

During the Dragonrise, they placed magic within a great stone so that the Elder Dragons could not consume it. This became the original Bloodstone.

Its earliest known purpose had nothing to do with the sacrifices, magical schools, or conflicts associated with it in later ages. It was created to preserve magic beyond the reach of the dragons.

The Seers also possessed older magical works, including the Shadowstone, which predates the Bloodstone and demonstrates that sophisticated Seer magical engineering existed before its creation.

Research Note // Bloodstone

The Shadowstone predates the Bloodstone, but the surviving record does not explain the complete technological relationship between the two.

VII

The Mursaat leave ordinary Tyria behind

After the failed assault against Zhaitan, the Mursaat abandoned direct resistance and chose concealment.

Their tablets describe them as having "slid sideways into another layer of awareness", placing themselves beyond ordinary perception and away from the Elder Dragons' reach.

This ability would remain one of the defining features of Mursaat magic in later history.

VIII

The Dragonrise ends

As the Elder Dragons consumed Tyria's accessible magic, the world around them diminished.

Cities fell, populations were reduced, and knowledge disappeared. The civilizations that survived did so through different means: preservation, concealment, fortified refuge, retreat, and Glint's protection.

Eventually the Elder Dragons returned to dormancy as accessible magic became scarce.

Tyria survived, but the ancient world that had entered the Dragonrise did not emerge unchanged.

IX

The survivors inherit a changed world

After the dragons returned to sleep, magic gradually began flowing back into Tyria.

The magic sealed inside the Seers' Bloodstone remained preserved. The Forgotten continued beside Glint. The Mursaat returned carrying old grievances, while the Dwarves and Jotun followed histories of their own.

Their common enemy had disappeared into slumber. The alliance that had briefly united them disappeared with it.

From this point forward, their histories would separate.

Archive Note

What remains uncertain

The Giganticus Lupicus

Their remains establish their presence in the ancient world, but no surviving account securely explains their disappearance.

The Forgotten chronology

The Forgotten are firmly connected to the previous Dragonrise, but parts of their later history do not align cleanly enough to reconstruct every event with certainty.

Ancient magical works

Tyria contains ancient magical structures, defenses, guardians, seals, and artifacts whose creators remain unidentified. They should not be assigned to a known civilization without supporting evidence.

End of Volume I

The alliance is over

The Elder Dragons slept again, leaving the surviving civilizations to rebuild in a world changed by the choices they had made during the crisis.
