Era 01 // The Oldest Echoes

Ancient Dragonrises

Tyria's oldest history does not begin with humanity, the Six Gods, or even the kingdoms whose ruins still cover the world. It begins in an age when Elder Dragons moved across the land in search of magic, and civilizations survived only by learning how to disappear.

Before 10,000 BE Ancient Tyria Record Status: Fragmentary

01 // Before Recorded History

A world already ruled by dragons

Long before the oldest surviving calendars, the Elder Dragons existed in Tyria. Their awakenings formed a repeating cycle: they rose when the world's ambient magic had grown abundant, consumed enormous quantities of that magic, and eventually returned to sleep when there was too little left to sustain them.

The earliest Dragonrise currently placed on the historical timeline is approximately 20,000 BE, although surviving records from such an ancient age are extremely limited.

02 // The Last Ancient Dragonrise

The world begins to disappear

Around 10,000 BE, another great Dragonrise began. Elder Dragons and their minions devastated civilizations while searching for magical energy.

The enormous creatures remembered as the Giganticus Lupicus, or Great Giants, disappeared from Tyria during this period. Their bones remain scattered across the world, but the civilization or societies they may have possessed are largely lost.

03 // An Alliance Against Extinction

Five peoples tried to resist

The elder races eventually formed an alliance against the Elder Dragons. Their efforts included attempts to fight the dragons directly and attempts to deny them the magic they needed to survive.

One known campaign involved the mursaat and Forgotten attempting to destroy Zhaitan. The effort failed and caused enormous losses.

The surviving evidence suggests this failure contributed to the distrust that later fractured the alliance.

04 // The Seers' Answer

Hide the magic

The Seers approached the problem differently. If the Elder Dragons could not find enough magical energy to consume, eventually they would be forced back into hibernation.

The Seers first created the Shadowstone and later created the original Bloodstone. The elder races could place their magic within the Bloodstone, concealing that energy from the dragons.

Most of the alliance accepted the plan.

The mursaat did not.

05 // The Betrayal

The mursaat vanished

Rather than surrender their magic, the mursaat abandoned the alliance and phased themselves into the Mists.

They survived the Dragonrise while the other elder races endured the destruction left behind.

Exactly how the betrayal unfolded remains uncertain. Later records suggest that the mursaat believed they had themselves been betrayed during the failed campaign against Zhaitan.

Whatever the original cause, the alliance was finished.

06 // Seer and Mursaat

Survival became another war

The conflict between the mursaat and the Seers continued for centuries.

The mursaat wielded devastating abilities such as Spectral Agony. By the end of the conflict, the Seers had been brought close to extinction.

Their ruined civilizations and scattered artifacts would eventually outlive both cultures as major powers.

07 // Glint

A dragon champion chooses another path

Glint was originally a champion of the Elder Dragon Kralkatorrik.

In approximately 1680 BE, the Forgotten performed a powerful ritual that freed her from Kralkatorrik's control. The ritual cost Glint much of her previous memory but gave her independent thought and free will.

Glint then helped conceal surviving peoples from the Elder Dragons as the world waited for the dragons to exhaust the available magic.

08 // The Dragons Sleep

The world emerges again

Eventually the supply of accessible magic became too low to sustain the Elder Dragons.

One after another, they entered hibernation.

But the world that emerged from the Dragonrise was not the world that had entered it. Civilizations were gone. Knowledge had been lost. The jotun had fallen far from the culture they once possessed, and the surviving elder races were shadows of their former strength.

Into that emptied world, younger peoples such as the charr, kodan, and tengu began to rise.

Thousands of years later, most inhabitants of Tyria would remember this age only through bones, ruins, inscriptions, and legends.
