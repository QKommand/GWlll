Ancient Dragonrises

On the Elder Dragons, the loss of the ancient world, and the civilizations that refused to disappear with it.

Author's Preface

Where does Tyrian history begin?

Every historian eventually reaches a point where the road disappears.

Names become uncertain. Dates become estimates. Written records give way to ruins, fossils, magical evidence, and the memories of peoples old enough to remember a world that no longer exists.

That is where this history begins.

Not with humanity. Not with Ascalon. Not even with the Six Gods.

It begins with Tyria itself enduring something that would happen again and again: the awakening of the Elder Dragons.

Note on Dating

The earliest recorded Dragonrise is placed at approximately 20,000 BE. The better documented previous Dragonrise appears much later, beginning around 10,000 BE. Events from these ancient periods should not be treated as belonging to one continuous awakening.

— Commander Q

I

The first shadow in the record

Around 20,000 BE, the surviving historical record places one of the earliest known awakenings of the Elder Dragons.

We know remarkably little about the civilizations that witnessed it. That absence is itself evidence of the scale of the age.

What can be established is that the Elder Dragons were already part of Tyria's natural history long before the nations recognizable to modern scholars existed.

They awakened, consumed immense quantities of magic, altered the lands around them, and eventually returned to slumber.

This was not the last time Tyria would endure them.

II

A world built around a cycle

Elder Dragons did not remain awake indefinitely.

Their activity followed a terrible rhythm tied to the world's magic.

When magic was abundant, the dragons could awaken and consume it. Their corruption spread through creatures and landscapes, while their minions extended their influence far beyond the dragons themselves.

As available magic diminished, the dragons eventually returned to slumber.

Ages could pass. Civilizations could rise. The world could begin to believe the danger belonged to history.

Then the cycle could begin again.

Commander Q // Observation

We tend to measure history by kingdoms and wars. The Elder Dragons force us to use a larger scale. Nations were chapters. The Dragon Cycle was the binding holding those chapters together.

III

The world before our world

By approximately 10,000 BE, another Dragonrise appears in the surviving record.

This age is clearer than the one that came before it, although its chronology remains incomplete.

Enormous creatures known as the Giganticus Lupicus inhabited ancient Tyria. By around this period, the last known members disappeared.

Their remains endured long after the creatures themselves, leaving fossils scattered across a world that would eventually forget almost everything else about them.

But other peoples survived.

Among them were civilizations whose names still appear throughout modern Tyrian history: the Seers, mursaat, Forgotten, dwarves, and jotun.

Their survival is where the ancient record becomes considerably more important to everything that follows.

IV

Those who refused to disappear

The peoples of this age did not simply wait for the dragons to consume them.

Efforts were made to resist the Elder Dragons and preserve the civilizations threatened by them.

The surviving evidence describes attempts at cooperation among several elder races, although those alliances were neither simple nor permanent.

The Forgotten and mursaat, for example, attempted to confront Zhaitan.

Military resistance alone was not enough.

The problem was larger than defeating armies. The dragons were feeding upon the same force that empowered the civilizations trying to stop them: magic.

V

Hide the magic

If the dragons could not be defeated through force, another answer was required.

The Seers pursued one.

Their experiments included the Shadowstone, an artifact connected to their efforts to preserve magic beyond the dragons' reach.

Those efforts eventually culminated in one of the most consequential creations in Tyrian history: the original Bloodstone.

The Seers gathered much of the world's remaining uncorrupted magic and sealed it within the great artifact, denying that power to the Elder Dragons.

This Bloodstone was not yet the collection of separate stones known to later civilizations.

At this point in history, it was one repository.

Research Note // Bloodstone

Surviving accounts connect the Shadowstone to the Seers' development of the Bloodstone, while other records describe additional resources involved in its creation. The exact process remains incompletely documented.

Commander Q // Observation

This is the first reason I ask readers to remember the Bloodstone. At its beginning, it was not created as a weapon for human wars. It was created because an ancient civilization was trying to keep magic out of a dragon's mouth.

VI

The long silence

Hiding so much uncorrupted magic helped deny the Elder Dragons the energy they sought.

It was not the only act that allowed civilizations to survive.

Different peoples found different means of endurance. Some hid. Some fled. Some sacrificed what they had. And some received protection from powers whose importance would only become clear later.

As accessible magic dwindled, the Elder Dragons gradually returned to slumber.

Tyria survived another cycle.

It did not survive unchanged.

VII

What the survivors inherited

When the dragons slept, the peoples who remained inherited a damaged world and the consequences of everything they had done to preserve it.

The Seers still had enemies. The mursaat had not forgotten old betrayals. The Forgotten carried knowledge that would shape events thousands of years later. The dwarves and jotun followed paths of their own.

And somewhere within that recovering world remained the Bloodstone, holding magic whose purpose would eventually be changed by hands that had not yet entered this history.

That is where this record ends.

The dragons are asleep.

Now we can finally follow those who survived them.

End of Volume I

The road continues.

The Dragonrise explains why the ancient world broke.

It does not explain what became of the civilizations that lived through it.

That question belongs to the next record.
