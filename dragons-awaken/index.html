THE OLD WORLD BEGINS TO CHANGE

Volume VIII // The New Dragonrise

The Dragons Awaken

On the return of the Elder Dragon Cycle and the centuries of migration, war, rebuilding, and political change that created the Tyria of 1325 AE.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights

Author's Preface

The Dragon Cycle becomes history again

The previous record ended with the discovery that the Destroyers were servants of Primordus. The Great Destroyer had been defeated, delaying the Elder Dragon's full awakening, but the warning could no longer be ignored.

The Dragon Cycle described in the earliest histories of Tyria was beginning again.

Over the next two and a half centuries, the consequences spread far beyond the Dragons themselves. Underground civilizations were driven toward the surface. Northern peoples abandoned ancestral homelands. Coastlines were destroyed. Old kingdoms disappeared, new cities rose, and populations that had once developed at great distances from one another were pushed into increasingly close contact.

The transformation was not caused by the Elder Dragons alone. Charr revolution, Palawa Joko's conquest of Elona, Canthan centralization, Human–Charr warfare, technological change, and the emergence of the Sylvari all belonged to the same period.

By 1325 AE, Central Tyria looked very different from the world left behind by the heroes of the original Guild Wars.

Much of that new world had been built by people who were forced to leave somewhere else.

— Commander Q

I

Human Ascalon reaches its end

In 1080 AE, the Ebon Vanguard withdrew south and fortified Ebonhawke, establishing one of the last major Human strongholds in Ascalon.

The Human kingdom continued resisting Charr conquest for another decade, but by 1090 AE Ascalon City itself was about to fall.

King Adelbern refused surrender. During the final Charr assault, he unleashed the Foefire.

The spell killed Adelbern and many of the Humans still defending Ascalon City and the surrounding lands. Their spirits remained bound to the kingdom as spectral soldiers who continued fighting the Charr long after their own deaths.

Human rule over most of Ascalon ended, but the conflict did not.

The Charr inherited a land filled with the ghosts of the people they had defeated, while Ebonhawke remained under Human control beyond the main centers of Charr power.

Ascalon became a place where victory and war existed at the same time.

II

New institutions rise from the old world

The early twelfth century saw the foundations of institutions that would later become central to the political and intellectual life of Tyria.

The Durmand Priory emerged in the Shiverpeaks as a scholarly order dedicated to preserving knowledge, recovering lost histories, and studying the ruins and magical traditions left behind by earlier civilizations.

Its purpose reflected the world around it. Centuries of war, magical disaster, and migration had left Tyria covered in abandoned cities, sealed ruins, forgotten artifacts, and records belonging to societies that no longer existed in their former shape.

The Charr were building something new as well. The Black Citadel rose over the ruins of Rin, former capital of Human Ascalon, becoming the principal center of the High Legions.

At the same time, resistance to the Flame Legion and its Shaman caste continued growing. The struggle eventually associated with Kalla Scorchrazor broke much of the old religious hierarchy's control over Charr society and strengthened the military structure of the High Legions.

Commander Q // Observation

The Black Citadel was not built beside the ruins of Human Ascalon. It was built over them. Few monuments explain a change of power so clearly.

III

Primordus awakens

In 1120 AE, Primordus fully awakened beneath Tyria.

The Destroyers that had appeared during the crisis of 1078 AE were no longer the scattered forces of an Elder Dragon beginning to stir. They now served an active power spreading through the subterranean world.

The consequences were greatest for peoples already living beneath the surface.

Asuran settlements were overrun as Destroyers moved through the underground network. Much of the Asuran population was forced upward, abandoning cities and laboratories that had supported their civilization for generations.

Skritt and other underground peoples were displaced as well.

The transformed Dwarves continued their war against Primordus and the Destroyers in the Depths, gradually disappearing from ordinary surface history as their struggle moved farther below.

The first Elder Dragon of the modern cycle was awake.

Timeline Note // Primordus

Primordus began stirring by 1078 AE, when the Great Destroyer led an invasion through the Depths. Its defeat delayed the Elder Dragon's full awakening until 1120 AE.

IV

Cantha closes while Joko conquers Elona

The political world beyond Central Tyria was also changing during the early Dragonrise.

In Cantha, Imperial authority expanded under Emperor Usoku. The Empire moved against the independent power of the Luxons and Kurzicks, bringing their territories under tighter Imperial control.

Foreign contact diminished as Cantha increasingly turned inward. The process had begun before the Elder Dragon awakenings and continued through the generations that followed.

Elona faced a different transformation.

Palawa Joko, released during the events surrounding Nightfall, rebuilt his power and resumed his campaigns against the living.

Joko altered the flow of the River Elon, using control of the region's water supply alongside his Awakened armies to weaken opposition.

By 1175 AE, Elona had fallen under his rule. The Sunspears were hunted, destroyed, forced into hiding, or incorporated into the new order as Joko eliminated institutions capable of challenging him.

Two of the great Human civilizations that had once participated in the wider international world were now becoming increasingly difficult for Central Tyria to reach.

V

Jormag drives the Norn south

In 1165 AE, Jormag awakened in the Far Shiverpeaks.

The Elder Dragon's influence spread through ice, corrupted creatures, and the whispers that had long been associated with powers in the far north. Icebrood forces advanced through lands inhabited by the Norn.

The Norn fought, but their society had never been built around defending a centralized kingdom. Individual homesteads, hunting grounds, and settlements could not hold back an Elder Dragon indefinitely.

The migration south became one of the defining events of Norn history.

Several Spirits of the Wild fought against Jormag's advance or disappeared during the retreat. The surviving Norn moved into the southern Shiverpeaks and established new homes around the places where the Spirits had guided them.

By 1169 AE, Hoelbrak had become the principal gathering place of the Norn.

The pattern that began below the surface now repeated in the north.

An Elder Dragon awakened, and an entire people moved.

VI

Zhaitan raises Orr

In 1219 AE, Zhaitan awakened beneath the sunken ruins of Orr.

The kingdom that had disappeared during the Cataclysm rose from the sea.

Orr did not return as the civilization that had once surrounded Arah. Zhaitan corrupted the dead throughout the peninsula, transforming them into Risen bound to the Elder Dragon's will.

Ancient Orrian soldiers, priests, citizens, sailors, and creatures became part of a new army. Ships that had once belonged to a Human maritime kingdom were repurposed by the dead, while magical artifacts and ruined structures became part of a landscape now controlled by Zhaitan.

The physical rising of Orr displaced enormous quantities of seawater. Waves and flooding devastated coastlines throughout the Sea of Sorrows and destroyed old Lion's Arch.

Maritime routes also became increasingly dangerous as Risen ships and creatures spread through nearby waters.

One of the great centers of Human history had returned.

It had returned as an Elder Dragon's kingdom.

Commander Q // Observation

Orr had already been destroyed once by Human magic. Zhaitan's awakening turned what remained of that catastrophe into an army.

VII

New cities rise after the floods

Zhaitan's awakening destroyed important coastal settlements, but the survivors did not simply abandon the Sea of Sorrows.

Kryta increasingly concentrated its population around Divinity's Reach. The city became a center for Humans whose families traced their histories to several different regions, including refugees from lands no longer under Human control.

The destruction of old Lion's Arch created a different opportunity.

Sailors, merchants, pirates, refugees, and adventurers returned to the flooded harbor. Wrecked ships and salvaged materials became part of a new settlement constructed over and around the ruins of the old city.

Under figures such as Cobiah Marriner, the new Lion's Arch emerged as an independent maritime power.

Unlike the old Human capital, the rebuilt city belonged increasingly to anyone willing to live, trade, or sail there.

The destruction caused by one Elder Dragon had helped create one of the first major multiracial cities of the coming age.

VIII

Central Tyria becomes a meeting place

The geography of the new age was created as much by migration as conquest.

The Asura rebuilt their civilization upon the surface after losing much of their underground world to Primordus.

The Norn established themselves throughout the southern Shiverpeaks after Jormag drove them from the north.

Elonian refugees escaping Palawa Joko reached Kryta, joining Human communities already descended from several earlier kingdoms.

Other migrations were occurring beneath the waves and along the coast. Quaggans moved toward shallower waters as dangers spread through their former homes, while Krait migrations placed additional pressure upon coastal peoples.

Lion's Arch became one of the clearest results of these movements. Humans, Charr, Norn, Asura, and other peoples increasingly met in a city that did not belong exclusively to any of them.

Tyria's peoples were becoming neighbors not because centuries of disagreement had disappeared, but because the world had become much harder to survive alone.

IX

The Sylvari enter history

During an age defined by ancient powers returning, Tyria also witnessed the beginning of something new.

In 1302 AE, the first Sylvari awakened beneath the Pale Tree on the Tarnished Coast.

These first individuals became known as the Firstborn.

A second generation followed in 1304 AE, with later Sylvari continuing to awaken from the Tree in the years that followed.

Their historical position was unusual.

The Humans carried memories of lost kingdoms. The Charr carried the history of Ascalon and their revolution against the Flame Legion. The Norn and Asura remembered homes taken from them by Elder Dragons.

The Sylvari had none of those ancestral memories.

They entered a world already shaped by thousands of years of conflict and had to learn its history while deciding what place they would occupy within it.

X

Destiny's Edge

By 1319 AE, the armies and Champions of the Elder Dragons were threatening several regions at once.

An adventuring guild eventually known as Destiny's Edge brought together individuals from several of Tyria's major peoples: Eir Stegalkin, Rytlock Brimstone, Logan Thackeray, Zojja, and Caithe.

Their cooperation was notable not simply because of who they were, but because of what their societies represented.

Human and Charr members fought beside one another while their nations were still technically enemies. A Norn displaced by Jormag fought alongside an Asura whose people had been displaced by Primordus, while one of the young Sylvari joined veterans of histories much older than her own race.

Destiny's Edge defeated several powerful Dragon Champions and demonstrated that even major servants of the Elder Dragons could be killed.

For a time, the guild seemed to offer a model for how the larger world might eventually confront the Dragons.

XI

Kralkatorrik creates the Dragonbrand

In 1320 AE, Kralkatorrik awakened.

The Crystal Elder Dragon flew south across Ascalon toward the Crystal Desert.

The land beneath its flight was transformed into the Dragonbrand, a vast scar of corrupted crystal stretching through territory controlled by the Charr and beyond.

Creatures caught within the corruption became Branded, joining another expanding Elder Dragon army.

Destiny's Edge prepared to confront Kralkatorrik with the help of Glint.

The significance of that alliance reached back to the previous Dragonrise. The Forgotten had freed Glint from Kralkatorrik's control thousands of years earlier, and she had spent ages preparing for a future confrontation with her former master.

The plan failed.

Glint was killed during the battle. Destiny's Edge fractured in the aftermath, its members blaming themselves and one another for what had gone wrong.

Tyria lost both a powerful ally and one of the few living beings whose knowledge of the Dragon Cycle reached back into the ancient world.

Commander Q // Observation

Glint survived one Dragonrise and spent the long sleep between cycles preparing for the next. Kralkatorrik's awakening ended that preparation in a single battle.

XII

The world begins organizing against the Dragons

By the early 1320s, several Elder Dragons were active at the same time, yet no single organization possessed the authority or strength to confront all of them.

Different groups developed different answers.

The Durmand Priory searched the past, believing forgotten knowledge might contain methods for understanding or defeating threats the modern world had forgotten.

The Vigil built military strength, arguing that the Dragons and their armies had to be confronted directly.

The Order of Whispers relied on intelligence, infiltration, secrecy, and networks whose roots stretched back through earlier Elonian history.

None of these approaches was sufficient by itself.

Meanwhile, old mortal conflicts were beginning to bend under the pressure of larger dangers. Humans and Charr found increasing reasons to cooperate despite centuries of war, and the recovery of the Claw of the Khan-Ur became part of negotiations between Kryta and the High Legions.

Peace had not yet arrived.

But the possibility of it now mattered because the Elder Dragons did not recognize the borders their enemies had spent centuries fighting over.

XIII

The Tyria of 1325 AE

By 1325 AE, the modern Dragonrise had transformed the political map of Central Tyria.

The Charr controlled most of Ascalon from the Black Citadel, while the surviving Human kingdom of Kryta was centered upon Divinity's Reach.

The Norn had rebuilt their society in the southern Shiverpeaks around Hoelbrak.

The Asura had established a new surface civilization and connected distant regions through their gate network.

The young Sylvari were entering a history that had begun long before their first awakening.

Between them stood Lion's Arch, a city whose existence reflected the migrations and cooperation of the age more clearly than almost any older capital.

Around these civilizations were active Elder Dragons, corrupted armies, displaced populations, old political rivalries, and orders preparing for wars that individual kingdoms could no longer fight alone.

The world had spent more than two centuries adapting separately to the return of the Dragon Cycle.

That approach was reaching its limit.

The next record begins with the individual who would eventually become known as the Pact Commander.

Commander Q // Observation

The previous Dragonrise scattered civilizations into hiding, fortresses, magical refuges, and other forms of survival. This Dragonrise pushed peoples toward one another. Whether that difference would be enough was about to be tested.

End of Volume VIII

The world prepares to fight back

Primordus had driven civilizations from beneath the earth. Jormag had forced the Norn south. Zhaitan had raised Orr and destroyed the old coast. Kralkatorrik had carved the Dragonbrand across Ascalon and killed Glint.

Yet the same upheavals had also created a Tyria in which Humans, Charr, Norn, Asura, and Sylvari lived closer together than their histories might otherwise have allowed.

The Dragon Cycle had returned.

This time, the world would attempt something different.
