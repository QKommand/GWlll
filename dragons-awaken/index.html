THE OLD WORLD BEGINS TO CHANGE

Volume VIII // The New Dragonrise

The Dragons Awaken

On the return of the Elder Dragon Cycle and the two and a half centuries that transformed the world of Guild Wars into the Tyria of Guild Wars 2.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

The ancient cycle returns

The previous record ended with Primordus stirring beneath Tyria. The Great Destroyer had been defeated, but the discovery behind it was far more important than the victory itself. The Elder Dragons were not merely creatures remembered from an ancient world. Their cycle had begun again.

Over the next two and a half centuries, that cycle reshaped nearly every major civilization. The Asura were driven toward the surface, the Norn lost their northern homeland, Orr rose from the sea, the Charr consolidated their control of Ascalon, and entirely new centers of civilization appeared.

This period should not be read as a list of Dragon attacks. The awakenings changed migration, politics, trade, borders, cities, and the relationships between peoples. The Tyria of 1325 AE was built as much by displacement and adaptation as it was by war.

— Commander Q

I

Ascalon reaches the end of its Human kingdom

In 1080 AE, the Ebon Vanguard withdrew from the north and fortified Ebonhawke, creating one of the last great Human strongholds in Ascalon.

Ten years later, the Charr finally captured Ascalon City. Facing the destruction of his kingdom, King Adelbern unleashed the Foefire.

The spell killed many of the Humans who remained within the city and surrounding lands, binding their spirits to Ascalon as spectral defenders who continued fighting the Charr long after death.

Human rule over most of Ascalon had ended, but the war itself did not disappear. Ebonhawke remained, the Foefire ghosts remained, and both Humans and Charr carried centuries of memory into the generations that followed.

II

A different political world takes shape

The early twelfth century saw institutions rise that belonged much more clearly to the world of Guild Wars 2 than to the kingdoms of the previous age.

In 1105 AE, the Durmand Priory was established in the Shiverpeaks. Its scholars dedicated themselves to recovering and preserving knowledge from the ruins and cultures of Tyria.

The Charr were changing as well. In 1112 AE, they constructed the Black Citadel over the ruins of Rin, creating a new center for the High Legions in territory once ruled by Humans.

Resistance to the Flame Legion also grew into a wider revolution. The struggle associated with Kalla Scorchrazor helped break the power of the Shaman caste and reshape Charr society around the High Legions.

Commander Q // Observation

The ruins of one age frequently become the foundations of the next. The Black Citadel standing over Rin makes that unusually literal.

III

Primordus becomes the first Dragon to awaken

In 1120 AE, Primordus fully awakened. The Destroyers that had appeared during the events of 1078 AE were no longer the warning signs of a sleeping power. They were the servants of an active Elder Dragon.

Primordus devastated the subterranean regions of Tyria. Ancient tunnels, settlements, and underground cities became battlefields as Destroyer armies spread through the depths.

The Asura, whose civilization had flourished beneath the surface, were driven upward. The Skritt and other subterranean peoples were displaced as well.

The Dwarves, already transformed into living stone during the earlier Destroyer crisis, continued their war against Primordus below the surface.

Timeline Note

Primordus stirred in 1078 AE but did not fully awaken until 1120 AE. The distinction matters: the first event warned Tyria that the Dragon Cycle was returning, while the second marked the beginning of the modern Dragonrise itself.

IV

Old Human civilizations change direction

While Primordus transformed the underground world, the great Human civilizations beyond Central Tyria were undergoing political changes of their own.

In 1127 AE, Emperor Usoku began campaigns against the Luxons and Kurzicks, eventually bringing their territories under stronger Imperial control. Cantha increasingly turned inward as the Empire tightened its authority and reduced contact with the outside world.

Elona followed an even darker path. Beginning in the twelfth century, Palawa Joko renewed his campaigns against the living kingdoms. He altered the flow of the River Elon, using control of water and his Awakened armies to weaken resistance.

By 1175 AE, Elona had fallen under Joko's rule. The Sunspears were hunted, destroyed, converted, or forced into hiding as the Undead king consolidated his power.

V

Jormag drives the Norn south

In 1165 AE, Jormag awakened in the Far Shiverpeaks. The Ice Dragon's rise brought years of unnatural winter and the growing presence of the Icebrood.

Norn settlements that had endured the northern climate for generations were overwhelmed. Jormag's influence also affected the spiritual world of the Norn, while several Spirits of the Wild were lost or disappeared during the migration that followed.

The Norn moved south into the Shiverpeaks. By 1169 AE, they had established Hoelbrak around the lodge of Bear.

Like the Asura before them, an entire people had been forced into a new homeland by an Elder Dragon.

VI

Zhaitan raises Orr from the sea

In 1219 AE, Zhaitan awakened beneath the ruins of Orr. The peninsula that had lain beneath the sea since the Cataclysm rose once again.

Orr did not return as the kingdom that had vanished in 1071 AE. The dead of the region became Risen, bound to the Elder Dragon and organized into armies that spread across the surrounding waters and coastline.

Orr's rising displaced enormous quantities of water. The resulting floods and waves devastated coastal settlements throughout the region and destroyed old Lion's Arch.

The effects reached much farther than Central Tyria. Coastal regions across the ocean were struck as well, while Risen ships and other Dragon forces made maritime travel increasingly dangerous.

Commander Q // Observation

Orr had already died once. Zhaitan's awakening did something worse than bring it back. The Dragon turned the ruins, the dead, and the memory of the kingdom into weapons.

VII

New cities rise from displacement

Zhaitan's awakening destroyed settlements, trade routes, and political centers, but the response was not simply retreat. The peoples of Tyria began building new places suited to the world that now existed.

Divinity's Reach became the principal Human city of Kryta, sheltering a population that increasingly included refugees from territories lost beyond the kingdom's borders.

The flooded remains surrounding old Lion's Arch were eventually occupied by sailors, pirates, merchants, and other survivors. Under figures such as Cobiah Marriner, a new settlement emerged from the wreckage.

The new Lion's Arch developed not as the capital of a single race, but as a maritime city shared by many peoples.

VIII

The new Tyria is built by refugees

The Dragonrise and the political upheavals surrounding it pushed entire populations across borders that had once separated distant cultures.

Elonian refugees fleeing Palawa Joko settled in Kryta. Quaggans moved toward the shores of Central Tyria as dangers increased in the deeper ocean, while hostile Krait migrations placed additional pressure on coastal populations.

Asura built a new civilization upon the surface. Norn settled the southern Shiverpeaks. Humans from several former kingdoms increasingly shared Kryta. Lion's Arch became a place where populations displaced from very different histories encountered one another.

The five great peoples of the coming age were not originally gathered together by diplomacy. Much of their proximity was created by survival.

IX

A new people enters the world

The changes of this era were not limited to ancient peoples losing their homes. In 1302 AE, the first Sylvari awakened beneath the Pale Tree on the Tarnished Coast.

These first Sylvari became known as the Firstborn. The Secondborn followed in 1304 AE, and later generations continued emerging from the Tree.

Unlike the Humans, Norn, Charr, or Asura, the Sylvari had no ancient kingdom behind them and no ancestral homeland they remembered losing. They entered history as a young people trying to understand a world that was already in crisis.

X

Heroes begin crossing racial boundaries

By 1319 AE, Elder Dragon forces threatened several parts of Tyria at once. The response increasingly required cooperation between peoples whose histories had rarely prepared them to trust one another.

The adventuring group later known as Destiny's Edge brought together champions from several races. They confronted powerful Dragon minions and defeated enemies that individual nations had struggled to overcome.

Their victories suggested that Dragon Champions were not invincible. More importantly, they demonstrated that coordinated forces from different societies could achieve what isolated kingdoms could not.

XI

Kralkatorrik scars the land

In 1320 AE, Kralkatorrik awakened and flew south from the Charr lands toward the Crystal Desert.

The Elder Dragon's passage created the Dragonbrand, a vast scar of corrupted crystal stretching across Ascalon. Creatures and landscapes caught within it were transformed into Branded.

Destiny's Edge attempted to confront Kralkatorrik with the aid of Glint, the former Champion who had resisted the Elder Dragon since the ancient age.

The plan failed. Glint was killed, and Destiny's Edge fractured in the aftermath.

One of Tyria's greatest sources of knowledge about the Dragon Cycle was gone just as the need for that knowledge was becoming impossible to ignore.

XII

Tyria learns to live between Dragons

Kralkatorrik's awakening did not produce an immediate collapse of Central Tyria. Instead, the major peoples continued adapting to a world in which several Elder Dragons were simultaneously active.

Lion's Arch expanded as a multiracial trading center. The Asura connected distant settlements through their gate network. The Durmand Priory gathered ancient knowledge, while the Vigil and Order of Whispers pursued very different strategies for confronting the growing Dragon threat.

Old enemies were also beginning to reconsider their wars. Cooperation between Humans and Charr against common threats created openings for diplomacy, while the return of the Claw of the Khan-Ur helped support negotiations between Kryta and the High Legions.

One of Tyria's longest mortal conflicts was beginning to bend toward peace because both sides were learning that the world contained enemies far larger than either of them.

XIII

Tyria reaches the beginning of Guild Wars 2

By 1325 AE, nearly every major people of Central Tyria inhabited a world shaped by the modern Dragonrise.

The Charr controlled most of Ascalon from the Black Citadel, while Humans had consolidated much of their surviving power around Divinity's Reach. The Norn lived throughout the southern Shiverpeaks around Hoelbrak, and the Asura had rebuilt their civilization upon the surface.

The young Sylvari were entering a history that had begun long before their awakening. Between them all stood Lion's Arch, increasingly important as a multiracial city connecting societies that had once developed largely apart.

Around these civilizations were active Elder Dragons, their Champions, and their armies. Tyria had spent centuries responding to each crisis separately.

That approach was about to fail.

The next chapter of the historical record begins with the rise of the individual who would eventually be known simply as the Commander.

Commander Q // Observation

Volume I began with civilizations trying to survive a Dragonrise they could not stop. Thousands of years later, Tyria has reached the same problem again. The difference is that this time the peoples of the world are close enough to one another that they may finally have a chance to face it together.

End of Volume VIII

Four Elder Dragons were awake. The peoples of Central Tyria had been scattered, rebuilt, and pushed into a world where isolation was becoming increasingly impossible.
