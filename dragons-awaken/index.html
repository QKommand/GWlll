Volume VIII // The New Dragonrise

The Dragons Awaken

The return of the Elder Dragons and the transformation of Tyria between 1080 and 1325 AE.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

The Dragon Cycle returns

The defeat of the Great Destroyer delayed Primordus, but it did not stop the Elder Dragon Cycle.

Over the next two and a half centuries, Elder Dragons awakened beneath Tyria, in the northern mountains, beneath Orr, and beyond Ascalon.

Entire populations moved. Old capitals disappeared. New cities and political powers rose in their place.

By 1325 AE, Tyria had become a world shaped as much by displacement and rebuilding as by the Dragons themselves.

— Commander Q

I

Human Ascalon falls

In 1080 AE, the Ebon Vanguard fortified Ebonhawke, preserving one of the last major Human positions in Ascalon.

Ten years later, Ascalon City itself faced its final Charr assault.

King Adelbern refused surrender and unleashed the Foefire.

The spell killed Adelbern and many of the Humans still defending the kingdom. Their spirits remained bound to Ascalon as spectral soldiers who continued fighting the Charr after death.

Human rule over most of Ascalon ended. Ebonhawke remained.

II

Primordus awakens

In 1120 AE, Primordus fully awakened beneath Tyria.

Destroyers spread through the subterranean world, overrunning settlements and forcing underground peoples toward the surface.

The Asura lost much of their old underground civilization and established themselves above ground.

Skritt communities were displaced as well, while the transformed Dwarves continued their war against Primordus in the Depths.

The first Elder Dragon of the modern cycle was awake.

Timeline Note // Primordus

Primordus was already stirring by 1078 AE. The defeat of the Great Destroyer delayed the Elder Dragon's full awakening until 1120 AE.

III

Jormag rises while Joko takes Elona

In 1165 AE, Jormag awakened in the Far Shiverpeaks.

Icebrood forces spread through Norn lands, forcing the Norn south from their ancestral homes.

By 1169 AE, Hoelbrak had become their principal gathering place in the southern Shiverpeaks.

Far to the south, Palawa Joko had rebuilt his strength after his release during Nightfall.

Joko conquered Elona through military force and control of the region's water supply. By 1175 AE, Elona had fallen under his rule and the Sunspears had been driven into hiding or destroyed as an open power.

During the same broad period, Cantha continued centralizing Imperial authority and reducing contact with the outside world.

Commander Q // Observation

Not every great change of this age came from an Elder Dragon. Tyria's older rivalries and ambitions did not stop simply because the Dragons were waking.

IV

Zhaitan raises Orr

In 1219 AE, Zhaitan awakened beneath the sunken ruins of Orr.

The lost kingdom rose from the sea.

Zhaitan corrupted the dead throughout Orr, creating the Risen and turning the remains of the old kingdom into an Elder Dragon's domain.

The rising peninsula displaced enormous quantities of seawater. Flooding and waves devastated coastlines around the Sea of Sorrows and destroyed old Lion's Arch.

Risen ships and creatures also made the surrounding seas increasingly dangerous.

Orr had returned to the surface, but the civilization that once surrounded Arah had not returned with it.

Commander Q // Observation

Orr had already been destroyed once. Zhaitan turned the remains of that destruction into a kingdom of the dead.

V

Migration rebuilds Central Tyria

The Dragon awakenings pushed several peoples into new regions at nearly the same time.

The Asura rebuilt on the surface after Primordus drove them from much of the Depths. The Norn settled throughout the southern Shiverpeaks after losing their northern homes to Jormag.

Human refugees from Elona reached Kryta as Palawa Joko tightened his rule.

Along the coast, other displaced peoples moved through waters made increasingly dangerous by new threats.

The destruction of old Lion's Arch produced one of the clearest symbols of this new world.

Sailors, merchants, pirates, refugees, and adventurers rebuilt the harbor from wreckage and salvaged material. Under leaders including Cobiah Marriner, the new Lion's Arch became an independent maritime city shared by several peoples.

Kryta likewise concentrated more of its Human population around Divinity's Reach.

Central Tyria was becoming a meeting place for peoples whose histories had once developed much farther apart.

VI

The Sylvari enter history

In 1302 AE, the first Sylvari awakened beneath the Pale Tree.

These first individuals became known as the Firstborn.

A second generation followed in 1304 AE, with later Sylvari continuing to awaken in the years ahead.

Their place in Tyria was unusual. Humans, Charr, Norn, and Asura carried long histories of kingdoms, wars, migration, and loss.

The Sylvari entered that world without an ancestral civilization of their own to remember.

VII

Destiny's Edge and the awakening of Kralkatorrik

By 1319 AE, Dragon Champions were threatening several regions at once.

The adventuring guild Destiny's Edge brought together Eir Stegalkin, Rytlock Brimstone, Logan Thackeray, Zojja, and Caithe.

Their victories against powerful Dragon Champions demonstrated what cooperation across racial and political divisions could accomplish.

In 1320 AE, Kralkatorrik awakened and flew south across Ascalon.

The land beneath its flight became the Dragonbrand, while creatures caught within its corruption became Branded.

Destiny's Edge attempted to confront the Elder Dragon with Glint, the former Champion freed from Kralkatorrik during the previous Dragonrise.

Glint was killed.

Destiny's Edge fractured after the battle, and Tyria lost one of the few living beings whose knowledge of the Elder Dragon Cycle reached back thousands of years.

Commander Q // Observation

Glint had survived the previous Dragonrise. She did not survive the beginning of this one.

VIII

Tyria begins organizing against the Dragons

By the early 1320s, several Elder Dragons were active, but no kingdom could confront the threat alone.

The Durmand Priory searched the past for knowledge that might help explain or defeat the Dragons.

The Vigil built military strength for direct confrontation.

The Order of Whispers relied upon intelligence, secrecy, and infiltration.

Old political conflicts also began bending under the pressure of larger threats. Human and Charr leaders found increasing reasons to negotiate despite centuries of warfare.

The Dragon Cycle did not recognize the borders their enemies had spent generations defending.

IX

Tyria in 1325 AE

By 1325 AE, Central Tyria bore little resemblance to the world of 1080 AE.

The Charr controlled most of Ascalon from the Black Citadel. Kryta was centered upon Divinity's Reach.

The Norn lived throughout the southern Shiverpeaks around Hoelbrak, while the Asura had built a new civilization on the surface.

The young Sylvari had joined a world whose older peoples were already struggling against active Elder Dragons.

Between them stood Lion's Arch, rebuilt as a city shared by several peoples rather than the capital of one kingdom.

Primordus was awake beneath Tyria. Jormag ruled the far north. Zhaitan controlled Orr. Kralkatorrik had carved the Dragonbrand across the land.

The peoples of Tyria had spent more than two centuries surviving these changes separately.

The next age would test whether they could fight the Dragons together.

Commander Q // Observation

The previous Dragonrise drove civilizations apart in search of survival. This one had already begun pushing their descendants toward the same roads, cities, and enemies.

End of Volume VIII

The fight against the Dragons begins

Four Elder Dragons were awake, and their influence had already remade the political and physical world.

Tyria had survived their return.

It had not yet learned how to defeat them.
