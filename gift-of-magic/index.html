Author's Preface

Magic was never new

The previous record ended as Humanity approached one of the great turning points of its early history. By 1 BE, Human civilizations had spread across much of the known world while the Gods remained active within mortal affairs.

Magic itself was already ancient. The Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, and other peoples had practiced powerful forms of it thousands of years before Humans arrived in Tyria. The Bloodstone had also existed since the previous Dragonrise, when the Seers used it to place much of the world's uncorrupted magic beyond the reach of the Elder Dragons.

What changed in 1 BE was not the existence of magic, but access to it. Abaddon distributed magical power across the mortal races on a scale that transformed civilization almost immediately. What followed forced the Gods to confront a problem that their own Gift had helped create.

— Commander Q

I

Abaddon distributes the Gift

In 1 BE, the Gods decided to extend magical power among the sentient races of Tyria. Abaddon, then the God of Knowledge and Water, took responsibility for distributing the Gift.

The ancient Bloodstone became part of this effort. Power that had once been concentrated within the artifact became available on a far broader scale, allowing mortal civilizations access to forms of magic that could dramatically alter their lives.

The intention was not to create magic, because magic had always been part of Tyria. The change was that magical power became available to far more people, and civilizations quickly discovered uses for it beyond those the Gods may have intended.

Commander Q // Observation

It is easy to call this the moment when the Gods "gave Tyria magic," but that wording erases nearly everything we have already learned about the ancient world. What they gave mortals was access. The power itself was much older than any Human kingdom.

II

The Gift becomes a weapon

The spread of magic quickly intensified existing rivalries among the peoples of Tyria. Civilizations that had gained new magical capabilities began turning those powers against one another, and disputes that might once have remained limited became increasingly destructive.

The resulting conflicts caused widespread casualties. The Gods had expanded what mortals were capable of doing, but greater power had not produced greater restraint. Instead, magic became another means by which competing peoples could pursue conquest and settle old rivalries.

The crisis grew severe enough that a mortal ruler eventually sought Divine intervention.

III

King Doric travels to Arah

King Doric, ruler of the Human kingdoms of the Tyrian continent, witnessed the growing devastation caused by the wars of magic. Seeking an end to the bloodshed, he traveled to Arah and pleaded directly with the Gods to restrain the power they had released.

The appeal placed the Gods in an unusual position. The Gift had been intended to empower mortal civilization, yet the same power was now being used to destroy it. Most of the pantheon agreed that magical access had to be reduced.

Their solution returned them to an artifact created for an entirely different crisis thousands of years earlier.

IV

The Bloodstone becomes a restraint

The Gods turned to the original Bloodstone, the Seer-created repository that had once helped protect magic from the Elder Dragons. This time, the artifact would be used to limit the amount of magical power available to mortals.

Much of the Gift was drawn back into the Bloodstone, reducing the power available throughout Tyria. The purpose was different from the one envisioned by the Seers, but the underlying principle remained familiar: magic could be controlled by concentrating it and limiting access to it.

The Gods went further than simply restoring the Bloodstone. They changed the artifact itself so that no single person could easily seize control over the full concentration of magic it contained.

Research Note // The Same Artifact

This is the same Bloodstone introduced in the previous Dragonrise. It was not a new Human or Divine creation. Its purpose had changed from hiding uncorrupted magic from the Elder Dragons to limiting the magical power available to mortal civilizations.

V

One Bloodstone becomes five

At an altar in Arah, the Gods divided the original Bloodstone into five major pieces. Four became associated with the traditional schools of magic, while the fifth served as the Keystone required to reunite them.

Fifth Piece

The Keystone

The fifth major fragment was required to bring the four school-aligned Bloodstones together again.

The division created a safeguard. The magical power of the original Bloodstone no longer rested within a single intact artifact, and restoring that original concentration required possession of the Keystone.

VI

Doric's blood seals the Stones

King Doric's role did not end with his appeal to the Gods. His blood was used to seal the Bloodstones, and surviving traditions state that his bloodline was given responsibility for guarding them.

The five major Stones were then taken from Arah and placed within the great volcano of the Ring of Fire that became known as Abaddon's Mouth. There they were intended to remain beyond easy mortal reach.

The immediate crisis had been answered. Magical power was restricted, the Bloodstone had been divided, and the means of restoring it had been separated among multiple pieces.

The decision solved one problem while creating another. The God who had distributed the Gift did not accept its removal.

VII

Abaddon turns against the pantheon

Abaddon fiercely opposed the decision to restrict the Gift he had distributed. What began as a disagreement over mortal access to magic became one part of a much larger conflict between Abaddon and his fellow Gods.

Abaddon's devoted Margonite followers rallied to him. He began transforming some of them into demonic beings, with Jadoth remembered among the first to undergo the change. The maritime people introduced in the previous volume were becoming the army of a rebellious God.

Abaddon eventually declared war upon the other Gods and assaulted the Gates of Heaven. The dispute that had begun with the distribution and restriction of magic had now become a Divine rebellion.

Commander Q // Observation

The Bloodstone had now served two very different civilizations in two very different emergencies. The Seers used it because Dragons consumed magic. The Gods used it because mortals consumed one another with magic. The artifact stayed the same. The danger around it changed.

End of Volume IV

The road continues

The Gift had been restricted, but the argument over magic was no longer the greatest danger facing Tyria. Abaddon had turned against the pantheon, and the Margonites had become his army.
