Author's Preface

The Gift was not the beginning of magic

Magic was already ancient when Humanity came to Tyria. The Seers, Mursaat, Forgotten, and other Elder Races had used it thousands of years earlier, and the Bloodstone itself had been created during the previous Dragonrise to protect uncorrupted magic from the Elder Dragons.

For most early Humans, however, that power remained beyond reach. The first known mortal access came in Orr, where Dwayna and Melandru granted the newly formed Order of the Vael the knowledge needed to create and refine magical signets. Other Orrian guilds later gained access to the same craft, and signets became valuable objects traded across Tyria.

The Gift of Magic therefore belongs to a later stage of the story. In 1 BE, magical power moved far beyond the controlled access of a few sanctioned guilds. Abaddon's Gift opened magic to the mortal races on a much greater scale, and that sudden change altered the balance of power across Tyria.

The consequences came quickly.

— Commander Q

I

The first mortal magic

Years before the Gift of Magic, the Vaelwardens of Orr became the first known mortals to receive magical power. Their order had been formed with the support of Dwayna and Melandru to protect the Vael and the magical wilds where those spirits lived.

To help the order fulfill that duty, the two Goddesses gave the Vaelwardens the ability to create and refine signets. These small gems held fixed expressions of magical power and allowed their users to call upon abilities that had previously remained in the hands of the Gods and Tyria's older magical races.

The Vaelwardens did not keep this distinction forever. As Orr grew, other guilds were also allowed to make signets. Over the following decades, the objects spread through trade, theft, and black markets reaching far beyond Orr.

Mortal magic therefore existed before 1 BE, but access remained limited. Knowledge of signet-making rested with chosen guilds, and the power contained within a signet was fixed rather than freely shaped by every person who possessed one.

Commander Q // Observation

This changes the way the Gift of Magic should be understood. Abaddon was not placing magic into mortal hands for the first time. The Vaelwardens had crossed that line generations earlier. What happened in 1 BE was much larger: the old limits on who could reach magic began to fall away.

II

Abaddon distributes the Gift

In 1 BE, access to magic changed on a scale far beyond the signet traditions of Orr. Abaddon, then the God of Knowledge and Water, became the central figure in distributing magical power among the mortal races of Tyria.

The change allowed mortals to wield magic with far greater freedom than the earlier system of sanctioned guilds and crafted signets had allowed. What had been guarded knowledge became a force capable of reshaping entire societies.

The ancient Bloodstone, created by the Seers during the previous Dragonrise, was already in the possession of the Gods during this period. It would soon become central to their attempt to control the consequences of the Gift.

Research Note // What Changed

Older histories often describe 1 BE as the moment when the Gods gave magic to the world. The surviving record is now more precise. Magic had always existed, and some mortals had already received limited access through signets. The Gift marks a much broader distribution of magical power among the mortal races.

III

The Gift becomes a weapon

The wider spread of magic quickly changed existing rivalries between the peoples of Tyria. Disputes that had once been fought with armies, wealth, and political influence could now be carried out with powers capable of causing destruction on a much greater scale.

The problem was not that every mortal who received magic immediately turned to war. Magic also offered new ways to heal, protect, build, travel, and understand the world. The danger came from how quickly that power entered societies that already had borders, ambitions, enemies, and old grievances.

As conflicts grew more destructive, the cost became difficult to ignore. The Gods had greatly expanded mortal power, but they had not removed the reasons mortals fought one another.

Eventually, a Human king traveled to Arah and asked them to intervene.

IV

King Doric travels to Arah

King Doric, ruler of the united Human kingdoms of the Tyrian continent, witnessed the growing damage caused by the wars of magic. Seeking an end to the bloodshed, he traveled to Arah and appealed directly to the Gods.

Doric asked them to reduce the magical power that had been released into the world. The request placed the pantheon in a difficult position. Greater access to magic had changed mortal civilization, but the same power was now making old conflicts far more dangerous.

Most of the Gods agreed that the situation could not continue. Their answer was not to remove magic from Tyria, which would have been impossible and contrary to the nature of the world itself. Instead, they sought to restrict how much of that power mortals could freely reach.

To do so, they returned to an artifact far older than any Human kingdom.

V

The Bloodstone becomes a restraint

The Gods turned to the original Bloodstone, the great magical repository created by the Seers during the previous Dragonrise. The Seers had used the artifact to keep uncorrupted magic away from the Elder Dragons. The Gods now used the same ability for a different purpose.

Much of the widely available magic was drawn back into the Bloodstone, reducing the amount of power that mortals could freely command. The artifact again became a place where magic could be concentrated and kept apart from the wider world.

The Gods did not stop there. An intact Bloodstone containing such a large concentration of magic would itself be dangerous if someone gained control of it, so they changed the artifact more deeply.

Research Note // The Original Bloodstone

The Bloodstone used here was not created by the Gods. It was the ancient Seer artifact that had survived from the previous Dragonrise. Human histories later remembered the Gods' work upon the Bloodstone more clearly than the much older history of its creation.

VI

One Bloodstone becomes five

At Arah, the Gods divided the original Bloodstone into five major pieces. Four became connected to the traditional schools of magic, while the fifth became the Keystone needed to bring the divided system together again.

The Keystone

Dividing the Bloodstone created another layer of protection. The great concentration of magic no longer rested within one complete artifact, and restoring the original system required the Keystone.

The four magical schools would remain part of how later generations understood and organized magic, even as magical practice continued to change over the centuries.

VII

Doric's blood seals the Stones

King Doric's role did not end with his appeal to the Gods. His blood was used to seal the divided Bloodstones, tying the Human king directly to the protections placed around them.

Doric and his descendants were given responsibility for guarding the Stones. The five pieces were then removed from Arah and placed within the great volcano of the Ring of Fire Islands known as Abaddon's Mouth.

There they were meant to remain beyond easy mortal reach. The great reserve of magic had been restricted, separated, sealed, and hidden.

For most of the pantheon, the immediate crisis had been answered. For Abaddon, the decision had created a new grievance.

VIII

Abaddon refuses the new limits

Abaddon opposed the decision to restrict the magical power he had helped spread. The dispute added to the growing division between Abaddon and the other members of the pantheon.

He was not without followers. The Margonites, the Human maritime civilization that ruled the Crystal Sea and much of its surrounding coastline, had long been devoted to him. Their relationship with Abaddon would now pull them into the conflict between the Gods.

The Margonites were still a Human people at the beginning of this period. Their cities, ships, and coastal holdings belonged to a civilization that had existed before Abaddon's fall. Only as the rebellion deepened did some of his followers begin the transformation into the beings remembered by later history.

Jadoth became one of the most famous of those followers. His story would become closely tied to Abaddon's rebellion and the fate of the Margonites themselves.

IX

The dispute becomes a Divine war

The conflict over magic did not remain an argument within the pantheon. Abaddon eventually turned against the other Gods, while his Margonite followers rallied around him.

What followed became one of the great disasters of early Human history. Abaddon's rebellion brought Divine power into direct conflict and carried the fighting into the lands surrounding the Crystal Sea.

The Gift of Magic had therefore led to consequences far beyond the mortal wars that first brought King Doric to Arah. The effort to restrict magic had opened a deeper division among the Gods themselves.

The next record follows that war, the destruction of the Crystal Sea, the fall of Abaddon, and the event from which later Human calendars would count their years.

Commander Q // Observation

The history is clearer now than the old phrase "the Gods gave magic to mortals" ever suggested. The Vaelwardens received the first controlled access. Other guilds followed. Abaddon's Gift then opened the door much wider, and the Gods eventually tried to close it again. The war that followed was not really about whether magic should exist. It was about who should be allowed to reach it, and how much power anyone should be trusted to hold.
