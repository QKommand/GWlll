THE GIFT OF MAGIC

Volume IV // The Divine and Human Age

The Gift of Magic

On Abaddon's expansion of mortal magic, the wars that followed, King Doric's appeal, the division of the Bloodstone, and the rebellion that broke the Divine order.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

Magic was not new

The phrase "Gift of Magic" can be misleading if it is read as the moment magic first entered mortal life. Magic had existed in Tyria for ages. Ancient peoples had built civilizations around it, and Human societies had already learned to study, regulate, craft, and use magical power.

Orr provides one of the clearest examples. By the age of King Doric, magic could support ordinary work, communication, commerce, entertainment, and defense, while more powerful forms were entrusted to guilds chosen by the High Council and the Six Gods.

What changed under Abaddon was the scale of access.

Greater magical power became available to mortals, upsetting a system that had previously placed stronger limits upon who could wield it. The result was not one war or one kingdom's collapse, but a magical crisis severe enough to bring King Doric to Arah in search of Divine intervention.

The solution the Gods chose would reach back thousands of years into Tyrian history.

— Commander Q

I

Abaddon's Gift

In the final year before the Exodus calendar began, Abaddon dramatically expanded mortal access to magic.

Abaddon was then one of the Six Gods, associated with knowledge, secrets, water, and the deeper mysteries of magical power. He had lived and governed alongside the rest of the pantheon in Arah, designed the Divine reliquaries, and participated in a civilization where magical knowledge was already carefully preserved.

Surviving histories remember his Gift as a major change in the relationship between mortals and magic. Power that had previously been more restricted became available on a far greater scale.

The change affected more than one kingdom or one profession. Magic became a force capable of shifting the balance between peoples who already possessed old rivalries, ambitions, and territorial disputes.

Research Note // The Gift

Surviving accounts differ in how they describe the mechanics of Abaddon's Gift. What remains consistent is the larger change: mortal access to magical power increased dramatically. The Gift should therefore not be described as the creation of magic itself.

II

Power spreads faster than restraint

Greater access to magic did not create peace.

Mortal conflicts intensified as peoples gained the ability to wield destructive magical power on a scale that had previously been harder to reach.

Old disputes became magical wars. Spellcraft that could enrich cities, protect settlements, and transform everyday life could also destroy armies, fortifications, and entire communities.

The surviving histories describe the violence growing severe enough that the benefits of greater magical freedom could no longer be separated from its cost.

The crisis eventually reached the point where mortal rulers looked again toward the Gods who had expanded, taught, and regulated magic in the first place.

Commander Q // Observation

Magic was not the cause of every rivalry. It changed what those rivalries could do. Conflicts that once had limits suddenly possessed weapons capable of breaking those limits.

III

King Doric goes to Arah

As the magical wars worsened, King Doric traveled to Arah and appealed directly to the Gods for an end to the destruction.

The journey is important because it shows that the earlier retreat of the Gods into Arah had not removed them from Tyria.

Doric did not petition distant beings who had already vanished into the Mists. He went to their city.

The Gods were still present, still capable of receiving mortal appeals, and still able to intervene in the magical order they had helped shape.

Doric's request forced the pantheon to confront the consequences of expanded magical access.

Their answer was already inside Arah.

IV

The Seer solution returns

Among the treasures gathered within Arah was the original Bloodstone.

The artifact had been created by the Seers during the previous Dragonrise to preserve magic beyond the reach of the Elder Dragons.

Thousands of years later, the problem facing the Gods was different. Elder Dragons were not consuming the world's power. Mortals were using increasingly destructive magic against one another.

Yet the ancient principle remained useful.

Magic could be removed from unrestricted circulation, placed within the Bloodstone, and made more difficult to access.

The Gods therefore turned an ancient Seer instrument of preservation toward a new purpose: the regulation of mortal magic.

Commander Q // Observation

The Seers had hidden magic because dragons were consuming it. The Gods used the same artifact because mortals were destroying one another with it. The threat had changed. The principle had not.

V

The Bloodstone is divided

The Gods did more than reuse the original Bloodstone. They transformed its structure.

Using King Doric's blood as part of the sealing process, the great stone was divided into five pieces.

Four of the Bloodstones became associated with different aspects of magic: Aggression, Destruction, Preservation, and Denial.

The fifth served as a keystone capable of bringing the divided powers together.

The original Seer repository had therefore become something new: a system through which the flow and use of magic could be divided and controlled.

Research Note // Bloodstones' Unmaking

Arah contained specialized Bloodstone-related equipment within Heavencrystal Palace, known as the Bloodstones' Unmaking. Its presence demonstrates that the Gods possessed equipment capable of working with Bloodstone material. The surviving record does not securely identify every step or device used in the division itself.

VI

The stones are placed beyond easy reach

After the division, the Bloodstones were carried away from Arah.

Surviving histories state that the Gods cast the divided stones into a great volcano. An eruption later scattered them across the surrounding world.

The stones were no longer gathered in one Divine repository where a single power might easily control them.

Their influence, however, did not disappear. Bloodstone magic continued to affect the regions around the fragments, and pieces broken from the greater stones retained traces of that power.

The artifact created to preserve magic during the Dragonrise had now become permanently embedded in the geography and magical history of Tyria.

VII

The Gods restrict magic

The division of the Bloodstone represented a reversal of the direction established by Abaddon's Gift.

Mortal access to magic was reduced and divided. The power that had spread so widely was placed once again behind limits.

For the Gods who supported the decision, the magical wars had demonstrated that unrestricted access could destroy the societies it was meant to empower.

Abaddon did not accept that conclusion.

The disagreement was no longer only about how magic should be used.

It became a conflict over whether the other Gods had the right to take that power back.

Commander Q // Observation

This is the point where the Gift stops being only a history of magic. The argument becomes political and Divine: who decides how much power another people is allowed to possess?

VIII

Abaddon refuses the decision

Abaddon's disagreement with the other Gods became open defiance.

The God of Knowledge had supported greater mortal access to magic. When the rest of the pantheon acted to restrict that power, he opposed them rather than accept the new order.

Surviving Human traditions remember Abaddon afterward as the Betrayer, but that title belongs to the history written after his defeat.

Before the rebellion, he had been one of the Six: keeper of knowledge, designer of the Divine reliquaries, patron of the Margonites, and a member of the same pantheon he was now prepared to fight.

What had begun as a disagreement over magic had become a fracture within the Gods themselves.

IX

The Margonites choose their God

Abaddon did not enter the coming conflict alone.

The Margonites had worshiped him during their long history as a maritime people of the Crystal Sea. When Abaddon turned against the other Gods, many of his followers remained loyal to him.

Their devotion tied the fate of their civilization to his rebellion.

The Margonites would eventually be transformed into the demonic beings remembered from later ages, but their earlier history should not be erased by what they became.

They entered the crisis as a Human people whose religious loyalty placed them on the side of a God now at war with the rest of his pantheon.

Research Note // Margonite Transformation

The surviving record clearly establishes the later demonic transformation of Abaddon's Margonite followers, but the exact sequence and timing of every stage should be distinguished from the earlier Human maritime civilization described in the previous volume.

X

The war of the Gods

The conflict eventually became a Divine war.

Abaddon and his followers fought against the other Gods and the forces aligned with them.

The scale of that fighting permanently transformed the southern world.

The Crystal Sea, once crossed by Margonite ships, was devastated during the conflict. What remained became the Crystal Desert.

Other lands were left scarred by sulfur and Divine destruction, creating the region later known as the Desolation.

The war demonstrated a danger beyond anything seen in the earlier mortal magical conflicts. Gods fighting Gods could remake the geography of Tyria itself.

Commander Q // Observation

The magical wars had convinced the Gods that mortal power needed limits. Their own war demonstrated that Divine power could be even harder for the world to survive.

XI

The fall of Abaddon

Abaddon was eventually defeated by the other Gods.

He was not destroyed.

Instead, the fallen God was imprisoned within the Realm of Torment, separated from physical Tyria but still alive.

His followers were driven with him into a new age of exile, while the accepted Human pantheon was reduced from Six to Five.

Abaddon's name and symbols were suppressed. His place in public worship disappeared, monuments associated with him were hidden or forgotten, and later generations increasingly remembered him through the title of the fallen God rather than as one of the Divine rulers who had once lived in Arah.

Yet the erasure was never complete.

His hidden reliquary remained in Orr, and traces of his earlier place within the pantheon survived beneath the religious landscape that replaced him.

XII

The world after the Divine war

Abaddon's defeat solved the immediate crisis, but the Gods could not undo what their conflict had done to Tyria.

The Crystal Sea was gone.

The Margonite civilization that had ruled its waters had fallen with the God it followed.

The Bloodstone had been divided and scattered. Mortal magic now existed under new limits.

The pantheon itself had been broken by war between its members.

And the remaining Gods had witnessed what happened when Divine power was brought fully into conflict on the physical world.

They had already withdrawn once before, leaving public life for the protection of Arah while remaining inside Tyria.

Their next withdrawal would be far greater.

Archive Note

Where the record remains contested

The Gift of Magic and Abaddon's rebellion survive through histories written largely by cultures that accepted the victorious pantheon. The broad events are well established, but motive is more difficult to recover.

Abaddon's intent

Surviving sources establish that Abaddon expanded mortal access to magic and later opposed the other Gods when that access was restricted. They do not provide a neutral record of every argument within the pantheon or allow us to reconstruct Abaddon's complete original motives.

The mechanics of the Gift

Different accounts describe the Gift through the language of Divine power, magical access, and the Bloodstone. The historical effect is clearer than every mechanism involved.

The Bloodstones' Unmaking

A Bloodstone-related apparatus existed within Heavencrystal Palace in Arah. Its exact role in the division of the original Bloodstone has not been securely established.

Abaddon's erasure

Later Human religion deliberately suppressed Abaddon's place among the Six. Hidden statues, reliquaries, and surviving records therefore provide evidence of a history that later generations were encouraged to forget.

The difference between event and motive matters especially here. We can follow what happened without pretending that the victors preserved every side of the argument that caused it.

End of Volume IV

The Gods prepare to leave

The Gift of Magic had exposed the danger of mortal power without restraint. The war against Abaddon revealed something more troubling: the Gods themselves could devastate the world when their disagreements became physical conflict.

The remaining Five still possessed Arah, their temples, reliquaries, knowledge, and connection to Humanity.

But their age of physical rule was ending.
