Volume IX // The Age of the Commander

The Age of the Commander

On Tyria's war against the Elder Dragons, the collapse of the ancient Dragon Cycle, and the search for a new balance of magic.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

The world decides to fight back

The previous record ended in 1325 AE with several Elder Dragons active and the peoples of Central Tyria living in a world already transformed by their awakenings. The Asura and Norn had lost ancestral homelands, Orr had risen from the sea, Kralkatorrik had carved the Dragonbrand through Ascalon, and the young Sylvari had entered a crisis thousands of years older than their own people.

Previous civilizations had answered Dragonrises by hiding magic, retreating into fortresses, escaping into other layers of reality, or simply enduring until the Dragons returned to sleep. The peoples of the modern age attempted something different.

They organized to kill them.

At first, that strategy appeared to work. Zhaitan fell. Mordremoth followed. The old belief that Elder Dragons were unstoppable forces of nature was broken within only a few years.

Then Tyria began discovering what the oldest histories had never fully explained.

Elder Dragons did more than consume magic. They were entangled with the way magic moved through the world. Their deaths released what they had accumulated, and that power did not disappear. It moved into Ley Lines, Bloodstones, other Dragons, Divine beings, artifacts, and eventually the fabric of the world itself.

The central question of the age therefore changed.

Tyria no longer needed only to learn how to kill an Elder Dragon.

It needed to learn how to survive without the old Dragon Cycle.

— Commander Q

I

The Orders unite against Zhaitan

The war against Zhaitan brought three organizations with very different traditions into the same conflict.

The Durmand Priory believed knowledge preserved in ruins, artifacts, and forgotten histories might reveal weaknesses in the Elder Dragons.

The Vigil favored direct military resistance, arguing that Dragon armies had to be met with organized force.

The Order of Whispers relied upon secrecy, intelligence, infiltration, and networks that extended across national borders.

Each possessed part of what the coming war required. None possessed enough alone.

Zhaitan's assault upon Claw Island made that weakness impossible to ignore. The island guarded the approaches to Lion's Arch, and its fall demonstrated that the Risen could project military power far beyond Orr.

Under Trahearne, the three Orders combined their knowledge, soldiers, intelligence, technology, and resources to create the Pact.

Trahearne became the organization's Marshal. The adventurer who had risen through the campaign became its field commander.

For the first time in the modern Dragonrise, several peoples had created an army whose primary purpose was not to defend a kingdom from an Elder Dragon.

It was created to attack one.

II

The Pact enters Orr

The campaign against Zhaitan revealed that an Elder Dragon's strength could not be understood only by looking at the Dragon itself.

Zhaitan controlled an entire system.

Eyes of Zhaitan extended its sight. Mouths of Zhaitan consumed magical artifacts and other sources of power. Risen forces recovered bodies, weapons, ships, and relics from the lands around them, while specialized Champions defended important parts of the Dragon's army.

The Pact therefore attacked that system before attempting to reach Zhaitan itself.

Supply lines were broken. Champions were killed. Magical resources were denied to the Dragon. Pact forces pushed south while Trahearne pursued the Wyld Hunt that had defined his life: cleansing the corruption that covered Orr.

At the Artesian Waters, the Source of Orr was cleansed through Trahearne and Caladbolg. The act did not instantly restore the ruined kingdom, but it broke the corruption at one of the most important magical and natural places in Orr.

The road to Arah opened.

III

The first Elder Dragon falls

In 1325 AE, Pact forces carried their campaign into the skies above Arah.

By then, Zhaitan had already been weakened through the destruction of important minions, supply systems, and sources of magical sustenance.

The Pact fleet used weapons developed from the combined research and technology of several peoples to strike the Elder Dragon directly.

Zhaitan was killed.

The victory transformed the way modern Tyria understood the Dragon threat. The Elder Dragons were no longer merely catastrophes that civilizations had to outlast.

They could die.

What remained unclear was what happened to the enormous stores of magic an Elder Dragon had consumed during its life.

Zhaitan's body was dead.

Its magic was not.

Commander Q // Observation

The Pact answered the question everyone had been asking: can an Elder Dragon be killed? History would spend the next several years discovering that this was only the first question.

IV

Scarlet Briar follows the Ley Lines

Zhaitan's death was followed by a series of conflicts that initially appeared unrelated.

The Sylvari engineer Scarlet Briar created alliances among groups that had little obvious reason to cooperate: the Molten Alliance, Aetherblades, Toxic Alliance, Twisted Watchwork, and other forces.

Their attacks served a larger investigation.

Scarlet had become aware of the magical pathways now commonly described as Ley Lines. She searched Central Tyria for places where those currents intersected and where large concentrations of magical energy could be reached.

Her experiments eventually identified something beneath Lion's Arch.

A major convergence of Ley energy lay below the city.

Scarlet built the Breachmaker to reach it.

V

The attack on Lion's Arch wakes Mordremoth

Scarlet launched a full assault upon Lion's Arch.

Her armies occupied the city while the Breachmaker drilled toward the magical convergence beneath it. Scarlet was eventually killed, but stopping her did not undo what the machine had already accomplished.

The disturbance traveled through Tyria's magical network toward the Heart of Maguuma.

Mordremoth awakened.

Vines and Mordrem spread far beyond the jungle. Ley energy surged through places that had previously appeared stable, while the connection between Elder Dragons and Tyria's larger magical structure became increasingly visible.

The Pact prepared to repeat what it had accomplished against Zhaitan.

It assembled an army and went looking for the Dragon.

Mordremoth was waiting for them.

VI

The Pact breaks over Maguuma

In 1328 AE, the Pact launched a major airship offensive into the Heart of Maguuma.

The campaign failed almost immediately.

Mordremoth destroyed much of the fleet, scattering soldiers and wreckage throughout the jungle. Pact survivors found themselves fighting through territory dominated by Mordrem, hostile environments, and the Dragon's influence over the plants surrounding them.

The disaster also exposed the secret of the Sylvari.

Their people were connected to Mordremoth. The Pale Tree had protected generations of Sylvari from the Elder Dragon's influence, but Mordremoth could reach their minds and call them toward service.

Some resisted.

Others became the Mordrem Guard.

The revelation transformed a young people who had spent only decades trying to understand their place in Tyria. Their origin was not merely connected to the Dragon crisis.

It was part of it.

VII

Mordremoth dies within its own mind

Mordremoth could not be fought in exactly the same way as Zhaitan.

Its physical presence extended through the jungle, roots, Mordrem, Blighting Trees, and the wider network under its control. Its consciousness could also extend through minds linked to it.

The Commander and allies exploited that connection.

While Pact and allied forces assaulted Mordremoth's physical network, the Commander entered a direct confrontation with the Dragon's mind.

Mordremoth was defeated.

The final remnants of its consciousness persisted through Trahearne, forcing one last decision. Trahearne was killed to prevent the Dragon from reestablishing itself through him.

In 1328 AE, the second Elder Dragon of the modern campaign was dead.

The released magic spread outward.

This time, Tyria noticed.

Commander Q // Observation

Zhaitan taught Tyria that Elder Dragons could die. Mordremoth taught Tyria that killing them changed everything around them.

VIII

The world fills with released magic

The deaths of Zhaitan and Mordremoth released enormous quantities of magic back into Tyria.

Ley Lines carried that energy across the world. Creatures, artifacts, and places already sensitive to magic began changing as ambient levels increased.

The consequences became especially visible around the Bloodstones.

In 1329 AE, the Maguuma Bloodstone exploded, devastating the surrounding region and releasing a massive concentration of power.

Magical anomalies appeared elsewhere. Old organizations returned to compete over the power being released.

The White Mantle again entered open conflict in Kryta, while the last known Mursaat became connected to struggles over the unstable magical landscape.

The pattern was becoming harder to ignore.

Killing Elder Dragons solved one crisis by creating another.

IX

Aurene enters the world

The answer Glint had prepared for the future began to take shape in 1329 AE.

Her surviving egg hatched within Tarir.

The young Dragon was named Aurene.

Aurene formed a bond with the Commander and began developing in the presence of mortal allies rather than through domination over servants.

Her importance was not immediately understood in full. Glint and the Exalted had prepared for her existence as part of a much older plan concerning the future of Dragon magic.

As later events revealed, Aurene possessed an unusual ability to receive and process forms of magic that had belonged to other powerful beings.

Tyria had spent years learning how to remove Elder Dragons from the magical system.

Aurene represented the possibility that something else might eventually take their place.

X

Balthazar enters the Dragon War

The growing concentrations of magical energy attracted another ancient power.

A figure claiming to be the Mursaat Lazarus the Dire was eventually revealed as Balthazar, the former Human God of War.

Balthazar had returned to Tyria stripped of much of his former Divine power. He intended to restore himself by taking magic from the Elder Dragons.

His first efforts involved Primordus and Jormag.

Research into the magical balance of the world revealed that simply allowing either Dragon to die could be catastrophic. With Zhaitan and Mordremoth already gone, the remaining Elder Dragons were carrying increasing portions of the world's unstable magic.

Balthazar was denied the power of Primordus and Jormag.

He chose another target.

Kralkatorrik.

XI

The God of War dies in Elona

Balthazar carried his campaign through the Crystal Desert and into Elona, accompanied by his Forged army.

His war brought the Commander into direct conflict with both Balthazar and the forces of Palawa Joko, whose empire had ruled Elona for generations.

In 1330 AE, Balthazar was confronted in the Domain of Vabbi.

He was killed.

As with Abaddon more than two centuries earlier, Divine death did not mean the disappearance of Divine power.

The energy released from Balthazar was absorbed by Aurene and Kralkatorrik.

Aurene grew stronger.

So did the Elder Dragon Tyria still needed to defeat.

Commander Q // Observation

By now the pattern is difficult to miss. A powerful being dies and another inherits what was released. Magic does not vanish when its wielder does.

XII

Palawa Joko's reign ends

Balthazar's defeat did not free Elona from the ruler who had controlled it for generations.

Palawa Joko remained at the center of an empire built upon the Awakened, control of historical memory, and the deliberate cultivation of his own Divine image.

Joko eventually attempted to carry a renewed Scarab Plague into Central Tyria.

The campaign was stopped before the disease could achieve the devastation of the ancient outbreak that had ended the Primeval Dynasty.

The conflict returned to Elona.

In 1331 AE, Joko was defeated.

Killing a Lich had never guaranteed that the Lich would remain dead.

Aurene solved that problem by consuming him.

In doing so, she absorbed the unusual magical quality associated with Joko's ability to return from death.

At the time, this appeared almost incidental.

It would soon save her life.

XIII

Kralkatorrik carries the Dragon War into the Mists

Kralkatorrik had absorbed magic released by fallen Elder Dragons and by Balthazar.

The different powers accumulating within the Crystal Dragon did not coexist peacefully. Kralkatorrik became increasingly unstable while his corruption expanded beyond its older boundaries.

The Elder Dragon eventually entered the Mists.

There, Kralkatorrik consumed magical energies from places connected to the former Divine realms and other parts of the Mists.

The Dragon War was no longer limited to the physical geography of Tyria.

Aurene had meanwhile grown into the role for which Glint had prepared her.

At Thunderhead Peaks, Aurene and her allies attempted to trap and kill Kralkatorrik.

The plan failed.

Kralkatorrik survived.

Aurene was impaled upon the Dragon's crystalline spikes and appeared to die.

XIV

Aurene returns from death

Aurene's death did not last.

The magic she had absorbed from Palawa Joko allowed her to resurrect herself.

She returned to the fight and pursued Kralkatorrik through the Mists with the Commander.

The wounded Elder Dragon eventually crashed back into Tyria while carrying fragments of several Mist realms with him.

Those fragments formed the strange landscape later known as Dragonfall.

There, the final assault upon Kralkatorrik began.

The Dragon was attacked from outside while Aurene and the Commander eventually confronted the powers and consciousness within him.

Kralkatorrik died.

Aurene absorbed the released magic and ascended as an Elder Dragon.

For the first time in the modern cycle, the death of one of the old Elder Dragons had produced a successor capable of cooperation with mortal civilization.

Commander Q // Observation

Glint's plan was never simply to kill Kralkatorrik. The world needed somewhere for his place in the magical system to go.

XV

Jormag turns a Charr conflict into a Dragon war

Aurene's ascension did not end the Elder Dragon crisis.

Within Charr society, Bangar Ruinbringer used fear of Aurene's growing power and distrust of outside influence to gather followers around his own campaign.

Bangar argued that the Charr required an Elder Dragon of their own.

His target was Jormag.

The Ice Dragon had always used more than physical force. Jormag whispered, persuaded, promised, and exploited doubt before transforming those who accepted its influence.

The conflict within the Charr Legions became a Civil War.

Ryland Steelcatcher rose through Bangar's forces and eventually became deeply connected to Jormag. Charr corrupted by the Ice Dragon became the Frost Legion.

Bangar's attempt to control Jormag failed.

The Dragon had been controlling the situation far more effectively than he had.

XVI

Fire and ice are turned against one another

Jormag's greatest weakness was tied to Primordus.

The two Elder Dragons represented opposed magical forces, creating a possibility that had not existed in earlier campaigns.

Instead of killing one Dragon and releasing its power into the surviving system, Tyria could attempt to make the Dragons destroy one another.

Braham Eirsson became connected to Primordus and acted as its Champion, while Ryland Steelcatcher served as the Champion of Jormag.

The Dragons were drawn into direct confrontation during the battle remembered as Dragonstorm.

In 1334 AE, Primordus and Jormag destroyed one another.

Their ancient opposition had been used to remove both from the cycle at once.

Yet their deaths still released the magic they had carried.

The energy moved through the world.

Much of it traveled toward Cantha.

XVII

Tyria returns to Cantha

In 1335 AE, the activities of the Aetherblades carried the Commander, Aurene, and their allies across the ocean to Cantha.

The Empire encountered there had changed greatly since the era of Shiro Tagachi and the Ministry of Purity.

Canthan civilization now relied heavily upon Dragonjade, a technology capable of storing and using magical energy to power transportation, machinery, communication, industry, and other parts of everyday life.

The system represented one of the most advanced forms of magical infrastructure in the modern world.

It was also connected to a secret.

Cantha had maintained a relationship with an Elder Dragon unknown to most of the outside world.

Her name was Soo-Won.

XVIII

The oldest Dragon explains the cycle

Soo-Won was not another Elder Dragon that had awakened during the modern Dragonrise.

She was the oldest of them.

Soo-Won revealed herself as the mother of the other Elder Dragons and described an age when the world's magic existed in a far more chaotic state.

The force underlying that chaos became known as the Void.

Soo-Won had once carried the burden of bringing order to that magic herself. The system later associated with the Elder Dragons divided the magical burden among multiple beings and domains.

Over immense spans of time, the Dragons became the creatures recorded throughout Tyrian history: consuming enormous quantities of magic, corrupting living beings, devastating civilizations, and eventually returning to sleep when ambient magical levels fell.

The old cycle therefore contained a contradiction.

The Elder Dragons threatened civilization.

They had also become part of the system regulating the magic upon which civilization depended.

Research Note // The Dragon Cycle

The modern record greatly expands what earlier civilizations understood about the Elder Dragons. Their ancient behavior can still be described through the surviving evidence of earlier Dragonrises, but the explanation involving Soo-Won, the magical domains, and the Void belongs to knowledge recovered only near the end of the modern cycle.

Commander Q // Observation

This is where the beginning of the archive finally meets its end. The Dragon Cycle was never only about monsters waking from beneath the world. Beneath the destruction was a broken way of keeping magic from returning to chaos.

XIX

The old system collapses

By the time the Commander reached Cantha, five of Soo-Won's fellow Elder Dragons were dead.

Their magic had not vanished.

Much of it had eventually returned through Tyria's magical system and accumulated within Soo-Won.

The strain became increasingly difficult to contain. The different magical domains mixed within her while the Void began emerging through the instability.

Canthan Dragonjade technology, which relied upon controlled access to Soo-Won's magic, began experiencing failures as that instability increased.

Soo-Won understood that she could no longer maintain the balance alone.

She believed Aurene, whose prismatic nature allowed her to receive and filter different forms of magic, could succeed where the old system could not.

The final struggle was therefore not a conventional campaign to destroy another Dragon.

It was an attempt to prevent the collapse of the magical order itself.

XX

Aurene inherits the balance

At the Harvest Temple, Soo-Won was overwhelmed as the Dragonvoid manifested through the collapsing magical system.

The Commander, Aurene, and their allies confronted the crisis while trying to separate the destructive Void from Soo-Won herself.

Soo-Won died.

The Dragonvoid remained and had to be confronted as a separate threat before its spread could continue unraveling the world.

Aurene absorbed the remaining Dragon magic and became the sole surviving Elder Dragon.

Her role was fundamentally different from the cycle followed by those who came before her.

She did not need to consume the world's magic until civilizations starved beneath her.

She could receive different magical energies, filter them, and return that power to the world in a more stable form.

After learning to manage that burden, Aurene withdrew from ordinary affairs to concentrate upon maintaining the magical balance.

The ancient cycle of Dragonrise, consumption, destruction, magical famine, and renewed slumber had ended.

For the first time in the surviving history of Tyria, another Dragonrise was not waiting somewhere in the future.

The world had escaped the cycle.

It had not escaped history.

Commander Q // Observation

The Seers once hid magic so civilization might survive the Dragons. Thousands of years later, civilization survived by changing the role of the Dragons themselves. The problem was never simply magic or Dragons. It was finding a balance that did not require the world to die every time that balance was restored.

End of Volume IX

The oldest cycle ends

The first volumes of this archive began with civilizations facing a Dragonrise they could not stop. The Seers preserved magic. The Mursaat escaped from its reach. The Forgotten protected Glint. The Dwarves withdrew into their strongholds, and the Jotun carried fragments of an older civilization into the age that followed.

Thousands of years later, another Dragonrise began.

This time the peoples of Tyria did not simply endure until the Dragons returned to sleep. They formed alliances, killed the old Elder Dragons, discovered the danger created by those deaths, and eventually found a successor capable of maintaining magical balance without continuing the old cycle.

Aurene remained.

The age defined by the Elder Dragons did not.

For the first time, Tyrian history could move forward without another Dragonrise waiting to repeat everything that had come before.
