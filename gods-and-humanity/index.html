THE GODS ENTER THE RECORD

Volume III // The Divine and Human Age

Gods and Humanity

On the Human Gods, the migration and spread of Humanity, the rise of Arah, and the Divine Age that preceded the Gift of Magic.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

When the Gods walked openly

The previous records followed the civilizations of the ancient world through the Dragonrise and the long aftermath that followed. The next major transition begins with beings who came to Tyria from the Mists and whose presence reshaped the history of the world.

The earliest part of their story survives largely through Human religious traditions, particularly those preserved in Orr. Later records become firmer as Human settlements, kingdoms, dynasties, and calendars enter the chronology.

The Gods did not arrive, establish Arah, and then immediately disappear. For a long period they remained physically present in Orr. They lived among followers, collected ancient artifacts, maintained reliquaries, influenced the regulation of magic, and eventually withdrew from public life while remaining inside Arah.

Only much later would they leave Tyria entirely.

Historical Caution // Divine Tradition

Orr preserves some of the most detailed accounts of the Gods' arrival. These writings combine history, religion, and sacred memory. Where the surviving evidence becomes uncertain, this archive treats those traditions as traditions rather than forcing them into an exact chronology.

— Commander Q

I

The Gods arrive from the Mists

Orrian sacred histories remember Dwayna as the first of the Gods to enter Tyria from the Mists.

According to these accounts, Dwayna opened the way and brought Humanity with her. The traditions then describe Balthazar crossing Orr in cleansing flame and Melandru covering the land in new growth.

The twins Ilya and Lyss, remembered together as Lyssa, are described as living among the people and bringing joy while the great Divine city was being prepared.

The precise sequence should be read cautiously. The traditions use religious and poetic language, and even the statement that Dwayna first placed her foot upon the stones of Arah leaves open whether an earlier structure existed at the site or whether the writer was describing the future city poetically.

What remains clear is the importance of Orr and the Artesian Waters. The Waters were an extraordinary source of natural magic and became closely connected with the earliest Divine presence remembered in Tyria.

II

Humanity enters Tyria

Humanity's origins lie beyond Tyria. The surviving traditions connect Human migration to the Mists and to the Gods who guided that movement.

The original Human homeland has not been identified, nor does the surviving record provide a complete route from that world to the settlements that later appeared across Tyria.

The Orrian histories suggest that Humanity did not immediately remain in Orr while the land was being prepared. Human populations spread elsewhere, and later maritime migrations carried communities toward regions such as Cantha and Elona.

The arrival of the Gods, the passage of Humanity through the Mists, and the establishment of later settlements should therefore be understood as parts of a larger migration rather than one neatly dated event.

Research Note // Before Tyria

The Gods themselves possessed histories before their arrival in Tyria. Balthazar had a father and a half-brother, Menzies, while other parts of Divine history likewise extend into the Mists. The surviving record does not establish the exact relationship between the Gods' earlier history and Humanity's original homeland.

III

Human settlement in Cantha

By 786 BE, Human settlement enters firmer chronology in Cantha.

Communities spread across the continent and developed into clans and regional powers. Their political traditions gradually consolidated until Kaineng Tah united the clans in 510 BE.

His rule established the political foundation of the Empire of the Dragon and became the starting point of the Canthan Calendar.

From this point forward, dynasties and formal calendars begin providing firmer chronological anchors than the religious traditions surrounding the earliest migration.

Commander Q // Observation

Cantha marks one of the places where the character of the record changes. Sacred memory does not disappear, but rulers, calendars, and political institutions begin leaving a clearer trail.

IV

Arah, City of the Gods

In Orr, Human builders worked on a city intended to house the Gods themselves.

Arah grew into far more than a temple. It became a vast Divine and Human capital containing palaces, temples, gardens, plazas, crypts, vaults, shipyards, defensive works, and ceremonial districts.

The Grand Temple of the Pantheon stood near the heart of the city, while the Divine Piazza and Godpath Gardens formed part of its public and religious landscape.

The Courts of the Highborn Kings and Estates of the Favored point to the presence of political and elite society, while the Shipfall served as a major naval shipyard.

Arah also possessed secure vaults, magical defenses, crypts, canals, and an Arsenal of the Blessed protected by automated magical weaponry.

The physical city therefore reflects a society in which Divine worship, mortal government, scholarship, defense, trade, and ordinary civic life occupied the same capital.

V

The reliquaries and the treasures of Arah

As Arah developed, Abaddon, God of Knowledge, designed a series of magically warded reliquaries in the region now known as Siren's Landing.

The reliquaries protected valuable artifacts, relics, and knowledge associated with the Gods. Their enchantments were designed to defend those collections from thieves and defilers.

Surviving reliquaries are associated with Balthazar, Dwayna, Grenth, Lyssa, Melandru, and Abaddon.

Abaddon's reliquary was later hidden after his fall, while no comparable ancient Orrian reliquary exists for Kormir, who did not ascend until more than a thousand years after the Gods had left Tyria.

The reliquaries provide physical evidence that the Gods maintained their own collections while they were still active in Orr.

VI

Arah gathers the ancient world

The Gods did not limit their collections to objects they created themselves. Powerful works from older civilizations were brought into Arah for safekeeping, reconstruction, and study.

Among the most important was the original Bloodstone, created by the Seers during the previous Dragonrise to preserve magic from the Elder Dragons.

The Gods also acquired an ancient Jotun Mystic Telescope, brought it to Arah, and reconstructed the device within the city.

A major complex known as the Heavencrystal Palace housed the Bloodstones' Unmaking, demonstrating that specialized Bloodstone-related equipment existed within Arah.

The exact role of that apparatus in later events is not fully documented, but its presence establishes that Bloodstone material was not merely locked away. The Gods possessed the means to work with it.

Arah therefore functioned as more than a Divine residence. It was also a treasury, archive, armory, and center for the preservation of magical knowledge from across Tyria.

Commander Q // Observation

The collections of Arah tell us something important about the Gods. They did not behave as though the world had nothing to teach them. They preserved and reconstructed the work of civilizations whose histories began long before Arah existed.

VII

The Gods withdraw into Arah

For a time, the Gods lived openly among their followers. That closeness eventually created a practical problem: prolonged exposure to Divine radiance could blind mortals who looked upon them.

Dwayna commissioned the Orrian sculptor Malchor to preserve accurate likenesses of the Gods so their followers could remember their appearances without enduring the danger of direct exposure.

As the statues were completed, the Gods withdrew from ordinary public life and into Arah.

Surviving Orrian statuary preserves the sequence associated with that retreat as Balthazar, Lyssa, Melandru, Dwayna, and Grenth.

The surviving arrangement requires some caution. Grenth belonged to a changing Divine pantheon, while Abaddon's image and worship were later deliberately suppressed after his rebellion.

What matters most is what the retreat was not.

The Gods had not left Tyria.

They remained in Arah.

Research Note // Retreat and Exodus

The withdrawal into Arah and the later Exodus are separate events. The first ended routine public appearances by the Gods. The second removed the Gods from physical Tyria entirely.

VIII

The pantheon changes

The group later remembered simply as the Six did not possess an unchanging membership.

Dhuum preceded Grenth as the ruler associated with death and the Underworld. Grenth, remembered in Orrian tradition as the son of Dwayna and the first God born in Tyria, eventually defeated Dhuum and took his place within the pantheon.

Grenth's presence in ancient Orr became firmly established. Arah contained a statue dedicated to him, its crypts carried his imagery, and one of the Divine reliquaries was devoted to his relics.

Abaddon remained part of the same pantheon during this period. He was the keeper of knowledge and secrets, designer of the reliquaries, and one of the Divine powers governing from Arah.

His later rebellion therefore began within a pantheon whose members had already lived and governed alongside one another.

IX

Human civilizations expand

While Arah developed as the center of Divine life, Human societies continued expanding across the world.

By 205 BE, Human settlement was firmly established in Orr and Istan. Around 200 BE, the Primeval Dynasty began its rule in Elona, with Fahranur remembered as the First City.

Human expansion also reached Ascalon, where settlement brought Humans into direct territorial conflict with the Charr.

These regions did not develop as one political society. Cantha, Elona, Orr, and Ascalon formed different kingdoms, institutions, traditions, and relationships with the Gods.

Cantha

Empire of the Dragon

An Imperial civilization with its own dynasties, calendar, and regional traditions.

Elona

Primeval Dynasty

A royal tradition centered in the southern lands and the early cities of Istan.

Orr

Divine Kingdom

A kingdom growing beneath Arah and deeply shaped by the physical presence of the Gods.

Ascalon

Contested Frontier

A developing Human realm whose expansion created a lasting territorial struggle with the Charr.

X

The Margonites and the southern seas

The Margonites developed into a powerful Human maritime culture around the Crystal Sea and the southern coastlines.

Their ships traveled widely, and surviving Margonite sailing traditions preserve some of the earliest known references to distant Castora.

Their society became closely associated with the worship of Abaddon, then the God of Knowledge, Secrets, and Water.

The Margonites of this period remained Human. Their later transformation belonged to the crisis surrounding Abaddon's rebellion and should not be projected backward onto their earlier maritime civilization.

Commander Q // Observation

Later history remembers what the Margonites became. Their earlier history belonged to ships, trade, distant coastlines, and a sea that would not survive the coming Divine war.

XI

Magic under Divine rule

By the reign of King Doric, Arah had become one of the great centers of the known world. Orrian accounts describe the shining city rising above the surrounding kingdom and visible from great distances.

The Gods remained inside the city even though they had withdrawn from ordinary public life.

Magic during this period was not absent from mortal society, but access to significant magical power could be regulated. Orrian histories describe magic being entrusted to peacekeeping guilds chosen by the High Council and the Six Gods.

This reveals a system in which Divine authority and mortal government both participated in deciding how magical power was used.

At the same time, surviving accounts of Orr show how deeply magic could become integrated into ordinary life. Visitors described enchanted markets where magic assisted with labor, communication, entertainment, and the presentation of ordinary goods.

Orr therefore does not appear to have been a society containing only a small class of isolated spellcasters. It was a society whose infrastructure had learned to use magic in everyday ways.

Research Note // Magical Aptitude

One surviving Orrian writer wondered whether some Orrians possessed an unusual natural ability to use magic without the education others required. The writer presents this as speculation, and it should not be treated as proof of a distinct Orrian magical bloodline.

Commander Q // Observation

The later phrase "Gift of Magic" can obscure the world that existed beforehand. Magic was already known, taught, regulated, crafted into objects, and used in daily life. What was about to change was not the existence of magic, but the scale of mortal access to it.

XII

The Gods remain part of mortal life

Even after withdrawing into Arah, the Gods remained active powers within Human religion and government.

The Scriptures of Melandru, dated to 48 BE, preserve one example of Divine intervention from this late period.

They tell of Ewan and his people, whose repeated destruction of the natural world drew Melandru's judgment. According to the scripture, Melandru appeared and transformed them, binding their fate to the land they had mistreated.

The account remains a religious text, but it reflects a Human world that continued to understand the Gods as active beings rather than distant symbols.

Their retreat into Arah had changed the manner of Divine contact.

It had not yet ended it.

XIII

The balance of magic changes

By 1 BE, magic was already woven through parts of mortal civilization.

Orr possessed a sophisticated magical culture. Peacekeeping guilds could be entrusted with power under the authority of the High Council and the Gods. Ancient artifacts and magical knowledge remained stored within Arah.

Among those treasures was the original Seer Bloodstone.

Then Abaddon dramatically expanded mortal access to magic.

The change was not the invention of magic, nor the first moment mortals encountered it. It was a change in scale and availability.

Power that had previously been more limited or regulated became available much more widely.

The consequences were immediate enough to transform the relationship between mortals and the Gods and eventually bring King Doric back to Arah seeking an answer.

That crisis belongs to the next record.

Archive Note

Where the Divine record remains uncertain

The Divine Age is better documented than the previous Dragonrise, but its earliest centuries still combine religious tradition with later historical reconstruction.

Humanity's original homeland

Humanity came to Tyria from beyond the world, but the surviving record does not identify its original homeland or fully explain the circumstances that forced or encouraged the migration.

The earliest form of Arah

Orrian tradition places Dwayna's first step upon the stones of Arah, while other evidence describes the city being built for the Gods afterward. Whether the wording preserves an earlier structure or is simply poetic remains unresolved.

The statues and the retreat

Surviving statues preserve an order associated with the Gods' withdrawal from public life, but the changing membership of the pantheon and the later suppression of Abaddon complicate reconstruction of the original arrangement.

The Bloodstones' Unmaking

The device establishes that Bloodstone-related magical equipment existed within Arah. Its exact relationship to the later division of the original Bloodstone should remain open until the surviving evidence identifies that function directly.

These uncertainties belong in the historical record. They mark the places where surviving evidence ends and interpretation would otherwise begin.

End of Volume III

The Divine Age reaches a breaking point

By the end of this period, Humanity had developed kingdoms, empires, maritime cultures, and magical institutions across much of the known world.

Arah stood at the center of Divine life. The Gods had withdrawn from ordinary public sight but remained physically present, maintaining their reliquaries, treasures, temples, and influence over mortal affairs.

Magic already supported everyday life in places such as Orr, while powerful artifacts from older civilizations rested inside the city of the Gods.

Abaddon's decision to expand mortal access to magic changed that balance.
