THE GODS ENTER THE RECORD

Volume III // The Divine and Human Age

Gods and Humanity

On the Human Gods, the migration of Humanity, and the rise of the civilizations that reshaped much of the known world.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

A new chapter in Tyrian history

The previous volumes followed the Dragonrise and the Elder Races that survived it. The next major change in the record begins with the beings later worshiped as the Human Gods and the people who followed them into Tyria.

The earliest part of this history is difficult to separate from religious tradition. Orr preserves some of the most detailed accounts of the Gods' arrival, while later calendars provide firmer dates for the spread of Human settlement and the rise of organized states.

This record therefore begins cautiously. It follows what the surviving traditions say, distinguishes those accounts from later dated history, and traces Humanity from migration into the emergence of Cantha, Elona, Orr, Ascalon, and other Human societies.

— Commander Q

I

The Human Gods

The earliest surviving Human histories describe a group of powerful beings who crossed from the Mists and became deeply involved in the lives of the people who worshiped them.

The Divine figures remembered from this ancient period include Dwayna, Balthazar, Melandru, Lyssa, Abaddon, and Dhuum.

The pantheon did not remain unchanged. Grenth, remembered in Orrian tradition as the son of Dwayna and the first God born in Tyria, eventually defeated Dhuum and assumed his place as the God of Death.

Abaddon likewise belonged to the pantheon before his later rebellion and removal from accepted Human worship. Much later still, Kormir would inherit his Divine position.

Research Note // Divine History

"The Six" describes a Divine pantheon whose membership changed over time. References to the Six in different periods therefore do not always refer to exactly the same individuals.

II

The Orrian account

Some of the richest surviving traditions concerning the Gods come from Orr, where sacred texts describe their arrival through a sequence of Divine acts.

According to these accounts, Dwayna stepped from the Mists, placed her foot upon the stones of Tyria, opened the gates, and brought Humanity with her.

Balthazar followed and swept across Orr in flame, claiming the land for Humanity. Melandru then transformed the region, covering it in growth and urging peace with the peoples already living there.

The twins Ilya and Lyss, remembered together as Lyssa, are described as living among Humanity while the other Gods built Arah. The tradition says that Lyssa brought joy and helped people forget the hardships of the past before being summoned to the completed city.

These writings also remember Abaddon as a former keeper of secrets whose name was later condemned, while Grenth appears as the Divine successor who defeated Dhuum.

Historical Caution // Sacred Tradition

These accounts preserve Orrian religious memory. They are important evidence for how Orr understood its relationship with the Gods, but their theological language should not be confused with a neutral eyewitness chronology.

III

Humanity enters Tyria

The surviving record places Humanity's origins outside Tyria and connects its arrival to the Gods and the Mists.

The exact homeland from which Humans came, the route they traveled, and the full circumstances of the migration remain uncertain. What survives more clearly is the tradition that their movement into Tyria occurred under Divine guidance.

Dwayna occupies a particularly important place in these accounts, while Orr later remembered the arrival of the Gods and Humanity as part of the sacred history of the land.

The migration itself should not be treated as one neatly dated event. The Divine arrival traditions and the later establishment of dated Human settlements belong to a broad sequence whose middle remains only partially documented.

IV

Humanity establishes itself in Cantha

By 786 BE, Human settlement is firmly anchored in the historical chronology of Cantha.

Communities grew across the continent and developed into clans and regional powers. Over the following centuries, those societies became increasingly organized and eventually entered a period of political unification.

In 510 BE, Kaineng Tah united the Human clans and founded the political order that became the Empire of the Dragon.

This event also became the beginning of the Canthan Calendar, giving later historians one of the most useful chronological systems for reconstructing early Human history.

Commander Q // Observation

Once Cantha enters the dated record, the character of the evidence begins to change. Myth and sacred memory do not disappear, but kings, dynasties, wars, and calendars begin giving us firmer ground to stand on.

V

Orr grows beneath Arah

Orr developed into one of the most important centers of Human religious and magical life. At its heart stood Arah, the city associated directly with the Gods.

The region's connection to the Divine shaped much of Orrian culture. Temples, monuments, sacred complexes, and later architectural traditions repeatedly drew upon the relationship between mortal life and the Gods.

Orrian architecture could itself carry religious meaning. Surviving descriptions explain that labyrinths represented Humanity's struggle through life. Visitors passed through difficult paths before reaching gardens and magical works at their center.

Orr also became renowned for magic. Later records show a society where magical knowledge was deeply integrated into religion, scholarship, craftsmanship, defense, and eventually ordinary civic life.

Research Note // Orrian Magic

Later Orrian accounts describe magic becoming deeply integrated into everyday life, but those texts should not automatically be projected backward into every earlier century. Orr's magical culture developed over a long period.

VI

Kingdoms rise in Elona

By the third century before the Exodus, Human settlement had become firmly established in Elona.

Istan became an important center of settlement, trade, and political power. Around 200 BE, the rise of the Primeval Dynasty gave Elona one of its earliest enduring royal traditions.

Fahranur, the First City, became the seat of the Primeval Kings and one of the great early centers of Elonian civilization.

Elona would eventually develop several distinct regions and political traditions, but the Primeval period established a royal legacy that endured in memory long after the dynasty itself was gone.

VII

The Margonites and the Crystal Sea

Another Human culture developed around the waters of the Crystal Sea. The Margonites became skilled sailors, traders, and maritime settlers whose influence extended across the sea and its surrounding coastline.

Their ships traveled widely, leaving records of distant voyages and places that would later pass into rumor. Ancient Margonite sailing accounts are among the early traditions connected to the distant island of Castora.

Margonite religion became closely associated with Abaddon, then worshiped as the God of Water and Secrets.

The Margonites of this period should be distinguished from the demonic forms encountered in later history. They began as a Human maritime civilization whose fate became bound to the God they followed.

Commander Q // Observation

The later fall of the Margonites tends to overshadow everything that came before it. Yet their oldest traces belong to sailors, coastlines, trade routes, and journeys across waters that would eventually disappear from the map.

VIII

Humanity expands into Ascalon

By approximately 100 BE, Human expansion had reached Ascalon.

The land was already contested. The Charr controlled much of the region, having built their own power through earlier wars and territorial expansion.

Human settlement pushed the Charr northward from part of the territory and began a conflict that would endure across generations.

Ascalon developed into a heavily fortified Human kingdom shaped by borders, warfare, and a powerful attachment to the land itself. Those competing claims would remain central to both Human and Charr history.

IX

The Gods in Human memory

Even as Human states became more established, religious traditions continued to describe the Gods as active participants in mortal affairs.

The Scriptures of Melandru, dated to 48 BE, preserve one such story. They tell of Ewan and his people, who repeatedly exhausted the lands through which they traveled.

According to the scripture, the earth itself prevented their departure and Melandru appeared before them. She transformed the offenders and bound them to the natural world they had abused.

Whether read as Divine history, religious teaching, or both, the account reveals how Humans of the period understood the relationship between Divine authority, nature, and mortal responsibility.

Research Note // Scriptures

Religious texts can preserve names, dates, places, and traditions while also serving a theological purpose. They remain historical evidence, but their genre should always be kept in view.

X

A world of Human civilizations

By the final years before the Exodus calendar began, Humanity had developed into a collection of distinct civilizations spread across several regions.

Cantha

Empire of the Dragon

An Imperial state with its own calendar, dynasties, political traditions, and regional cultures.

Elona

Primeval Kingdom

A royal civilization centered in the south whose early dynasties left enduring monuments and political traditions.

Orr

Sacred Kingdom

A society centered around Arah and deeply shaped by religion, scholarship, architecture, and magic.

Ascalon

Contested Kingdom

A heavily defended Human realm whose expansion created a lasting struggle with the Charr.

Margonites

Crystal Sea Mariners

A seafaring people whose trade, voyages, and devotion to Abaddon tied them closely to the southern seas.

These societies shared ancestry and many religious traditions, but they did not develop as one political culture. Geography, trade, war, local customs, and different relationships with the Gods gave each region its own identity.

XI

Magic before the Gift

Magic was not absent from mortal life before 1 BE.

The histories of the Elder Races already demonstrate that magic had existed and been used for thousands of years. Human societies also possessed magical knowledge before Abaddon's later Gift.

Orr became particularly associated with magical study. Surviving Orrian writings describe magic as something that could be drawn, contained, studied, and shaped through knowledge, materials, and Divine influence.

Later Orrian society would integrate magic so deeply into everyday life that merchants, laborers, musicians, and families could use enchanted objects and spells for ordinary tasks.

The change that came at the end of this period was therefore not the creation of magic itself. It was a dramatic change in mortal access to it.

Commander Q // Observation

The phrase "Gift of Magic" can make the earlier world sound magically empty. It wasn't. The difference was access. Magic already existed; what changed was who could reach it and how much they could wield.

XII

The balance is about to change

By 1 BE, Human civilizations had spread across much of the known world, the Gods remained central to Human religion, and magic already formed part of mortal society.

Then Abaddon dramatically expanded mortal access to magical power.

A surviving history of the Bloodstones remembers Abaddon giving access to magic that had previously been held by the Gods. Whatever the precise mechanics, the effect was profound. Power that had once been more limited became available on a far greater scale.

Conflict followed.

The ancient Bloodstone, created by the Seers during the previous Dragonrise and later held among the treasures of Arah, was about to return to the center of Tyrian history.

End of Volume III

The road continues

By the end of this age, Human civilization had taken root across several regions and developed distinct kingdoms, empires, religions, and magical traditions.

The next change would affect far more than Humanity. Greater access to magic brought extraordinary possibilities, but it also gave old rivalries and new ambitions far more destructive power.
