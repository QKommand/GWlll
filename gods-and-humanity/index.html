THE GODS ENTER TYRIA

Volume III // The Divine and Human Age

Gods and Humanity

On the arrival of the Human Gods, the migration of Humanity into Tyria, and the civilizations that rose under Divine influence.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

A new people enters an old world

The previous records followed civilizations whose histories reached back into the age of the Elder Dragons. Humanity belongs to a different chapter. Humans were not native to Tyria, and the Gods they worshipped did not create the world they eventually came to inhabit.

Both arrived from beyond Tyria. The Human Gods crossed from the Mists, while surviving traditions maintain that Humanity was brought from another world under their guidance. By the time Humans began establishing civilizations of their own, they were settling lands already marked by the ruins, conflicts, and magical inheritance of peoples who had come before them.

This is therefore not the beginning of Tyrian history. It is the point where Humanity enters that history and gradually becomes one of its dominant political forces.

— Commander Q

I

The Gods cross from the Mists

At an unknown point before 786 BE, powerful Divine beings crossed from the Mists and entered Tyria. Human traditions remember them as the Gods who would guide Humanity through much of its early history.

The older Divine generation included Dwayna, Balthazar, Melandru, Lyssa, Abaddon, and Dhuum. The pantheon familiar to later Humans changed over time. Most notably, Grenth, who was born on Tyria, eventually defeated Dhuum and took his place as the God of Death.

According to the Orrian History Scrolls, Dwayna was the first of the Gods to appear in Tyria. These accounts survive through Human religious tradition, which makes them valuable sources while also reminding us that much of this early period is being remembered through the cultures that followed.

Research Note // The Six

The membership of the Six changed over time. Dhuum belonged to an older generation before Grenth replaced him, while Kormir would eventually replace Abaddon much later in Human history.

II

The Gods establish themselves in Orr

The earliest known Divine presence in Tyria is associated with the Artesian Waters, a powerful subterranean source of magic beneath the region that would become Orr. The Gods were drawn to this magical center after entering the world.

They established themselves around the Waters, and Arah eventually rose above them. Known as the City of the Gods, Arah became the center of Divine activity and later the spiritual heart of the Human kingdom of Orr.

The land surrounding Arah was very different from the Orr known in the modern age. Ancient accounts remember a fertile region strongly associated with Melandru and the presence of the Gods. The catastrophes that eventually transformed Orr still lay far in the future.

Commander Q // Observation

Arah is sometimes treated as though its construction marks the beginning of civilization itself. It does not. The Gods established their city in a world whose oldest ruins were already ancient when they arrived.

III

Humanity comes from another world

Humanity did not originate on Tyria. Human traditions and later scholarship describe the Gods as bringing Humans from another world connected to the Mists, although the identity and location of that original homeland have not survived in the historical record.

The Orrian traditions associate Dwayna particularly closely with this migration. They portray her as leading her people toward a new home and a more peaceful existence, although the exact sequence of Humanity's movement between worlds remains uncertain.

This distinction matters because the arrival of the Gods, the arrival of Humanity, and the establishment of the first securely dated Human settlements are separate points in the record. The centuries between them remain one of the least certain periods of early Human history.

Historical Caution

The surviving chronology does not support treating the arrival of the Gods and the settlement of Cantha as one continuous event. Early Human migration remains incomplete, and later records provide clearer dates than the traditions describing Humanity's first entrance into Tyria.

IV

Humanity establishes itself in Cantha

In 786 BE, Humans arrived along the northern coast of Cantha. This remains the earliest firmly dated Human settlement in the known chronology and provides the first clear anchor for the growth of Human civilization.

Human communities spread throughout the continent and developed into clans, settlements, and regional powers. Over the following centuries, these societies became increasingly organized until political unification created one of Humanity's oldest enduring states.

In 510 BE, Kaineng Tah unified the Human clans and established the Empire of the Dragon. The same period became the foundation of the Canthan Calendar, giving later historians one of the clearest chronological systems inherited from ancient Human civilization.

V

Human settlement reaches Orr and Elona

By 205 BE, Human groups had arrived by sea in Orr, Istan, and the southern regions of Elona. The surviving record describes these migrants as arriving from unknown lands and does not preserve a complete route connecting them to earlier Human populations.

In Orr, Human civilization developed around Arah and the sacred lands associated with the Gods. In Elona, settlement produced a powerful monarchy within only a few generations.

By 200 BE, the Primeval Dynasty had begun, with Fahranur, the First City serving as the seat of the Primeval Kings. Human civilization was now taking distinct forms across multiple regions of the world.

VI

The Margonites build a maritime civilization

By approximately 175 BE, Margonite communities had appeared along the coasts north and west of Elona. Their ships traveled widely across the Unending Ocean, and maritime trade became one of the defining features of their civilization.

The Margonites also developed a particularly strong devotion to Abaddon, whose domains included Water and Knowledge during this period. That relationship would eventually place them at the center of one of the greatest conflicts between the Gods.

The Margonites of this era were still Human. Their later transformation into the demonic beings known from subsequent history occurred much later and belongs to the story of Abaddon's rebellion and fall.

VII

Humanity enters Ascalon

By approximately 100 BE, Human expansion had reached Ascalon, a region whose history already included other peoples and the growing military power of the Charr.

The Charr had previously conquered much of the region from populations such as the Grawl. Human expansion challenged Charr control and forced them northward from portions of the territory they had claimed.

The resulting struggle did not end with the first Human settlements. Competition over Ascalon became one of the longest conflicts in the history of both Humans and Charr, with generations on each side developing their own claims to the land.

Commander Q // Observation

Ascalon's history becomes easier to understand once we stop looking for a single moment when the land became someone's rightful homeland. Peoples conquered, settled, lost, reclaimed, and remembered the same territory across generations. Later conflicts carried all of those memories at once.

VIII

The Gods still intervene in mortal life

Human civilization had expanded considerably by the first century before the Exodus, but the Gods were still remembered as beings capable of intervening directly in mortal affairs. The Scriptures of Melandru preserve one such account from 48 BE.

The scripture tells of Ewan and his tribe, who rejected magic and repeatedly damaged the natural lands through which they traveled. When they attempted to abandon another exhausted campsite, the earth prevented their departure and Melandru appeared before them.

According to the account, Melandru transformed Ewan and his people, binding them physically to the natural world they had mistreated. The story reflects a period when Humans still understood the Gods as active powers capable of appearing, speaking, and permanently changing mortal lives.

Research Note // Religious Sources

Scriptures preserve historical traditions, but they were also written as religious texts. Their accounts are useful parts of the record, provided their theological purpose remains visible alongside the events they describe.

IX

Human civilizations take their place in Tyria

By the end of this period, Humanity had changed from migrant populations guided into Tyria by the Gods into a collection of kingdoms, empires, maritime cultures, and regional societies spread across much of the known world.

Empire of the Dragon

An Imperial civilization rooted in the earliest securely dated Human settlement.

Elona

Primeval Dynasty

A powerful Human monarchy whose earliest rulers established themselves in Istan.

Orr

Sacred Kingdom

Human civilization developed around Arah and the Divine center of Human religious life.

Ascalon

Human settlement entered a region already shaped by Charr conquest and older populations.

Margonites

Maritime Civilization

A seafaring Human culture whose growing devotion to Abaddon would eventually carry enormous consequences.

These societies did not form one unified Human world. Their governments, calendars, traditions, and rivalries developed along different paths, even while worship of the Gods created cultural connections between many of them.

X

Abaddon changes the balance

By 1 BE, Humanity had spread across much of the known world while the Gods remained deeply involved in mortal civilization. At this point, Abaddon made a decision that would transform the relationship between mortals, magic, and the Divine.

The ancient Bloodstone, created by the Seers long before Humanity arrived in Tyria, had eventually come into the possession of the Gods. Abaddon used its power to grant widespread access to magic.

Magic opened extraordinary possibilities, but its spread also gave mortal societies new ways to pursue conquest, rivalry, and war. The gift quickly became a problem that the Gods themselves could no longer ignore.

The next record follows that decision and its consequences, returning an ancient artifact from the age of the Elder Races to the center of Human history.
