Historical Archive // Record 03

The Gods and Humanity

Ancient Era – 1 BE

The previous archive left Tyria in the long aftermath of the Elder Dragonrise. The Elder Races had survived, but their civilizations followed very different paths as the world moved into another age. Into that already ancient world came powerful beings from the Mists, followed by a people whose descendants would eventually establish kingdoms across much of the known world.

This is where the history of the Six Gods and Humanity enters the Tyrian record.

Recorded by Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights // Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Researcher's Note

The earliest history of Humanity requires some care. Humans did not originate on the world of Tyria, and surviving records do not identify their original homeland with certainty. The Six Gods brought Humanity into Tyria from elsewhere, but the chronology between the arrival of the Gods and the first firmly dated Human civilizations remains incomplete.

What can be established is that the Gods entered a world already shaped by the Elder Dragons and the civilizations that survived them. Their arrival did not begin Tyrian history. It began another chapter within it.

Record 01 // From the Mists

The Six Gods enter Tyria

Sometime before 786 BE, the divine beings later worshipped as the Six Human Gods entered Tyria from the Mists. Orrian tradition remembers Dwayna as the first to arrive, followed by Balthazar, Melandru, Lyssa, and Abaddon. Grenth would later take his place among the Six.

They entered neither an empty world nor a young one. Tyria already possessed ancient magic, the scars of the Elder Dragons, and civilizations whose histories stretched thousands of years into the past.

Commander Q // Field Observation

It is easy to mistake the beginning of Human history for the beginning of Tyrian history. The ruins say otherwise. By the time the Gods arrived, they were walking into a story that had already survived several endings.

Record 02 // Orr

The Gods establish themselves in Orr

The earliest known divine presence in Tyria is closely associated with the Artesian Waters, a powerful magical spring beneath Orr. The Gods were drawn to this source of magic and eventually established Arah above it.

Arah became the great city of the Gods and the spiritual center of ancient Orr. Long before the ruined and corrupted landscape known to modern Tyrians, Orr was remembered as a fertile and beautiful land where divine beings openly walked among mortals.

From this center, the influence of the Gods spread through the cultures that gathered around them. Temples, traditions, magical practices, and eventually entire civilizations would carry their influence far beyond Orr itself.

Record 03 // Humanity

Humanity is brought into an older world

Humanity came to Tyria from somewhere beyond the world, brought by the Gods whose worship would become inseparable from early Human civilization. The identity of Humanity's original homeland remains unresolved, as does the complete route by which the first Human populations reached the regions where they later established themselves.

This uncertainty creates an important gap in the archive. The arrival of the Gods and the earliest securely dated Human civilizations cannot simply be treated as a single event. Somewhere between those points lies a period of migration and settlement whose full history has not survived.

Historical Caution

The absence of a complete record should not be replaced with certainty. The archive distinguishes between what surviving sources establish and what remains unknown.

Record 04 // 786 BE

The first firmly dated Human presence appears in Cantha

In 786 BE, Humans arrived along the northern coastline of Cantha. This marks the earliest firmly dated Human presence preserved in the known historical record.

Human communities spread across the continent over the generations that followed. Their settlements grew into organized societies, and those societies eventually formed one of the oldest enduring Human civilizations known to Tyrian history.

Record 05 // Cantha

The Empire of the Dragon is founded

By 510 BE, Human settlement in Cantha had developed far beyond scattered communities. Lord Emperor Kaineng Tah unified the Human clans and established the Empire of the Dragon, creating a political order whose history would continue across many centuries.

His reign also established the beginning of the Canthan Calendar, giving later historians one of the clearest chronological anchors available for early Human history.

Record 06 // 205 BE

Human settlement reaches Orr and Elona

In 205 BE, Human tribes arrived by sea in Orr, Istan, and the southern valleys of Elona. Surviving accounts describe these settlers as arriving from unknown lands, leaving their precise relationship to the earlier Humans of Cantha uncertain.

These migrations established the foundations for several major Human civilizations. Orr grew around the sacred presence of Arah, while the communities of Elona developed their own kingdoms and traditions.

Record 07 // Elona

The Primeval Dynasty begins

Around 200 BE, the first of the Primeval Kings ruled from Fahranur, the First City, in Istan. This marked the beginning of the Primeval Dynasty and the Dynastic Reckoning calendar.

Within only a few generations of the migrations recorded in 205 BE, organized Human kingdoms had therefore begun to emerge in Elona. Humanity was no longer merely entering new lands. It was establishing states capable of preserving dynasties, institutions, and historical records of their own.

Record 08 // The Margonites

A Human civilization turns toward the sea

By 175 BE, the Margonites had established settlements along the coastlines north and west of Elona. Their ships became a major presence upon the Unending Ocean, establishing them as a maritime Human civilization.

The Margonites of this period should not be confused with the transformed beings encountered in later history. They began as Humans. Their eventual demonic transformation came through their devotion to Abaddon and belongs to a later chapter of this archive.

Record 09 // Ascalon

Human expansion reaches Charr territory

By approximately 100 BE, Humanity had established itself in Ascalon, but the region was not an unclaimed frontier. The Charr already held power there, and Human expansion forced them northward from lands they had previously conquered.

The struggle that followed became more than a dispute over territory. Ascalon would develop into a homeland central to Human identity while remaining part of the ancestral history of the Charr. The competing claims created a wound that would remain open for centuries.

Commander Q // Researcher's Observation

Ascalon's history makes more sense when neither side is treated as arriving in an empty land. Kingdoms remember borders. Peoples remember what existed before the borders were drawn. Those memories do not always agree.

Record 10 // 48 BE

Melandru intervenes directly in mortal life

The Scriptures of Melandru preserve an account from 48 BE in which the Goddess directly confronted a wandering Human tribe whose passage damaged the natural world. Their leader, Ewan, belonged to a people who rejected magic and threatened those among them who practiced it.

According to the scripture, when the tribe attempted to abandon the land they had harmed, thorned growth blocked their path and a great tree rose before them. Melandru revealed herself and bound the tribe to the land, transforming them so that branches grew from their bodies and their blood became like the sap of trees.

Whether read as religious testimony, historical record, or both, the account preserves something important about this period: only forty-eight years before the Exodus, Human tradition still remembered the Gods as beings capable of appearing before mortals and directly altering their lives.

Record 11 // A Human Age

Humanity becomes a power in the known world

By the final years before the Exodus, Human civilization had spread across much of the known world. The peoples who had once entered an ancient Tyria now possessed kingdoms, dynasties, trade networks, religions, rivalries, and territorial ambitions of their own.

Record 12 // The Turning Point

The Divine Age approaches its breaking point

By 1 BE, the Gods had influenced Human civilization for generations, while Humanity itself had become one of the major political forces of the world. The relationship between mortals and the divine was about to change because of something that had shaped Tyria long before either Humanity or the Gods arrived: magic.

Abaddon would grant widespread access to magical power. What followed would reveal how quickly a divine gift could become a mortal weapon.

The resulting conflict would eventually draw the Gods back to the ancient Bloodstone, connecting Humanity's young civilizations to a solution first created by the Seers during the age of the Elder Dragons.

Commander Q // Closing Note

This is one of the reasons I keep following the road backward. Tyrian history rarely leaves anything buried forever. A tool created to survive the Elder Dragons would eventually decide how Humanity itself was allowed to wield magic.
