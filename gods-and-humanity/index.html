Volume III // The Divine and Human Age

Gods and Humanity

The arrival of the Six, the spread of Humanity, the rise of Arah, and the age before the Gift of Magic.

Compiled and Annotated By

Author's Preface

When the Gods entered Tyria

The next great change in the historical record begins with the arrival of beings from the Mists and the people who followed them into Tyria.

The earliest accounts survive mainly through Orrian religious tradition. Later centuries become easier to place as Human settlements, dynasties, kingdoms, and calendars enter the record.

Historical Caution // Divine Tradition

Early Orrian accounts combine sacred memory and history. Their broad events are important, but poetic details should not always be read as precise chronology.

— Commander Q

I

The Gods arrive from the Mists

Orrian tradition remembers Dwayna as the first of the Gods to enter Tyria from the Mists.

The same traditions describe Balthazar, Melandru, and the twins Ilya and Lyss joining the Divine presence in Orr.

Their earliest history became closely tied to Orr, the Artesian Waters, and the land where Arah would become the center of Divine life.

II

Humanity enters Tyria

Humanity came from another world and crossed the Mists under the guidance of the Gods.

The exact cause of that migration and the fate of the Human world of origin remain unknown.

From this shared beginning, Human populations spread into different regions and eventually developed into distinct cultures and political powers.

III

Cantha becomes the oldest Human center

By 786 BE, Human settlement appears firmly in the chronology of Cantha.

Communities developed into clans and regional powers until Kaineng Tah united them in 510 BE.

His rule established the political foundation of the Empire of the Dragon and the beginning of the Canthan Calendar.

IV

Arah becomes the City of the Gods

In Orr, Arah grew into the great Divine and Human capital.

The city contained temples, gardens, plazas, royal estates, crypts, vaults, shipyards, defensive works, and districts devoted to both religious and civic life.

The Gods remained physically present there, while Human government, scholarship, worship, and commerce developed around them.

V

Arah preserves the ancient world

Arah also became a repository for powerful artifacts and knowledge from civilizations older than Humanity.

Among the objects brought into the city were the original Bloodstone created by the Seers and an ancient Jotun Mystic Telescope.

Abaddon designed magically protected reliquaries for Divine relics and knowledge, while other parts of Arah contained equipment associated with the study and handling of Bloodstone material.

The city therefore served as residence, capital, treasury, archive, and center of magical study.

Commander Q // Observation

Arah did not stand apart from the ancient world. Its collections show the Gods preserving and studying works created long before Humanity reached Tyria.

VI

The pantheon changes

The pantheon later remembered as the Six did not remain unchanged throughout its history.

Dhuum preceded Grenth as ruler of the Underworld. Grenth later defeated him and took his place among the Gods.

Abaddon remained part of the pantheon during this period as the God of Knowledge, Secrets, and Water.

Long before his rebellion, he was therefore already an established member of the Divine order centered in Arah.

VII

The Gods withdraw from ordinary public life

The Gods eventually stopped living openly among their followers and withdrew into Arah.

Orrian tradition connects this change with Malchor, the sculptor commissioned to preserve accurate likenesses of the Gods after prolonged exposure to their radiance proved dangerous to mortals.

This retreat was not the later Exodus. The Gods remained physically present in Tyria and continued influencing Human affairs from Arah.

VIII

Human civilizations spread

Human settlement continued beyond Cantha as distinct political centers formed across Tyria.

In 205 BE, Humans entered the record in Orr and Istan. Around 200 BE, the Primeval Dynasty began its rule in Elona.

Human expansion also reached Ascalon, where settlement brought lasting territorial conflict with the Charr.

Cantha, Elona, Orr, and Ascalon developed into separate societies rather than branches of one unified Human kingdom.

IX

The Margonites rule the Crystal Sea

The Margonites developed into a powerful Human maritime civilization around the Crystal Sea and its coastlines.

Their ships traveled widely, and their surviving traditions contain some of the earliest known references to Castora.

Margonite culture became closely associated with Abaddon, but the people of this period were still Human. Their later transformation belongs to the crisis surrounding his rebellion.

X

Magic is regulated under Divine rule

By the reign of King Doric, magic was already part of Human civilization.

In Orr it supported daily life, craftsmanship, communication, and public infrastructure.

More powerful access could also be regulated. Orrian histories describe peacekeeping guilds receiving magical authority through the High Council and the Six Gods.

The important distinction is that magic existed before the event later called the Gift of Magic. What changed afterward was the scale of access.

XI

Abaddon expands mortal access to magic

In 1 BE, Abaddon dramatically expanded mortal access to magical power.

This was not the creation of magic. Mortals had already studied and used it for generations.

The change was one of availability. Power that had been limited, controlled, or harder to reach became far more widespread.

The resulting conflicts would drive King Doric to Arah and force the Gods to reconsider how mortal magic was governed.

That crisis begins the next record.

Archive Note

What remains uncertain

Humanity's world of origin

Humanity came from beyond Tyria, but the location, condition, and fate of its original world remain unknown.

The earliest form of Arah

Orrian sacred tradition refers to the stones of Arah before other accounts describe the city being built. Whether this reflects an earlier structure or poetic language remains unresolved.

The Bloodstones' Unmaking

Bloodstone-related equipment existed within Arah, but the surviving record does not securely establish every function of that machinery.

End of Volume III

The balance of magic breaks

Humanity had spread across several major regions, Arah stood at the center of Divine life, and magic already supported both government and daily life.

Abaddon's decision to expand access to that power changed the balance.
