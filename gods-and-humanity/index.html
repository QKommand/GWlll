THE GODS ENTER TYRIA

Volume III // The Divine and Human Age

Gods and Humanity

On the arrival of the Human Gods, Humanity's passage through the Mists, and the civilizations that grew across an ancient and magical world.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

A new people enters an old world

The previous records followed civilizations whose histories reached back into the age of the Elder Dragons. Humanity belongs to a later chapter. Humans were not native to Tyria, and the Gods they followed did not create the world that eventually became their home.

Humanity came from a realm beyond the Mists and was brought to Tyria by the Six Gods. The reason their people left their original world is still not fully understood, but the journey succeeded. Humans survived the passage and began building settlements in a world already filled with ancient peoples, powerful magic, and the remains of civilizations far older than their own.

Their rise was not immediate. Human communities grew across different regions and followed different paths, eventually becoming the kingdoms, empires, guilds, and maritime powers remembered by later history. This record follows that long beginning, before the Gift of Magic changed the balance between mortals and the Gods.

— Commander Q

I

The Gods cross from the Mists

At an unknown point before 786 BE, powerful Divine beings crossed from the Mists and entered Tyria. Human traditions remember them as the Gods who guided Humanity through much of its early history.

The older Divine generation included Dwayna, Balthazar, Melandru, Lyssa, Abaddon, and Dhuum. The pantheon familiar to later Humans changed over time. Most notably, Grenth, who was born on Tyria, eventually defeated Dhuum and took his place as the God of Death.

According to the Orrian History Scrolls, Dwayna was the first of the Gods to appear in Tyria. These accounts survive through Human religious tradition, making them valuable records of an age for which few direct witnesses remain.

Research Note // The Six

The membership of the Six changed over time. Dhuum belonged to an older generation before Grenth replaced him, while Kormir would eventually replace Abaddon much later in Human history.

II

The Gods establish themselves in Orr

The earliest known Divine presence in Tyria is closely tied to the Artesian Waters, a powerful source of magic beneath the land that would become Orr. The region was so rich in magical energy that it drew the Gods after their arrival from the Mists.

They established themselves around the Waters, and Arah eventually rose above them. Known as the City of the Gods, Arah became the center of Divine activity and later the spiritual heart of Human Orr.

The surrounding land was not empty. Orr possessed an unusually rich magical environment where magic could gather in the soil, plants, and living landscape. During the age that followed, this abundance would give rise to magical spirits known collectively as the Vael.

Commander Q // Observation

Arah is sometimes treated as though its construction marks the beginning of civilization in Orr. It does not. The Gods entered a world whose oldest histories were already ancient, and even the land around their new city possessed a magical life of its own.

III

Humanity comes from beyond the Mists

Humanity did not originate on Tyria. The Six Gods brought Humans from another realm beyond the Mists after their people left an earlier world whose identity has not survived in the historical record.

The exact reason for that departure remains uncertain. What is clear is that the migration was deliberate. Humanity crossed the Mists under Divine guidance and survived long enough to begin making a new home in Tyria.

The Orrian traditions associate Dwayna especially closely with this journey, portraying her as a guiding figure in Humanity's search for a new beginning. The surviving accounts do not provide a complete route between Humanity's original world and its earliest settlements, leaving much of that migration beyond our reach.

Historical Caution

The arrival of the Gods, Humanity's crossing of the Mists, and the later creation of firmly dated Human settlements should not be treated as a single event. The broad sequence is known, but many of the dates between those stages remain uncertain.

IV

Humanity establishes itself in Cantha

In 786 BE, Humans arrived along the northern coast of Cantha. This remains the earliest firmly dated Human settlement in the known chronology and provides the first clear anchor for the growth of Human civilization.

Human communities spread throughout the continent and developed into clans, settlements, and regional powers. Over the following centuries, these societies became increasingly organized until political unification created one of Humanity's oldest enduring states.

In 510 BE, Kaineng Tah unified the Human clans and established the Empire of the Dragon. The same period became the foundation of the Canthan Calendar, giving later historians one of the clearest chronological systems inherited from ancient Human civilization.

V

Human settlement grows in Orr

Humanity also established itself in Orr long before the Gift of Magic. The region became one of Humanity's great frontiers, growing beneath the presence of Arah while settlers, explorers, craftsmen, and fortune-seekers moved into lands filled with unusually strong natural magic.

The Vael were already part of that landscape. These magical spirits appeared throughout Orr in many forms and were closely tied to the magical abundance of the region. Human settlement brought both wonder and conflict as new communities attempted to live beside, protect, study, or profit from them.

During the earliest period of Human settlement, a catastrophe caused one of the first great losses among the Vael. Those devoted to protecting the spirits appealed to the Gods for help. Dwayna and Melandru supported their cause and sanctioned the creation of the Order of the Vael, the first guild chartered in Orr.

The Vaelwardens established themselves in the eastern Tughra Basin and began a watch that would last for more than a century and a half. Their task was not simply to guard land. They acted as mediators between the people settling Orr and the magical life that already existed there.

Research Note // Orr's Early Settlement

Older summaries often placed major Human settlement in Orr near 205 BE. The surviving record now shows that Humanity had already been living and organizing guilds in Orr well before that date. The exact year of the earliest Human settlement remains uncertain.

VI

Human kingdoms rise in Elona

By 205 BE, Human settlement had become firmly established in Istan and the southern lands of Elona. These communities developed quickly, supported by trade, agriculture, and their position along important routes between the surrounding seas and inland regions.

By 200 BE, the Primeval Dynasty had begun, with Fahranur, the First City serving as the seat of the Primeval Kings. Elona became one of Humanity's great centers of political and cultural power.

Human civilization was no longer following a single path. Cantha had already developed centuries of Imperial tradition, Orr was growing around its powerful magical landscape and Divine center, and Elona was building a monarchy of its own.

VII

The Margonites rule the Crystal Sea

By the second century before the Exodus, the Margonites had become one of the important Human peoples of the southern world. They were a seafaring civilization whose ships traveled the waters around Elona and the Crystal Sea.

Their strength rested along the coast. The Margonites came to rule the Crystal Sea and many miles of the coastline surrounding it, giving them influence over a large maritime region rather than a single isolated settlement. Their ships and coastal holdings connected them to trade and movement across southern Tyria.

Margonite society was also closely tied to the worship of Abaddon, then the God of Water and Knowledge. This devotion became one of the defining features of their civilization and would eventually draw them into the conflict that followed Abaddon's rebellion.

The Margonites of this period were still Human. Their later transformation into the beings encountered during the Nightfall crisis happened after they followed Abaddon into a very different chapter of history.

Commander Q // Observation

Later history makes it easy to remember the Margonites only as servants of a fallen God. That misses what came first. Before their transformation, they were a Human maritime power ruling a sea that no longer exists.

VIII

Humanity enters Ascalon

By approximately 100 BE, Human expansion had reached Ascalon, the youngest of the major Human holdings that developed during this period.

The region was not unclaimed. The Charr had already built their own power there after earlier conflicts with peoples such as the Grawl. Human settlement challenged that control and forced the Charr northward from portions of the territory.

The resulting struggle continued far beyond the first generation of settlers. Ascalon became a land of borders, armies, and competing memories, with both Humans and Charr developing strong claims to the same territory.

Commander Q // Observation

Ascalon's history becomes easier to understand once we stop looking for one moment when the land became someone's permanent possession. People conquered, settled, lost, reclaimed, and remembered the same ground across generations.

IX

The Gods remain part of mortal life

Human civilization had expanded considerably by the first century before the Exodus, but the Gods were still remembered as beings capable of intervening directly in mortal affairs. The Scriptures of Melandru preserve one such account from 48 BE.

The scripture tells of Ewan and his tribe, who rejected magic and repeatedly damaged the natural lands through which they traveled. When they attempted to abandon another exhausted campsite, the earth prevented their departure and Melandru appeared before them.

According to the account, Melandru transformed Ewan and his people, binding them physically to the natural world they had mistreated. The story reflects a period when Humans still understood the Gods as active powers capable of appearing, speaking, and permanently changing mortal lives.

Research Note // Religious Sources

Scriptures preserve historical traditions, but they were also written as religious texts. Their accounts remain useful parts of the record when their theological purpose is kept in view.

X

The Orrian Golden Age

In the years before the Gift of Magic, Orr had grown into a rich and changing Human frontier. The Elder Dragons remained deep in their slumber, allowing the region's natural magic to flourish without the great predators that had consumed it during earlier ages.

The Vael became one of the clearest signs of that abundance. Some Humans treated them with respect, while others saw Orr's magical landscape as a source of wealth waiting to be used. Magic could crystallize in the soil, gather within plants, and produce valuable materials that drew settlers and fortune-seekers from across Tyria.

Guilds became increasingly important to Orrian life. The old Vaelwardens continued their effort to protect the spirits, while newer groups such as the Mavenwrights argued that Orr's magical resources could be used to build a stronger and more advanced future.

The Gods had also allowed limited mortal use of magic long before the later Gift. Dwayna and Melandru had granted the Vaelwardens the knowledge needed to create and refine signets, small gems that held fixed expressions of magical power. Over time, other guilds gained access to signet-making, and these objects became valuable enough to be traded, stolen, and sold through markets far beyond Orr.

This did not mean that mortals possessed the broad magical freedom that came later. Access remained controlled and uneven, often tied to Divine approval, guild knowledge, or crafted artifacts. Even so, the Orrian Golden Age makes one point clear: mortal magic did not begin in 1 BE.

The political world was changing as well. The Vaelwardens had already guarded Orr for more than one hundred and fifty years, Dhuum's removal from the pantheon was still remembered as a recent Divine event, and the Gods were becoming less involved in the daily affairs of the guilds beneath Arah.

Commander Q // Observation

This period changes how I read the later Gift of Magic. Abaddon did not introduce mortals to something they had never touched. Orr already had guilds using controlled forms of magic. What came next was a change in scale.

XI

Humanity becomes a world of many powers

By the end of this period, Humanity had changed from a people brought through the Mists into a collection of kingdoms, empires, guilds, frontier settlements, and maritime societies spread across much of the known world.

Cantha

Empire of the Dragon

Humanity's oldest major center, already carrying centuries of dynasties and Imperial tradition.

Elona

Primeval Dynasty

A prosperous Human monarchy centered around Istan and connected to wider trade and political life.

Orr

The Great Frontier

A magically rich Human society growing beneath Arah, shaped by guilds, the Vael, and competing ideas about progress.

Ascalon

Youngest Human Holding

A proud and heavily contested frontier built through conflict with the Charr.

Margonites

Rulers of the Crystal Sea

A maritime Human civilization devoted to Abaddon and dominant across the Crystal Sea and its surrounding coastlines.

These societies did not form a single Human nation. Their governments, beliefs, guilds, economies, and regional identities developed in different directions. What connected many of them was their shared history of migration and their relationship with the Gods who had brought their ancestors to Tyria.

XII

Abaddon changes the balance

By 1 BE, magic was already part of mortal life in limited forms. The Vaelwardens and other Orrian guilds had used signets for generations, while older races possessed magical traditions reaching back thousands of years.

What changed under Abaddon was the reach and scale of that power. Greater access to magic was opened to the mortal races, moving it beyond the narrower systems that had controlled its use before.

The ancient Bloodstone, created by the Seers during the previous Dragonrise, had long since entered the possession of the Gods. It would soon become central to their attempt to manage the consequences of this new age of magic.
