THE GODS ENTER TYRIA

Volume III // The Divine and Human Age

Gods and Humanity

On the arrival of the Human Gods, the migration of Humanity into Tyria, and the civilizations that rose under Divine influence.

Compiled and Annotated By

Commander Q

Captain Commander of the Vanguard Knights, an offshoot of the Ascalon Knights
Researcher of Tyrian History and the Mists

Author's Preface

A new people enters an old world

The previous records followed civilizations whose histories reached back into the age of the Elder Dragons. Humanity belongs to a different chapter. Humans were not native to Tyria, and the Gods they worshipped did not create the world they eventually came to inhabit.

Both arrived from beyond Tyria. The Human Gods crossed from the Mists, while surviving traditions maintain that Humanity was brought from another world under their guidance. By the time Humans began establishing civilizations of their own, they were settling lands already marked by the ruins, conflicts, and magical inheritance of peoples who had come before them.

This is therefore not the beginning of Tyrian history. It is the point where Humanity enters that history and gradually becomes one of its dominant political forces.

— Commander Q

I

The Gods cross from the Mists

At an unknown point before 786 BE, powerful Divine beings crossed from the Mists and entered Tyria. Human tradition remembers them as the Gods who would guide Humanity through much of its early history.

The original Divine company included Dwayna, Balthazar, Melandru, Lyssa, Abaddon, and Dhuum. The pantheon familiar to later Humans changed over time. Most notably, Grenth, who was born on Tyria, eventually defeated Dhuum and took his place as the God associated with Death.

According to the Orrian History Scrolls, Dwayna was the first of the Gods to appear in Tyria. These accounts were written through a Human religious tradition, so they remain valuable historical sources without necessarily providing a complete account of everything that occurred before Humanity began preserving its own records.

Research Note // The Six

Referring to the "Six Human Gods" across the entire historical record can hide an important detail: the membership of the Six changed. Dhuum belonged to the older Divine generation, while Grenth later replaced him. Kormir would eventually replace Abaddon thousands of years afterward.

II

The Artesian Waters draw the Gods to Orr

The earliest known Divine presence in Tyria is associated with the Artesian Waters, a powerful subterranean source of magic beneath the region that would become Orr. The magic concentrated there was powerful enough to draw the attention of the Gods after they entered the world.

The Gods established themselves around this magical center and transformed the surrounding region. Arah eventually rose above the Artesian Waters and became known as the City of the Gods, serving as the center of Divine activity in Tyria.

Later generations would remember Orr as a fertile and sacred land rather than the ruined kingdom familiar to the modern age. The contrast is worth remembering, because nearly every later account of Orr is shaped by catastrophes that had not yet occurred.

Commander Q // Observation

Arah is sometimes treated as though its construction marks the beginning of civilization itself. It does not. The Gods built their city over a world whose oldest ruins were already ancient by the time they arrived.

III

Humanity comes from another world

Humanity did not originate on Tyria. Human traditions and later scholarship agree that the Gods brought Humans from another world connected to the Mists, although the name, location, and ultimate fate of that homeland remain unknown.

The surviving Orrian accounts associate Dwayna especially closely with this migration. They describe her as bringing her people to Tyria in search of a more peaceful future, while other passages suggest that memories of Humanity's earlier existence were allowed to fade.

The exact sequence between the Gods' arrival, Humanity's entrance into Tyria, and the later settlement of the continents cannot be reconstructed with certainty. What we do possess are increasingly reliable dates once Human settlements begin appearing in regional records.

Historical Caution

The arrival of the Gods, the arrival of Humanity, and the establishment of the first securely dated Human settlements should not be treated as a single event. The sources leave an uncertain period between these developments.

IV

The first firmly dated Human settlement

In 786 BE, Humans were brought to the northern coast of Cantha. This remains the earliest securely dated Human settlement preserved in the known chronology, although scattered traditions leave open the possibility that Human communities may have existed elsewhere before this date.

From these settlements, Human populations expanded throughout Cantha and formed communities that would eventually develop into one of the longest-lasting civilizations in Human history.

In 510 BE, Kaineng Tah unified the Human clans and established the Empire of the Dragon. The same period became the foundation of the Canthan Calendar, giving later historians one of the clearest chronological anchors from the ancient Human world.

V

Human settlement spreads westward

By 205 BE, Human groups had arrived by sea in Orr, Istan, and the southern regions of Elona. The surviving record describes them as coming from unknown lands and does not preserve a simple migration route connecting every Human population of this era.

In Orr, Human society developed around the sacred territory of Arah and the presence of the Gods. In Elona, settlement quickly produced a powerful monarchy. By 200 BE, the Primeval Dynasty had begun, with Fahranur, the First City serving as the seat of the Primeval Kings.

Human history was therefore no longer concentrated in one region. Within a relatively short span of the surviving chronology, distinct societies were forming across Cantha, Elona, and the Tyrian continent.

VI

The Margonites take to the sea

By approximately 175 BE, Margonite communities had appeared along the coasts north and west of Elona. Their ships became an important presence upon the Unending Ocean, and their culture developed a particularly strong relationship with Abaddon.

It is important to distinguish these early Margonites from the demonic beings encountered much later in history. The Margonites began as a Human civilization. Their later transformation belongs to the story of Abaddon's fall and should not be projected backward onto their earlier society.

Their growing devotion to Abaddon would nevertheless make them central to events that eventually reshaped the relationship between Humanity and the Gods.

VII

Humanity enters Ascalon

By approximately 100 BE, Human expansion had reached Ascalon, a land whose history already included other peoples and the growing power of the Charr.

The Charr had established their own control over much of the region after conquering indigenous populations such as the Grawl. Human expansion challenged that control and drove the Charr northward from portions of the territory they had claimed.

This was not a brief border dispute. Competition for Ascalon became one of the longest-running conflicts in the history of both peoples, and later generations on each side would remember the land as part of their own inheritance.

Commander Q // Observation

Ascalon's history becomes easier to understand once we stop looking for a single moment when the land became someone's rightful homeland. Peoples conquered, settled, lost, reclaimed, and remembered the same territory across generations. The arguments that followed were built on all of those memories at once.

VIII

The Gods still walked among mortals

Human civilization had expanded considerably by the first century before the Exodus, yet the Gods were still remembered as beings capable of intervening directly in mortal life. The Scriptures of Melandru preserve one such account from 48 BE.

The scripture tells of Ewan and his tribe, who rejected magic and damaged the lands through which they traveled. When they attempted to abandon another exhausted campsite, the earth itself prevented their departure, and Melandru appeared before them.

According to the account, Melandru transformed Ewan and his people, binding them physically to the natural world they had mistreated. Whether read as literal Divine history, religious scripture, or both, the story shows how Humans of this period understood the Gods: not as distant creators remembered from an unreachable past, but as powers who could still appear and act within the world.

Research Note // Religious Sources

Scriptures preserve historical traditions, but their purpose is also religious. I treat their dates and recorded events as part of the historical record while keeping the nature of the source visible rather than presenting every theological detail as neutral observation.

IX

Human civilizations take their place in Tyria

By the end of this period, Humanity had developed from migrant populations guided into Tyria by the Gods into a collection of kingdoms, empires, maritime cultures, and regional societies spread across much of the known world.

Cantha

Empire of the Dragon

An ancient Imperial civilization rooted in the earliest securely dated Human settlement.

Elona

Primeval Dynasty

A monarchy centered first upon Istan and later connected to the wider development of Elona.

Orr

Sacred Kingdom

A Human civilization built around Arah and the Divine heart of the Human faith.

Ascalon

Contested Territory

Human settlement entered a region already shaped by Charr conquest and older native populations.

Margonites

Maritime Civilization

A seafaring Human culture whose devotion to Abaddon would carry enormous consequences.

These societies did not form one unified Human world. Their politics, traditions, calendars, and rivalries developed independently, even while devotion to the Gods provided an important cultural connection between many of them.

X

Abaddon changes the balance

By 1 BE, Humanity had spread across much of the known world while the Gods remained deeply connected to mortal civilization. At this point, Abaddon made a decision that would transform the relationship between mortals, magic, and the Divine.

The ancient Bloodstone, created long before Humanity arrived in Tyria, had eventually come into the possession of the Gods. Abaddon used its power to grant widespread access to magic.

The consequences were immediate and far larger than any one Human kingdom. Magic gave mortals extraordinary new possibilities, but it also provided new means of conquest and destruction.

The history of Humanity had reached another turning point. The Gods had brought Humans into Tyria and helped guide their earliest civilizations. Now one of those Gods was about to give mortals power that would test the order the pantheon had built.

End of Volume III

The road continues

Humanity had established itself across Tyria, and the Gods remained part of mortal life. The next change would come not from another migration or expanding kingdom, but from the sudden spread of magical power.
